## CHAPTER 307

SMALL TENEMENTS (RECOVERY OF POSSESSION) ACT
An Act relating to the recovery of possession of tenements after due determination
of the tenancy.
[Amended by Act No. 1 of 1971.]

[3rd December, 1874.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Small Tenements (Recovery of Possession) Act.
2\. Interpretation
In this Act—
“agent” means a person usually employed by the landlord in the letting of the
premises, or in the collection of the rents thereof, or specially authorised to act in the
particular matter, by writing under the hand of the landlord; and
“landlord” means the person entitled to the immediate reversion of the property, or
if the property is held in joint tenancy, co-partnery or tenancy in common, any one of
the persons entitled to the reversion.
3\. Procedure for obtaining possession of tenements held for a term not exceeding
seven years either without rent or at rent not exceeding three hundred dollars
So soon as the term or interest of the tenant in any house or land held by him or her at
will, or for any term not exceeding seven years, either without being liable to the payment
of any rent, or at a rent not exceeding the rate of three hundred dollars a year, and upon

which no fine shall have been reserved or made payable, shall have ended or shall have
duly determined by a legal notice to quit or otherwise, and the tenant or any occupier of
the house or land or part thereof neglects or refuses to deliver up possession, the landlord
or his or her agent may take out a summons against the tenant or occupier in a
magistrate’s court for recovery of possession; and if the tenant or occupier shall not
thereupon appear at the time and place appointed, and show to the satisfaction of the
magistrate reasonable cause why possession should not be given, the landlord or agent
may give to the magistrate proof of the holding, and of the end or other determination of
the tenancy, and, where the title of the landlord has accrued since the letting of the
premises, the right by which he or she claims the possession; and the magistrate may
issue a warrant to any police officer commanding him or her within a period therein
named, not less than three nor more than ten clear days from the date of the warrant, to
enter (by force if needful) into the premises, and give possession of the same to the
landlord or agent:
Provided that entry upon any such warrant shall not be made on a Sunday, Good
Friday or Christmas Day, or at any time except between the hours of nine in the morning
and four in the afternoon:
Provided also that nothing herein contained shall protect any person on whose
application and to whom any such warrant shall be granted, from any action which may
be brought against him or her by a tenant or occupier in respect of such entry and taking
possession where such person had not, at the time of the granting of the same, lawful
right to the possession of the premises:
Provided also that nothing herein contained shall affect any rights to which a person
may be entitled as outgoing tenant by the custom of the country or otherwise.
4\. Determination of tenancy by failure to pay rent
Where the tenant of any land, house or room held by him or her as a tenant from
month to month or for any shorter period at a rent not exceeding the rate of twenty-five
dollars each month, fails to pay the rent due within fourteen days after the day on which
such rent becomes due and payable, such failure shall be deemed a determination of the
tenancy, and the landlord may proceed to recover possession of the said land, house or
room, under the provisions of this Act.
5\. Remedy of tenant against person obtaining an illegal warrant
In every case in which the person to whom any such warrant shall be granted had not,
at the time of the granting of the same, lawful right to the possession of the premises, the
obtaining of the warrant shall be deemed a trespass by him or her against the tenant or
occupier of the premises, although no entry shall be made by virtue of the warrant; and in
case the tenant or occupier will become bound with two sureties approved by the
magistrate, in such sum as to him or her shall seem reasonable, regard being had to the
value of the premises, and to the probable cost of an action, to sue the person to whom the
warrant was granted with effect and without delay, and to pay all the costs of the
proceedings in such action in case a verdict shall pass for the defendant, or the plaintiff
shall discontinue or not prosecute his or her action or become non-suit therein, execution
of the warrant shall be delayed until judgement shall have been given in such action of
trespass; and if upon the trial of such action of trespass a verdict shall pass for the
plaintiff, such verdict and judgement thereupon shall supersede the warrant.
6\. Bond for trial of action to be at the costs of landlord
Every such bond as hereinbefore mentioned shall be made to the landlord or his or her
agent, at the costs of the landlord or agent, and shall be approved of and signed by the
magistrate.
