## CHAPTER 345

YACHTING ACT
An Act to encourage yachting, to provide for the regulation of yachts generally, and
for matters connected therewith and incidental thereto.
[Act No. 17 of 2000 amended by Act No. 43 of 2000.]

[6th June, 2000.]

## PART I

Preliminary
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Yachting Act.
2\. Interpretation
In this Act—
“arrival” means the anchoring, berthing or mooring of a yacht within the limits of a
port and “arrive” and cognate expressions shall be construed accordingly;
“commercial yacht” means any yacht offered for hire, for payment or for reward,
with or without crew, for the conveyance of passengers for a period of time;
“cruising” includes stopping in any part of the territorial seas of Grenada but does
not include mere passage through the territorial seas of Grenada;
“Director” means the Director of Maritime Affairs appointed for the purposes of
section 4 of the Shipping Act, Chapter 303;
“foreign based yacht” means any yacht other than a home based yacht;
“Grenadian yacht” means any yacht registered or licensed under the Shipping Act,

## Chapter 303;

“home based yacht” means any yacht registered in Grenada; home ported in Grenada,
kept in Grenada when not in operation; and having its charters originate in Grenada;
“import”, “imported” and cognate expressions, except otherwise provided, mean to
bring or cause to be brought into Grenada;
“master” means the person on board a vessel under whose command or subject to
whose personal direction it has arrived or is intended to arrive as a yacht;
“Minister” means the Minister responsible for shipping;

“owner” means any individual, partnership, limited liability company or other body
corporate which has title to the yacht;
“proper officer” means any officer whose right or duty under any enactment it may
be to exact the performance of or to perform the act referred to;
“ship’s stores” means goods as are required for consumption or use by or for the
yacht, its officers, crew and passengers and shall include the bona fide baggage of
such persons;
“territorial sea” means the territorial sea of Grenada as defined in section 5 of the
Territorial Sea and Maritime Boundaries Act, Chapter 318;
“yacht” means any vessel which is used for recreational purposes and either—
(a)

exceeds twenty feet in length overall and is primarily powered by sail; or

(b)

exceeds thirty-eight feet in length overall and is primarily powered other
than by sail.

## PART II

Procedure on Arrival and Departure of Yachts

3\. Procedure on arrival
(1) The master shall cause a yellow flag (the signal flag for “Q” in the International
Code of Signals) to be flown on that yacht at all times between its arrival in Grenada from
outside Grenada and the making of arrival report of that yacht in accordance with this
Act.
(2) Until an arrival report has been made no person shall move the yacht or unload or
remove any thing therefrom, except herein otherwise provided for, without the authority
of the proper officer.
(3) Nothing in this Act shall affect the provision of any enactments relating to public
health in Grenada or any part thereof with respect to a yacht being a vessel within the
meaning of those enactments.
4\. Arrival report
(1) The procedure for making arrival report of a yacht shall be as follows—
(a)

the master, or a person acting on his or her behalf, shall notify the arrival of
that yacht to the proper officer; and

(b)

the master shall deliver to the proper officer such form, duly completed, as
the Minister may direct for that purpose.

(2) The master, or the person acting on his or her behalf, shall notify the arrival of a
yacht within two hours of its arrival.
(3) The procedure for making arrival report in cases where the proper officer boards
the yacht before notification of its arrival shall be limited to that prescribed in
subsection (1)(b).
5\. Departure report

(1) Before departure the master or owner of the yacht shall, save as permitted by the
Minister, make departure report to the proper officer in such form and manner and
containing such particulars as the Minister may by order prescribe.
(2) A departure report shall be made up to twenty-four hours before departure.
(3) The master may make a departure report at the time of arrival provided that the
master intends to depart within seventy-two hours of arrival.
6\. Entry fee
(1) Every Grenadian yacht shall pay an entry fee, not exceeding five hundred dollars,
as the Minister may by order prescribe.
(2) The entry fee shall be paid by the master to the proper officer on arrival in Grenada.

## PART III

Charges and Fees Relating to Yachts and Related Matters
7\. Administrative charge
(1) Notwithstanding any provision to the contrary in the Customs (Control and
Management) Act, Chapter 75A, or the General Consumption Tax Act, Chapter 121A, or
any other Act but subject as hereinafter provided—
(a)

any yacht which is imported into Grenada;

(b)

any goods entered as ship’s stores and to be exported as such;

(c)

any goods entered as ship’s stores in respect of any yacht licensed under
section 10; or

(d)

any goods remaining on board a yacht for re-exportation or use as stores on
the yacht,

shall not be subject to any import duty, tax, levy or other impost whatsoever.
(2) The goods referred to in subsection (1)(b) and (c) shall be subject to the payment
of an administrative charge of two and one-half per cent of C.I.F. value thereof.
8\. Fees and overtime charges
(1) Notwithstanding anything contained in the Port Authority Act, Chapter 247, the
fees chargeable with respect to yachts shall be determined by or under this Act.
(2) Notwithstanding anything contained in the Environmental Levy Act, Chapter 90B,
that Act shall not apply to yachts within the meaning of this Act.
(3) Except as provided by or under this Act, no overtime or other charges shall be
imposed by the proper officer upon the arrival or departure of any yacht.

## PART IV

Cruising Permit and Commercial Licence
9\. Cruising permit

(1) Every yacht, other than a Grenadian yacht, or a yacht in respect of which there is
in effect a commercial licence granted pursuant to section 10 shall pay a cruising permit
fee in accordance with this section.
(2) The cruising permit fee—
(a)

shall be paid by the master to the proper officer upon arrival in Grenada;

(b)

shall be in such amount, not exceeding five hundred dollars, as the Minister
may prescribe by order;

(3) Any fee for the extension of a cruising permit shall be paid to the proper officer.
(4) The master shall produce the cruising permit to the proper officer on demand.
10\. Procedure for obtaining licence
(1) Subject to subsection (2), no person may offer for hire, for payment or for reward,
any unlicensed commercial yacht for use in the territorial waters whether such yacht is
foreign based or home based, or being the owner of any such yacht or the agent of such
owner, give or offer to give or hire, for payment or reward, any such vessel to any other
person for use in the territorial waters without first obtaining a licence.
(2) A foreign based yacht may make a maximum of ten charter pick-ups originating
within Grenada within any period of twelve months without having to obtain a licence.
(3) An application for a licence shall be made by the owner or the operator of the
yacht to the Director in the prescribed form.
(4) At the time of making the initial application the form shall be accompanied by—
(a)

a business plan outlining the proposed operation;

(b)

details of the numbers of persons to be employed on a permanent or seasonal
basis;

(c)

details as to how the operation is to be financed;

(d)

copies of yacht registration documents and insurance certificate.

(5) Where an application is made to the Director in accordance with subsection (3),
the Director may approve or disapprove the application.
(6) Where the Director—
(a)

approves an application, he or she shall issue in the prescribed form;

(b)

disapproves an application, he or she shall, within a period of seven days
after receipt of such application, notify the applicant of such disapproval.

(7) Any person who is aggrieved by a decision of the Director may within seven days
of receipt of notification of such decision, appeal in writing to the Minister, whose
decision shall be final.
11\. Nature of licence
(1) A licence granted under section 10 shall be—
(a)

an annual licence valid for twelve months from the date of issue; or

(b)

a temporary licence for a specified cruise.

(2) An annual licence shall remain valid until it is revoked or cancelled, and a
temporary licence for a specified cruise shall be valid only for the cruise, for which it is
granted, unless the licence is extended by the Director.
(3) A licence granted under section 10 may contain such conditions as the Director
may think it fit to impose with respect to the yacht and the cruise which it may undertake,
and such conditions may be imposed so as to apply to the yacht wherever it may be or
while in such waters or on such cruise as may be specified.
(4) Upon the payment by the applicant of the prescribed fee not exceeding five
thousand dollars, the Director may issue a licence and a licence pennant or other proof of
registration to the applicant.
(5) A licence granted under this Act is not transferable.
(6) The owner or operator of any licensed yacht is responsible for ensuring that the
licence is carried on the yacht and is available for inspection by the proper officer.
12\. Modification, etc., of licence
(1) A licence granted under this Act may be modified, suspended, revoked or
cancelled by the Director but no such licence shall be modified, suspended, revoked or
cancelled unless the licensee has been given an opportunity of making representations
against such modification, suspension, revocation or cancellation.
(2) Where a licence granted under this Act is revoked or cancelled or otherwise ceases
to be valid, the licensee shall, within sixty days after such revocation, cancellation or
cessation, return it or cause it to be returned to the Director.
13\. Register of yachts
(1) The Director shall maintain a register of yachts licensed to operate within the
territorial waters.
(2) The register for the purposes of subsection (1) must be in such form as the
Director determines.

## PART V

Offences, Penalties and Related Matters
14\. Offences and penalties
(1) A person or a master of a yacht, as the case requires, who—
(a)

offers to give or hire such yacht for payment without a licence under section
10;

(b)

operates a yacht, other than a Grenadian yacht, or a yacht licensed under
section 10, without a cruising permit;

(c)

fails or refuses to produce a cruising permit when requested to do so by the
proper officer;

(d)

fails to follow the procedure relating to the arrival of a yacht as set out in
sections 3 and 4; or

(e)

fails to deliver a departure report to the proper officer as set out in section 6,

commits an offence and shall be liable, on summary conviction, in the case of an offence
under paragraph (a) to a fine of ten thousand dollars or imprisonment for three months,
and in the case of offences under paragraphs (b) to (e) to a fine of two thousand five
hundred dollars or imprisonment for one month.

### 15.

Compounding of offences
(1) The Director may compound any offence committed by a person under this Act,
but no such offence shall be compounded where such person has contravened the
provisions of this Act on two previous occasions.
(2) Any offence which is compounded under this section shall include payment of the
full licence for the period the yacht has been in the territorial sea and a penalty of not less
than one half the prescribed fine.

## PART VI

Miscellaneous

### 16.

Regulations
(1) The Minister may make rules, regulations and orders for the purpose of giving
effect to the provisions of this Act.
(2) Without affecting the generality of subsection (1), the Minister may make
regulations, rules and orders prescribing—
(a)

the forms to be used for the purposes of this Act;

(b)

the fees payable under this Act;

(c)

the procedures respecting the arrival and departure of yachts;

(d)

the designation of marine parks; and

(e)

approved anchorages.

### 17.

Repeal and savings
(1) The Yachting Act, No. 38 of 1998, is hereby repealed.
(2) Any licences, permits or other like document issued in relation to a yacht pursuant
to the former Act shall continue in force until expiration as if it had been issued under this
Act.
(3) Any civil or criminal proceedings commenced under the former Act shall continue
until completed as if the former Act had not been repealed.

### 18.

Commencement
This Act comes into force on the 6th day of June, 2000.

YACHTING (FORMS AND FEES) REGULATIONS
[SRO 43 of 2001.]

[5th October, 2001.]
1\. Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Yachting (Forms and Fees) Regulations.
2\. Definition
In these Regulations, “commercial licence” means a licence issued pursuant to section
10 of the Act for use of a commercial yacht for hire or reward.
3\. Arrival and departure report form
The form in the First Schedule is prescribed as the form on which the master of a
yacht must report the arrival or departure of the yacht under section 4 or 5 of the Act, as
the case may be.

4\. Entry fee
The entry fee payable under section 6(1) of the Act by every Grenadian yacht is as set
out in the Second Schedule.
5\. Cruising permit form
The form in the Third Schedule is prescribed as the form for a cruising permit issued
under section 9 of the Act.
6\. Cruising permit fee
(1) The cruising permit fee payable under section 9(1) of the Act by a yacht other
than a Grenadian yacht, or a yacht in respect of which there is in effect a commercial
cruising licence, is as set out in the Fourth Schedule.
(2) A cruising permit is valid for one month or until the yacht leaves Grenada,
whichever is the sooner, but may be renewed upon application to the proper officer and
on payment of the appropriate fee.
(3) No cruising permit fee is payable for any complete calendar month during which
the yacht is on land or in dry dock.
7\. Commercial licence form
The form in the Fifth Schedule is prescribed as the form for a commercial licence
issued under section 10(6) of the Act.
8\. Commercial licence fees
(1) The commercial licence fee payable under section 11(4) of the Act is as set out in
the Sixth Schedule.
(2) A commercial licence may be issued to a person in respect of more than one
yacht.
(3) A commercial licence is valid for one year but may be renewed upon application
to the Director of Maritime Affairs and on payment of the appropriate fee.
