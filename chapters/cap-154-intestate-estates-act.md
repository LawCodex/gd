## CHAPTER 154

INTESTATE ESTATES ACT
An Act relating to the distribution of property on the death of the owner.
[Amended by Act No. 48 of 1991.]

[1st January, 1938.]

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Intestate Estates Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act—
“estate” includes both real and personal property;
“intestate” includes a person who leaves a will but dies intestate as to some
beneficial interest in his or her real or personal property and “estate of an intestate”
includes such beneficial interest;
“issue” means all children, whether born in wedlock or out of wedlock.

### 3.

Abolition of descent to heir, entails, curtesy, dower and escheat
(1) With regard to the real estate of every person dying after the commencement of
this Act there shall be abolished—
(a)

all existing modes, rules and canons of descent, and of devolution by special
occupancy or otherwise;

(b)

tenancy by the curtesy;

(c)

tenancy in tail;

(d)

dower of a wife in real estate as to which her husband dies intestate; and

(e)

escheat to the Crown for want of heirs.

(2) All words and expressions which on or before the date of the commencement of
this Act would have created or been deemed to create an estate tail shall create and be
deemed to create and estate in fee simple.

### 4.

Rules for distribution and devolution of undisposed estates
(1) The following persons shall be beneficially entitled to the estate of an undisposed
intestate dying after the commencement of this Act in the manner following, namely—
(a)

if the intestate leaves a husband or wife, but no issue, the surviving husband
or wife shall be entitled to one-half absolutely and the remaining half shall
devolve as if the deceased had left no husband or wife; provided that the
remaining half shall devolve on the surviving husband or wife in priority to
the Crown if all the remaining classes named in this section fail;

(b)

if the intestate leaves a husband or wife and issue, the surviving husband or
wife shall be entitled to one-half absolutely and the issue shall be entitled to
the remaining half in equal shares;

(c)

if the intestate leaves issue but no husband or wife, the issue shall be entitled
to the whole estate in equal shares, and if there be only one member of this
class he or she shall be entitled to the whole. Such issue shall be entitled in
equal shares, if more than one to the share which their parent would have
taken if living at the death of the intestate;

(d)

if the intestate leaves no husband or wife and no issue, then the father and
mother of the intestate shall be entitled to the whole estate in equal shares;

(e)

if the intestate leaves no husband or wife and no issue but one parent, then
that surviving parent shall be entitled to the whole estate absolutely;

(f)

if the intestate leaves no husband or wife and no issue or parent, then the
following persons shall be entitled—

Firstly:

The brothers and sisters of the intestate in equal shares, or if there
be only one such brother or sister, such brother or sister shall be
entitled to the whole; but if there be no such brother or sister, then

Secondly:

The grandparents of the intestate, if more than one survive, the
intestate shall take in equal shares, and if there be only one such
grandparent he or she shall be entitled to the whole; but if there be
no member of this class, then

Thirdly:

The uncles and aunts of the intestate shall be entitled in equal
shares, or if there be only one member of this class, then such
person shall be entitled to the whole; but if there be no such person,
then

Fourthly:

In default of any person taking the estate of the intestate under the
foregoing provisions, the same shall belong to the Crown as bona
vacantia and in lieu of any right to escheat, and shall form part of
general revenue of this Colony and be appropriated in the manner
provided for casual revenue of this Colony and be appropriated in
the manner provided for casual revenue by the Act in that behalf:

Provided always that should any member of the first or third classes mentioned in this
section pre-decease the intestate, then all or any of the issue living at the death of the
intestate, who attain the age of twenty-one years or marry under that age, of any such
member who predeceases the intestate shall take in equal shares if more than one, the
share which their parent would have taken if living at the death of the intestate, and so
that no issue shall take whose parent is living at the death of the intestate and is so
capable of taking.
(2) If the surviving spouse of an intestate on application made by the said surviving
spouse to the High Court proves on a balance of probabilities that he or she contributed,

whether directly or indirectly, to the building up of the estate of the intestate to such a
degree that he or she should be entitled to more than the share provided for him or her in
subsection (1), the High Court may make an order (hereinafter called a “variation order”)
increasing the share of the estate of the intestate to which he or she shall be entitled and
corresponding decreasing the shares of the other persons, if any, entitled to share in the
said estate to such extent as the Court adjudges meet and proper to the circumstances of
the case:
Provided that any person whose share of the estate of an intestate may be affected by a
variation order made under this subsection shall be entitled to appear and be heard in any
application for such a variation order.
