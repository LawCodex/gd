## CHAPTER 286

RENT RESTRICTION ACT
An act to restrict increases of rent of leased property, and the right to recovery of
possession thereof.
[Act No. 24 of 1982 amended by Act No. 9 of 1983, Act No. 37 of 1986.]

[21st May, 1982.]

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Rent Restriction Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act—
“agricultural land” does not include the garden of a house or building, or land
within the curtilage of a house or building;
“Board” means a Rent Assessment Board constituted under section 8;
“building land” means land let to a tenant for the purpose of the erection thereon by
the tenant of a building used, or to be used, as a dwelling, or for the public service or
for business, trade or professional purposes, or for any combination of such purposes,
or land on which the tenant has lawfully erected such a building, but does not include
any such land when let with agricultural land;
“district” means Magisterial District as constituted under section 3 of the
Magistrates Act, Chapter 177;
“dwelling” means a building, a part of a building separately let, or a room
separately let, which on the 31st December, 1948, was being mainly used as a
dwelling or place of residence and includes land occupied with the premises under the
tenancy, but does not include a building, part of a building, or room when let with
agricultural land;
“furniture” includes fittings, machinery and other articles used in premises but not
forming part thereof;
“landlord” includes any person deriving title under the original landlord and any
person who is, or would but for the provisions of this Act be, entitled to the possession
of the premises, and for the purpose of the enforcement of this Act whereby liability is
imposed on a landlord includes also any agent having charge, control or management
of the premises on behalf of the landlord;
“let” includes “sub-let”;
“let furnished” means let at a rent which includes payment for the use of furniture,
and “let unfurnished” shall be construed accordingly;
“Minister” means the Minister for the time being responsible for housing;
“public or commercial building” means a building or part of a building separately
let, or a room separately let, which at the material date was being used mainly for the
public service or for business, trade or professional purposes, and includes land
occupied therewith under the tenancy, but does not include a building, part of a
building or room when let with agricultural land;
“standard rent” means the standard rent of a dwelling or a public or commercial
building, or of building land ascertained in accordance with this Act;
“tenancy” includes “sub-tenancy”;

“tenant” includes a “sub-tenant” and any person deriving title from the original
tenant or sub-tenant, as the case may be.

### 3.

Application of Act
This Act shall apply to the following premises, namely, all land which is building land
at the commencement of this Act or becomes building land thereafter, and to all dwellings
and public or commercial buildings whether in existence or let at the commencement of
this Act or erected or let thereafter and whether let furnished or unfurnished:
Provided that this Act shall not apply to—
(a)

a dwelling bona fide let at a rent which includes payment in respect of board
or attendance;

(b)

building land which is let on a building lease, or a renewal or continuance of
a building lease, for a term of twenty-five years or more;

(c)

a dwelling for the time being let by a public body; or

(d)

building land or a dwelling or public or commercial building of any class
declared by Order made under section 7 to be exempted premises, save as
respects the provisions of section 11.

### 4.

Provisions where letting includes shared accommodation
(1) Where—
(a)

a tenant has the exclusive occupation of any accommodation (in this section
referred to as the “separate accommodation”);

(b)

the terms as between the tenant and his or her landlord on which he or she
holds the separate accommodation include the use of other accommodation
(in this section referred to as the “shared accommodation”) in common with
another person or other persons, whether or not including the landlord; and

(c)

by reason only of the circumstances mentioned in paragraph (b) the separate
accommodation would not apart from this subsection be a dwelling as
defined by this Act,

the separate accommodation shall be deemed to be a dwelling as so defined, and the
following provisions of this section shall have effect.
(2) For the avoidance of doubt it is hereby declared that where for the purpose of
determining the standard rent of the separate accommodation it is necessary to make an
apportionment under this Act, regard shall be had to the circumstances mentioned in
subsection (1)(b).
(3) For the purpose of ascertaining the standard rent, a previous letting of the separate
accommodation shall not be deemed not to be a letting of the same as a dwelling by
reason only of any such change of circumstances as the following, that is to say, any
increase or diminution of the rights of the tenant to use accommodation in common with
others, or any improvement or worsening of accommodation so used by the tenant.
(4) For the purposes of the provisions of this Act relating to increases of rent—
(a)

any such change of circumstances as is mentioned in subsection (3), being a
change affecting so much of the shared accommodation as is living
accommodation, shall be deemed to be an alteration of rent;

(b)

where, as the result of any such change as is mentioned in paragraph (a) of
this subsection, the terms on which the separate accommodation is held are
on the whole less favourable to the tenant than the previous terms, the rent
shall be deemed to be increased whether or not the sum periodically payable
by way of rent is increased;

(c)

an increase of rent in respect of any such change as is mentioned in
paragraph (a) of this subsection where, as a result of the change and of the
increase of rent, the terms on which the separate accommodation is held are
on the whole not less favourable to the tenant than the previous terms, shall
be deemed not to be an increase of rent.

(5) While a tenant is in possession of the separate accommodation, any term or
condition of the contract of tenancy terminating or modifying, or providing for the
termination or modification of his or her right to the use of any of the shared
accommodation which is living accommodation shall be of no effect:
Provided that where the terms and conditions of the contract of tenancy are such
that at any time during the tenancy the persons in common with whom the tenant is
entitled to the use of the shared accommodation could be varied, or their number could be
increased, nothing in this subsection shall prevent those terms and conditions from having
effect so far as they relate to such variation or increase.
(6) Subject to the provisions of section 22 and subsection (7) of this section and
without prejudice to the enforcement of any order made thereunder, while a tenant is in
possession of the separate accommodation, no order or judgement for the recovery of any
of the shared accommodation or for the ejectment of the tenant therefrom shall be made
or given, whether on the application of the immediate landlord of the tenant or on the
application of any person under whom the said landlord derives title, unless
notwithstanding section 22 a like order or judgement has been given, or is made or given
at the same time, in respect of the separate accommodation.
(7) Without prejudice to the provisions of subsection (4), the Board may, upon the
application of the landlord, make such order, either terminating the right of the tenant to
use the whole or any part of the shared accommodation other than living accommodation,
or modifying his or her right to use the whole or any part of the shared accommodation,
whether by varying the persons or increasing the number of persons entitled to the use of
that accommodation, or otherwise, as to the Board seems just:
Provided that no order shall be made under this subsection so as to effect any
termination or modification of rights of the tenant which, apart from subsection (5), could
not be effected by or under the terms of the contract of tenancy.
(8) Any question arising under subsection (4) shall be determined, on the application
either of the landlord or of the tenant, by the Board.
(9) In this section, “living accommodation” means accommodation of such a nature
that the fact that it constitutes or is included in the shared accommodation is sufficient to
bring the tenancy within subsection (1)(c).

### 5.

Certain sub-lettings not to exclude application of Act
Where a tenant of premises being a building or part of a building has sub-let a part but
not the whole of the premises, then as against his or her landlord or any superior landlord
(but without prejudice to the rights against and liabilities to each other of the tenant and
any person claiming under him or her, or of any two such persons) no part of the premises
shall be treated as not being a dwelling to which this Act applies by reason only—
(a)

that the terms on which any person claiming under the tenant holds some
part of the premises include the use of accommodation in common with
other persons; or

(b)

that some part of the premises is let to a person claiming under the tenant at
a rent which bona fide includes payment for board.

### 6.

Application of sections 4 and 5

Sections 4 and 5 shall apply whether the letting in question began before or after the
1st May, 1982, but not so as to affect rent in respect of any period before that date or
anything done or omitted during any such period.

### 7.

Power of Minister to exempt premises
(1) The Minister may by Order declare any class of premises specified in the Order to
be exempted premises in respect of the whole or any part of this Act.
(2) The declaration of any class of premises to be exempted premises by an Order
under subsection (1) shall not render recoverable by a landlord any sum which, before the
coming into force of the Order, was not recoverable by such landlord, or affect the right
of a landlord to recover any arrears of rent which before the coming into force of the
Order were recoverable by such landlord, or affect the right of a tenant to recover any rent
or sum which, before the coming into force of the Order, was recoverable by such tenant,
or prevent or affect the prosecution, conviction or punishment of any person for anything
done or omitted before the commencement of the Order.

### 8.

Rent Assessment Boards
(1) The Minister shall establish for the purposes of this Act a Rent Assessment Board
for each district to exercise the powers conferred and perform the duties imposed by this
Act.
(2) The magistrate shall be the chairperson of the Board for his or her own district and
in the absence of the magistrate, or his or her inability to act, the Minister may appoint a
person to act temporarily as chairperson.
(3) The Minister shall appoint a panel of persons (other than the chairperson) from
which members of the Board may be selected.
(4) A Board, at any sitting, shall consist of three members, namely, the chairperson
and two members selected by the chairperson from the panel for his or her district.
(5) The Minister may appoint in respect of each Board a secretary and valuation
officers and inspectors and other employees as he or she may consider necessary.
(6) All appointments under this section shall be at the Minister’s pleasure.
(7) The members of a Board, and the persons appointed under subsection (5), shall
receive such remuneration, if any, as the Minister may generally or specifically direct.
(8) No person may sit as a member of a Board in any matter in which he or she would
be disqualified from acting on the ground of self-interest.
(9) The establishment of the Boards and all appointments under this section shall be
published in the Gazette.

### 9.

Membership of Board or panel not to constitute an office of emolument
Notwithstanding anything to the contrary, no person shall be deemed to hold an office
of emolument under the Crown or under the Government, or an office of emolument, the
emolument of which is payable out of public funds, merely by reason of his or her having
been appointed to, or having served upon, a panel of persons constituted under section
8(3), or having been selected or having acted as a member of, or having received any fee
or allowance by reason of having acted as a member of, any Board.

### 10.

Procedure of Boards
(1) At sittings of a Board the decision of the majority of the members shall prevail.
The chairperson shall have an original vote and he or she shall also have a casting vote if
the votes are equally divided.

(2) The Boards shall, for the purposes of this Act, mutatis mutandis, follow the
procedure of a magistrate’s court in civil matters and subject to the provisions of this Act
shall have the same power and authority as that court.
(3) No member of a Board shall be liable to any action for any matter or thing done
by him or her in the exercise of his or her duties as such.
(4) (a) A Board may, notwithstanding the absence of formal proof, take into
consideration any relevant facts—
(i) which are shown by the minutes or other due records of a Board to have
been found to be proved at a meeting thereof,
(ii) within the personal knowledge of a member of the Board which were found
to be proved at a meeting of a Board of which he or she was at the material
time a member, or
(iii) contained in a return required under section 11;
(b) notwithstanding the provisions of paragraph (a), no such facts shall be taken
into consideration unless the party or parties present before the Board shall have been
informed of the substance of such facts and shall have been given an opportunity, if he or
she or they so desire, of producing evidence in regard thereto.
(5) The proceedings of Boards shall be open to the public, and minutes thereof,
including a summary of the evidence given and a statement of all facts taken into
consideration pursuant to subsection (4), shall be kept by the chairperson.
(6) An interested party may be represented before a Board by a barrister or by a
solicitor of the Supreme Court.
(7) An order of a Board shall operate from such date, whether before or after the date
on which the order is made, as may be specified in the order, but if no such date be
specified it shall operate from the date of the order. Any such order may be proved by
production of a copy of the order purporting to be signed by the chairperson or by the
secretary of the Board.
(8) A Board shall have power to award costs in accordance with any scale of costs
fixed by rules made under subsection (10).
(9) When an application has been made to a Board under this Act, the Board may
make an order on such application notwithstanding the non-attendance of the applicant or
any person interested before the Board.
(10) The Minister may make rules not inconsistent with this Act, as to the form and
manner of application to a Board, the fees to be paid on such application, the scale of
costs applicable to proceedings before a Board, the procedure of Boards, the forms of
documents to be issued by Boards and the manner in which such documents are to be
certified or served. With regard to any matter on which rules under this subsection have
not been made, each Board may give such directions and regulate its procedure in such
manner as may seem just:
Provided that no fee shall be taken except in accordance with rules made under this
subsection, and all fees shall be paid into the Consolidated Fund.
(11) A Board shall have the power to review once any decision or order made by it on
its own motion or on application by an aggrieved party made not less than three months
after the decision or order complained of.
(12) A person aggrieved by an order of a Board, or by a decision of a Board on
review, may appeal to the Minister within three weeks following receipt of notification of
such order, and the Minister shall have the same powers for that purpose, mutatis
mutandis, as are conferred on the Court of Appeal by section 27(1) of the Magistrates
Judgements (Appeals) Act, Chapter 178.

### 11.

Information concerning rented premises may be required
(1) A Board may require a landlord or a tenant, or landlords and tenants generally, or
landlords and tenants of any particular class or in any particular district, to render such
returns in such form and containing such particulars in relation to any premises or matters
affecting or directly or indirectly connected with the rental thereof as the Board may
require.
(2) Any person who contravenes a requirement lawfully issued pursuant to subsection
(1) shall be guilty of an offence against this Act.

### 12.

Duty to keep register, and search therein
(1) Every Board shall keep a register showing the permitted rent of every premises in
its district in respect of which the Board has determined the standard rent.
(2) A Board, upon demand by any member of the public who satisfies the Board that
he or she is, or formerly has been, or proposes to become, a tenant of any particular
premises shown on the register, shall make a search in the register and disclose to him or
her the maximum permitted rent of the premises.

### 13.

Powers of valuation officers and powers and duties of inspectors
(1) A valuation officer—
(a)

at any reasonable time may enter controlled premises and carry out such
investigations in relation to the value of those premises as he or she thinks
necessary for the purpose of giving evidence before a Board;

(b)

shall give, before a Board, evidence in relation to the value of controlled
premises in respect of which the Board wishes to determine the standard
rent.

(2) An inspector shall, in respect of the district for which he or she is appointed—
(a)

make investigations with a view to ensuring compliance with the provisions
of this Act;

(b)

perform such other duties as may be assigned to him or her by the Board.

(3) An inspector may—
(a)

at any reasonable time enter controlled premises and require the landlord and
any tenant to furnish, in respect of those premises, such relevant information
as the landlord or tenant, as the case may be, possesses;

(b)

institute proceedings against a landlord for the breach of a provision of this
Act.

### 14.

Offences in relation to inspectors and valuation officers
(1) A person who—
(a)

assaults or obstructs an inspector or valuation officer acting in the
performance of his or her duties under this Act;

(b)

supplies to an inspector or valuation officer information in connection with
any matter arising in the performance of his or her duties under this Act
which such person knows to be false in any material particular;

(c)

bribes or attempts to bribe an inspector or valuation officer in connection
with any matter arising in the performance of his or her duties under this
Act;

(d)

being an inspector or valuation officer solicits or accepts any bribe in
connection with any matter arising in the performance of his or her duties
under this Act,

shall be guilty of an offence against this Act.
(2) A prosecution for an offence against subsection (1)(c) or (d) shall not be instituted
without the sanction of the Director of Public Prosecutions.

### 15.

Standard rent pending determination by a Board
Until the standard rent of premises has been determined by a Board under section 17
the standard rent of premises shall be the rent at which they were let on the 31st
December, 1948, or, where the premises were not let on that date, the rent at which they
were last let before that date, or in the case of premises first let after that date, the rent at
which they were, or are hereafter, first let; except that, in the case of premises let at a
progressive rent payable under a tenancy agreement or lease, the standard rent shall, until
the tenancy is determined or until the standard rent shall have been determined by the
Board, be the maximum rent payable under the tenancy agreement or lease.

### 16.

Applications to Board to determine standard rent
(1) Where premises to which this Act applies are intended to be let, it shall be lawful
to apply to the Board for the district wherein the premises are situated to fix provisionally
the rent which will be the standard rent of the premises when they are so let, and the
Board may fix a provisional standard rent thereof. The applicant shall disclose to the
Board the terms and conditions of the proposed letting and all circumstances which may
affect the standard rent of the premises and, if the premises are later let substantially on
such terms and conditions and in such circumstances, the provisional standard rent fixed
by the Board shall be deemed to be the standard rent of the premises determined by the
Board.
(2) It shall be lawful for the Board at any time, by notice in writing served on the
landlord, to require him or her to apply to the Board within a time to be specified in the
notice for the determination of the standard rent and, if the landlord shall fail so to apply,
he or she shall be guilty of an offence against this Act and the Board may determine the
standard rent as though he or she had so applied.

### 17.

Determination of standard rent by Board
(1) A Board may at any time determine the standard rent of premises to which this
Act applies on the application of either the landlord or the tenant. The Board may, in
determining the standard rent, take into consideration—
(a)

the rent at which similar premises in the same locality were let on the
31st December, 1948;

(b)

the increased or decreased amenities of the locality;

(c)

where the premises were not erected on or before the 31st December, 1948,
the increased cost of building.

(2) Where, for the purpose of determining the standard rent of premises to which this
Act applies, it is necessary to apportion the rent at the date in relation to which the
standard rent is fixed, the Board, on the application of either the landlord or the tenant or
of any superior landlord, may make such apportionment as seems just and the decision of
the Board as to the amount to be apportioned to the premises shall be final and
conclusive.

### 18.

Maximum permitted rent

(1) Subject to the provisions of this Act where, at any time after the 21st May, 1982,
the rent of premises to which this Act applies exceeds the standard rent by more than the
amount permitted under this Act, the amount of the excess shall, notwithstanding any
provision to the contrary in a tenancy agreement or lease made before or after that date,
be irrecoverable from the tenant, and if it is paid by the tenant, shall be recoverable by
him or her, or by persons claiming through him or her, from the person to whom it was
paid or his or her personal representative, and may, without prejudice to any other method
of recovery, be deducted from any rent or money due or subsequently becoming due from
the tenant.
(2) Any transfer to the tenant of a burden or liability which, in calculating the
standard rent of premises, is treated as borne by the landlord shall, for the purposes of this
Act, be treated as an alteration of rent, and where, as a result of such a transfer, the terms
on which the premises are held are on the whole less favourable to the tenant, the rent
shall be deemed to be increased whether or not the sum periodically payable by way of
rent is increased; and an increase of rent in respect of a transfer to the landlord of a
burden or liability which, in calculating the standard rent of premises, is treated as borne
by the tenant where, as a result of such transfer, the terms on which the premises are held
are on the whole not less favourable to the tenant, shall be deemed not to be an increase
of rent for the purposes of this Act; except that for the purposes of this Act, the rent shall
not be deemed to be increased where a liability for rates or taxes is transferred by the
landlord to the tenant, if a corresponding reduction is made in the rent.
(3) A landlord who knowingly receives, or a tenant who knowingly pays, any rent
which is by this Act made irrecoverable, shall be guilty of an offence against this Act; and
if a landlord is convicted of such offence the court in which the conviction is obtained
may, without prejudice to any other right which the tenant may have to recover the rent
overpaid, order the landlord to repay the same.

### 19.

Additions to standard rent
(1) The amount by which the rent of premises to which this Act applies may exceed
the standard rent shall, subject to the provisions of this Act, be—
(a)

an amount proportionate to any increase in the amount of rates and taxes
payable by the landlord since the date by reference to which the standard
rent of the premises is determinable, exclusive of such amount as the
Minister may by Order determine;

(b)

an amount sanctioned by a Board on the application of the landlord where
the landlord has incurred expenditure in effecting—
(i) substantial improvements or structural alterations to the premises
other than ordinary or necessary repairs, or
(ii) substantial improvements to the amenities of the premises, or
substantial improvements made by the landlord in the locality, from
which the tenant derives benefit, other than necessary maintenance or
drainage,

but a Board may decline to sanction any amount under this paragraph in respect of
premises which, in the opinion of the Board, are not in tenantable repair:
Provided that the Board may decline to sanction any excess, or may sanction a
portion thereof, if the expenditure is or was entirely or, as the case may be, partly
unnecessary.
(2) The Minister may, by Order—
(a)

sanction increases of the rent of premises, either generally or in any district
specified in the Order, by such percentage of the respective standard rents as
he or she may specify in the Order;

(b)

declare that, subject to such exceptions as may be specified in the Order, no
increase in the rents of premises in any district specified in the Order shall be
made during such period as shall be specified in the Order; or

(c)

impose a decrease in the rents of premises, either generally or in any district
specified in the Order, by such percentage of the respective standard or
actual rents, and for such period, as he or she may specify in the Order.

### 20.

Notice showing standard rent to be exhibited
(1) Where the standard rent of premises to which this Act applies has been
determined, the landlord shall exhibit and keep exhibited in a prominent place therein and
open to view a notice in the prescribed form as to what is the standard rent and what are
the permitted increases in respect of such premises, or of any room or part thereof that is
separately let.
(2) If a landlord contravenes subsection (1) he or she shall be guilty of an offence and
liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of five hundred dollars, or in default of payment
to imprisonment for up to six months.

### 21.

Restriction on premiums, etc.
(1) A person shall not, as a condition of the grant, renewal or continuance of a
tenancy of any premises to which this Act applies, not being a tenancy under a lease, or a
renewal or continuance of a lease for a term of twenty-five years or more, require the
payment of any fine, premium or other like sum, or the giving of any consideration in
addition to the rent, and, where any such payment or consideration shall have been paid,
the amount or value thereof shall be recoverable by the person by whom it was made or
given or his or her personal representative.
(2) Any person requiring a payment or the giving of consideration in contravention of
subsection (1), and any person making any such payment or giving any such
consideration, shall be guilty of an offence against his or her Act and, if a person
convicted of requiring any payment or the giving of any consideration as aforesaid has
received such payment or consideration, the court in which the conviction is obtained, in
addition to any penalty imposed, may order him or her to repay the amount or value of
the same to the person from whom it was received.

### 22.

Restriction on recovery of possession
(1) Notwithstanding any provision of the Small Tenements (Recovery of Possession)
Act, Chapter 307, an order or judgement for the recovery of possession of premises to
which this Act applies or for the ejection of a tenant therefrom shall not be made or given
unless—
(a)

some rent lawfully due from the tenant has remained unpaid for at least
thirty days or in the case of a weekly tenant, fifteen days, after it became
due;

(b)

some other obligation of the tenancy (whether expressed or implied and
whether under a contract of tenancy or under this Act) so far as the same is
consistent with the provisions of this Act has been broken or not performed
and, in the case of the non-performance of any such obligation by the tenant,
the tenant has been in default for at least thirty days;

(c)

the tenant or any person residing or lodging with him or her or being his or
her sub-tenant has been guilty of conduct which is a nuisance or annoyance
to adjoining occupiers, or has been convicted of using the premises or
allowing the premises to be used for an immoral or illegal purpose, or the
condition of the premises has, in the opinion of the court, deteriorated or
become insanitary owing to acts of waste by, or the neglect or default of, the

tenant or any such person and, where such person is a lodger or sub-tenant,
the court is satisfied that the tenant has not, before the making or giving of
the order or judgement, taken such steps as he or she ought reasonably to
have taken for the removal of the lodger or sub-tenant;
(d)

the tenant has given notice to quit, and, in consequence of that notice, the
landlord has contracted to sell or let the dwelling-house or has taken some
other step as a result of which he or she would in the opinion of the court, be
seriously prejudiced if he or she could not obtain possession;

(e)

the premises, being a dwelling, are reasonably required by the landlord for
occupation as a residence for himself, herself or for some person wholly
dependent upon him or her, or for some person bona fide residing or to
reside with him or her, or for some person in his or her whole-time
employment;

(f)

the premises, being building land, are reasonably required by the landlord for
the erection of a building to be used as a dwelling;

(g)

the premises have, or a portion thereof has, been compulsorily acquired
under the Land Acquisition Act, Chapter 159;

(h)

the premises are required for the purpose of being repaired, improved, or
rebuilt;

(i)

the premises are required for public purposes;

(j)

the premises, being a dwelling or some other building erected by a tenant on
building land, are required by law to be demolished;

(k)

the tenant has sub-let, or parted with the possession of, the whole or some
part of the premises without either obtaining the consent of the landlord or
being expressly authorised by or under the tenancy agreement or lease to do
so;

(l)

the tenant of a dwelling, or of building land on which the building erected by
the tenant is used or is intended to be used mainly as a dwelling, uses the
house or building mainly for business, trade or professional purposes without
either obtaining the consent of the landlord or being authorised by or under
the tenancy agreement or lease to do so;

(m)

the tenant has been offered by the landlord in writing a new tenancy, at a
high rent permissible under this Act but in all other respects on the same
terms as the existing tenancy, and has failed to accept such offer in writing
within a reasonable time;

(n)

the premises comprise a building on building land that has been sold under
attachment for rent;

(o)

the premises comprise a dwelling that has been let to a tenant in the
employment of the landlord on condition that the tenancy shall subsist only
during the continuance of such employment, or only until the expiration of a
period not exceeding one month after the termination of such employment,
and the employment has terminated, or such period has expired, as the case
may be; or

(p)

the premises comprise a dwelling that has been let to a tenant in the
employment of the landlord in consequence of that employment, and the
employment has determined or the landlord has offered the tenant alternative
accommodation,

and unless in addition, in any such case as aforesaid, the court that is being asked to make
the order or give the judgement considers it reasonable to make such order or give such
judgement:

Provided that an order or judgement shall not be made or given on any ground
specified in paragraph (e), (f) or (h) unless the court is also satisfied that, having regard to
all the circumstances of the case, less hardship would be caused by granting the order or
judgement than by refusing to grant it; and such circumstances are hereby declared to
include—
(i) when the application is on a ground specified in paragraph (e) or (f), whether
or not other accommodation is available for the landlord or the tenant, and
(ii) when the application is on a ground specified in paragraph (h), whether or
not other accommodation is available for the tenant.
(2) (a) A court that is being asked to make such an order or to give such a
judgement—
(i) shall require the secretary of the Board in whose district the premises in
question are situated to furnish the court with a certificate setting out such
information as the Board possesses in relation to the premises in respect of
which the application is made,
(ii) may adjourn the application from time to time,
(iii) may stay or suspend execution of the order or judgement, or postpone the
date of possession, for such periods as it thinks fit, and may, from time to
time, grant further stays or suspensions of execution and further
postponements of the date of possession;
(b) an adjournment, stay, suspension or postponement pursuant to paragraph (a)
may be granted subject to any conditions the court sees fit to impose, and the court may,
in its discretion, discharge or rescind any order or judgement made or given pursuant to
paragraph (a) if any such condition is not complied with;
(c) a certificate purporting to be signed by the secretary of a Board in his or her
official capacity and furnished by him or her to a court in accordance with a requirement
under paragraph (a)(i) of this subsection, shall be admissible as prima facie evidence of
the matters stated therein:
Provided that any of the parties to the proceedings before the court may require the
secretary personally to attend and give evidence to the court.
(3) Nothing in this section shall prevent a court making an order for the ejectment of a
person where, in the opinion of the court, the ejectment is expedient for reasons of public
health or public safety.
(4) Where, in proceedings between a landlord and tenant brought under this section,
the court has to determine the bona fides of the landlord, then, if the proceedings were
instituted within six months of the date of an application by the tenant for assessment, or
the date of the determination of such application or the date of the determination of an
unsuccessful such application by the landlord, the court shall not conclude the existence
of bona fides without satisfactory evidence thereof.
(5) If, after a landlord has obtained an order or judgement for possession or ejectment
under this section, it is shown to the satisfaction of a court that the order was obtained by
misrepresentation or the concealment of material facts, the court may order the landlord
to pay to the former tenant such sum as appears to the court to be sufficient to compensate
him or her for damage or loss sustained by him or her in consequence of the order or
judgement.
(6) In granting an order or giving judgement under this section for possession or
ejectment in respect of building land, a court may require the landlord to pay to the tenant
such sum as appears to the court to be sufficient to compensate him or her for damage or
loss sustained by him or her, and the order or judgement shall not be executed until that
sum shall have been paid.

(7) Whenever a landlord has obtained an order or judgement for possession of
premises to which this Act applies on a ground specified in subsection (1)(e) or (f) and
the order or judgement is executed, or the tenant voluntarily gives up his or her tenancy in
consequence of that order or judgement, the landlord shall be guilty of an offence against
this Act if—
(a)

without first obtaining the permission of the court, he or she uses or permits
to be used, or occupies or permits to be occupied, or lets, the premises at any
time for any purpose other than the purpose which constituted the ground on
which the order was made or the judgement was given; or

(b)

having obtained the permission of the court as aforesaid, he or she fails to
comply with any terms or conditions which the court may have attached to
that permission,

and the court may decline to grant permission as aforesaid in any case in which the
landlord has failed to take such steps, if any, to renew the tenancy of the former tenant as
the court may have directed, or in any case in which the court is not satisfied that the
premises will be used, occupied or let to good advantage having regard to any prevailing
shortage of similar accommodation.
(8) Whenever a landlord has obtained an order or judgement for possession of
premises to which this Act applies on a ground specified in subsection (1)(h) and the
order or judgement is executed, or the tenant voluntarily gives up his or her tenancy in
consequence of that order or judgement, the landlord shall be guilty of an offence against
this Act if—
(a)

without first obtaining the permission of the court, he or she uses or permits
to be used, or occupies or permits to be occupied, or lets, the premises at any
time; or

(b)

having obtained the permission of the court as aforesaid he or she fails to
comply with any terms or conditions which the court may have attached to
the permission,

and the court may decline to grant permission as aforesaid on any ground on which the
court could decline permission under subsection (7).

### 23.

Conditions of statutory tenancy
(1) A tenant who, under the provisions of this Act, retains possession of any premises
shall, so long as he or she retains possession, observe and be entitled to the benefit of all
the terms and conditions of the original contract of tenancy, so far as the same are
consistent with the provisions of this Act, and shall be entitled to give up possession of
the premises only on giving such notice as would have been required under the original
contract of tenancy, except that, notwithstanding anything in the contract of tenancy, a
landlord who obtains an order or judgement for the recovery of possession of premises or
for the ejectment of a tenant retaining possession as aforesaid shall not be required to give
any notice to quit to the tenant.
(2) A tenant retaining possession as aforesaid shall not, as a condition of giving up
possession, require or request the payment of any sum or the giving of any other
consideration by any person other than the landord. A person acting in contravention of
this subsection shall be guilty of an offence against this Act, and the court by which he or
she is convicted may order any such payment or the value of any such consideration to be
returned to the person by whom the same was paid or given, but any such order shall be
in lieu of any other method of recovery prescribed by this Act.
(3) Where the interest of a tenant of a dwelling is determined in consequence of an
order or judgement for possession or ejectment, or for any other reason, any sub-tenant to
whom the dwelling was sub-let either with the consent of the landlord or in accordance
with express authority conferred by or under the contract of tenancy shall, subject to the

provisions of this Act, be deemed to become the tenant of the landlord on the same terms
as he or she would have held from the tenant had the tenant’s interest not been
determined.

### 24.

Harassment of residential occupier of premises forbidden
(1) Any person who deprives a residential occupier unlawfully of premises or any part
of them, or attempts to do so, shall be guilty of an offence against this section.
(2) If any person, with intent to cause the residential occupier of premises—
(a)

to give up the occupation of the premises or any part of them; or

(b)

to refrain from exercising any right or pursuing any remedy in respect of the
premises or any part of them,

does acts calculated to interfere with the peace or comfort of the residential occupier or
members of his or her household, or persistently withdraws or withholds services
reasonably required for the occupation of the premises as a residence, he or she shall be
guilty of an offence against this section.
(3) A person guilty of an offence against this section shall be liable on summary
conviction to a fine of two thousand dollars and to imprisonment for one year.
(4) A magistrate, upon receiving a claim or complaint brought by a residential
occupier of premises that he or she has been unlawfully evicted from the premises, may
order that he or she be immediately reinstated in possession of the premises. Where the
magistrate thinks it expedient to do so, he or she may make such order ex parte for a
limited period until the date fixed for hearing the claim or complaint.
(5) A magistrate hearing a claim or complaint brought under subsection (4) shall,
mutatis mutandis, follow the procedure of the magistrate’s court in civil matters and, for
the purposes of such hearing and the enforcement of such order, the court has the same
powers and authority as if it were exercising its civil jurisdiction.
(6) Nothing in this section shall prejudice any liability or remedy to which a person
guilty of an offence thereunder may be subject in civil proceedings, or affect the right of a
landlord to bring before a court a claim for possession of premises.
(7) In this section, “residential occupier” means a person occupying premises as a
residence, whether under a contract or by virtue of any enactment or rule of law giving
him or her the right to remain in occupation or restricting the right of recovery of
possession of the premises.

### 25.

Landlord’s responsibility to keep house fit for human habitation in certain
cases
(1) In any contract for letting a house for human habitation at a monthly rent of five
hundred dollars or less there shall, notwithstanding any stipulation to the contrary, be
implied a condition that the house at the commencement of the tenancy is, and an
undertaking that the house will be kept by the landlord throughout the tenancy, in such a
state that it remains reasonably fit for human habitation:
Provided that the condition and undertaking aforesaid shall not be implied when a
house is let for a single period of three years or more upon terms that it be put by the
lessee into a condition, and kept, reasonably fit for human habitation and that the lease is
not determinable at the option of either party before the expiration of at least three years.
(2) A landlord or person authorised by him or her in writing may, at any reasonable
time of the day, after the giving of not less than twenty-four hours’ notice in writing to
the tenant or occupier, enter such house as first before-mentioned for the purpose of
viewing the state thereof.

(3) In this section, “landlord” means the person who lets the house under the contract
referred to in subsection (1), and includes his or her successors in title, and “house”
includes any room or other part of the house.

### 26.

Employer’s responsibility under contract of employment to provide habitable
housing
Notwithstanding any stipulation to the contrary where, under the contract of
employment of a workman, the provision of a house, room or other part of a house for his
or her occupation forms part of his or her remuneration, and the provisions of the
foregoing section are inapplicable by reason only of the absence of a contract of letting,
there shall be implied in that workman’s contract of employment the same condition and
undertaking as would, under that section, otherwise have been implied and those
provisions shall apply with the substitution of the words “employer” and “contract of
employment” for, respectively, “landlord” and “tenancy”, and “workman” for “lessee”:
Provided that this section shall not affect the obligation of any person other than the
employer to repair a house to which this section applies or any remedy for enforcing such
obligation.

### 27.

Tenant may effect necessary repairs if landlord neglects to do so
(1) Where a landlord fails to keep premises to which this Act applies in good and
tenantable repair being under an obligation to do so, and fails also to comply with the
notice mentioned in subsection (2), the tenant at his or her own expense may effect such
repairs as are reasonably necessary to fulfil that obligation and may deduct the cost
thereof from any rent due from him or her to the landlord.
(2) Before effecting any repairs pursuant to subsection (1) the tenant shall send, by
registered post to the landlord at his or her last known address, notice of the nature and
estimated cost of the repairs with a request that the repairs be effected by and at the
expense of the landlord within the period of the next thirty days.
(3) At the request of the tenant the Grenada Housing Repair Unit may undertake the
repairs and, in that event, the Government shall be entitled to recover the cost thereof
directly from the landlord.

### 28.

False entries in rent books, etc.
If a person in any rent book or similar document makes an entry showing or
purporting to show a tenant as being in arrears in respect of a sum which by virtue of this
Act or any other written law is irrecoverable, that person shall be guilty of an offence
against this Act.

### 29.

General penalty
Any person who is guilty of an offence against this Act for which no specific
punishment is provided shall be liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand
dollars, and in default of payment to imprisonment for up to six months, and if such
person is a corporation, the corporation shall be liable to a fine of five thousand dollars.

### 30.

Jurisdiction of magistrates
The jurisdiction of magistrates under the Magistrates Act, Chapter 177, shall, during
the continuance of this Act, extend to all premises to which this Act for the time being
applies irrespective of the nature of any tenancy or the length of the term or the amount of
the rent thereunder.

### 31.

Control of rents

Notwithstanding any other written law, in respect of the tenancy of premises to which
this Act applies the rent payable for any period after the 31st May, 1982, shall not exceed
the amount which was paid in respect thereof for a corresponding period immediately
before the 31st May, 1982.

### 32.

Saving of Chapter No. 259 of 1958, in part
Anything lawfully done in pursuance of the former Rent Restriction Act (now
repealed) which would continue to have effect but for such repeal, shall, unless the
contrary intention appears, continue to have effect as if done in conformity with this Act.

RENT RESTRICTION ORDER
[Sections 7 and 19. SRO 7 of 1982.]

[1st May, 1982.]
1\. Citation
This Order may be cited as the Rent Restriction Order.
2\. Definitions
In this Order—
“Act” means the Rent Restriction Act;

“tourist letting” means a letting of premises to a non-resident of Grenada for a
fixed period not exceeding six months to which, apart from the provisions of this
Order, all or some of the provisions of the Act would apply.
3\. Exemptions
It is hereby declared that—
(a)

all tourist lettings are exempted from all the provisions of the Act;

(b)

all premises whose standard rents are assessed under the Act as being in
excess of three hundred dollars a month (or the equivalent rate in respect of
the relevant period for which the premises are let) are exempted from those
provisions of the Act dealing with assessment and restriction of rent only.

4\. Limitation on increases in rent
(1) No increase in the rents of any premises to which the Rent Restriction Act applies
shall be made from 1st May, 1982, until the premises are assessed under the provisions of
the said Act.
(2) Any increase in rent of such premises made between 1st May, 1982, and 2nd July,
1982, shall be of no effect and shall be refunded by the landlord.
