## CHAPTER 279

REFERENDUM ACT
An Act to regulate the manner of holding a national referendum.
[Act No. 36 of 1987.]

[31st December, 1987.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Referendum Act.
2\. Interpretation
In this Act—
“Commission” means the Referendum Commission established by section 7;
“elector” means a person entitled to vote in a referendum by virtue of section 4;
“referendum officer” means a referendum officer appointed pursuant to section 10,
and has the same meaning as therein defined.
3\. Power to order referendum
(1) The Prime Minister may, by Order published in the Gazette, direct that a
referendum be held to determine any question or questions specified in the Order if, in his
or her opinion, it is necessary or desirable to hold a referendum for that purpose.

(2) A question submitted to a referendum shall be framed in such a manner as to
require no answer except either the answer “yes” or the answer “no”.
(3) An Order made under subsection (1) shall specify the day or days on which voting
in the referendum shall take place.
4\. Entitlement to vote in referendum
All persons who at the time of a referendum are electors within the meaning of the
House of Representatives (Elections) Act, Chapter 139A, shall be entitled to vote in a
referendum.
5\. Application of Chapter 139A
The House of Representatives (Elections) Act, Chapter 139A, and the regulations
thereunder shall apply in relation to a referendum, subject to any variations, limitations
and exceptions that may be set out in the Order made under section 3(1).
6\. Method of taking the poll at a referendum
(1) For the purpose of taking the poll at a referendum, Grenada shall be divisible into
the same electoral districts as those districts for the time being established by law for the
purpose of electing members to the House of Representatives, and the poll shall be taken
separately in each district.
(2) For the purpose of taking the poll at a referendum, each electoral district shall be
divisible into the same polling divisions for the time being established by law for the
purpose of electing members to the House of Representatives.
7\. Establishment and membership of Referendum Commission
(1) There shall be established a Referendum Commission which shall consist of the
person for the time being holding or acting in the office of Supervisor of Elections as
chairperson, and two members appointed by the Governor-General after consultation with
the Prime Minister and the Leader of the Opposition.
(2) A person shall not be qualified to be appointed as a member of the Commission if
he or she is a Senator, a member of the House of Representatives, or a public officer,
except in the case of the person for the time being holding or acting in the office of
Supervisor of Elections.
(3) A member of the Commission shall vacate his or her office—
(a)

at the expiration of such period as may be specified by the Governor-General
at the time of his or her appointment;

(b)

if any circumstances arise that, if he or she were not a member of the
Commission, would cause him or her to be disqualified for appointment as
such; or

(c)

if the Governor-General so directs.

8\. Procedure of Commission
(1) The Commission may regulate its own procedure and, with the consent of the
Prime Minister, may confer powers and impose duties on any public officer or on any
authority of the Government for the purposes of the holding of a referendum.
(2) A decision of the Commission shall require the support of the chairperson and one
member.

(3) Subject to subsection (2), the Commission may act notwithstanding the absence of
a member or a vacancy in its membership.
9\. Conduct of referendum
The conduct of every referendum shall be subject to the direction and supervision of
the Commission.
10\. Appointment of referendum officers
(1) The Commission shall have power to appoint such number of referendum officers
as it may deem necessary for the purposes of the holding of a referendum.
(2) The Commission may at any time revoke the appointment of a referendum officer.
(3) Every referendum officer shall be paid, out of the Consolidated Fund, such
remuneration and allowances in respect of his or her duties as the Commission with the
approval of the Minister of Finance may determine.
(4) In this section, “referendum officer” includes any or all of the following appointees—
(a)

electoral district referendum officer;

(b)

returning officer;

(c)

presiding officer;

(d)

referendum clerk;

(e)

poll clerk;

(f)

enumerator.

11\. Power of Commission to obtain information
The Commission may at any time—
(a)

require a referendum officer to supply it with such inform action and returns as
the Commission considers necessary;

(b)

issue lawful instructions to a referendum officer in connection with the
performance of his or her duties.

12\. Breaches of official duty in connection with referendum
A referendum officer shall have duties and functions similar, mutatis mutandis, to
those of an election officer under the House of Representatives (Elections) Act, Chapter
139A, and if, without reasonable cause, he or she is guilty of an act or omission in breach
of his or her official duty, he or she shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary
conviction, to a fine of five hundred dollars and to imprisonment for six months.
13\. Bribery, treating, undue influence and impersonation
(1) A person who is guilty of bribery, treating, undue influence or impersonation
under this Act shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to
imprisonment for six months and to a fine of five hundred dollars.
(2) In this section, “bribery”, “treating”, “undue influence” and “impersonation” have
the meaning assigned to those words respectively in the House of Representatives
(Elections) Act, Chapter 139A.
