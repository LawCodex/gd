## CHAPTER 217

An Act relating to promissory and other oaths and prescribing the mode in which
such oaths may be administered.
[Amended by The Constitution.]

[5th May, 1897.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Oaths Act.
2\. Forms of oath
The oaths required to be taken under this Act shall be in the forms set forth in the First
Schedule.
3\. Allegiance and official oath, by whom to be taken
(1) The oath of allegiance and the official oath shall be tendered to and taken by the
Governor-General, by each of the Ministers and Parliamentary Secretaries, and by each of
the senior public officers named or included in the first part of the Second Schedule, as
soon as may be after his or her acceptance of office, in the manner in that behalf
mentioned in the first part of that Schedule.
(2) The oath of allegiance shall be tendered to and taken by each of the members of
the House of Representatives as soon as may be after his or her election as a member of
the House, and by each of the members of the Senate as soon as may be after his or her
appointment as a member of the Senate, in the manner in that behalf mentioned in the
first part of that Schedule.
4\. Allegiance and judicial oath, by whom to be taken
The oath of allegiance and the judicial oath shall be taken by each of the officers
mentioned in the second part of the Second Schedule as soon as may be after his or her
acceptance of office, in the manner mentioned in the second part of that Schedule.
5\. Oath of members of Cabinet
The oath of a member of the Cabinet shall be taken by every member of the Cabinet as
soon as may be after his or her acceptance of office and shall be tendered to him or her by
the Governor-General.
6\. Oath of Secretary to the Cabinet
Every person appointed or called upon to exercise the functions of the Secretary to the
Cabinet shall, before entering upon the duties of his or her office, take the oath of the
Secretary to the Cabinet set out in the First Schedule, which oath shall be tendered to him
or her by the Governor-General.
7\. Oath to be taken by persons not included in sections 3 to 6
Any person who may not come within any of the classes hereinbefore specified, and
who is by any law required to take the oath of allegiance or the official oath or both, shall
take such oath or oaths in the form mentioned in the First Schedule.
8\. Penalty on refusal or neglect to take oath
If any person bound by this Act to take the oath of allegiance of the official oath or
both, refuses or neglects to take it when duly tendered, he or she shall, if he or she has
already entered on his or her office, vacate it, and, if he or she has not entered, shall be

disqualified from entering on it, but no person shall be compelled, in respect of the same
appointment to an office to take the oath or oaths more than once.
9\. Name of Sovereign
Where in any oath under this Act the name of her present Majesty is expressed, the
name of the Sovereign of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland for
the time being shall be substituted from time to time.
10\. Declaration when permitted
Where any person is required or called upon, either by any rule of the common law or
by statute now existing or hereafter to be made or by this Act, to take an oath, if he or she
objects from alleged conscientious reasons to take it, or if he or she is ignorant of the
nature of an oath, or if it is objected that he or she is incompetent to take it, the GovernorGeneral or the Judge or any other person having authority to administer the oath may
permit him or her to make a solemn affirmation or declaration in substitution for the oath;
and thereupon he or she shall not be required to use the word “swear” or the words “so
help me God,” but shall, in lieu thereof, solemnly, sincerely, and truly affirm and declare
the truth of that which he or she would otherwise have to swear to, or as the case may be,
that he or she will do and act as required by the oath for which the affirmation or
declaration is substituted.
11\. Form of oath as in Scotland permitted
A person to whom an oath is administered may, if he or she so desires, swear with
uplifted hand in the form and manner in which an oath is administered in Scotland. The
person swearing in this manner shall swear standing and holding up his or her right hand
and shall repeat the words of the oath after the officer administering it. The oath shall
commence—“I swear by Almighty God as I shall answer to God at the great day of
judgement,” and shall proceed with the words of the oath or the substance thereof as
prescribed by law, but omitting any further words of imprecation or calling to witness.
12\. Declaration and oath taken in Scotch form to be followed by same results as
oath taken in ordinary form
(1) In all cases in which an oath is required to be taken by or administered to any
person, either by any rule or by any statute now existing or hereafter to be made or by this
Act either as a juryman, witness, deponent, party, Judge, arbitrator, officer, or any other
person, either in any civil or criminal proceeding, or on appointment to any office or
employment, or on any occasion whatsoever, a solemn affirmation or declaration may be
made by the persons and in the manner by section 10 detailed in lieu of the oath, or the
oath may be taken in the manner administered in Scotland as aforesaid; and the same
consequences and results shall in every respect follow and take place from the making of
the affirmation or declaration or the taking of the oath in Scotch form as would follow
and take place if the ordinary oath had been taken.
(2) In all cases in which the affirmation or declaration is substituted for the taking of
an oath, it shall be the duty of the Judge or other person having authority to administer the
oath, before allowing the affirmation or declaration to be made, to satisfy himself or herself
that the person about to make it understands the consequences and results aforesaid.
13\. Prohibition of unauthorised oaths
(1) It shall not be lawful for any justice of the peace, magistrate, or coroner to
administer or cause or allow to be received any oath, solemn declaration, or affidavit
touching any matter or thing whereof they have not jurisdiction or cognisance by some
rule of the common law or statute in force at the time.

(2) Nothing herein shall be construed to extend to any oath, solemn declaration, or
affidavit before any justice of the peace or magistrate in any matter or thing touching the
preservation of the peace, nor to any solemn declaration or affidavit which may be
required by the laws of any other country to give validity to instruments in writing
designed to be used outside Grenada.
14\. Confirmation of written instruments
Whenever the confirmation of a written instrument is required, or allegation or proof
of debts or of the execution of deeds or other matters, it shall be lawful for any person now
by law authorised to administer an oath to take and receive the declaration of any person
voluntarily making it before him or her.
15\. Form of declaration
Except where otherwise directed, a declaration shall be made in the following form—
“I, A. B., do solemnly and sincerely declare that, etc., and I make this solemn
declaration conscientiously believing it to be true and by virtue of the
provisions of the Oaths Act.”
16\. Offence of wilfully making untrue declaration
A person who wilfully and corruptly makes and subscribes any such declaration,
knowing it to be untrue in any particular, shall be guilty of a misdemeanour.
17\. Perjury
Whenever any court, Judge, magistrate, justice of the peace, officer, commissioner,
arbitrator, or other person is authorised by law or by consent of parties to hear or
determine any matter or thing, a power to take evidence and to administer oaths shall be
implied; and any person giving false evidence may be prosecuted for perjury in like manner
as if the false evidence had been given in a trial before the High Court.
18\. Oath of sheriff or returning officer
(1) A person appointed or called upon to exercise duties similar to those of sheriff or
returning officer at an election shall before entering upon his or her duties take the
following oath before a magistrate, and every magistrate is hereby empowered to
administer the oath in the following form—
“I, A. B., do swear that I will discharge my duties at the election now about
to take place, fairly and impartially, without favour or affection, malice or
hatred, and according to the best of my judgement. So help me God.”
(2) Any person failing to take the oath or failing to perform his or her duties in the
manner mentioned therein shall be liable, if convicted upon indictment in the High Court,
to a fine of thirty thousand dollars.
19\. Juror’s oath, civil causes
Jurors may be sworn in civil causes in open court in the following form or to the like
effect—
“You shall well and truly try the issues joined between the parties (or assess
the damages herein), and a true verdict give according to the evidence. So
help you God.”
20\. Juror’s oath, criminal trials

Jurors may be sworn in criminal trials, in felonies, in open court in the following form
or to the like effect—
“You shall well and truly try, and true deliverance make, between our
Sovereign Lady the Queen and the prisoner at the bar whom you shall have
in charge, and a true verdict give according to the evidence. So help you
God.”
Misdemeanours.—Or in misdemeanours the jurors may be sworn as follows or to the
like effect—
“You shall well and truly try the issue (or, issues) joined between our
Sovereign Lady the Queen and the defendant, and a true verdict give
according to the evidence. So help you God.”
21\. Oath of witness, civil causes
Witnesses may be sworn in civil causes in open court in the following form or to the
like effect—
“The evidence which you shall give to the court and jury sworn touching the
matters in question between the parties shall be the truth, the whole truth,
and nothing but the truth. So help you God.”
22\. Oath on the voire dire
Any person may be sworn on the voire dire in the following form or to the like
effect—
“You shall true answer make to all such questions as the court shall demand
of you. So help you God.”
23\. Oath of witness, criminal trials
Witnesses may be sworn in criminal trials in open court in the following form or to the
like effect—
“The evidence which you shall give to the court and jury sworn between Our
Sovereign Lady the Queen and the prisoner at the bar (or the defendant),
shall be the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth. So help you
God.”
24\. Form of oath of interpreter
Interpreters may be sworn in civil causes in open court in the following form or to the
like effect—
“You swear that you understand the language of the witness (or plaintiff, or
defendant) and are able to interpret between him or her and the court and
jury and all persons conversant with the English language. So help you
God.”
“You shall well and truly interpret and true explanation make between the
witness (or plaintiff, or defendant) and the court and jury, and all persons
conversant with the English language, and to the best of your knowledge,
skill and ability, and the evidence you shall give to the court and jury sworn
touching the matters in question shall be the truth, the whole truth, and
nothing but the truth. So help you God.”
25\. Interpreter may be sworn on the voire dire

Interpreters may be sworn in civil causes in open court to interpret on the voire dire in
the following form or to the like effect—
“You swear that you understand the language of the witness (or plaintiff or
defendant), and are able to interpret between him or her and the court and all
persons conversant with the English language. So help you God.”
“You shall well and truly interpret and true explanation make between the
witness (or plaintiff or defendant), and the court and all persons conversant
with the English language, to the best of your knowledge, skill and ability,
and shall true answer make to all such questions as the court shall demand of
you. So help you God.”
26\. Form of oath of interpreter for conducting arraignment of accused person
Interpreters may be sworn in open court for the purpose of conducting the arraignment
of any person accused, in the following form or to the like effect—
“You swear that you understand the language of the prisoner at the bar, and
are able to interpret between him or her and the court. So help you God.”
“You shall well and truly interpret and true explanation make between the
prisoner at the bar and the court to the best of your knowledge, skill and
ability, and you shall true answer make to all such questions as the court
shall demand of you. So help you God.”
And whenever on the trial of such person it may be necessary to examine a witness
who does not speak the English language on the voire dire, the interpreter’s oath may be
in the form given in section 25, or to the like effect, retaining the word “witness”
throughout.
27\. Form of oath of interpreter in criminal trial
Interpreters may be sworn for the purposes of a criminal trial in open court in the
following form or to the like effect—
“You swear that you understand the language of the prisoner at the bar (or
the defendant), and are able to interpret between him or her and the court and
the jury, and between him or her and all persons conversant with the English
language. So help you God.”
“You shall well and truly interpret and true explanation make between the
prisoner at the bar (or the defendant) and the court and jury, to the best of
your knowledge, skill and ability, and the evidence which you shall give to
the court and jury sworn between our Sovereign Lady the Queen and the
prisoner at the bar (or the defendant) shall be the truth, the whole truth, and
nothing but the truth. So help you God.”
“You swear that you understand the language of the witness, and are able to
interpret between him or her and the court, and the jury, and the prisoner and
all persons conversant with the English language. So help you God.”
“You shall well and truly interpret and true explanation make between the
witness and the court and jury, and the prisoner and all persons conversant
with the English language, and the evidence which you shall give to the
court and jury, sworn between our Sovereign Lady the Queen and the
prisoner at the bar, shall be the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the
truth. So help you God.”
Provided that when the witness and the prisoner are foreigners of different languages, and
the interpreter is unable to interpret to the prisoner, the reference to the prisoner shall be
omitted, and an additional interpreter sworn to interpret the English interpretation of the first
interpreter to the prisoner.

28\. Interpreters and form of oath where witness and prisoner are foreigners of
different languages
When in any criminal trial a witness and the prisoner are foreigners of different
languages, and a foreign interpreter can be found conversant with the languages of the
prisoner and the witness and able to interpret between them, and a second interpreter can
be found conversant with English and with any language with which the first interpreter
is conversant, and able to interpret from the last-named language into English, the first
interpreter may be sworn through the second interpreter, in open court, in the following
form or to the like effect—
“You swear that you understand the several languages of the witness and the
prisoner, and are able to interpret between them. So help you God.”
“You shall well and truly interpret and true explanation make, between the
witness and the prisoner at the bar, and the prisoner at the bar and the
witness, and between them and each of them and the court and jury, and all
interpreters, witnesses, and persons whatsoever, to the best of your
knowledge, skill and ability, and the evidence you shall give to the court and
the jury, sworn between our Sovereign Lady the Queen and the prisoner at
the bar, shall be the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth. So help
you God.”
And whatever be the number of interpreters necessary before the statements of the
prisoner and the witness can be interpreted into one and the same language and into
English, the same forms of oath shall be administered mutatis mutandis to each
interpreter in succession, and the like provision shall apply in civil causes as far as may
be.
29\. Presiding Judge to declare manner of swearing interpreter
If it appears to the presiding Judge in any criminal proceedings that the person called
as interpreter understands the language of the accused or other person between whom and
the court he or she is called to interpret, sufficiently to be able to make true explanation of
the evidence and other proceedings, but that he or she cannot for any cause be sworn in
the form and manner herein prescribed, it shall be the duty of the presiding Judge to
declare in what manner he or she shall be sworn or otherwise bound to make true
declaration; and it shall in that case be the further duty of the presiding Judge to ascertain
that true explanation of the evidence and all other proceedings is made to the accused
person; and, if the presiding Judge is satisfied that true explanation is so made, the trial,
and any verdict given thereat, shall be as valid as if the interpreter had been sworn in the
ordinary manner.
30\. New trial by reason of incompetence of interpreter
If at any trial for a criminal offence it appears to the presiding Judge, after the accused
has been given in charge to the jury, that true explanation of the evidence cannot by
reason of the incompetence of any interpreter be made to the accused, he or she shall not
by reason thereof be entitled to be acquitted, but it shall be the duty of the presiding Judge
to discharge the jury from giving any verdict, and the accused shall be liable to be again
tried as if the first trial had not been commenced.

Second Schedule

Oath Administered by the Governor-General
[Sections 3 and 4.]

## PART I

The Governor-General
The oaths are to be tendered by a Judge or, if he or she be unavoidably absent, by the Prime
Minister or, if he or she be unavoidably absent, by the senior member of the Cabinet present, and
are to be taken in the presence of as many members of the Cabinet as can conveniently be present.
Members of the House of Representatives and of the Senate
The oaths are to be tendered by the Governor-General.
Senior Public Officers
Members of the Public Service Commission
The Attorney-General (if a public officer)
The Permanent Secretary (Finance)
Ministers of the Government and Parliamentary Secretaries
The oaths are to be tendered by the Governor-General.

## PART II

The Chief Justice and the Judges of the Supreme Court
The oaths are to be tendered by the Governor-General.
Magistrates and Justices of the Peace
The oaths are to be tendered by the Governor-General or such person as the Governor-General
may appoint.
