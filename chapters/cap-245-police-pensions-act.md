## CHAPTER 245

POLICE PENSIONS ACT
An Act to regulate the grant of pensions and other allowances to non-commissioned
officers and constables of the Police Force.
[Amended by Act No. 33 of 1974, Act No. 24 of 1983, Act No. 27 of 1995.]

[15th January, 1931.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Police Pensions Act.
2\. Interpretation
In this Act—

“constable” means Police Constable appointed to the Force;
“Force” includes the whole body of men employed under the authority of the
Police Act, Chapter 244, with the exception of rural constables;
“non-commissioned officer” means Inspector of Police, Cadet Officer, Police
Sergeant, and Police Corporal appointed to the Force;
“pay” includes good conduct pay, and the personal, detective, house and lodging
allowances (including the estimated value of free quarters) which a non-commissioned
officer or constable is receiving at the time when he or she ceases to be a member of
the Force:
Provided that the amount to be allowed for house and lodging allowances shall not
exceed one-sixth of the pay of such non-commissioned officer or constable.
3\. Pensions to be charged on Consolidated Fund
There shall be charged on and paid out of the Consolidated Fund as hereinafter
provided all such sums of money as may from time to time be granted by way of pension,
gratuity, or other allowance in accordance with this Act to persons who have been in the
Force.
4\. Grant of pensions
Subject to the provisions of this Act, every non-commissioned officer and constable
who has served in the Force for ten years or upwards, may be granted on his or her
retirement a pension at the rate of fifteen-sixtieths of his or her pay with an addition of
one-sixtieth in respect of each complete year of such service in excess of ten:
Provided that no pension granted by virtue of this section shall exceed two-thirds of
the average pay which shall include personal, detective, house and lodging allowances
(including the estimated value of free quarters) of such non-commissioned officer or
constable during the three years immediately preceding his or her retirement.
4A. Joining of broken service
Where the Cabinet considers it just and equitable to do so, the Cabinet may direct that
any break in the service of a non-commissioned officer or a constable shall be
disregarded and the whole of his or her service shall be taken as pensionable service
including service after the 4th day of April, 1983.
5\. Cases in which pensions may not be granted
No pension shall be granted—
(a)

to any constable or non-commissioned officer below the rank of Police
Sergeant who has not either attained the age of fifty years or served in the
Force for twenty years;

(b)

to any non-commissioned officer of or above the rank of Police Sergeant
who has not either attained the age of fifty-five years or served in the Force
for twenty years,

and except—
(i) on medical evidence to the satisfaction of the Governor-General that such
non-commissioned officer or constable is incapacitated by reason of some
infirmity of mind or body for further service in the Force and that such
infirmity is likely to be permanent, and

(ii) on a certificate of the Chief of Police that such non-commissioned officer or
constable has discharged his or her duties with such diligence and fidelity as
to justify the grant to him or her of a pension.
6\. Gratuities
A non-commissioned officer or constable who has not completed ten years’ service in
the Force may be granted a gratuity at the rate of half a month’s salary for each complete
six months of service:
Provided that the Governor-General is satisfied upon medical evidence that such
non-commissioned officer or constable is incapacitated by reason of some infirmity of
mind or body for further service in the Force, and that such infirmity is likely to be
permanent:
Provided also that the Chief of Police certifies that such non-commissioned officer
or constable has discharged his or her duties with such diligence and fidelity as to justify
the grant to him or her of a gratuity.
7\. Allowance in case of injury
Where upon medical evidence the Governor-General is satisfied that a noncommissioned officer or constable has been permanently injured—
(a)

in the actual execution of his or her duty;

(b)

without his or her own default; and

(c)

by some injury specifically attributable to the nature of his or her duty,

and the retirement of such non-commissioned officer or constable is thereby necessitated
or materially accelerated, the Governor-General may, in addition to the pension, if any,
receivable by such non-commissioned officer or constable under this Act, grant to him or
her such annual allowance as the Governor-General shall think fit, but so that any
allowance hereby granted together with the pension awarded, shall not exceed two-thirds
of his or her pay.
8\. Compulsory retirement
The Governor-General, upon the advice of the Public Service Commission or, as the
case may be, the Chief of Police, may order a member of the Force to be retired after he
or she shall have completed twenty years of service or shall have attained—
(a)

in the case of any non-commissioned officer of or above the rank of Police
Sergeant, fifty-five years;

(b)

in the case of any member below the rank of Police Sergeant, fifty years.

9\. Right to take reduced pension and gratuity
(1) Every non-commissioned officer or constable who shall become eligible for
pension under the provisions of this Act may elect to take on his or her retirement a
reduced pension and gratuity in lieu of the pension provided for by this Act.
(2) Every non-commissioned officer or constable who shall become eligible for
pension shall notify the Governor-General of his or her election within six months of the
date when he or she shall have so become eligible.
(3) An election once exercised shall be irrevocable.
10\. Definition of reduced pension and gratuity

Reduced pension shall be a pension equal to three-fourths of the amount of pension
which would be payable under this Act, including any allowance granted under section 7,
and a gratuity shall be the amount which one-fourth of the pension payable under this
Act, including such allowance when multiplied by twelve and one-half, shall represent.
11\. Gratuity where an officer dies in the public service or after retirement
(1) (a) Subject to the provisions of section 12, when a non-commissioned officer or
constable who is not serving on probation, and who has served in the Force for not less
than five years, dies while still in the public service, it shall be lawful for the GovernorGeneral to grant to his or her legal personal representative a gratuity of an amount not
exceeding his or her annual pensionable emoluments or his or her commuted pension
gratuity (if any) whichever is the greater—
(b) for the purpose of this subsection—
(i) “annual pensionable emoluments” means the emoluments which would be
taken for the purpose of computing any pension or gratuity granted to such
person if he or she had retired at the date of his or her death by reason of
infirmity of mind or body in the circumstances described in section 5,
(ii) “commuted pension gratuity” means the gratuity, if any, which might have
been granted under section 10.
(2) Where any such person to whom a pension, gratuity or other allowance has been
granted under this Act dies after retirement from the public service and the sums paid or
payable to him or her at his or her death on account of any pension, gratuity or other
allowance in respect of his or her public service are less than the amount of the annual
pensionable emoluments enjoyed by him or her at the date of his or her retirement, it shall
be lawful for the Governor-General to grant to his or her legal personal representative a
gratuity equal to the deficiency.
12\. Gratuity when death due to injuries
When a non-commissioned officer or constable dies as the result of injuries received
in the execution of his or her duty the Governor-General may at his or her discretion grant
to the legal personal representative of such non-commissioned officer or constable a
gratuity of an amount to be at the discretion of the Governor-General but not exceeding
two years’ pensionable emoluments of such non-commissioned officer or constable:
Provided that if a gratuity of an amount equal to or exceeding one year’s
pensionable emoluments of such non-commissioned officer or constable is granted under
the provisions of this section no gratuity shall be payable under the provisions of section
11\.
13\. Pension not of right
No non-commissioned officer or constable shall have an absolute right to
compensation for past services, or to any pension, gratuity or other allowance under this
Act, nor shall anything herein limit the right of the Crown, the Governor-General, the
Public Service Commission or the Chief of Police to dismiss any such non-commissioned
officer or constable without compensation.
14\. Pension not to be assignable, etc.
No pension granted under this Act shall be assignable or transferable or liable to be
attached, sequestered, or levied upon, for or in respect of any debt or claim whatsoever.
15\. Pension to cease on conviction

If any non-commissioned officer or constable to whom a pension has been granted
under this Act is convicted by a court of any offence for which he or she is sentenced to
death or any term of imprisonment exceeding twelve months, and does not within two
months after such conviction receive a free pardon of Her Majesty, or of the GovernorGeneral on Her Majesty’s behalf under section 72(1)(a) of the Constitution, then, in every
such case, such pension shall forthwith cease:
Provided always that in the case of a non-commissioned officer or constable who
after conviction as above described receives a free pardon at any time, the GovernorGeneral may, if he or she thinks fit, restore the pension.
16\. Pension to cease on bankruptcy
If any non-commissioned officer or constable to whom a pension has been granted
under this Act becomes a bankrupt, then such pension shall forthwith cease:
Provided that in any case where a pension ceases by reason of the bankruptcy of the
pensioner the Governor-General may, from time to time during the remainder of such
pensioner’s life, or during such shorter period or periods, either continuous or
discontinuous, as the Governor-General shall think fit, pay all or any part of the moneys
to which such pensioner would have been entitled by way of pension had he or she not
become a bankrupt to, or apply the same for the maintenance and personal support or
benefit of all or any, exclusive of the other or others, of the following persons, namely,
such pensioner and any wife, child, or children of his or her, in such proportions and
manner as the Governor-General thinks proper.
17\. Pensionable service to include war service, etc.
Service only in a pensionable office shall be regarded as pensionable service, except
that—
(a)

if a period of service in a civil capacity other than a pensionable office is
followed immediately by service in a pensionable office and the noncommissioned officer or constable is confirmed therein, the whole of the
period may, with the approval of the Governor-General, be regarded as
pensionable service;

(b)

if a period of war service is followed within six years of demobilisation by
service in a pensionable office and the non-commissioned officer or
constable is confirmed therein, the whole period of war service may, with
the approval of the Governor-General, be regarded as pensionable service.

18\. Disqualification
No pension, gratuity or other allowance under this Act shall be granted to a noncommissioned officer or constable first appointed to the Force on or after the 4th April,
1983\.
19\. Regulations
The Governor-General may make regulations for the better carrying out of the
provisions of this Act, but no such regulations shall have force or effect unless approved
by resolution of the House of Representatives and of the Senate.
