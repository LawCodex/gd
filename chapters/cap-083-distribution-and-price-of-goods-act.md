## CHAPTER 83

DISTRIBUTION AND PRICE OF GOODS ACT
An Act to make provision for the regulation of prices, and of the supply of goods.
[Act No. 17 of 1972.]

[2nd September, 1972.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Distribution and Price of Goods Act.
2\. Interpretation
In this Act—
“Controller of Supplies” means the person appointed as Controller of Supplies for
the purposes of this Act;
“goods” includes all kinds of movable or personal property, including animals;

“maximum price” means the maximum price of goods fixed in accordance with the
provisions of section 3;
“price” includes any valuable consideration of any kind whatsoever whether direct
or indirect;
“price control officer” means a person appointed to be a price control officer for
the purposes of this Act;
“retail”, when used in relation to a sale, means a sale by a trader to a purchaser who
buys or offers to buy goods otherwise than for the purpose of resale;
“sale” includes an agreement to sell, an offer to sell, the exposure of goods for sale,
the publication of a price list, the furnishing of a quotation, or any act or notification
whatsoever whereby willingness to enter into any transaction or sale is expressed;
“trader” means a person who carries on the business of selling goods and includes a
person carrying on business in the course of which he or she supplies goods for the
purpose of, or in pursuance of, a contract made by him or her for work, labour and
materials, and a commission agent.
3\. Fixing of maximum price of goods
(1) The Minister, if it appears to him or her to be necessary for the purpose of
maintaining, controlling or regulating supplies so as to secure a sufficiency of those
essential to the well-being of the community, their equitable distribution and their
availability at fair price, may by Order published in the Gazette fix and declare the
maximum price for which goods may be sold by retail or wholesale either throughout
Grenada or in a specified part thereof.
(2) An Order made under subsection (1) may direct that the maximum price of goods
shall not exceed a specified sum, or may direct the manner in which the maximum price
shall be ascertained, and may—
(a)

fix different maximum prices for the same class of goods having regard to
the terms upon which, and the circumstances in which, the goods are
manufactured, imported or sold; and

(b)

fix alternative methods by which the maximum price may be ascertained for
the same or different classes of goods.

(3) Any Order made under this section may contain such incidental and supplementary
provisions as are necessary for giving effect to the purpose mentioned in subsection (1).
4\. Effect of Order and penalty for infringement
(1) Goods specified in an Order made under section 3 shall not be sold, by whatever
unit of weight, measurement or quantity the sale is effected, at a price which corresponds
to a price in excess of the maximum price specified in the Order.
(2) Any person who contravenes any provision of an Order made under section 3 shall
be guilty of an offence.
5\. Sale at more than maximum price, and upon certain conditions, and hoarding of
goods, prohibited
(1) No person shall, in respect of goods the maximum price of which has been fixed
under the provisions of section 3—

(a)

sell or buy or agree to buy any such goods at a price more than the maximum
price;

(b)

impose, whether at the time of delivery of any such goods or at a subsequent
time prior to payment therefor, any condition relating to the sale of such
goods or to payment therefor other than a condition requiring immediate
payment for such goods; or

(c)

with the object of speculating or profiteering hoard any such goods.

(2) A person who contravenes any of the provisions of subsection (1) shall be guilty
of an offence and liable—
(a)

on summary conviction, to imprisonment for three months and to a fine of
one thousand five hundred dollars;

(b)

on conviction on indictment, to imprisonment for two years and to a fine of
eight thousand dollars.

6\. Sales may be prohibited unless price of goods is marked
The Controller of Supplies, by notice in the Gazette and in a newspaper circulating in
Grenada, may specify any kind of goods the price of which has been fixed and prohibit
traders from selling goods of that kind by retail unless the selling price has first been
conspicuously marked thereon in a legible manner, and a trader who in disobedience to
such a notice sells by retail any goods of that kind unmarked or insufficiently marked
with the true selling price shall be guilty of an offence.
7\. Refusal to sell
(1) A person carrying on a business in the course of which goods of any description in
respect of which an Order has been made under section 3 are normally sold and having in
his or her possession a stock of goods of such description who—
(a)

falsely denies that he or she has such goods in his or her possession; or

(b)

without reasonable cause refuses to sell such goods in reasonable quantities
upon tender of immediate payment of the maximum price fixed therefor,

shall be guilty of an offence and, on summary conviction, liable to imprisonment for three
months and to a fine of one thousand five hundred dollars.
(2) Nothing in this section shall be deemed to require a person to sell any goods
which he or she has in stock for the purpose of advertisement or as samples.
8\. Invoice to be supplied if requested
(1) A trader who whether by wholesale or retail sells goods the maximum price of
which has been fixed shall, on request, supply to the purchaser at the time of sale an
invoice showing the date of sale, a description of the goods sufficient to identify them, the
quantity of such goods and the price charged therefor.
(2) A trader who contravenes the provisions of subsection (1) shall be guilty of an
offence.
9\. Powers of a price control officer
(1) A price control officer may—

(a)

require a trader to supply him or her either orally or in writing with
information in his or her possession relating to goods;

(b)

at any reasonable time require a trader to produce for his or her inspection
any document or account whatsoever relating wholly or in part to the sale of
goods by such trader in the course of his or her business;

(c)

require a trader to furnish him or her with copies of any such document as is
referred to in paragraph (b);

(d)

during business hours enter any place or premises in the occupation of a
trader for the purpose of ascertaining whether or not the provisions of this
Act are being complied with, and search for and inspect any document of the
kind referred to in paragraph (b) and make copies or abstracts of such a
document;

(e)

take possession of any document which appears to him or her to constitute
prima facie evidence of a breach of an Order made or notice given or
published under the provisions of this Act.

(2) Any person who—
(a)

obstructs a price control officer in the exercise of his or her powers under
subsection (1);

(b)

wilfully fails to give any information or to produce any document lawfully
required of him or her under the provisions of subsection (1); or

(c)

knowingly gives to a price control officer any false information relating to
goods,

shall be guilty of an offence.
10\. Records to be kept by traders
(1) Every trader dealing in goods the maximum price of which has been fixed shall
maintain a record containing details of all purchases of such goods.
(2) The Controller of Supplies may by notice in writing require any trader to keep
such records or accounts as are specified in the notice.
(3) A trader who contravenes the provisions of this section or any requirement of a
notice given thereunder shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction,
to a fine of seven hundred and fifty dollars.
11\. Power to requisition goods
(1) Where it appears to the Minister to be necessary for the purpose of ensuring a
sufficient supply of foodstuffs or essential commodities for use or consumption in
Grenada or their equitable distribution therein, he or she may authorise the Controller of
Supplies to requisition or cause to be requisitioned goods appropriate to that purpose and
to provide for their distribution and sale.
(2) The Minister shall not act in pursuance of subsection (1) until written notice of his
or her intention so to act has been served upon the person having possession or control of
the goods specified and, on being served with the notice, that person shall forthwith hold
the goods to the order of the Controller of Supplies in accordance with the terms of such
notice.

(3) Upon requisitioning the goods the Controller of Supplies shall pay as
compensation to the person having possession or control of such goods a sum equal to the
price which that person might reasonably have been expected to obtain upon a sale
thereof, regard being had to the condition of the goods.
(4) If the person having possession or control of goods requisitioned pursuant to this
section fails without reasonable cause to hold such goods in accordance with the terms of
the notice any officer specially authorised in that behalf in writing by the Controller of
Supplies may enter any premises whereon such goods are kept and seize them.
(5) Any person who contravenes the provisions of this section or of a notice served
pursuant to subsection (2) shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary
conviction, to imprisonment for three months and to a fine of one thousand five hundred
dollars.
12\. Power to order distribution and sale of essential goods
Where it appears to the Minister necessary for the purpose of ensuring an equitable
distribution of foodstuffs or essential commodities in Grenada or any part thereof he or
she may, by Order, authorise the Controller of Supplies to arrange exclusive or limited
right of distribution and sale of such foodstuffs or commodities through a trader or traders
to be approved by the Controller of Supplies.
13\. Liability of officers of corporation
Where a person charged with an offence under this Act is a corporation every person
who, at the time of the commission of the offence, was a director or officer of the
corporation may be charged jointly in the same proceedings with such body corporate, and
where such corporation is convicted of the offence every such director or officer shall be
deemed to be guilty of that offence unless he or she proves that the offence was committed
without his or her knowledge or that he or she exercised all due diligence to prevent the
commission of the offence.
14\. Appearance and plea by corporation
(1) Where a corporation is charged with an offence against this Act the corporation
may appear and plead to the charge or indictment by its representative, by entering a plea
in writing; and if either the corporation does not appear by representative or, though it
does so appear, fails to enter any plea the court shall cause a plea of not guilty to be
recorded and the trial shall proceed accordingly.
(2) Where a corporation is charged, whether alone or jointly with some other person,
with an indictable offence punishable under this Act the magistrate may, if he or she is of
opinion that the evidence offered on the part of the prosecution is sufficient to put the
accused corporation upon trial, commit the corporation for trial.
(3) Any indictment filed against a corporation or other document addressed to a
corporation may be served upon the corporation by leaving it at, or sending it by post to,
the corporation at any place in Grenada at which it trades or conducts business.
(4) In this section, the word “representative” means a person duly appointed by the
corporation to represent it for the purpose of appearing and pleading to a charge or
indictment, and a representative so appointed shall not, by virtue only of being so
appointed, be qualified to act on behalf of the corporation before any court for any other
purpose.

(5) A representative for the purpose of this section need not be appointed under the
seal of the corporation, and a statement in writing purporting to be signed by a managing
director of the corporation, or by any person (by whatever name called) having, or being
one of the persons having, the management of the affairs of the corporation, to the effect
that the person named in the statement has been appointed as the representative of the
corporation for the purposes of appearance or plea shall be prima facie evidence that that
person has been so appointed.
15\. Liability of employers and others
A person shall be liable under this Act for the acts and omissions of a partner, agent,
or employee in so far as they relate to the business of such person and if such partner,
agent, or employee performs any act or suffers any omission which contravenes this Act
such person and his or her partner, agent or employee may be charged jointly and
severally and shall be liable to the penalties provided by this Act, unless it is proved that
the contravention occurred without his or her knowledge or that he or she exercised all
due diligence to prevent its occurrence.
16\. Compensation to purchaser of goods at more than the maximum price
Where a trader is convicted of selling goods at more than the maximum price and—
(a)

the court considers that the purchaser of the goods did not know and could
not reasonably be expected to know that the price paid by him or her for
such goods was more than the maximum price; or

(b)

the purchaser of the goods reported the sale to the Controller of Supplies or
to a police officer of or above the rank of Police Inspector within forty-eight
hours of such sale,

the court may, in addition to any other penalty which it may impose, order the trader to
pay to the purchaser the difference between the maximum price and the price at which the
goods were sold together with such sum, if any, as the court may deem adequate to
compensate the purchaser for any expense incurred by him or her.
17\. General penalty
A person guilty of an offence against this Act for which no penalty is expressly
provided shall, on summary conviction, be liable to a fine of seven hundred and fifty
dollars and to imprisonment for three months.

DISTRIBUTION AND PRICE OF GOODS (FUEL AND COOKING GAS) ORDER
In exercise of the power conferred by section 3 of the Distribution and Price of
Goods Act, Chapter 83, the Minister makes the following Order.
[SRO 17 of 2001.]

[22nd December, 2000.]
1\. Citation
This Order may be cited as the Distribution and Price of Goods (Fuel and Cooking
Gas) Order, 2001.
2\. Maximum price of goods
(1) The maximum retail price in Grenada of the goods listed in column 1 of Part 1 of
the Schedule shall not exceed the sums respectively specified in column 2 of that Part.
(2) The maximum retail price in Carriacou and Petite Martinique of the goods listed
in column 1 of Part 2 of the Schedule shall not exceed the sums respectively specified in
column 2 of that Part.
3\. Commencement
This Order is deemed to have come into operation on 22nd December, 2000.
