## CHAPTER 162

LAND SURVEYORS ACT
An Act to provide for the licensing of land surveyors, for regulating their
professional conduct, for the making land surveys, and for connected matters.
[Act No. 24 of 1990 amended by Act No. 52 of 1991, SRO 82 of 2001, Act No. 21 of 2007, Act
No. 33 of 2007.]

[14th December, 1990.]

## PART I

Preliminary

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Land Surveyors Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act—
“Board” means the Land Surveyors Board established under section 4;
“certificate” means a certificate of registration issued under section 4I;
“Director” means the Director of Lands and Surveys appointed under section 3;
“Minister” means the Minister responsible for matters of and concerning land survey;
“owner”, in relation to land, means any person receiving or entitled to receive rents
or profits from a tenant or occupier thereof whether on his or her own account or as an
agent or trustee for any other person or any person who would so receive the rents or
profits if the land were let;
“plan” includes a map, plot, diagram, aerial photograph or a mosaic (compiled
from aerial photographs) approved by the Director as suitable for survey purposes;
“public survey” means a survey of land which is owned by a public body or in
which a public body has an interest, or a survey which forms part of the survey of such
land;
“Register” means the register of surveyors kept pursuant to section 4D;
“registered surveyor” means a person registered as a land surveyor pursuant to the
provisions of this Act;
“regulations” means regulations made under section 28;
“survey” means the act or process of—
(a)

determining the form, contour, position, area, height, depth or any other
particulars of—
(i) the earth’s surface, whether of land or water, or
(ii) any natural or artificial features on, below or above any part of the
earth’s surface; or

(b)

planning the position or the boundary lines of a part of the earth’s surface, or
of any natural or artificial features referred to in paragraph (a)(ii),

and includes the act or process of making or obtaining any plan therefrom;
“Survey Department” means the department of Government exercising functions
relating to surveys.

### 2A.

Surveyors within the Surveyor’s Department deemed registered
(1) A person may hold any office to which this section applies and shall, so long as he
or she continues to hold such office, be deemed to be a registered surveyor in accordance
with the provisions of this Act.
(2) Subsection (1) applies to any office within the Surveyor’s Department
appointment to which a person is required to be qualified to practise as a surveyor in
Grenada.

### 3.

Appointment and duties of Director of Lands and Surveys
(1) There shall be a Director of Lands and Surveys, who shall be a public officer and
a registered surveyor.
(2) The Director of Lands and Surveys shall be the authority for the preparation and
publication of the official map of Grenada and who, subject to the provisions of this Act,
shall—

(a)

supervise and control all surveys;

(b)

direct all public surveys;

(c)

examine and approve all general and specific survey plans before registration
pursuant to the Deeds and Lands Registry Act, Chapter 79;

(d)

take charge of and preserve all survey records;

(e)

cancel, or amend in accordance with the provisions of any Act, any survey
plan or diagram found to be incorrect, outdated or inadequate; and

(f)

prepare, certify and issue at the request of any person, upon payment of the
prescribed fee, copies of diagrams and documents available to the public and
kept within the Survey Department.

(3) A person shall not, in the preparation or publication of any map, use any material
published in the official map of Grenada without having obtained written consent of the
Director.

## PART II

The Land and Surveys Board

### 4.

Establishment of Land and Surveys Board
(1) There is hereby established a Board to be known as the Land and Surveys Board
consisting of—
(a)

the Director;

(b)

two registered surveyors appointed by the Minister;

(c)

an attorney-at-law of not less than seven years standing appointed by the
Minister;

(d)

the Head of the Physical Planning Unit or his or her nominee.

(2) The Minister shall appoint one of those persons to be Chairperson of the Board.
(3) The first two surveyors appointed by the Minister under subsection (1)(b) shall be
deemed to be surveyors registered pursuant to the provisions of this Act.
(4) The Board shall appoint a secretary.
(5) A member of the Board appointed pursuant to subsection (1)(b) and (c), shall be
appointed for a term not exceeding two years but may, from time to time, be reappointed, and may be removed from office by the Minister.

### 4A.

Meetings of the Board
(1) The Board shall meet at such times and place as the Chairperson may decide.
(2) At any meeting of the Board three members present shall form a quorum and no
business shall be transacted at any meetings unless a quorum is present.
(3) At any meeting of the Board, the Chairperson shall preside and in his or her
absence, the members present shall elect from among themselves one member to preside
over the meeting.
(4) The member presiding at any meeting shall have a deliberative vote and, in the
case of an equality of votes, shall also have a casting vote.
(5) Subject to the provisions of this Act, the Board may determine its own procedure.

(6) The Board may appoint such committees from among its members or other
persons, whether or not they are registered surveyors, as it thinks fit to assist or advise the
Board on such matters arising out of its functions under this Act as are referred to them
by the Board with the consent of the Minister.

### 4B.

Objectives of the Board
The objectives of the Board are to—
(a)

regulate the practice of professional and land surveying and govern all
registered surveyors in a manner that best serves and protects the public;

(b)

establish, maintain and develop standards for practise of land surveying and
professional conduct of surveyors; and

(c)

promote public awareness of the role of the Board and communicate and cooperate with other professional organisations for the advancement of the best
interests of the profession.

### 4C.

Functions of the Board
The functions of the Board shall be to—
(a)

keep and maintain a register of surveyors;

(b)

conduct such professional interviews as the Board considers necessary for
the purpose of enabling persons to qualify for registration under this Act
using if required external qualified persons;

(c)

approve or reject applications for registration under section 4G or to approve
any such applications subject to such restrictions as it thinks to impose;

(d)

establish, monitor, maintain and develop the standard of professional
conduct or ethics of registered surveyors;

(e)

hear and determine disciplinary proceedings against surveyors in accordance
with the provisions of this Act.

### 4D.

Register
(1) The Board shall keep and maintain a register of surveyors in which shall be
entered the names of all persons registered under this Act, the qualifications by virtue of
which they are so registered and such other particulars in relation thereto as may, from
time to time, be determined by the Board.
(2) A person may, on payment of the prescribed fee, inspect the register at any time
during the office hours of the Board.
(3) A copy of any entry in the Register kept and maintained under this section
purporting to be certified by the Chairperson as a true copy thereof shall in all
proceedings be prima facie evidence of the matters specified therein.

### 4E.

Common seal of the Board
(1) The common seal of the Board shall bear such device as the Board may approve
and the seal may, from time to time, be broken, changed, altered or made anew by the
Board as it thinks fit.
(2) The common seal shall be kept in the custody of the Chairperson and shall be
authenticated by the Chairperson or other member acting in the absence of the Chairperson.
(3) Any document purporting to be sealed and authenticated under subsection (2)
shall, until the contrary is proved, be deemed to be validly executed.

### 4F.

Illegal practice
(1) Subject to the provisions of this Act, a person shall not certify to the correctness or
accuracy of any survey unless he or she is registered as a surveyor pursuant to the
provisions of this Act.
(2) Subject to the provisions of this Act, a person shall not—
(a)

use or cause or permit to be used any written works, titles or initials or any
abbreviation thereof which are intended to cause or may reasonably cause
any person to believe that the person using them is authorised to supply
survey services in Grenada; or

(b)

advertise or hold himself or herself out or conduct himself or herself in any
way or by any means as a person authorised to supply survey services in
Grenada,

unless at the time of doing so the person is a registered surveyor.
(3) Subject to the provisions of this Act, a person shall not perform or execute any
survey or engage in any survey work in Grenada unless he or she is a registered surveyor.
(4) A person who contravenes the provisions of this section commits an offence and is
liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding five thousand dollars or to a term of
imprisonment not exceeding three months.

### 4G.

Persons entitled to be registered
(1) A person who wishes to be registered as a surveyor under this Act shall make an
application in writing to the Board accompanied by the following—
(a)

a declaration in writing stating—
(i) his or her full name,
(ii) the principal address at which he or she practises in Grenada,
(iii) certified copies of his or her qualifications;

(b)

a certificate of good character; and

(c)

such other information as the Board may require.

(2) Where upon receipt of the information required under subsection (1), the Board is
satisfied that the applicant—
(a)

is the holder of a certificate, diploma or degree granted by a recognised
university, technical institute or professional institution in land survey and
that he or she is entitled to practice as a land surveyor in the country in
which the certificate, diploma or degree was granted;

(b)

has such proper training in surveying recognised by the Board and passed
such other examination as the Board may require; or

(c)

has passed a professional interview conducted by the Board to determine
whether he or she has the aptitude and knowledge to effectively perform or
engage in survey work in Grenada.

(3) Notwithstanding subsection (2), the Board may refuse to register any person who
is not, in the opinion of the Board, of good character and reputation.
(4) The Board may require an applicant for registration to produce such documents or
other evidence relating to his or her qualifications or submit to such examination as the
Board shall prescribe.

### 4H.

Certificate of registration

(1) Where the Board decides to approve an application, it shall grant the applicant a
certificate of registration as provided for in the Schedule, on the payment of the
prescribed fees.
(2) Where the Board decides not to approve an application, it shall by notice in
writing inform the applicant of its decision and shall state its reasons for refusing to grant
the application and shall inform the applicant of his or her right to appeal pursuant to
section 4J.
(3) If the certificate of registration is cancelled or suspended the holder of the
certificate of registration shall forthwith deliver the certificate of registration to the
Director.

### 4I.

Cancellation of registration
(1) The Board shall cancel the registration of a surveyor if it is satisfied that he or
she—
(a)

is an undischarged bankrupt;

(b)

has obtained his or her certificate by fraud, misrepresentation or mistake;

(c)

has been convicted of an offence involving fraud, dishonesty or moral
turpitude;

(d)

has been guilty of such improper conduct which renders him or her, in the
opinion of the Board, unfit to be a registered surveyor;

(e)

has qualification for registration pursuant to section 4G which has been
withdrawn or cancelled by the authority through which it was acquired or by
which it was awarded.

(2) A person whose registration has been cancelled may apply for re-instatement and
upon receipt of satisfactory evidence of proper reasons for his or her re-instatement the
Board may re-instate his or her certificate of registration.

### 4J.

Appeals against decisions of the Board
A person aggrieved by—
(a)

the refusal of the Board of his or her application for registration;

(b)

the cancellation of his or her registration under section 4I; or

(c)

the refusal of the Board to re-instate his or her registration, may appeal to the
High Court.

### 4K.

Complaints of unprofessional conduct
(1) Any complaint of the conduct of a surveyor in his or her professional capacity
must be made in writing to the Board and must contain specific allegations of acts or
omissions on which the complaint is based.
(2) The Board shall deal with all disciplinary matters in accordance with the
provisions of this Act.

### 4L.

Conduct to disciplinary proceedings
In any disciplinary proceedings before the Board the following rules shall apply—
(a)

sufficient notice of the time and place of the hearing together with particulars
of the complaint shall be given to the person against whom the complaint has
been made and the complainant;

(b)

any party to the proceedings shall, if he or she so requires, be entitled to be
heard by the Board and to be represented or assisted by an attorney-at-law;

(c)

unless the Board otherwise directs, the proceedings shall be held in public.

### 4M.

Evidence in disciplinary proceedings
(1) The Board, by notice in writing signed by the Chairperson or the secretary, may
require any person to attend and give evidence before it at the hearing of a complaint
under this Act, and to produce any documents in the custody or control of that person
relating to the subject matter of the complaint.
(2) The Board shall require evidence to be given on oath, and either orally or in
writing, and for that purpose the Chairperson may administer any oath.
(3) Witnesses shall have the same immunities and privileges in relation to
proceedings before the Board as in proceedings in a court of law.
(4) The secretary shall keep the minutes of the proceedings and record the evidence in
such manner as the Board shall direct.
(5) A person who, without lawful excuse, refuses or fails to attend and give evidence
when required to do so by the Board, or to answer truly any question put to him or her or
to produce any document required of him or her commits an offence and is liable, on
summary conviction, to a fine not exceeding two thousand dollars.

### 4N.

Disciplinary powers of the Board
(1) If the Board finds that the acts or omissions alleged in the complaint—
(a)

have not been proved to the satisfaction of the Board;

(b)

do not constitute misconduct of the surveyor in his or her professional
capacity; or

(c)

are of a trivial nature,

the Board shall dismiss the complaint.
(2) If the Board finds a surveyor guilty of misconduct in his or her professional
capacity, it may, having regard to the gravity of the allegations proved in the
proceedings—
(a)

order that his or her certificate of registration be cancelled for a period not
exceeding two years as the Board shall think fit; or

(b)

reprimand the surveyor.

### 4O.

Appeal
A person who has been found guilty of misconduct by the Board under section 4M may
appeal to the High Court.

### 4P.

Persons registered prior to 19th July, 2007
A person who has been licensed as a surveyor under this Act prior to 19th July, 2007,
shall be deemed to have been registered in accordance with section 4G, and the Board
shall, upon proof that such person had in fact been so licensed, issue to him or her, a
certificate of registration in accordance with the provisions of section 4H.

## PART III

The Conduct of Surveys

### 13.

Surveys to be conducted in accordance with Act and regulations
(1) Subject to subsection (3) a surveyor shall carry out every survey undertaken by
him or her in such manner as will ensure that the survey accords in all respects with the
provisions of this Act and regulations.
(2) A surveyor shall be responsible for the correctness and completeness of every
survey carried out by him or her or under his or her supervision.
(3) The Director may, in his or her discretion in the case of a particular survey, by
notice in writing to the surveyor direct that the standards of accuracy prescribed may be
relaxed in such manner, to such extent, and subject to such conditions as he or she may
specify in the notice.
(4) Neither the Government nor any public officer shall be liable for any defective
survey, or any work appertaining thereto, performed by a licensed surveyor,
notwithstanding that any plan relating to such survey has been authenticated by the
signature of the Director.

### 14.

Only a surveyor may survey land for certain purposes
No person, other than a surveyor, shall—
(a)

survey any holding or land for the purpose of preparing any plan which is
attached to, or is referred to in, any document or instrument purporting to
confer, declare, transfer, limit, extinguish or otherwise deal with or affect
any right, title or interest, whether vested or contingent in or over any
holding or land, being a document or instrument which is required to be
registered; or

(b)

perform any survey which affects or may affect the definition of the
boundaries, or the location of survey marks, of any holding or land.

### 15.

Notice of entry before public survey
(1) For the purposes of a public survey the Director or any surveyor authorised by
him or her may enter upon land with such number of assistants as may reasonably be
required, and may affix or set up or place thereon or therein trigonometrical stations,
monuments, survey beacons, marks or poles, and do all things necessary for such survey.

(2) The surveyor shall give reasonable notice to the owner or occupier of the land of
his or her intention to enter thereon. Where the owner or occupier cannot be found notice
may be placed in a conspicuous place on the land.

### 16.

Compensation payable by Director
(1) Compensation shall be payable by the Director to the owner of any crops or trees
cut or damaged in the exercise of any of the powers conferred by section 15 (in this
section called “the claimant”) and if any question shall arise as to the amount of
compensation to be paid or the right of the claimant to recover compensation such
question shall, in default of agreement between the Director and all persons concerned, be
finally determined by a magistrate on application made by the Director or a person
authorised in writing by him or her in that behalf or by the claimant:
Provided that, save at the discretion of the Director, such application shall not be
granted if it is made by the claimant more than thirty days after the date on which the
crops or trees in respect of which the claim is brought were cut or damaged.
(2) Compensation payable under subsection (1) shall be charged upon the
Consolidated Fund.

### 17.

Notice of entry before survey other than public survey
For the purposes of any survey, other than a public survey, the surveyor shall give
reasonable notice to the owner or occupier of the land of his or her intention to enter
thereon. Where the owner or occupier cannot be found notice may be placed in a
conspicuous place on the land.

### 18.

Entry on land and compensation for damage by surveyor
(1) After giving notice as required by section 17 a surveyor may, with any other
person directly under his or her control to assist him or her, for the purpose of surveying
the land which he or she is employed to survey, enter on and pass over the land, whether
private or public causing as little inconvenience to the owner or occupier of such land as
is consistent with his or her duties.
(2) Compensation shall be payable to the owner or occupier of the land for any
damage done to the land by reason of the exercise of the powers contained in subsection
(1).
(3) Compensation payable shall be subject to agreement between the surveyor and the
owner or occupier of the land, but where such agreement is not possible the Director shall
act as arbitrator and in default of agreement the question of compensation shall be
determined by a magistrate and charged as provided in section 16.

## PART IV

The Preservation of Survey Marks

### 19.

Obliterating, etc., survey marks an offence
An unauthorised person who wilfully obliterates, removes or damages any
trigonometrical station, monument, survey beacon, mark or pole, or any boundary mark
affixed, set up or placed for the purpose of conducting any public or other survey under
this Act, shall be guilty of an offence and may, in addition to any punishment for the
offence, be ordered by the court to pay the cost of repairing or replacing the thing
obliterated, removed or damaged and of making any survey rendered necessary by the act
giving rise to the conviction.

### 20.

Obstruction, etc., of surveyor
A person who wilfully obstructs, hinders, resists or threatens a surveyor in the
execution of his or her duty in or about the conduct of any public survey or other survey
under this Act, or any assistant, workman or other person acting in aid of any such
surveyor, shall be guilty of an offence.

### 21.

Director may sue for monies due
Any sum due under the provisions of this Act may be sued for and recovered by the
Director or any person authorised by him or her in writing by action in any court.

### 22.

Director may delegate functions
The Director may, by notice published in the Gazette, delegate any of his or her
functions under this Act to any officer of the Survey Department by name or office and
may at any time revoke such appointment by notice published in the Gazette.

## PART V

General

### 23.

Survey plans, etc., to be sent to Director
(1) Every surveyor who executes a survey in accordance with the provisions of this
Act and regulations shall send to the Director all plans, field notes and computations
relating thereto, and all such plans, field notes and computations shall be deposited in the
Survey Department and shall become and remain the property of the Government.
(2) No person shall alter or in any way amend any plan deposited in the Survey
Department in accordance with subsection (1) without the written permission of the Director.

### 24.

Checks by Director and correction of errors in surveys
(1) The Director may at any time undertake such field and office checks on the survey
of a licensed surveyor as he or she thinks fit.
(2) The Director may, under section 25, by notice in writing, instruct any licensed
surveyor to correct at his or her own expense within a time specified in such notice any
error made by him or her in the survey represented by the plan submitted for authentication.
(3) In the event of such licensed surveyor refusing or neglecting within the time
specified to correct such error, the Director shall undertake such correction and recover
the whole cost of such correction from the licensed surveyor concerned.
(4) If a licensed surveyor refuses or neglects to pay the cost of the correction referred
to in subsection (3) within fourteen days of the same having been demanded of him or her
by the Director in writing, the Director may report the facts to the Board for disciplinary
action and, after due inquiry, the Board may order the licensed surveyor to pay the cost of
correction to the Director; and if such licensed surveyor refuses or neglects to comply
with the order within fourteen days from the date of the order, the Board may,
notwithstanding the provisions of section 21, suspend the licence of the licensed surveyor
until the cost of the correction has been paid, or for a period not exceeding three years,
whichever it thinks fit.

### 25.

Need and effect of authentication of plans by Director
(1) No land shall be deemed to have been surveyed or resurveyed until the plan
thereof has been authenticated by the signature of the Director.

(2) Every plan authenticated by the signature of the Director shall in any proceeding
of a legal or quasi-legal nature be conclusive evidence of the survey information
comprised therein unless and until such plan is cancelled by the Director by virtue of
section 26.
(3) Every plan purporting to bear the signature of the Director shall be deemed to be
properly authenticated unless and until the contrary is proved.

### 26.

Director may cancel inaccurate plans
(1) Where, in the case of a document or instrument to which an authenticated plan is
attached, or in which reference to such a plan is made—
(a)

the plan is found to be inaccurate by reason of any error or omission in the
survey; or

(b)

the plan does not conform with the terms and conditions subject to which
permission to subdivide the land to which the plan relates has been given,

the provisions of section 24 shall apply, mutatis mutandis, and the Director may cancel
the authentication of such plan and may recall any copies which have been issued.
(2) The Director shall forthwith upon the cancellation of the authentication of any
plan notify in writing—
(a)

the owner of the land to which such plan relates;

(b)

the surveyor by whom the survey was executed; and

(c)

the Registrar.

### 27.

Aerial photography for use in mapping
(1) No person shall carry out any aerial photography of Grenada for use in mapping
or any similar purpose, unless he or she shall have given not less than one month’s notice
in writing to the Director of his or her intention to do so.
(2) A person who carries out any such aerial photography shall, if the Director so
requires in writing—
(a)

produce to the Director for his or her inspection all the photographs thereby
produced or such of them as the Director may specify; and

(b)

supply to the Director, at the expense of the Director, such copies,
dispositive and diagrams as the Director may require of such photographs.

(3) The supply of photographs to the Director under this section shall not in any way
affect the copyright therein of the person supplying them or other owner of such copyright.
(4) Any person who contravenes the provisions of subsection (1) shall be guilty of an
offence.

### 28.

Regulations
The Minister may make regulations—
(a)

prescribing the manner in which surveys are to be made, the records to be
kept by licensed surveyors and the manner of keeping the same;

(b)

prescribing the manner in which survey marks shall be constructed;

(c)

with regard to plans of survey and their preparation and the matters to be
shown thereon;

(d)

with regard to the publication, issue, service and form of the notices to be
published, issued or served under this Act or regulations;

(e)

prescribing the returns to be made by licensed surveyors to the Director;

(f)

requiring surveyors to report to the Director matters connected with surveys
on which they are engaged or with previous surveys, ascertained by them
during the course of their work;

(g)

for securing the maintenance of survey marks in their correct position and
the preservation of the same;

(h)

providing for the checking of surveys alleged to be erroneous and for the
payment of the expenses of such checking;

(i)

providing for the checking of distance measuring equipment and instruments
used by surveyors;

(j)

providing for and regulating the inspection and the taking of copies of plans
of surveys in any office of the Survey Department;

(k)

prescribing fees which may be charged under this Act;

(l)

prescribing other matters required or permitted to be prescribed.

### 29.

General penalty
A person guilty of an offence under this Act shall, on summary conviction, be liable to
a fine of five hundred dollars and to imprisonment for six months.

### 30.

Joint and several liability
Where, in connection with matters relating to this Act, there exists a relationship of
master and servant, principal and agent, or corporation and officer of that corporation,
each of the persons in that relationship shall be jointly and severally responsible for
compliance with the provisions of this Act.

### 31.

Repeal of Chapter No. 155 of 1958
Subject to the next ensuing section, Chapter No. 155 of the 1958 Revised Editions of
the Laws of Grenada is repealed.

### 32.

Surveyors licensed under Chapter No. 155 of 1958 deemed licensed under this
Act
Every surveyor who at the commencement of this Act is licensed under Chapter No.
155 of the 1958 Revised Edition of the Laws of Grenada shall be deemed to be licensed
under this Act.

LAND SURVEYORS RULES

### 1.

Citation
These Rules may be cited as the Land Surveyors Rules.

## PART I

Admission of Surveyors to Practise

### 2.

Legal qualifications required
Any person desiring to practise the profession of Authorised Surveyor in Grenada
shall comply with the following rules—
(a)

\*

He or she shall produce a licence to practise as a surveyor or shall be legally
qualified to practise and produce a certificate to that effect from any College
or Institute of Surveyors in Great Britain or Ireland or in any Commonwealth

Note.—(Of the Land Surveyors Ordinance, Chapter No. 155 of the Revised Edition 1958).

country where regulations exist or examinations are held for the qualification
of surveyors.
(b)

His or her certificate shall be accompanied by testimonials or duly certified
copies thereof as to actual experience in the field and office of any country in
which he or she has been practising with the duration of such service and a
satisfactory report from the Government of any Commonwealth country in
which he or she may have been practising as to accuracy of work, steadiness
and general trustworthiness.

### 3.

Proof of experience
Any person who is unable to comply with the requirements of rule 2 shall produce a
certificate of three years continuous articled service under a legally qualified Surveyor in
Great Britain or Ireland or in any Commonwealth country in which regulations exist for
qualification of surveyors. During two years at least of such service he or she shall have
been actively employed in the field and during the whole of which he or she shall have
been actually learning his or her profession as a surveyor.

### 4.

Examples of previous work
(1) Certificates under rules 2 and 3 shall be accompanied by—
(a)

an original Field Book showing an actual survey carried out by the candidate
of a surround not less than one mile in chainage with plot from Field Book,
and calculations, showing mathematical close and area by double longitudes;

(b)

a finished plan of survey as a specimen of drawing drawn with due regard to
technical notation and conforming generally with rules under rule 23 of Part
II of these Rules.

(2) The survey chosen should have at least one natural feature boundary.

### 5.

Examination by Board of Examiners
On receipt of the above documents by the Chairperson of the Board of Examiners the
candidate will be informed as to the subjects, if any, in which he or she will be required to
submit to examination, with the date and place at which such examination will be held.

### 6.

Instructions to candidate
The candidate shall then inform the Chairperson of the Board of his or her intention to
present himself or herself for examination, and, at least one month before the date thereof,
shall tender to the Permanent Secretary (Finance) of the State in which such examination
shall be held a fee of twenty-five dollars to be afterwards paid to the Board of Examiners.

### 7.

Subjects for examinations
The subjects for examination will consist of—
(a)

adjustments of instruments and chains used in surveying with methods of
allowance for known errors;

(b)

solar and stellar observations for determination of latitude and true meridian.
Calculations dependent on such observations and methods of applying
differences of meridian and variations of needle;

(c)

surveying with chain alone, with instruments and chain including traverse
surveying, and dealing with obstacles to angular or linear measurement; and

(d)

practical geometry and trigonometry.

### 8.

Requisite study
The questions on these subjects will embrace a knowledge of the first three books of
Euclid and problems in algebra requiring quadratic equations for solution.

### 9.

Test of proficiency
The candidate may be required to complete a survey in the State as a test of his or her
proficiency and expedition in field work.

### 10.

Board of examiners report to Cabinet
On completion of the examination the Board of Examiners shall report to the Cabinet
as to the qualifications of each candidate with a recommendation that a licence should or
should not issue.

### 11.

Unsuccessful candidates
Unsuccessful candidates will be eligible to present themselves at two subsequent
examinations before disqualification, provided that not less than six months shall elapse
between each examination.

### 12.

Licence subject to withdrawal
All licences shall be subject to withdrawal by the Cabinet as provided by rule 43 of
these Rules.

## PART II

System of Surveys

### 13.

Initiation of surveys
Surveys shall be initiated in the following manner: Instructions shall issue on a
prescribed form bearing the signature of the Chief Technical Officer (Surveys).

### 14.

Incomplete surveys
Instructions to survey if not completed shall be returned to the Survey Office on the
last day of each month with an explanation in writing of the cause of delay. If the
explanation be deemed satisfactory the instructions will re-issue.

### 15.

Unforeseen causes
In cases where from unforeseen causes the instructions appear to be impracticable they
shall be returned forthwith with report in writing.

### 16.

Surveyors to ascertain practicality of instructions
Surveyors shall ascertain as far as possible by reference to office records whether the
instructions are practicable or not.

### 17.

Required instruments
Instruments used for angular measurement shall have an Azimuth circle of not less
than 5 in. diameter, Transit Instruments being preferable.

### 18.

Measurements laid down at Survey Office
All measurements to be made with Gunter’s chain of 66 feet or 100 links in length a
standard for which is laid down at the Survey Office.

### 19.

Field books
(1) Field books shall be kept on the single line system as a rule, diagrams being only
admissible for marginal reference for small surveys such as town and village allotments.
(2) All field books and notes shall be open to inspection by the Chief Technical
Officer (Surveys). Field books and notes of surveys undertaken under instructions from
survey office shall be lodged as records when complete.

### 20.

Road surveys
Road surveys shall be carried on by a single traverse the stations of which shall be
practically in the centre of the road formation. Where the boundaries of properties or
allotments fronting the road are to be defined the points of intersection are to be laid off
through the stations of centre traverse to the requisite width in the usual manner.

### 21.

Feature surveys
Feature surveys shall be carried on by a traverse within 50 links average of the edge of
a river or other feature with offsets to the bank at every chain in Country Surveys and 25
links apart in towns and villages or in small allotments.

### 22.

Accuracy of instruments
Before commencing any survey the surveyor shall assure himself or herself of the
accuracy of his or her instruments and chain in the presence of the Chief Technical
Officer (Surveys) or his or her representative and shall also periodically test the same at
the place appointed for that purpose and at least once every month.

### 23.

Solar and stellar observations
The true bearing of the first line of any survey must be accurately obtained from solar
or stellar observation and in town and village surveys a meridian shall be laid down
permanently in one of the streets.

### 24.

All bearings to refer to true meridian
All bearings of surveys given in plan must without exception refer to the true
meridian.

### 25.

Boundary line
Every boundary line surveyed and shown on plan must be accurately run and
measured in the field with theodolite and chain. A calculated line will be accepted only in
cases where the actual line is inaccessible for instrument and chain.

### 26.

Actual line
In every case the actual line must be cleared through and calculated from an
independent traverse from point to point closing the survey independently of the
unchained line.

### 27.

Boundary of country lots

In surveying boundaries of country lots, the lines may be run by traversing by the back
angle provided all possible care is taken to ensure the correctness of the angular work by
testing for adjustment and by check bearing to distant traverse points.

### 28.

Entries in field book
Where features, fences, roads, trenches, or paths are crossed by any line of survey or
any distinguishing mark encountered, the cut of such object shall be accurately read on
the chain and entered in the field book, intersection angles being taken to any house or
object within a reasonable distance of the line.

### 29.

Survey of country lots
In surveys of country lots all information gathered during the work should be entered
in the field book and shown on the plan of survey, such as hills, ravines, houses,
cultivation, nature of soil and produce, swamps, marshes, etc.

### 30.

Connections
Connections should be run to any recent survey within 40 chains, and will be paid for
according to the tariff.

### 31.

Bearings to be taken on trigonometrical marks
Where the trigonometrical marks are visible, bearings should be taken to two of them
from at least two points of the survey. The position of the survey can then be verified on
the map of the State.

### 32.

Numbers of surveyed land
(1) From the date of these Rules coming into force every parcel of land surveyed shall
carry its proper consecutive number in its Parish as “53 St. Mark’s,” the first number
following on the present highest number.
(2) In towns and villages the number shall refer to the allotment.

### 33.

Boundary marks
(1) In addition to the boundary marks required by law, surveyors shall mark surveys
in the following manner—
(a)

when an old boundary so marked is resurveyed, hard wood pegs would be
established on a 5-link offset so that an instrument can be set up at new
corner and on new line. The old line can then be shown on the plan as the
boundary, while the new line becomes a base for future surveys, and should
be shown in blue as a traverse line;

(b)

where new boundaries are laid down, the hard wood peg will be driven into
the ground on the exact corner or intersection of lines of survey throughout
the work; where a large tree is available, a reference should be given to cut
on tree from the peg, to facilitate finding the spot for future work. Balata (or
Bully tree) or an equivalent native wood should be used for pegs. The corner
post or tree, or both, should be marked with a chisel as 15 (Broad Arrow) 16.
Subdivisions of these surveys in the future being numbered 15 A.B. & C.;

(c)

where a post or tree forms a common corner to two measurements, the
marking should be as 15 (Broad Arrow) 16;

(d)

when practicable, the neck or part of a glass bottle should be buried on the
point of the peg, by which means the exact position of the corner may be
verified when the peg has decayed;

(e)

in surveys of towns or villages the lines of survey should hold to the centres
of the street the intersection of such lines being marked with an iron bolt
driven flush with the ground. The boundaries of allotments being laid off at
the requisite width from the centre lines;

(f)

in road surveys the points of intersection of traverse lines shall be marked
with iron bolts driven down flush with surface.

(2) Extra marks to be left at every half mile and mile with a concrete pillar on side of
road set off at right angles to the line of chainage and the distance given on plan of
survey. Where mile posts are established the iron peg to be set off at right angles from the
post.
(3) Additional iron pegs to be driven down in line at every culvert bridge or other
work and distance and bearing given from parapet or other permanent mark and the
position shown on plan.

### 34.

Drawing of plans
(1) All plans should be drawn on the best quality of double elephant or antiquarian
drawing paper.
(2) All plans shall be neatly and carefully drawn and exhibit the following
information on the face, namely—
(a)

a title setting forth the name of the State, Quarter, District, Estate, or Town
and the number of the Act under which it has been measured;

(b)

a correct plot of the survey drawn with due regard to conventional signs as to
features, etc.—actual boundaries in black, subsidiary traverses and check
lines in blue. The plot to be tinted round the edge of actual area and area
given, and to show in different tints reserves, roads, rivers, and boundaries of
adjacent properties to show correctly the topography of the portion and
adjacent lands as far as practicable;

(c)

a traverse table of reference to show the correct bearings of all lines on plan
from true north and the correct length of each line chained by standard—
calculated bearings and lengths to be shown in blue;

(d)

a table of reference to corners by letters showing the distinctive mark or
marks left at each corner of survey;

(e)

memorandum setting forth scale of plan, instrument and chain used, method
of obtaining true meridian, with actual observations, date of survey, date of
transmission, and the signature of surveyor in attestations;

### 35.

Computation of areas
All areas are to be computed in acres, roods and perches, with the exception of town
and village lots, the areas of which shall be given in square feet. The area should be given
on the face of plan.

### 36.

Survey report
The report of the survey shall give all possible information as to occupations,
cultivation, clearing, and other improvements, with the names of owners or makers of
such improvement.

### 37.

Certification of report and plan
(1) The report and plan shall be certified as correct, and such certificate shall mean
that all the requirements of these Rules have been complied with in conducting the
survey.
(2) When on examination the report or plan is found incorrect, or unsatisfactory, the
survey fees shall be retained by the Chief Technical Officer (Surveys) until the
regulations have been complied with.
(3) When a survey has been completed to the satisfaction of the Chief Technical
Officer (Surveys), he or she shall certify a claim for the surveyor who made such survey
for the amount of his or her fees, according to the tariff of fees for surveyors for the time
being in force.

## PART III

Offences by Land Surveyors and Others

### 38.

Apprentice to be supervised by employer
No apprentice of under two years standing shall make and complete a survey
independently of the actual supervision of his or her employer.

### 39.

Illegal survey
No surveyor shall sign such plan or documents of an illegal survey.

### 40.

Surveyor to make survey as per these Rules
No surveyor shall neglect to mark his or her survey as laid down by these Rules.

### 41.

Destruction of survey mark without orders

No surveyor shall, without orders from the Chief Technical Officer (Surveys) destroy
any survey mark.

### 42.

False measurements
No apprentice or assistant to a surveyor shall wilfully make a false measurement with
intent to falsify a survey or wilfully make a false entry on field book, plan or any other
document.

### 43.

Liability for withdrawal of licence
Any surveyor who either wilfully or negligently contravenes the letter or spirit of the
Act or of these Rules made under it and who shall have been proved guilty of any offence
against the Act, or against the regulations, or who shall prove incompetent or
untrustworthy, shall be liable to have his or her licence withdrawn by the Cabinet.

### 44.

Inquiries into offences
Inquiries into offences committed by land surveyors shall be conducted by the Chief
Technical Officer (Surveys) or such officer as the Minister may for the purpose appoint.

### 45.

Nature of offences
The nature of the offence into which an inquiry is to be held shall be communicated to
the surveyor concerned, in writing, by the Chief Technical Officer (Surveys) or other
officer appointed, and the surveyor shall be called upon to state in writing before a day to
be specified, a reasonable interval being allowed, any grounds on which he or she relies
to exculpate himself or herself.

### 46.

Surveyor may be required to defend in person
If the surveyor fails to exculpate himself or herself to the satisfaction of the Chief
Technical Officer (Surveys) or other officer appointed, he or she may be required to
appear and defend himself or herself in person.

### 47.

Witnesses: surveyor allowed to question
If any witnesses are examined by the Chief Technical Officer (Surveys) or other
officer, the surveyor must be present and be allowed to ask questions on his or her own
behalf, and he or she must also be allowed to see any documents produced against him or
her.

### 48.

Witnesses: summoned by Chief Technical Officer (Surveys)
The Chief Technical Officer (Surveys) or other officer appointed may summon any
witness to give evidence at such inquiry, and shall forward to the Minister, for his or her
consideration, his or her report on the inquiry, together with any statements of the
witnesses, with as little delay as possible.
