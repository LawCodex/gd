## CHAPTER 103

FIRE BRIGADES ACT
An Act relating to Fire Brigades.
[26th January, 1957.]

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Fire Brigades Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act—
“Fire Brigades” means the Fire Brigade and the Volunteer Fire Brigade;
“fireman” means a member of the Fire Brigade;
“volunteer fireman” means a member of the Volunteer Fire Brigade.

### 3.

Establishment of fire fighting service
There shall be organised and established a fire fighting service under the supervision
and control of the Chief of Police comprising the following—
(a)

the Fire Brigade consisting of such members of the Police Force as shall be
posted for duty as firemen, such other members of the Police Force as may,
from time to time, be called out for duty as firemen, and such other fit and

proper persons as may be appointed by the Chief of Police as members of
the Fire Brigade;
(b)

the Volunteer Fire Brigade consisting of the members of the Royal Grenada
Special Reserve Police Force and such other fit and proper persons as may
be appointed by the Chief of Police as members of the Volunteer Fire
Brigade.

### 4.

Declaration on appointment as volunteer fireman
Every volunteer fireman shall on his or her appointment make and sign before the
Chief of Police the following declaration in a book to be kept by the Chief of Police: “I,
A.B., having been appointed a member of the Volunteer Fire Brigade, do hereby declare
that I will abide by the rules and regulations which are or may be established under the
provisions of the Fire Brigades Act”.

### 5.

Remuneration of Chief of Police and firemen
(1) The Chief of Police and other firemen shall, for their services under this Act,
receive such allowances as may be approved annually by the House of Representatives.
(2) Volunteer firemen undergoing training shall, for their services under this Act,
receive drill pay and shall, when called out for service by the Chief of Police, be paid for
their services from such moneys as may be approved annually by the House of
Representatives at the same rate as firemen of equivalent rank.

### 6.

Duties of firemen and volunteer firemen
It shall be the duty of every fireman and volunteer fireman and of every member of the
Police Force when called out for the purpose—
(a)

to meet for instruction and drill at such times as may be required of them by
the Chief of Police;

(b)

immediately on the alarm of fire, to turn out promptly with such equipment
as is considered necessary by the Chief of Police or the fireman in charge
who will take all steps necessary to bring the fire under control and to
extinguish it as soon as possible;

(c)

when on duty, to wear such distinguishing dress or badge as the Minister
may, from time to time, direct and as may be supplied to them by the Chief
of Police;

(d)

to obey such rules as may be made in pursuance of this Act for the
governance of the Fire Brigades and all lawful directions of the Chief of
Police or the fireman in charge.

### 7.

Penalties for neglect of duty
If any member of the Fire Brigades—
(a)

on any alarm of fire and without valid reason, shall fail to repair to the
nearest fire station; or

(b)

having repaired to the station shall, before being discharged from duty by the
Chief of Police or other person in charge, refuse or neglect to perform his or
her duty, or shall desert his or her post or otherwise misconduct himself or
herself; or

(c)

shall at any time damage, destroy or lose any article forming part of the fire
extinguishing apparatus or any article of dress supplied to him or her; or

(d)

be guilty of any neglect of duty,

he or she may, if a volunteer fireman, be liable to such fine as may be prescribed by rules
made under section 14 or to suspension or dismissal at the discretion of the Chief of
Police and, if a fireman, shall be liable to be dealt with in manner provided by the Police
Act, Chapter 244, for breach of discipline.

### 8.

Powers of Chief of Police, etc., on occasion of fire
(1) On the occasion of a fire the Chief of Police or other person in charge of a Fire
Brigade may in his or her discretion—
(a)

take command of other persons who may voluntarily place their services at
his or her disposal;

(b)

remove, or order a member of the Fire Brigades to remove, any person who by
his or her presence interferes with or obstructs the operations of a Brigade;

(c)

employ persons to aid in extinguishing fire or in removing furniture or goods
from any building on fire or in danger of fire, or to guard and secure the
same;

(d)

cause water to be shut off from the mains and pipes of any district in order to
give a greater supply and pressure of water in the district in which the fire
occurred or fire may break out;

(e)

direct the closing of any street in or near which the fire is burning;

(f)

generally, take any measures that may appear expedient for the protection of
life and property, with power by himself or herself or his or her men to break
into or through, or take possession of, or pull down, any premises for the
purpose of putting an end to the fire, doing as little damage as possible.

### 9.

Protection to persons acting under this Act
No liability shall attach to the Chief of Police, or to the firemen, or to the volunteer
firemen, or to persons volunteering or employed as aforesaid, for any damage or for any
act caused or done bona fide in the exercise of the powers conferred by this Act.

### 10.

Special provision as to damage for insurance purposes
Any damage occasioned by any member of the Fire Brigades or any person acting under
the instructions of a Fire Brigades Officer in the execution of his or her duty at the scene
of a fire, or of an apprehended fire, shall be deemed to be damage by fire within the
meaning of any policy of insurance against fire.

### 11.

Control of fire fighting until Fire Brigades arrive
Until the Fire Brigades arrive at the scene of a fire—
(a)

the Senior Police Officer present may, in his or her discretion, take
command of all police officers and of any persons who may voluntarily
place themselves at his or her disposal, and may remove, or order a police
officer to remove, any person who interferes by his or her presence with the
operations of those under his or her command, and generally he or she may
take any measures that seem to him or her expedient for the protection of life
and property, with power to himself or herself, or those commanded so to do
by him or her, to break into or through or take possession of or pull down
any premises for the purpose of putting an end to the fire, doing as little
damage as possible;

(b)

all such police officers and other persons are hereby authorised to act under
the command and in the manner aforesaid;

(c)

any damage occasioned by a person acting under command and in the
manner aforesaid, or acting under the instructions of a Fire Brigades Officer in
the execution of his or her duty, at the scene of a fire, or of an apprehended
fire, shall be deemed to be damage by fire within the meaning of any policy
of insurance against fire.

### 12.

Special duties of Chief of Police
It shall be the special duty of the Chief of Police—
(a)

to maintain the discipline of the Fire Brigades;

(b)

to see that the engines and all other fire apparatus are kept in good order and
thorough repair;

(c)

to inquire for and to examine into all shops and other places where shavings
or other combustible or dangerous materials may be collected and deposited
or to authorise any other member of the Police Force so to do and at all times
to be vigilant in the removal of the same when in his or her opinion the same
may be a source of danger by fire, and to direct the tenant or occupier of
such shops or other places to remove the same, and, in the case of such
tenant or occupier’s neglect or refusal to do so, to cause the same to be
removed at the expense of the occupants or tenants who shall in addition be
guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of two
hundred and fifty dollars and to imprisonment for one month for such
neglect or refusal;

(d)

to take cognisance of and to cause prosecutions to be instituted either in his
or her own name or in the name of a person authorised in writing by him or
her in all cases of infraction of the laws, for the prevention of fire within the
limits of any town or district;

(e)

at the close of every financial year, or more often if so required by the
Minister, to report to him or her in writing on the condition of the stations,
hydrants, hose, carriages, hosepipes, and other fire apparatus and property
under his or her charge; the income and expenditure of the fire fighting
service in detail for the previous year; the number and location of fire alarm
stations; the number of fires and the causes thereof, or as near as can be
ascertained; the number and description of buildings destroyed or injured,
together with the names of the owners or occupants; the amount of
insurances, if any; all accidents by fire which may happen within any city,
borough, town, or district; and to give, to the Minister, such other
information or suggestions as may in his or her opinion be desirable.

### 13.

Offences
Every person who—
(a)

assaults or impedes or obstructs any member of the Fire Brigades in the
execution of his or her duty; or

(b)

aids or abets any other person in assaulting or obstructing any member of the
Fire Brigades in the execution of his or her duty; or

(c)

wilfully removes, or assists or is concerned in removing any chain, rope, or
other barrier placed in or upon or across any street under the authority of this
Act; or

(d)

not being a member of the Fire Brigades wears the uniform of a Brigade at
any fire; or

(e)

obstructs any fire-plug or hydrant by placing or causing to be placed thereon
or thereby any matter or thing whatsoever; or

(f)

wilfully damages or injures any vehicle, engine, hose, fire-plug, hydrant, or
other apparatus of a Brigade; or

(g)

unless authorised by a member of a Brigade or without other lawful
authority removes or is concerned in removing the furniture or goods from
any building on fire or in danger of fire,

shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of five hundred
dollars and to imprisonment for two months.

### 14.

Rules
The Chief of Police may, from time to time, subject to the approval of the Minister,
appoint fire stations and make such rules as he or she may deem proper for the good
government of the Fire Brigades and for securing their efficiency.

### 15.

Saving
A Fire Brigade existing at the date of the coming into operation of this Act shall be
deemed to have been duly constituted thereunder.
