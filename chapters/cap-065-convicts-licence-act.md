## CHAPTER 65

CONVICTS LICENCE ACT
An Act to provide for the granting of convicts’ licences.
[23rd March, 1916.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Convicts Licence Act.
2\. Interpretation
In this Act—
“district” means magisterial district within the meaning of the Magistrates Act;
“imprisonment” means imprisonment in the prison in St. George’s;
“licence-holder” means the person to whom a licence is granted under this Act and
who holds or is entitled to hold such licence;

“Minister” means the Minister for the time being responsible for home affairs;
“officer of the court”, in section 16, means the clerk of the magistrate’s court or, as
the case may be, the Registrar of the High Court;
“prisoner” means any person sentenced to, and now undergoing or hereafter to
undergo, imprisonment for a period of twelve calender months or upwards, whether by
one sentence or cumulative sentences.
3\. Governor-General may grant, revoke or alter licence
(1) It shall be lawful for the Governor-General by an order in writing under his or her
hand, to grant to any prisoner a licence to be at large, or in such place as in such licence
shall be expressed, during such portion of his or her imprisonment and upon such
conditions in all respects as to the Governor-General may seem fit, and it shall be lawful for
the Governor-General to revoke or alter such licence by order under his or her hand at his or
her pleasure.
(2) The licence may be in the form set forth in the First Schedule or in such other
form as the Governor-General may order.
(3) On any licence being granted a copy thereof shall be kept in the office of the
Secretary to the Cabinet and another copy thereof shall be sent to the Chief of Police, and
shall be kept by him or her at his or her office.
4\. Licence-holder not liable to be imprisoned, etc.
(1) So long as a licence continues in force and unrevoked, the licence-holder shall not
be liable to be imprisoned by reason of his or her sentence but shall be allowed to go and
remain at large according to the terms of the licence.
(2) A licence-holder, notwithstanding his or her conviction of felony, may acquire and
hold personal property, and may maintain actions for its recovery, or in respect of any
damage thereto, if and during such time as the licence continues unrevoked and
unforfeited.
5\. Warrant for apprehension to be issued on revocation of licence
Whenever the Governor-General revokes a licence other than in consequence of a
conviction for any offence it shall be lawful for the Minister to signify under his or her
hand to any magistrate that such licence has been revoked, and the magistrate shall
forthwith issue a warrant under his or her hand for the apprehension of the licence-holder.
The licence-holder when apprehended under the warrant shall be brought, as soon as
conveniently may be, before a magistrate, and the magistrate shall thereupon make out a
warrant under his or her hand for the recommitment of the licence-holder to the prison
from which he or she was released by virtue of the said licence, and such licence-holder
shall be so recommitted accordingly, and shall thereupon be remitted to his or her original
sentence, and shall undergo the residue thereof as if such licence had never been granted:
Provided that the time during which the licence-holder was at large under his or her
licence shall be reckoned as served under this sentence.
6\. Liabilities of licence-holder
Every licence-holder shall within twenty-four hours after he or she is liberated from
prison, notify the place of his or her residence to the magistrate of the district in which he
or she resides, and whenever he or she changes such residence within the same district,
shall notify such change to the magistrate of the district, and whenever he or she is about
to leave a district he or she shall notify such his or her intention to the magistrate of that
district stating the place to which he or she is going, and also, if required, and so far as is
practicable, his or her address at that place, and whenever he or she arrives in any district

he or she shall forthwith notify his or her place of residence to the magistrate of such last
mentioned district; moreover, every male licence-holder shall once in each month report
himself or herself either to the Chief of Police or to the police officer in charge of the
nearest police station, and such report shall be made at such time in the month, and either
personally or by letter, as the Chief of Police may direct, and it shall be the duty of every
police officer who receives any such report to forward it immediately to the Chief of Police.
If a licence-holder to whom this section applies fails to comply with any of the
requirements of this section, he or she shall in every such case be guilty of an offence
unless he or she proves to the satisfaction of the magistrate before whom he or she is tried,
either that being on journey he or she tarried no longer in the place, in respect of which he
or she is charged with failing to notify his or her place of residence, than was reasonably
necessary, or that otherwise he or she did his or her best to act in conformity with the law;
and on conviction of such offence it shall be lawful for the magistrate in his or her
discretion, either to forfeit his or her licence, or to sentence him or her to imprisonment
for a term not exceeding three months.
7\. Forfeiture of licence
If a licence-holder is convicted either by the verdict of a jury or upon his or her own
confession of any offence for which he or she is indicted, his or her licence shall be
forthwith forfeited by virtue of his or her conviction.
8\. Penalty for failure to produce licence
If a licence-holder—
(a)

fails to produce his or her licence when required to do so by any Judge or
magistrate before whom he or she may be brought charged with any offence,
or by any police officer in whose custody he or she may be, and also fails to
make any reasonable excuse why he or she does not produce the same; or

(b)

breaks any of the other conditions of his or her licence by an act that is not of
itself punishable either upon indictment or upon summary conviction,

he or she shall be deemed guilty of an offence punishable summarily by imprisonment for
three months.
9\. Offences to be prosecuted summarily
Any offence under this Act may be prosecuted summarily before a magistrate.
10\. Penalty on holders of licences getting their livelihood by dishonest means
A police officer may without warrant take into custody a licence-holder if it appears to
such police officer that the licence-holder is getting his or her living by dishonest means,
and may bring him or her before a magistrate for adjudication. If it appears from the facts
proved before such magistrate that there are reasonable grounds for believing that the
licence-holder so brought before him or her is getting his or her livelihood by dishonest
means, such licence-holder shall be deemed to be guilty of an offence against this Act and
his or her licence shall be forthwith forfeited.
11\. Apprehension of licensees
(1) Any police officer may without warrant take into custody any licence-holder
whom he or she reasonably suspects of having committed any offence and may take him
or her before a magistrate to be dealt with according to law.
(2) A licence-holder may be convicted before a magistrate of an offence against the
last preceding section although he or she was brought before the magistrate on some other
charge, or not in accordance with that section.

12\. Notice of convictions to be forwarded to the Minister without delay
When a licence-holder is convicted of an offence under this Act, the punishment for
which has been other than forfeiture of his or her licence, or for any offence under any
other Act, the magistrate by whom the licence-holder has been convicted shall, without
delay, forward by post or otherwise to the Minister for the information of the GovernorGeneral, a certificate in the form set forth in the Second Schedule, and thereupon the
licence may be revoked in the manner provided by this Act.
13\. Effect of forfeiture of revocation of licence
Where a licence is forfeited by a conviction on indictment of any offence or is revoked
in pursuance of a summary conviction under this or any other Act, the licence-holder shall,
in addition to any imprisonment or penalty to which he or she may be sentenced for the
substantive offence in consequence of which his or her licence is forfeited or revoked,
further undergo a period of imprisonment equal to the portion of his or her term of
imprisonment that remained unexpired at the time of his or her licence being granted, and
shall be dealt with in all respects as if such period had formed part of his or her original
sentence and such period, if imprisonment be adjudged in respect of the substantive
offence, shall commence at the expiration of such last mentioned imprisonment.
14\. Punishment on forfeiture of licence
Where a licence is forfeited under section 6 or 10, the licence-holder shall undergo a
term of imprisonment equal to the portion of his or her imprisonment that remained
unexpired at the time his or her licence was granted, and he or she shall for that purpose be
committed to prison under the order of the magistrate before whom he or she is convicted,
there to be dealt with accordingly.
15\. Warrant for apprehension of licence-holder on revocation in pursuance of
summary conviction
Whenever the Governor-General revokes a licence in pursuance of a summary
conviction under this or any other Act, the Minister shall forthwith signify under his or
her hand to any magistrate that such licence has been revoked, and such magistrate
shall—
(a)

if the licence-holder is not undergoing imprisonment in respect of the
offence in consequence of which his or her licence is revoked, forthwith
issue his or her warrant under his or her hand for the apprehension of such
licence-holder, and on such licence-holder being brought before any
magistrate on such warrant, the magistrate shall by warrant under his or her
hand commit him or her to prison, there to undergo the term of
imprisonment to which he or she has become liable by virtue of section 13;
or

(b)

if the licence-holder is undergoing imprisonment in respect of the offence in
consequence of which his or her licence is revoked, forthwith notify the
revocation to the Commissioner of Prisons, and thereupon the licence-holder
shall be deemed to be committed to prison to further undergo the term of
imprisonment to which he or she has become liable by virtue of section 13.

16\. Committal of licence-holder on forfeiture of licence
Where a licence is forfeited under section 7, the officer of the court before which the
licence-holder has been convicted shall forthwith notify forfeiture to the Commissioner of
Prisons, and thereupon the licence-holder shall be deemed to be committed to prison to
further undergo the term of imprisonment to which he or she has become liable by virtue
of section 13.

17\. Minister may prescribe forms
The Minister may prescribe forms to be used under this Act.
18\. Reservation of prerogative of mercy
Nothing in this Act shall affect Her Majesty’s Royal Prerogative of Mercy, or any like
prerogative or power, exercisable by the Governor-General under the Constitution.

CONDITIONS

---

### 1.

The holder shall preserve this licence and produce it when called upon to do so by a Judge,
magistrate or police officer.

### 2.

The holder shall abstain from any violation of the law.

### 3.

The holder shall not habitually associate with notoriously bad characters such as reputed
thieves or prostitutes.

### 4.

The holder shall not lead an idle and dissolute life without visible means of obtaining an
honest livelihood.
If this licence is revoked by the Governor-General, the holder shall be liable to be remitted to
his or her original sentence, and shall undergo the residue thereof as if such licence had never
been granted, and if this licence be revoked by the Governor-General in pursuance of a
summary conviction, or is forfeited in consequence of a conviction of the holder for any
offence, the holder shall be liable to undergo the unexpired term of imprisonment
abovementioned in addition to the punishment inflicted on him or her for such offence.
