## CHAPTER 300

SEDITION ACT
An Act to define the offence of sedition, and to provide for the
prosecution and punishment thereof.
[Act No. 10 of 1964.]

[29th February, 1964.]

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Sedition Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act—
“import” includes—
(a)

to bring into Grenada by sea or air;

(b)

to bring within the inland waters of Grenada, whether or not the publication
is brought ashore and whether or not there is an intention to bring the same
ashore;

“periodical publication” includes every publication issued periodically or in parts
or numbers at intervals whether regular or irregular;
“publication” includes all written or printed matter and everything, whether of a
nature similar to written or printed matter or not, containing any visible representation,
or by its form, shape, or in any manner capable of suggesting words or ideas, and
every copy and reproduction of any publication;
“seditious assembly” means five or more persons assembled together with a
seditious intention;
“seditious publication” means a publication having a seditious intention;

“seditious words” means words having a seditious intention.

### 3.

Seditious intention explained
(1) A “seditious intention” is an intention to—
(a)

bring into hatred or contempt or to excite disaffection against the person of
Her Majesty, Her heirs or successors, or the Government as by law
established;

(b)

excite Her Majesty’s subjects or inhabitants of Grenada to attempt to procure
the alteration, otherwise than by lawful means, of any matter in Grenada as
by law established;

(c)

bring into hatred or contempt or to excite disaffection against the
administration of justice in Grenada;

(d)

raise discontent or disaffection amongst Her Majesty’s subjects or
inhabitants of Grenada;

(e)

promote feelings of ill-will and hostility between different classes of the
population of Grenada; or

(f)

incite any person to commit any crime against the person, against property,
against public order or otherwise in disturbance of the peace,

but an act, speech or publication is not seditious by reason only that it intends to—
(i) show that Her Majesty has been misled or mistaken in any of Her measures,
(ii) point out errors or defects in the Government or the Constitution as by law
established or in legislation or in the administration of justice with a view to
the remedying of such errors or defects,
(iii) persuade Her Majesty’s subjects or inhabitants of Grenada to attempt to
procure by lawful means the alteration of any matter in Grenada as by law
established, or
(iv) point out, with a view to their removal, any matters which are producing or
have a tendency to produce feelings of ill-will and enmity between different
classes of the population of Grenada.
(2) In determining whether the intention with which any act was done, any words
were spoken, or any document was published, was or was not seditious, every person
shall be deemed to intend the consequences which would naturally follow from his or her
conduct at the time and under the circumstances in which he or she so conducted himself
or herself.

### 4.

Offence of seditious assembly
Every person who is guilty of having been a party to a seditious assembly shall be
guilty of the offence of seditious assembly and liable for a first offence to imprisonment
for two years and to a fine of five thousand dollars and for any subsequent offence to
imprisonment for three years.

### 5.

Offence of sedition
(1) A person who—
(a)

does or attempts to do, or makes any preparation to do, or conspires with any
person to do, any act with a seditious intention;

(b)

utters any seditious words;

(c)

prints, publishes, sells, offers for sale, distributes or reproduces any seditious
publication; or

(d)

imports any seditious publication, unless he or she has no reason to believe
that it is seditious,

shall be guilty of the offence of sedition and liable for a first offence to imprisonment for
two years and to a fine of five thousand dollars and for any subsequent offence to
imprisonment for three years, and any seditious publication shall be forfeited.
(2) A person who without lawful excuse has in his or her possession any seditious
publication shall be guilty of an offence and liable for a first offence to imprisonment for
one year and to a fine of three thousand dollars and for any subsequent offence to
imprisonment for two years, and such publication shall be forfeited.
(3) It shall be no defence to a charge under this section that the publication or the
words alleged to have a seditious intention is or are true.

### 6.

Limitations as to prosecution
(1) No prosecution for an offence under section 5 shall be begin except within six
months after the offence is committed.
(2) A person shall not be prosecuted for an offence under section 5 without the
written consent of the Attorney-General.

### 7.

Evidential provision
No person shall be convicted of an offence under section 5 on the uncorroborated
testimony of one witness.
