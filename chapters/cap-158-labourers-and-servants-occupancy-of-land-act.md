## CHAPTER 158

LABOURERS AND SERVANTS OCCUPANCY OF LAND ACT
An Act to provide for the termination of the occupancy of lands held incidental to
service, and for the appraisement and payment of the value of growing crops on
such lands.
[11th August, 1886.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Labourers and Servants Occupancy of Land Act.
2\. Interpretation
In this Act, the word “owner” includes the attorney, agent or manager of the owner of
land, or any other person representing the owner, and also a lessee.
3\. Notice to terminate tenancy

In all cases in which the relationship of master and servant or employer and employee
exists between the owner and occupier of any cultivable land, in which no fixed term for
the duration of the occupancy has been agreed on between the parties, and when the
occupancy is liable to cease on the relationship being determined, it shall be lawful for
either party to terminate the occupancy of the land by giving one month’s notice expiring
at any time, either verbally in the presence of a witness or in writing, to the other party of
his or her intention to put an end to it.
4\. If occupier refuses to quit after notice, owner may apply for
warrant of ejectment
If the occupier refuses to give up possession at the expiration of the notice, the owner
may serve the occupier with a notice in writing in the form in the First Schedule, or to the
like effect, of his or her intention to apply verbally on a given date, not less than three
clear days from the service of the notice, to the magistrate of the district in which the land
is situate for a warrant of ejectment against the occupier. The notice may be served
personally or left with some person at the last place of abode of the occupier.
5\. Magistrate to hear application and, if satisfied, to grant warrant
On application made in open Court under section 4 the magistrate shall hear the
evidence of the applicant and his or her witnesses; and also, if he or she appears, the
evidence of the occupier and his or her witnesses; and if satisfied that the applicant is
entitled to have the occupier ejected, the magistrate shall forthwith issue his or her
warrant, in the form set forth in the Second Schedule, directing any police officer to eject
the occupier therefrom and deliver possession to the owner of the land, which warrant shall
be executed as soon as possible.
6\. Owner to pay value of any growing crop or permit occupier to reap it
When the notice to terminate the occupancy of land is given by the owner, and there is
at the date of the expiration of the notice any growing crop on the land planted by the
occupier before the notice to terminate the occupancy was given, the owner shall pay to
the occupier the value of the growing crop at the date of the expiration of the notice:
Provided that if both parties shall agree thereto the occupier, in lieu of being paid the
value, shall be allowed to reap the crop at maturity, and for this purpose to have free
access to the land until the same be reaped.
7\. If parties cannot agree as to value, application may be made for appraisement
If the value of the growing crop cannot be agreed upon between the owner and
occupier, or the value if agreed upon is not paid forthwith, either party may serve the
other with a notice in writing in the form in the Third Schedule, or to the like effect, of his
or her intention to apply verbally on a given date, not less than three clear days from the
service of the notice, to the magistrate of the district in which the land is situate to appoint
an appraiser to value the crop.
8\. Magistrate to appoint an appraiser
Upon any such application as last aforesaid, the magistrate, after hearing the parties and
their witnesses as in the case of application for ejectment shall, if satisfied that the applicant
is entitled to have the growing crop appraised, forthwith appoint some fit person who may
be willing to appraise it.
9\. Appraiser to attend on land, value growing crop and certify to magistrate

The appraiser shall forthwith attend personally upon the land in question, and after
appraising the value of the growing crop, shall within seven days of his or her
appraisement certify the value to the magistrate in writing in the form in the Fourth
Schedule and such valuation shall be final unless for good cause shown the magistrate
thinks fit to direct a fresh appraisement, which he or she is hereby empowered to do.
10\. Appraiser refusing to act guilty of contempt of court
If the appraiser so appointed shall, without showing good cause, refuse or neglect to act,
he or she shall be adjudged guilty of a contempt of court.
11\. Remuneration of appraiser
The appraiser shall receive for his or her services such sum not exceeding two dollars
and fifty cents as the magistrate at the time of appointing him or her shall fix, which sum
shall be paid in the first instance to the magistrate by the person on whose application the
appraiser is appointed, at the time of his or her appointment.
12\. After appraisement magistrate to decide who shall bear costs
After the appraiser has certified the value of the growing crop to the magistrate, the
magistrate shall decide who shall bear the costs of and incidental to the application and
appraisement, and for that purpose may, if he or she thinks fit, direct that the parties be
verbally summoned to attend before him or her on a given date.
13\. Magistrate to make order for payment of appraised value and costs
The magistrate shall then proceed to make an order on the owner for the payment to
the occupier of the appraised value of the growing crop, together with such costs as may
have been paid in the first instance by the occupier and which the magistrate shall have
decided shall be borne by the owner; and a copy of the order shall be forthwith served
upon the owner by a police officer. The order shall be in the form set forth in the Fifth
Schedule.
14\. Order how enforced
The order, if not obeyed forthwith, may be enforced in the same way as a judgement.
