## CHAPTER 174

LIQUOR DEALERS’ LICENCES ACT
An Act relating to the sale of intoxicating liquor.
[Amended by Act No. 6 of 1968, Act No. 5 of 1970, Act No. 45 of 1973, Act No. 7 of 1988.]

[1st January, 1911.]

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Liquor Dealers’ Licences Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act—
“certificate” means a certificate granted by a Licensing Board under this Act;
“gallon” means an imperial gallon according to the standard legally established in
Grenada;
“information” includes any complaint or charge;
“intoxicating liquor” includes every description of spirits, wines, beer, ale, porter,
stout, cider, perry and other malt liquor;
“licence” means a licence issued under this Act authorising the selling or bartering of
intoxicating liquor;
“licensed dealer” means a person duly licensed under this Act to sell or barter
intoxicating liquor, and such expression includes a person duly authorised by the
Permanent Secretary to temporarily carry on the business of any licensed dealer under
the provisions of this Act;
“licensed premises” means any house, shop, room, office or bar specified in the
licence of a licensed dealer as the place where he or she may sell or barter intoxicating
liquor;
“Minister” means the Minister for the time being responsible for finance;
“Permanent Secretary” means the Permanent Secretary (Finance);
“proof ” means of the strength of proof as ascertained by Syke’s hydrometer;
“revenue officer” includes all persons employed on any duty or service relating to
the Customs and Excise laws, by the order or with the concurrence, whether
previously given or not, of the Permanent Secretary;
“spirits” means every description of brandy, hollands, gin, whisky, rum, bitters,
liqueurs or cordials, whether mixed with any other fluid or unmixed, but does not
include malt liquor, wine, methylated spirit, bay rum, or any spirit which is perfumed or
otherwise treated and is used and described for purposes of sale as a perfume, scent or
flavouring essence.

Licence Required for Sale of Intoxicating Liquor

### 3.

No intoxicating liquor to be sold without a licence
(1) Any person who shall sell or expose for sale any intoxicating liquor without being
duly licensed to sell the same, or at any place or in any manner that he or she is not
authorised by his or her licence to sell the same, shall be guilty of an offence.
(2) It shall not be an offence to sell or expose for sale intoxicating liquor without a
licence under any of the following conditions—
(a)

where the intoxicating liquor shall be sold by virtue of any legal process or
any law authorising the sale;

(b)

where the intoxicating liquor shall be in the nature of medicated spirits or
spirits made up in medicine and sold by a duly qualified medical practitioner
or a registered pharmacist;

(c)

where the intoxicating liquor shall form part of the estate of a deceased
person, if the Permanent Secretary shall authorise its sale, or where the
intoxicating liquor shall be sold by a licensed auctioneer under conditions
approved by the Permanent Secretary;

(d)

on board any steamer or vessel calling at Grenada, any sale thereon of
intoxicating liquor to be consumed on shipboard;

(e)

any sale authorised under and conducted according to the provisions of the
Excise Tax Act, Chapter 94.

### 4.

Licence granted only under certificate of Licensing Board
A licence to sell intoxicating liquor may be issued only upon production of a
certificate granted by a Licensing Board under the provisions of this Act.
Licensing Boards, Districts and Sessions

### 5.

Certificate how and where obtainable
(1) Any person who desires to obtain a licence under this Act shall apply for a
certificate as to his or her fitness to hold a licence and as to the suitability of the premises
sought by him or her to be licensed; and such application shall be heard in open court at a
Licensing Session of the district in which are situated the premises wherein he or she
intends to sell intoxicating liquor.
(2) Any such certificate shall be granted only by a Licensing Board or a majority of
such Board present at such Licensing Session; and in the event of an equal division of the
Board, the chairperson shall have a casting vote.

### 6.

Licensing districts and constitution of Licensing Boards
(1) For the purposes of this Act, Grenada shall be divided into the following seven
Licensing Districts, namely—
(a)

the parish of Saint George;

(b)

the parish of Saint David;

(c)

the parish of Saint Andrew;

(d)

the parish of Saint Patrick;

(e)

the parish of Saint Mark;

(f)

the parish of Saint John;

(g)

the islands of Carriacou and Petit Martinique and the neighbouring islets
lying to the North of the parallel of twelve degrees and twenty minutes of
North latitude.

(2) For each of the Licensing Districts there shall be established a Licensing Board
consisting of the magistrate of the district and three other persons to be appointed by the
Governor-General.
(3) The quorum of a Licensing Board shall be three members including the magistrate
of the district, and the magistrate shall be the chairperson of the Board.
(4) In case of an equality of votes in a question at a session the chairperson shall have
a second or casting vote.
(5) If for any cause less than three members of a Licensing Board are able to attend at
the time and place appointed for a session the power of the Board to adjourn may be
exercised by the members or member present.
(6) Each member of a Licensing Board appointed by the Governor-General shall,
subject to the terms of his or her appointment, hold office for one year.

### 7.

Annual Licensing Sessions and duration of certificates of fitness
(1) An annual Licensing Session for the hearing of applications for certificates under
this Act shall be held at the magistrate’s court in each district in the month of December
in each year on days which shall be fixed by the magistrate and published in the Gazette.
(2) All certificates granted at an annual Licensing Session shall be in force from the
first day of January after the granting thereof for one year thence next ensuing and no
longer.
(3) A Licensing Board acting at an annual Licensing Session shall continue such
session by adjournment to some specified day and place not less than fifteen nor more
than eighteen days after the date of such adjournment.

### 8.

Quarterly Licensing Sessions
(1) Where any person shall, at least seven days before the first day of the months of
March, June or September, give notice in writing to the magistrate of the district of his or
her intention to apply for a certificate in such month, a quarterly Licensing Session shall be
held in the month specified in such notice for the purpose of hearing the application.
(2) Such session shall be held at the place and on the day of the month appointed
under section 7.
(3) A certificate granted at a quarterly Licensing Session shall expire at the end of the
year in which such certificate was granted.

### 9.

Procedure where applicant is licensed in respect of premises named
When the application for a certificate is made by the licensed dealer who holds a
licence in force at the time of such application in respect of the premises sought to be
licensed, the following provisions shall have effect—

(a)

Notice by applicant.—The applicant shall, ten days at least before the day
appointed for the Licensing Session, make his or her application in writing to
the magistrate of the district in which the premises sought to be licensed are
situate; which application shall state the name, occupation and residence of
the applicant and the situation of the premises sought to be licensed;

(b)

Application in person not requisite.—The applicant need not appear at the
Licensing Session, unless he or she is required by the Licensing Board so to
attend;

(c)

Notice of intention to oppose grant of certificate must be served in writing by
opponent before Sessions.—The Licensing Board shall not entertain any
objection to granting such certificate or take any evidence with respect
thereto, unless written notices of an intention to oppose the grant and stating
in general terms the grounds of opposition shall have been served upon the
magistrate and upon the licensed dealer not less than seven days before the
day fixed for the holding of the Licensing Session or before the day fixed for
the hearing of the application:
Provided that the Licensing Board may, notwithstanding that no notice of
opposition or objection shall have been given, adjourn the granting of the
certificate to a future day, and require the attendance of the licensed dealer
on such day when the case will be heard and the objection considered, as if
the notice hereinbefore prescribed had been given;

(d)

Power to postpone applications.—When any applicant for a certificate has,
through inadvertence or misadventure, failed to comply with any of the
preliminary requirements of this Act, the Licensing Board may, if they shall
so think fit, and upon such terms as they think proper, postpone the
consideration of the application to an adjourned session, and if at such
adjourned session the Board shall be satisfied that such terms have been
complied with, they may proceed to grant or withhold such certificate as if
the preliminary requirements of this Act had been complied with.

### 10.

Procedure upon application for new licence in respect of unlicensed premises
When the application for a certificate is made by a person who does not at the time of
application hold a licence in respect of the premises sought to be licensed, the following
provisions shall have effect—
(a)

Notice.—The applicant shall, at least seven days before the first day of any
month in which a Licensing Session is appointed to be held under this Act,
make his or her application in writing to the magistrate of the district in
which the premises sought to be licensed are situate; which application shall
state the name, occupation and residence of the applicant and the situation of
the premises sought to be licensed;

(b)

Applicant to attend in person.—The applicant shall attend in person before
the Licensing Board when his or her application shall be heard.

### 11.

Holding of sessions to be advertised
(1) Notice of the day or days and place or places appointed for any Licensing Session
shall be given by the magistrate not less than fourteen days prior to the day or days so
appointed, by causing the same to be advertised in the Gazette, and in such other way as
the Minister may direct, and also in each parish of the district by affixing the notice

thereof on or in a conspicuous place near to the principal door of the court house and to
the principal door of every police station.
(2) Each notice as aforesaid shall have appended thereto the particulars of any
application for a new certificate, that is to say, any application made by a person who
does not at the time of application hold a licence in respect of the premises sought to be
licensed; and such notice shall be affixed to, and in a conspicuous place upon, each of the
premises named in the notice.

### 12.

Adjournments of sessions
A Licensing Board may cause any session, duly advertised as aforesaid, to be
adjourned by public oral notice given in court to any specified day or place; and all
persons bound to be present at the sessions so adjourned shall be equally bound to be
present at the time appointed by the notice.
Powers and Disabilities of a Licensing Board

### 13.

Grant of a certificate and its form
(1) Subject to the provisions of this Act, the granting of a certificate shall be at the
entire discretion of the Licensing Board and upon such conditions as the Board may
determine; and the certificate shall, subject to any special conditions imposed by the
Board in any particular case, be in the form prescribed by the Minister.
(2) Opposition to grant.—Any person shall be at liberty to oppose the grant of a
certificate, provided that in case of the applicant being a licensed dealer, notices of such
opposition shall have been given as required by section 9.
(3) Evidence.—A Licensing Board shall not receive any evidence with respect to an
application for a certificate which is not given on oath or affirmation.
(4) Appeals.—Any applicant, or any person objecting to the granting of a certificate,
who shall be dissatisfied with the decision of a Licensing Board, shall have a right of
appeal to a Judge of the High Court, and in such event, the provisions relating to appeals
from magistrates contained in the Magistrates Act, Chapter 177, shall apply to such
appeal.

### 14.

Conditions precedent to the granting of a certificate
The Licensing Board shall not grant a certificate unless the applicant therefor shall
satisfy the Board—
(a)

that the applicant is of good character;

(b)

that the applicant is not disqualified from holding a licence;

(c)

that from the general nature of the locality in which the premises sought to
be licensed are situated, it is not for any reason undesirable for a licence to
be granted in respect thereof; and

(d)

that the said premises are suitable for the purpose.

### 15.

Certificate for Sunday licence
Upon special application made by a licensed dealer who keeps a restaurant or eatinghouse, it shall be lawful for a Licensing Board, with the sanction of the Minister, to grant
a certificate authorising the Permanent Secretary to issue a special licence, to be called a

“Sunday Licence”, which licence shall entitle the licensee to open and keep open his or
her licensed premises on any Sunday, Christmas Day, Good Friday and any day appointed
as a Bank Holiday, during such hours as are specified in that behalf by this Act.
Licences, Fees and Trade Duty

### 16.

Issue of licence
(1) Subject to section 17 the Permanent Secretary or the officer in charge of a district
revenue office shall, upon the production of a certificate granted by a Licensing Board
and payment of the appropriate licence fee prescribed in the Schedule, issue a licence to
the person named in the certificate in respect of the premises therein specified.
(2) Notwithstanding subsection (1)—
(a)

the Permanent Secretary or district revenue officer may refuse to issue the
licence until the applicant has satisfied him or her that he or she does not
have in his or her possession any spirits upon which the trade duty, if any, is
unpaid or, if he or she is in possession of such spirits, until he or she pays the
amount certified by the Permanent Secretary to be payable by way of any
trade duty thereon under the provisions of this Act;

(b)

if an objection to the grant of the certificate has been raised but overruled
and the person objecting has given to the Permanent Secretary notice of an
appeal against that decision, the Permanent Secretary or district revenue
officer may refuse to issue the licence pending the result of the appeal.

### 17.

Power of Minister to remit or refund licence fee
The Minister, upon the application of a licence holder or an applicant for a licence,
may remit or refund the whole or any part of the licence fee payable or paid by the
licence holder or applicant if he or she is satisfied that it is just and equitable that he or
she should do so.
Hotel and Occasional Licences

### 18.

Registration of hotels, boarding houses and refreshment houses
(1) The Minister may, if he or she thinks fit, authorise the Permanent Secretary to
register as an hotel, boarding-house or refreshment house, as the case may be, any
premises which are used as such, and may likewise cause such registration to be cancelled
at any time thereafter.
(2) There shall be paid to the Permanent Secretary in respect of such registration the
fees set forth in the Schedule.
(3) On receipt of the prescribed fee as aforesaid the Permanent Secretary shall issue to
the person making the application a certificate of registration in respect of such hotel,
boarding house or refreshment house.
(4) Such certificate shall remain in force until the end of the year in which it is
granted, and may thereafter be renewed annually on payment of the prescribed fee, unless
the registration is cancelled under subsection (1).
(5) The owner of any hotel, boarding-house or refreshment house in respect of which
a certificate of registration is granted may, without taking out any licence therefor, sell,

for consumption on the premises, intoxicating liquor on any weekday or Sunday at the
following times and to the following classes—
(a)

in the case of hotels, to members of the public between the hours of 8 a.m.
and midnight, and to residents or persons taking meals, at any hour of the
day or night;

(b)

in the case of boarding houses, to boarders only at any hour of the day or
night; and

(c)

in the case of refreshment houses, to members of the public between the
hours of 8 a.m. and midnight.

(6) The owner of premises in respect of which a certificate of registration is granted
under this section shall pay such trade duty, if any, and comply with such provisions of
the Act relating to stock books and permits as apply to a licensed dealer, and shall in all
respects (subject to the provisions of this section) conduct and manage his or her business
as if he or she held a licence under this Act.

### 19.

Occasional licences granted to licensed dealers
A magistrate, whenever he or she considers it conducive to public convenience,
comfort and order, may on the application of a licensed dealer grant him or her an
occasional licence empowering him or her to sell intoxicating liquor at any specified
place other than his or her licensed premises during such time as the magistrate shall
approve, but not exceeding four consecutive days at any one time unless with the sanction
of the Minister. The cost of an occasional licence shall be as specified in the Schedule.
Miscellaneous Provisions with Respect to Licences

### 20.

Licences to specify premises licensed
A licence issued under this Act shall only authorise the holder of the licence to sell
intoxicating liquor in the premises specified in the licence, and shall be in the form
prescribed by the Minister.

### 21.

Duplicate of lost licence
Upon proof being given to the satisfaction of the Permanent Secretary that a licence
has been lost or destroyed, he or she shall grant a duplicate of it, which duplicate shall
operate in all respects as the original licence.

### 22.

Duration of licences
(1) Licences under this Act shall, unless a contrary intention is expressed, be yearly
licences or half-yearly licences.
(2) Every yearly licence shall expire on the thirty-first day of December in the year in
which it is issued and every half-yearly licence shall expire on the thirtieth day of June or
the thirty-first day of December in the year in which it is issued:
Provided that—
(a)

in the case of any yearly licence issued after the thirty-first day of March in
any year the Permanent Secretary shall accept seventy-five per cent only of
the fee payable for such licence prescribed in the Schedule hereto;

(b)

in the case of a half-yearly licence issued after the thirty-first day of March
or the thirtieth day of September in any year the Permanent Secretary shall
accept fifty per cent only of the fee payable for such licence prescribed in the
Schedule.

### 23.

Name of licensee, etc., to be displayed on licensed premises
(1) Every person holding a licence issued under this Act shall, before selling or
bartering any intoxicating liquor under the same, set up and affix a sign board over the
door or on some other conspicuous part of the front of the premises in respect of which
the licence is granted, such board to have the name of the licensed dealer and also the
words “Licensed to Sell Intoxicating Liquor” painted thereon in large white letters, not
less than two inches in length on a black ground, or in black letters on a light ground.
(2) Every licensed dealer who shall fail to comply with any requirement of this
section and every person who, not being a licensed dealer, shall put or have on his or her
premises any sign board or inscription importing that such person is a licensed dealer
shall be guilty of an offence.

### 24.

Removal to proposed new premises; how effected
If the holder of a licence desires to remove from the premises in respect of which his
or her licence was granted, and to use such licence in any other proposed premises he or
she shall, before doing so, give notice in writing, at least fourteen days previous to the
holding of the next Licensing Session for the district in which the premises, to which he
or she proposes to remove, are situate; such notice shall be addressed to the magistrate of
such district and shall contain, for the purposes of advertisement by the magistrate, such
particulars with respect to the proposed premises as are hereinbefore required to be given
and advertised upon application for a certificate; and the Licensing Board shall at the next
Licensing Session decide whether the removal shall be permitted and if the removal be
permitted shall give to the applicant a certificate to that effect. The Permanent Secretary, on
the production of such certificate, shall indorse upon the applicant’s licence the particulars
relating to such change of premises, and upon such indorsation such applicant shall be at
liberty to trade with his or her licence at the premises named in such indorsation.

### 25.

Transfer of licence to another name
Any person holding a licence under this Act may dispose of such licence, by sale or
otherwise, and the purchaser or proposed transferee of such licence will be entitled to
have the licence transferred to his or her name by the Permanent Secretary:
Provided that such purchaser or transferee shall obtain at the next Licensing Session of
the district in which the licensed premises are situate, a certificate from the Licensing
Board of such district stating that he or she is of good character and not disqualified from
holding a licence.

### 26.

Permanent Secretary’s powers as to transfers of licences and removals, in
urgent cases
Where a licensed dealer, by reason of any urgent and unforeseen necessity, shall at
any time find it requisite either to transfer his or her licence to another person or to
remove from his or her licensed premises to proposed new premises in the same licensing
district before the holding of the next Licensing Session, it shall be lawful for the
Permanent Secretary if satisfied by such licensed dealer as to such urgency and necessity
and as to the character of the proposed transferee or new premises and all other

circumstances of the case, to issue his or her authority enabling the business of such
licensed dealer to be temporarily carried on, as the case may be, either by such transferee
or at such new premises as proposed by the licensed dealer; and, upon the Permanent
Secretary indorsing the particulars of such authority upon the licence of such licensed
dealer, the business may be carried on in the manner authorised by such indorsation until
the holding of the next Licensing Session.

### 27.

Powers of personal representative of deceased licensee
(1) Where a licensed dealer shall die before the expiration of his or her licence, the
personal representative of such dealer may sell or expose for sale any intoxicating liquor
upon the premises specified in such licence until the holding of the Licensing Session then
next ensuing in the district or (if such Licensing Session be holden within fourteen days
next after the death of the said dealer) until the holding of the Licensing Session holden in
the district next after such Licensing Session as last aforesaid:
Provided that, in the meantime and as soon as practicable after the death of the said
dealer, the personal representative, dealing under such licence as aforesaid, shall notify
the fact to the Permanent Secretary and to the magistrate of the district and shall state
whether it is his or her intention to apply, at the session to be next holden as aforesaid, for
a certificate that the licence be transferred to his or her name or to the name of any other
person.
(2) If any such certificate shall be applied for and granted, the Permanent Secretary
shall upon production thereof transfer the licence, which had been issued to the deceased
licensed dealer, to the person named in the certificate, and indorse the licence
accordingly; and the licence so indorsed shall be taken, for all purposes under this Act, to
be the licence of the person named in such indorsation.
Hours of Closing

### 28.

Hours of closing licensed premises
(1) Except as hereinafter provided, no licensed dealer shall sell or expose for sale any
intoxicating liquor, or open or keep open his or her licensed premises, on any night after 9
p.m., or on any morning before 6 a.m.
(2) All licensed premises shall be closed throughout any Sunday, Christmas Day,
Good Friday and any day appointed as a bank holiday, excepting that the licensed
premises of a dealer holding a Sunday licence may be opened for the sale of intoxicating
liquor on any of the days last hereinbefore mentioned between the following hours, that is
to say, from 9 a.m. until 11 a.m., and from 1.30 p.m. until 4.30 p.m., or during such other
hours as the Minister may hereafter fix by rules.
(3) A person failing to comply with any requirement of this section shall be guilty of
an offence.

### 29.

Extension of business hours
The Minister, upon such evidence as he or she may deem sufficient to show that it is
necessary or desirable to do so for the accommodation of any considerable number of
persons, may grant to any licensed dealer an Order exempting him or her from the
provisions of section 28 during such time as may be specified in the Order. Every such
Order shall be published in the Gazette.

### 30.

Minister may order temporary closing of licensed premises
Where any riot or tumult happens or is expected to happen, the Minister may direct
every licensed dealer on or near the place where the riot or tumult happens or is expected
to happen to close his or her licensed premises during any time which the Minister may
direct; and any licensed dealer who keeps open his or her licensed premises for the sale of
intoxicating liquor during any time at which the Minister has directed them to be closed
shall be guilty of an offence, and any person acting in obedience to the direction of the
Minister may use such force as may be necessary for the purpose of closing the premises.
Stock Book, Permits and Supervision of Licensed Premises

### 31.

Stock book to be kept by each licensee
Every licensed dealer shall provide himself or herself with and keep a stock book
according to a pattern to be obtained on application at the Treasury or district revenue
office and shall, on receiving or sending out any spirits required to be accompanied by a
permit under the provisions of this Act or of the Excise Tax Act, Chapter 94, enter
immediately into his or her stock book the following particulars—
[Cap. 94.]

On Receipt—
Quantity, description and strength of spirits received:
Date of receipt:
Name and address of person or persons from whom received:
On Sending Out—
Quantity, description and strength of spirits sent out:
Date of sending out:
Name and address of person or persons to whom sent:

### 32.

Spirits of whatever quantity supplied by one licensee to another must be
accompanied by permit
A licensed dealer shall not supply another licensed dealer with spirits, in any quantity,
unless accompanied by a permit in the form prescribed under the provisions of the Excise
Tax Act, Chapter 94, for removal of spirits exceeding in quantity one gallon; and any
licensed dealer supplying spirits to or receiving spirits from any other licensed dealer in
any quantity, unless accompanied by such a permit, shall be guilty of an offence.

### 33.

Stock book to be kept open for inspection for one year
Every licensed dealer shall—
(a)

keep the stock book, on his or her premises, open for the purpose of
inspection by any revenue officer; and

(b)

keep open his or her stock book for the purposes of such inspection for not
less than twelve months after it is filled up.

### 34.

Penalty for not keeping stock book properly, etc.
If any licensed dealer—

(a)

fails to obtain, provide, keep or produce any stock book as by this Act
required, or to make therein the entries by this Act required;

(b)

hinders or obstructs any officer in examining such stock book or making any
entry therein or extracts therefrom;

(c)

cancels, alters, obliterates or destroys any part of the stock book or any entry
therein, in contravention of the provisions of this Act; or

(d)

makes a false entry in a stock book,

he or she shall be guilty of an offence.

### 35.

Powers of revenue officers to search licensed premises
(1) It shall be lawful for a revenue officer, when and so often as he or she thinks
proper, to enter into any licensed premises and into and upon the premises belonging
thereto and to remain there for such time as may be necessary, to overhaul and to examine
the premises and to take an account of the quantity of intoxicating liquor in the stock
book or in the possession of the licensed dealer and gauge and test the liquors and take
samples thereof, paying for such samples the usual price.
(2) If any licensed dealer, or any employee or other person in his or her employ or by
his or her direction, refuses to admit or does not admit any revenue officer demanding
admittance, the licensed dealer shall be guilty of an offence.
(3) If the quantity found on taking the account exceeds the quantity which ought
according to the stock book to be in his or her possession, the excess shall be forfeited
and the licensed dealer shall incur a penalty of fifty dollars for every gallon of the excess.
Offences by Liquor Dealers

### 36.

Selling liquor on unlicensed premises
(1) No licensed dealer shall sell or barter intoxicating liquor in any premises other
than those in respect of which his or her licence is granted, except as provided in this Act.
(2) Any licensed dealer acting in contravention of this section shall be guilty of an
offence.

### 37.

Prohibition of sale of spirits below certain strength
(1) No licensed dealer shall sell or have on his or her premises any rum of less
strength than twenty-five degrees under proof and all rum of less strength than twentyfive degrees under proof found on the premises of a licensed dealer shall be forfeited.
(2) No licensed dealer shall sell or have on his or her premises any spirits other than
rum of less strength than thirty degrees under proof, and any such spirits of less strength
than thirty degrees under proof found on the premises of a licensed dealer shall be forfeited:
Provided that this subsection shall not apply to bitters, liqueurs or cordials.
(3) Any licensed dealer acting in contravention of this section shall be guilty of an
offence.

### 38.

Permitting drunkenness
If a licensed dealer—

(a)

permits drunkenness or violent, quarrelsome, disorderly or riotous conduct to
take place on his or her premises, or sells any intoxicating liquor to any
drunken person;

(b)

Allowing premises to be the resort of prostitutes.—Knowingly permits his or
her premises to be the habitual resort, or place of meeting, of reputed
prostitutes, whether the object of their so resorting or meeting is or is not
prostitution, if he or she allows them to remain thereon longer than is
necessary for the purpose of obtaining reasonable refreshment;

(c)

Harbouring constable on duty, or supplying him or her with drink, or
bribing constable.—Knowingly harbours or knowingly suffers to remain on
his or her premises any police officer during any part of the time appointed
for such police officer being on duty, unless for the purpose of keeping or
restoring order or in execution of his or her duty, or supplies any liquor or
refreshment, whether by way of gift or sale, to any police officer on duty,
unless by authority of some superior officer of such police officer, or bribes
or attempts to bribe any police officer; or

(d)

Permitting gambling.—Suffers any gaming or any unlawful games to be
carried on in his or her premises,

he or she shall be guilty of an offence.

### 39.

Selling liquor to juveniles
A licensed dealer who shall—
(a)

sell or deliver or supply or knowingly permit to be sold or delivered or
supplied to any person under the age of sixteen years any intoxicating liquor
(excepting such liquors as are sold or delivered or supplied in corked or sealed
vessels for consumption off the premises only); or

(b)

permit any such person to drink any intoxicating liquor on his or her premises,

shall be guilty of an offence.

### 40.

Removal of disorderly person
(1) A licensed dealer, or his or her agent or his or her employee, may refuse to admit
to and may turn out of the premises in respect of which his or her licence is granted, any
person who is drunk, violent, quarrelsome or disorderly and any person whose presence
on his or her premises would subject him or her to a fine under this Act.
(2) Refusal to quit.—Any such person who, upon being requested in pursuance of this
section by such licensed dealer or his or her agent or employee or by any police officer to
quit such premises, refuses or fails so to do shall be guilty of an offence and liable to a
fine of one hundred dollars.

### 41.

Label showing quantity of liquor sold to be affixed to container in which liquor
is sold
(1) No licensed dealer shall sell or barter on his or her premises any intoxicating
liquor unless there is affixed to the container in which the liquor is sold, a label showing
in gallons or multiples or parts of a gallon the quantity of intoxicating liquor being sold or
bartered.
(2) Any licensed dealer acting in contravention of this section shall be guilty of an
offence.

General Offences and Penalties

### 42.

Bartering, etc., liquor without a licence, to be prohibited
If any person shall without a licence either barter, truck or exchange, or shall, in any
manner, give, lend or dispose of, intoxicating liquor either for provisions or for goods or
labour or as the pay, wages, reward or other remuneration or part remuneration for labour,
he or she shall be taken to have sold or disposed of intoxicating liquor without a licence
and contrary to the provisions of this Act and shall be guilty of an offence.

### 43.

Penalty for being in possession of liquor for unlawful purposes, or of
adulterated rum
Every person who—
(a)

has in possession any intoxicating liquor for the purpose of selling, bartering,
exposing for sale or disposing of the same contrary to the provisions of this
Act; or

(b)

has in possession for the purpose of sale or barter any rum adulterated with
any drug or any pernicious ingredient,

shall be guilty of an offence and the liquor and rum shall be liable to forfeiture.

### 44.

Obstructing persons acting under legal authority
Whoever obstructs or otherwise interferes with any public officer or any of his or her
employees in the discharge of an authority conferred or duty imposed under this Act shall
be guilty of an offence.

### 45.

General penalty
A person found guilty of an offence against this Act shall, if no other penalty is
provided, be liable for the first offence to a fine of three thousand dollars and to
imprisonment for three months and, for the second or any subsequent offence, to a fine of
five thousand dollars and to imprisonment for six months.

### 46.

After second or subsequent conviction, licence may be forfeited
If any licensed dealer is convicted of a second or subsequent offence against this Act
he or she may, by order of the court by which he or she is tried, forfeit his or her licence
and be disqualified for any term of years, or for ever, from holding any licence under this
Act.
Powers of Entry and Search

### 47.

Search warrant
(1) If, upon information given on oath, it appears to a magistrate or Justice of the
Peace that there is reasonable cause for suspecting that any intoxicating liquor is kept for
the purpose of sale by any person not duly licensed under this Act, or by any licensed
dealer in any premises not specified in his or her licence, the magistrate or Justice may by
warrant under his or her hand directed to a police officer authorise the police officer to
enter and search such premises, and by the said warrant may, if he or she thinks fit,
specially authorise the police officer to enter and search such premises at any time of the

day or night and moreover, if he or she thinks fit, may specially authorise the police
officer with or without assistance, after having made known their authority, to break open
or otherwise use force in order to effect an entry.
(2) If upon search under this section any intoxicating liquor or vessel used for the
holding or measuring the same is found, the police officer executing the warrant shall
take possession of and secure such intoxicating liquor or vessel and apprehend and bring
before a magistrate not only the person in whose premises the same are found but also
every other person found in such premises who appears to have been employed or
assisting in the sale of intoxicating liquor; and, unless it is made to appear to such
magistrate that such intoxicating liquor was not deposited for the purpose of being sold in
such premises, the person in whose premises such intoxicating liquor is found and every
person so appearing to have been employed or assisting in the sale thereof in such
premises shall be guilty of an offence.
(3) All intoxicating liquor found on any search under this section, together with all
vessels used for holding or measuring the same or in any way in and about the retailing
thereof, shall upon any conviction in respect thereof be forfeited.

### 48.

Search under writ of assistance
A revenue officer armed with a writ of assistance may enter, if necessary by force, and
search any premises or vessel where there is probable cause to believe that intoxicating
liquor liable to forfeiture is kept or concealed, and may seize the liquor; and any person who
delays or refuses to admit the officer after he or she has declared his or her name and
business and produced the writ (if so requested) shall be guilty of an offence:
Provided that any damage caused by forcible entry shall, if no liquor liable to
forfeiture is found on the premises or vessel, be paid by the Treasury to the owner of the
premises or vessel.

### 49.

Power of police to enter licensed premises
It shall be lawful for a police officer to enter any licensed premises at any time for the
purpose of preventing or detecting the violation of any of the provisions of this Act which
it is his or her duty to enforce.
Procedural and Evidential Provisions

### 50.

Proceedings to be summary
(1) All proceedings for offences against this Act, or for the recovery of any penalty,
forfeiture or money under this Act shall be before a magistrate, who may dispose thereof
summarily.
(2) All such proceedings may be prosecuted in the name or at the instance of the
Permanent Secretary or the Chief of Police.

### 51.

Onus of proof generally
In all proceedings taken against any person for selling intoxicating liquor contrary to
this Act the onus of proving that he or she is licensed to sell intoxicating liquor or that on
other grounds he or she is not liable under this Act shall lie on the defendant.

### 52.

Evidence of sale or consumption

In proving the sale or consumption of intoxicating liquor for the purpose of any
proceeding relative to any offence under this Act it shall not be necessary to show that
any money actually passed or that any liquor was actually consumed, if the magistrate
hearing the case is satisfied that a transaction in the nature of a sale actually took place;
and proof of consumption or intended consumption of intoxicating liquor, on licensed
premises, by some person other than the occupier of or an employee in such premises,
shall be evidence that such liquor was sold to the person consuming, or being about to
consume, or carrying away the same by or on behalf of the licensed dealer.

### 53.

Presumption of guilt
In order to warrant the conviction of any person for bartering, selling, trading in,
exposing or offering for sale, any intoxicating liquor in any place or premises without a
licence, it shall be sufficient in absence of contrary evidence to prove that some person,
other than the occupier of or employee in such place or premises, shall, at the time
charged, have been found in such place or premises drinking or having had intoxicating
liquor supplied to him or her therein, and that such place or premises is or are by repute
kept for the purpose of selling intoxicating liquor contrary to the provisions of this Act, or
that such place or premises at the time charged contained drinking utensils and fittings
usually found in licensed premises.

### 54.

Rewards to persons giving evidence
If any person shall, on any information, give evidence whereby any other person is
convicted of an offence or ordered to pay any forfeiture or other sum of money under this
Act, the Minister may, if he or she sees fit, order payment to the person giving the
evidence of any sum not exceeding one-half of the fine or other money recovered on
conviction of the defendant on the information.
Miscellaneous

### 55.

Appropriation of penalties and other money
All penalties, forfeitures, and fines, imposed under this Act shall be due to the
Government, and the same and all other sums of money ordered to be paid and received
under this Act, and the net proceeds of all sales of liquors and packages sold by virtue of
this Act, shall (except when otherwise directed by this Act) be paid to the Permanent
Secretary and credited to the Consolidated Fund.

### 56.

Power to alter times specified in this Act
The Minister may, in any special case and on the application of the person principally
concerned, extend or alter the time fixed for the performance of any duty under this Act.

### 57.

Power of Minister to make rules
(1) The Minister shall, from time to time as occasion may require and as it may be
deemed expedient, make rules for carrying into effect the provisions of this Act, for
prescribing the several forms to be used, for settling the scale of fees to be paid by
persons applying for certificates at Licensing Sessions, and generally respecting all
matters incidental to the working of this Act.
(2) By such rules there may be imposed, in respect of the breach of any of them, a
fine of five hundred dollars.
