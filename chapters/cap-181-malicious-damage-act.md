## CHAPTER 181

MALICIOUS DAMAGE ACT
An Act to make provision for the punishment of certain offences of malicious
damage to public and private property, and for connected matters.
[Act No. 39 of 1973 amended by Act No. 52 of 1991.]

[31st May, 1974.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Malicious Damage Act.
2\. Interpretation
In this Act—
“pole” includes equipment used or intended for use to carry any telephone or
electric wires or any telegraph or television apparatus;
“road” has the same meaning as in the Roads Act, Chapter 290, and includes a
street;

“tree” includes any kind of flowering, decorative or ornamental plant, fern, creeper
or liana;
“vehicle” has the same meaning as in the Motor Vehicles and Road Traffic Act,

## Chapter 194, and includes any aircraft, motor boat, yacht or ship and any vehicular

stone crusher, roller, tractor, excavator, crane or fork-lift truck, and any wheelchair or
perambulator.
3\. Damage to trees, etc., growing in specific places
Whosoever shall unlawfully and maliciously cut, break, bark, root-up, burn by fire or
by any other means or otherwise destroy or damage the whole or any part of any tree,
sapling, shrub or underwood growing in any park, pleasure ground, field, garden,
conservatory, orchard, nursery ground, hothouse, greenhouse, or alongside any road, or in
any place adjoining or belonging to any dwelling or other building, shall be guilty of an
offence and liable, on summary conviction, to imprisonment for not less than three
months and not more than twelve months.
4\. Damage to trees, etc., growing anywhere
Whosoever shall unlawfully and maliciously cut, break, bark, root-up, or otherwise
destroy or damage the whole or any part of any tree, sapling or shrub growing anywhere
on public or private property shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary
conviction, to imprisonment for not less than three months and not more than twelve
months.
5\. Damage to and destruction of specific things
Whosoever shall unlawfully and maliciously cut, break, root-up, throw down, set fire
to, burn or otherwise damage or destroy any fence of any description whatsoever, or any
pole, stand, wall, stile, gate, picket, stake, road or street sign, bunting, flag, or any part
thereof, shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to imprisonment
for not less than three months and not more than twelve months.
6\. Attempt at or actual destruction, etc., of specific things
Whosoever shall unlawfully and maliciously attempt to, or actually does, break down
or burn, any building or letter-box, or any telephone booth, kiosk or cabinet or installation
or thing connected therewith, or place in or against any of the same any match, flame or
dangerous or explosive substance, or filth, or in any other manner or by any other means
whatsoever damage or destroy the same, shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on
summary conviction, to a fine of fifteen thousand dollars and to imprisonment for three
years.
7\. Increased penalty in certain circumstances
If it be proved at the trial for any offence committed under this Act that—
(a)

the offence was committed between 8 p.m. and 5 a.m. the following day;

(b)

the tree, shrub or sapling with respect to which the offence was committed
was planted by the Ministry of Agriculture or any group, association or body
of persons for public or private purposes of decoration, adornment or
beautification; or

(c)

a dwelling or other building with respect to which the offence was
committed is a place of worship, school, place of entertainment, theatre,
court house or other place where the public occasionally or regularly gather
together,

the penalty for that offence shall be imprisonment for not less than six months and not
more than two years.
8\. Damage to vehicles
Whosoever shall unlawfully and maliciously cause any damage whatsoever to any
vehicle shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one
thousand five hundred dollars.
9\. Persons concealing commission of offence
If it be proven at any time within six months after the commission of an offence under
this Act that any person having knowledge of and information regarding its commission
has failed to bring to the attention of the police such knowledge and other information as
he or she has, he or she shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction,
to be punished in like manner as if he or she, himself or herself, had committed the
offence:
Provided that the police shall not be bound to disclose the name of, or to call as a
witness, any informant in connection with an offence under this section.
10\. Informant may be rewarded
A person giving to the police information leading to the conviction of a person for an
offence against this Act may be considered for a reward in money or monies worth from
public funds or property either for himself or herself or his or her immediate family, as
the Governor-General sees fit.
11\. Previous conviction incurs police supervision
(1) When any person is convicted of an offence under this Act and a previous such
offence or similar offence is proven against him or her, the court before which he or she
has been convicted shall, in addition to a sentence of imprisonment imposed under the
section creating the offence, direct that he or she be subject to the supervision of the Chief
of Police for a period of at least six months but not more than three years commencing
immediately after the expiration of the sentence.
(2) A previous conviction may be proved for the purposes of this section—
(a)

by producing a record of such conviction signed by the magistrate or the
clerk of the court in whose presence the offender was convicted and by a
police or prison officer identifying him or her; or

(b)

by a prison officer identifying the offender as having been admitted to
prison, served his or her sentence and been then discharged in accordance
with a warrant to that effect which must be produced.

12\. Punishment for neglecting duties under supervision
Every offender subject to police supervision under this Act shall notify the police of
any change of his or her place of residence within forty-eight hours of the change and
shall report to the Chief of Police at least once in every month or render himself or herself
liable to imprisonment not exceeding one month, at the expiration of which supervision
shall continue. The Chief of Police may delegate supervision to any police officer of or
above the rank of sergeant and, in the case of a female offender, to a woman police
officer of whatever rank.
13\. Duty of individuals to apprehend and assist in apprehension of offenders;
rewards

(1) It is the duty of an individual to apprehend an offender found committing an
offence under this Act between 8 p.m. and 5 a.m. the following day and to deliver him or
her to a police officer or convey him or her to the nearest police station.
(2) Every individual in the vicinity when an offender is being apprehended for having
committed an offence under this Act has a duty to assist the person apprehending and, if
necessary, restrain the offender. Any individual who fails, without reasonable excuse, to
perform that duty shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a
fine of three hundred dollars.
(3) Individuals who duly perform the duty under this section may be rewarded in the
same manner as provided in section 10.
14\. Duty of police to charge offences under this Act
(1) Where under any other written law there is provided an offence which is the same
as or so similar to an offence under this Act as to enable the bringing of a charge for that
offence properly under this Act, the charge shall be brought under this Act unless the
punishment for that offence under the other written law is greater than the punishment
provided in respect of that offence under this Act.
(2) It is the duty of a police officer to observe the provisions of subsection (1).
