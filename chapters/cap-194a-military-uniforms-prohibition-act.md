## CHAPTER 194A

Military Uniforms (Prohibition) Act
An Act to prohibit the wearing, importation and sale of military uniforms.
[Act No. 9 of 2000.]

[8th March, 2000.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Military Uniforms (Prohibition) Act.
2\. Definitions
In this Act—
“authorised persons” means a member of the Royal Grenada Police Force or a
member of a bona fide foreign military force or police force on a visit to Grenada;
“military” includes police;
“military uniforms” means combat camouflage or other distinctive dress generally
worn by members of the Royal Grenada Police Force or any other police or military
force and includes any wearing apparel designed to resemble such dress or any part
thereof but does not include footwear;
“minister” means the Minister responsible for national security;
“Royal Grenada Police Force” means the Royal Grenada Police Force established
by section 3 of the Police Act, Chapter 244.
3\. Prohibition against wearing, sale, etc., of military uniforms

(1) Subject to this section, no person, other than an authorised person, shall wear any
military uniform or any clothing having the appearance of or bearing any of the
regimental or other distinctive marks of any such uniforms.
(2) No person shall import, trade, sell or deal in military uniforms or decorations
except with the approval of the Minister.
The Minister may, upon written application made to him or her for permission to wear
military uniform for a cultural event, permit such wearing for a specific period.
4\. Unlawful purchase of military stores
(1) Any person who acquires any military stores or solicits or procures any person to
dispose of any military stores or acts for any person in disposing of military stores
commits an offence, unless he or she proves either—
(a)

that he or she did not know, and could not reasonably be expected to know
that the chattels in question were military stores; or

(b)

that the chattels had been disposed of by order or with consent of a person or
authority whom he or she had reason or cause to believe had the power to
order or consent to the disposal.

(2) For the purpose of this section—
“acquire” means to buy, take in exchange, take in, pawn or otherwise receive,
whether apart from this section, the receiving is lawful or not;
“dispose” means to sell, give in exchange, pledge or otherwise hand over, whether
apart from this section, the handing over is lawful or not;
“military stores” means chattel of any description belonging to the Government,
which has been issued for use for military purposes or is held in store for the purpose
of being so issued when required, and includes any chattel which had belonged or had
been issued or held at some time in the past by the Government.
(3) Property shall be deemed to be in the possession of a person if he or she has it
under his or her control, whether he or she has it for his or her own use or benefit or for
the use or benefit of another person.
5\. Power of seizure
A police officer may arrest without warrant any person whom he or she has reasonable
grounds to suspect has committed an offence under this Act and may seize any goods in
his or her possession which is or resembles military uniforms or accessories.
6\. Offences
(1) A person commits an offence if he or she —
(a)

wears in a public place, without the written permission of the Minister, any
military uniform;

(b)

imports, sells or trades in military uniforms;

(c)

has in his or her possession or under his or her control, without the written
permission of the Minister, articles or clothing material or decorations
resembling those used by or authorised by or under the Police Act,

## Chapter 244;

(d)

enters Grenada dressed in military uniform without lawful authority;

(e)

uses, wears, without lawful authority any naval, military or air force
decorations, badge, wound stripe or emblem;

(f)

brings into Grenada any military uniform or any of the patterned materials
commonly used for making military uniforms;

(g)

falsely represents himself or herself to be a person who is entitled to wear,
use or sell any decoration, military uniform, badge, stripe or emblem used by
the Royal Grenada Police Force or any other police or military force.

7\. Penalties
A person who is guilty of an offence under this Act shall be liable, on summary
conviction, to a fine of ten thousand dollars or to imprisonment for one year, or to both
such fine and imprisonment.
8\. Regulations
The Minister may make regulations generally to give effect to the provisions of this
Act.
9\. Repeal
The Uniforms Act, Chapter 304 (Revised Laws of Grenada, 1958) is hereby repealed.
10\. Commencement
This Act comes into force on the 8th day of March, 2000.
