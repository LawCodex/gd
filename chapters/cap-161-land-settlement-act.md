## CHAPTER 161

LAND SETTLEMENT ACT
An Act to provide for the establishment and location of small holdings and for other
purposes.
[Amended by Act No. 15 of 1966, Act No. 25 of 1969.]

[30th December, 1933.]

## PART I

Preliminary

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Land Settlement Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act—
“Board” means the Land Settlement Development Board established by section 3;
“peasant proprietor” means any person who is the owner or occupier of not more
than ten acres of land, and who is an agriculturist, labourer, domestic or menial
servant, artificer, handicraftsman, or who is otherwise engaged in manual labour;
“small holding” means an agricultural holding which does not exceed five acres
situate within the boundaries of any land declared to be a land settlement area under
this Act.

### 3.

Land Settlement Development Board
(1) A body corporate is hereby established to be known as the Land Settlement
Development Board with perpetual succession and a Common Seal, with power in that
name to contract, to sue and be sued, to acquire by purchase, lease, exchange, gift or
otherwise and to hold and dispose of all kinds of property whether movable or
immovable, and to draw, accept, make, endorse and negotiate bills of exchange,
promissory notes, bills of lading and other negotiable instruments.
(2) The Land Settlement Development Board shall be governed by the provisions of
the First Schedule.

### 4.

Power to acquire land for small holdings
(1) The Minister or, with his or her approval, the Board may, for the purpose of
providing small holdings, purchase, take or lease land by agreement with the owner, and
the Minister may by Order declare such land to be a land settlement area for the purposes
of this Act.
(2) If the Minister or the Board is unable to acquire by agreement, and on reasonable
terms, suitable land for the purpose of providing small holdings, the Governor-General
may for that purpose acquire land compulsorily in accordance with the provisions of the
Land Acquisition Act, Chapter 159, or any other Act which may hereafter be passed
dealing with the compulsory acquisition of land by the Government and, for that purpose,
the establishment and location of small holdings shall be deemed to be a public purpose.

### 5.

Power to declare Crown Land as land settlement area
The Governor-General may, by Order, declare any area of Crown Land in Grenada to
be a land settlement area.

### 6.

Appropriation of land for establishment of small holdings
(1) Land purchased, taken, leased or acquired for land settlement purposes under this
Act, and land declared to be a land settlement area under section 5, shall be deemed to be
appropriated to the uses of establishing and locating small holdings thereon under such a
system of administration and disposal thereof as the Minister may by rules made under
this Act from time to time prescribe.

(2) The Minister or, with his or her approval, the Board may sell, exchange, let or
otherwise dispose of any such land as may not be required for such uses.
(3) In cases of any such sale, lease or other disposal of such land all monies derived
therefrom after payment of all necessary charges and expenses shall be paid into the
Treasury to the credit of a fund to be known as the Land Settlement Development Fund
or, with the approval of the Minister, into the Consolidated Fund.
(4) In case of any exchange the land taken in exchange shall be subject to the
provisions of this Act and the Accountant-General may pay out of the Land Settlement
Development Fund all expenses attendant on such exchange.

## PART II

Restriction on Alienation of Lands Allotted and Sold for Small Holdings

### 7.

Restriction on alienation of small holdings
(1) No small holding shall be let or encumbered for any period exceeding three years,
or sold before the expiry of fifteen years, from the date of allocation without the consent
in writing of the Minister where the owner of a holding is a peasant proprietor; and every
attempted transfer or letting in contravention of this provision shall be void, and on any
such contravention the holding shall, at the option of the Minister, revest in the Minister.
(2) Notwithstanding anything in section 23 of the Civil Procedure Act, Chapter 55, a
decree for the payment of money shall not bind any small holding and the same shall not
be deemed to be attached by virtue of any such decree.
(3) The provisions of sections 49 and 50 of the Civil Procedure Act, Chapter 55
(which relate to the sale of judgement debtor’s land) shall not apply to any small holding.
(4) The provisions of sections 112 to 119 of the Civil Procedure Act, Chapter 55
(which relate to the execution against land of judgements of a Magistrate’s Court) shall
not apply to any small holding.

### 8.

Application of section 7
The provisions of section 7 shall apply to all small holdings whether made before or
after the 12th March, 1915.

## PART III

Miscellaneous

### 9.

Power of Minister to make regulations
(1) The Minister may make regulations for all or any of the following purposes—
(a)

regulating the system of establishing and locating small holders on land
settlement areas;

(b)

the administration and disposal of small holdings and all matters connected
therewith or incidental thereto;

(c)

providing for the clearance and maintenance of paths and drains on land
settlement areas;

(d)

controlling and prohibiting the cultivation of certain kinds of crops on small
holdings, and prescribing the conditions under which such holdings may be
cultivated, including the cultivation thereon of certain specified crops;

(e)

prescribing the terms and conditions on or subject to which small holdings
are to be sold or allotted;

(f)

prescribing the forms to be used under this Act;

(g)

the further and better carrying into effect the purposes of this Act.

(2) Any such regulations may provide that the Governor-General may order forfeiture
of all rights under the system thereby regulated without any appeal from such order to any
court whatsoever in case of breach of any of the conditions of tenure under such system:
Provided that on the forfeiture of such rights there shall be repaid to the person whose
rights are forfeited such monies as he or she may have paid towards the purchase of his or
her holding under such system subject to a deduction therefrom of such an amount for
each year or part of a year during which such person shall have been occupying such
holding as the Governor-General may in each case deem just, and, in the event of damage
being caused to the land, such additional deduction as in the opinion of the GovernorGeneral will fairly represent the value of such damage.

### 10.

Extending provisions of Small Tenements (Recovery of Possession) Act to this
Act
An occupier of any small holding forfeited under this Act or under regulations made
under the last preceding section who shall refuse to quit and deliver up possession thereof,
or any other person who shall unlawfully enter upon, take or hold possession of any such
holding or any part thereof, shall be deemed to be a tenant at will and the Permanent
Secretary (Finance) or the secretary of the Board, as is appropriate, or such other person
as the Minister may appoint may recover possession according to the mode prescribed by
the Small Tenements (Recovery of Possession) Act, Chapter 307, in such and in the same
manner as if the Permanent Secretary (Finance), the secretary, or the person appointed, as
the case may be, were a landlord and as if such over-holding occupier or other person
were a tenant at will neglecting or refusing to quit and deliver up possession within the
meaning of the last mentioned Act.

### 11.

Land settlement areas
(1) The lands mentioned and described in the Second Schedule shall be deemed to be
land settlement areas established for small holdings within the meaning of this Act.
(2) The Governor-General may, by Order, amend the Second Schedule.

### 12.

Application of other regulations to this Act
All regulations made under the Land Acquisition Act, Chapter 159, and the Crown
Lands Act, Chapter 73, relating to land settlement shall be deemed to have been made
under the provisions of this Act and shall continue in force until other provisions shall be
made by virtue of this Act.

First Schedule
LAND SETTLEMENT ACT
Composition and Functions of Land Settlement Development Board
[Section 3.]

1\. Interpretation
In this Schedule—

“chairperson” means the chairperson of the Board;
“secretary” means the secretary of the Board.
2\. Authentication of common seal, etc.
(1) The common seal of the Board shall be authenticated by the signature of the
chairperson or one of the members thereof authorised by the Board to act in that behalf;
and such seal shall be officially and judicially noticed.
(2) All documents (other than those required by law to be under seal) made by the
Board and all decisions of the Board may be signified under the hand of the chairperson
or of any member thereof authorised by the Board to act in that behalf, or of the secretary.
3\. Membership of the Board
(1) The Board shall consist of a chairperson and not more than six other members all
of whom shall be appointed by the Minister.
(2) Subject to the provisions of subparagraph (3), the chairperson and every other
member of the Board shall, unless his or her appointment be sooner determined by the
Minister or he or she sooner resigns by giving notice in writing to the Minister or he or
she sooner dies, hold office for a period of two years from the date of his or her
appointment but shall be eligible for re-appointment as occasion arises.
(3) A member of the Board shall, ipso facto, cease to be a member thereof if he or she
is absent from three consecutive meetings of the Board without leave of the Board or if he
or she is absent from Grenada for more than one month without such leave.
4\. Meetings and procedure of the Board
(1) The Board shall meet as often as may be required for the transaction of business.
Meetings of the Board shall be held at such place and time and on such days as the Board
may determine.
(2) The chairperson may at any time call a special meeting of the Board to be held
within seven days of the notice of such meeting.
(3) An extraordinary meeting of the Board shall be summoned by the secretary within
seven days of a request in that behalf addressed to him or her by any two members of the
Board or by the Minister. The request shall be in writing and, except by leave of the Board,
no business other than that specified in the request shall be transacted at the extraordinary
meeting.
(4) Subject to the provisions of this Act, the Board may regulate its own procedure.
(5) Any four members of the Board, including the chairperson, shall form a quorum.
(6) The chairperson shall preside at all meetings of the Board:
Provided that in the event of his or her being unable to attend a meeting the members of the Board
present and constituting a quorum shall elect one of their number to preside at that meeting.

(7) In the event of an equality of votes on a question at a meeting the chairperson or
person presiding at the meeting shall have a second or casting vote in respect of that
question.
(8) The validity of any proceedings of the Board shall not be affected by any vacancy
in the membership thereof or by any defect in the appointment of a member.
5\. Officers and employees of the Board
(1) The Public Service Commission may, from time to time, appoint fit and proper
persons to be, respectively, the secretary and the accountant to the Board; their conditions
of service and responsibilities, other than as specified under this Act, shall be as
contained in written directions of the Commission.

(2) The Public Service Commission may appoint such officers and employees as may
be necessary for the efficient exercise of the functions and obligations of the Board under
this Act.
(3) The officers and employees of the Board shall receive such salaries and
allowances as may be provided from time to time by Parliament.
6\. Duties of the secretary
The secretary shall be the chief executive officer of the Board and shall be responsible
to the Board for the general supervision of the activities of the Board. The secretary shall
have general administrative control of all other officers, the employees and agents of the
Board.
7\. Secretary to furnish reports to the Board
The secretary shall furnish to the Board, not later than such date as the Board may
specify, quarterly and annual reports relating to the affairs and finances of the Board.
8\. Incidental powers of the Board
The Board may—
(a)

accept grants of any kind from any source;

(b)

in respect of property vested in the Board, with the approval of the Minister,
mortgage, charge, pledge, assign or let the property by way of security for
the repayment of any sum of money borrowed by the Board and secured by
way of debenture, mortgage, deed of trust or other security and may deal
with such property for the purpose of paying off, reducing, consolidating or
making substitution for any existing or future debenture, mortgage, charge or
pledge thereof or any part thereof and may make or concur in any transfer of
or alteration in the terms of any existing or future debenture, mortgage,
charge or pledge thereof;

(c)

do all things incidental to, necessary or expedient for carrying out its
functions and obligations under this Act.

9\. Minister may give directions to the Board
The Minister may, from time to time, give special or general directions in writing to
the Board relating to the performance of its functions and obligations under this Act, and
the Board shall comply with such directions.
10\. Funds of the Board
(1) The funds of the Board shall consist of—
(a) such sums of money as may, from time to time, be provided by Parliament
for use by the Board;
(b) such sums of money as from time to time accrue to the Board by way of
interest on the funds of the Board;
(c)
such sums of money as accrue to the Board by way of payments made to the
Board for the allotment of small holdings made by the Board;
(d) any sums of money borrowed by the Board.
(2) All monies received by the Board, including monies raised by the Board by way
of any loan under the provisions of paragraph 11, shall be deposited in the Treasury, and
the Accountant-General shall place all monies so deposited to the credit of the Land
Settlement Development Fund.
11\. Borrowing power of the Board
(1) The Board may, with the approval of the Minister, from time to time, raise money
by way of loan contracted through any financial institution operating in Grenada and may

for that purpose mortgage, charge or pledge by way of security for such loan any property
vested in the Board.
(2) The principal monies and interest represented by any loan raised by the Board
under the provisions of subparagraph (1) shall constitute a charge on the Consolidated
Fund.
12\. Application of funds of the Board
The funds of the Board shall be applied solely towards—
(a)

the cost of acquiring land for the purpose of providing small holdings;

(b)

the payment of interest at the prescribed rate on sums borrowed by or placed
at the disposal of the Board, and the repayment of any such sums; and

(c)

the payment of all other expenses incurred by the Board in carrying out its
functions and obligations under this Act.

### 13.

Accounts of the Board
(1) The Board shall cause to be kept proper books of account to the satisfaction of the
Accountant-General and such accounts shall be audited annually by the Director of Audit.
(2) Notwithstanding the provisions of subparagraph (1) the Minister may, in his or her
discretion, at any time require the Director of Audit to examine and report on the
accounts of the Board, and the Board shall provide the Director of Audit with the
necessary and appropriate facilities for such examination.

### 14.

Reports
(1) The Board shall, not later than three months after the end of each financial year,
cause to be made and transmitted to the Minister—
(a)

a report dealing generally with the operations of the Board during the last
preceding financial year; and

(b)

a copy of the annual statement of accounts certified by the Director of Audit.

(2) The Minister, as soon as practicable after receipt, under subparagraph (1), of the
report and statement, shall cause—
(a)

a copy of the report together with the annual statement of accounts to be laid
before Parliament; and

(b)

a copy of the statement to be published in the Gazette.

Land Settlement Regulations

23rd October. 1909.

[Section 9.]

[15th January, 1935.]
1\. These Regulations may be cited as the Land Settlement Regulations.

## PART I

Land Settlement Committee
2\. There shall be appointed a committee to be called the Land Settlement Committee.
3\. The Land Settlement Committee shall consist of not more than six persons (one of
whom shall be Chairperson) to be appointed by the Minister.
4\. The duties of the Land Settlement Committee shall be to assist and advise
Government on all questions and matters affecting Land Settlement.
5\. Every member of the Land Settlement Committee shall hold his or her seat for a
period of one year from the date of his or her appointment.
6\. The settlements shall be visited by officers of the Ministry of Agriculture at the
request of the Land Settlement Officer who shall co-operate with him or her in the
agricultural supervision of the small holdings.

## PART II

Surveys
7\. Each area proposed for land settlement shall be surveyed and divided into small
holdings with permanent boundary marks. The land shall be selected by the Land
Settlement Officer with the advice of the Chief Technical Officer (Extension) and one
other person appointed by the Minister.
8\. Surveys shall contain all necessary reservations for rivers and their sources, roads,
nursery plots, ridge crowns and all forest land and other features as may be deemed
necessary and such reservations with the exception of the nursery plots shall be selected,
as far as practicable, from the land of least agricultural value.
9\. The remainder of the land shall be divided into small holdings of not more than
four acres and not less than one acre. Each holding shall be numbered and shall have its
number clearly displayed on some part of it in such manner as the Land Settlement
Officer shall direct.

## PART III

Applicants
10\. Applications for allotment of small holdings shall be called for by notice in the
Press.
11\. Allotments shall be made from time to time and in such manner and upon such
conditions as the Minister shall deem fit.
12\. Applicants may be medically examined in order to ascertain whether they are
physically capable of working their holdings, and may be required to furnish evidence
that they are, or have been, engaged in agriculture and have sufficient experience to
enable them to work their holdings.
13\. No holding shall be granted to any applicant who is already an owner of ten acres
of land, or so as to make him or her the owner of, in all, more than ten acres of land
without the sanction of the Cabinet.

14\. An existing tenant shall have the first choice of purchasing the small holding
within which his or her cultivation falls, unless the Minister on the report of the Land
Settlement Committee shall decide that such tenant is otherwise ineligible.

### 15.

After the initial deposit has been paid, the remainder of the amount due on the
allotment shall be paid on an equated annuity basis, covering a period of fifteen years,
interest being collected at five per cent.

### 16.

The price of each allotment shall, in each case, be determined by the Minister and
paid with interest due to the Permanent Secretary (Finance).

### 17.

In the event of a payment of capital or interest not being made within three
months of the date on which it falls due, such default shall be reported by the Land
Settlement Officer for the information of the Cabinet who may thereupon make such
order for forfeiture in respect of the allotment, or otherwise, as may be provided for under
the Act.

### 18.

A small holder may at any time pay off any instalment or instalments
outstanding, and may also at any time pay on account of any instalment any sum not less
than one dollar:
Provided that there shall be no deduction of interest by reason of any part payment of
an instalment.

### 19.

The conditions of tenure of small holders shall be as follows—
(a)

every purchaser shall, for a period of fifteen years reckoned from the date of
allocation, have his or her ordinary place of residence in the State of
Grenada; should an allottee or tenant find it necessary to leave the State,
before the lot has been paid for in full, or during the tenancy, the Land
Settlement Officer shall be informed in writing by the allottee or tenant of
the name of the person who is to be appointed agent or representative in his
or her absence;

(b)

no small holding shall be alienated, let or incumbered for a period of fifteen
years from the date of allocation without the consent of the Cabinet:
Provided that the word “alienated” shall not refer to or include an
involuntary alienation as in the case of descent or bankruptcy nor an
alienation partly involuntary as in the case of a devise by will;

(c)

no exclusive right shall vest in any person to any spring, stream, pond, well
or other natural source of water situate in or flowing through any land;

(d)

the Cabinet may at any time resume possession of any part or parts of a
holding not exceeding one-tenth of the said holding, for roads or other public
purposes, on paying therefor at the same rate per acre as that at which the
holding was originally sold, and also for the value of such crops as may be on
such selected portion of the holding;

(e)

no cocoa shall be planted on any land for a period of fifteen years from the
date of allocation without the consent in writing of the Cabinet;

(f)

all instalments and interest shall be punctually paid into the Treasury on or
before the day on which they are due.

### 20.

On failure by a holder of a small holding to comply with any of the conditions of
tenure hereinbefore set out, the Land Settlement Officer shall immediately report all the
facts of the case for the information of the Minister, who shall inquire as fully as possible
into all the circumstances of the default, whereupon the Cabinet may order that the right
to such holding and all crops and all other rights of the small holder shall be forfeited,
without any appeal to any Court, and may make such other order as may in such case be
deemed just and in accordance with the proviso contained in section 9(2) of the Act.

### 21.

Notice of an order so made shall be served upon the small holder or left at his or
her last known place of abode, and no act of re-entry shall be necessary, and such holding
shall be thenceforth at the disposal of the Cabinet, either to re-sell then or at any future
time subject to the provisions of the Act.

### 22.

On payment in full by a small holder of the purchase money of a holding, he or
she shall be entitled to a grant of the same. Such grant shall be in a form approved by the
Cabinet and shall be recorded in the Land Registry Office free of all cost to the small
holder, except stamp duty.

### 23.

Notwithstanding anything in these Rules contained the Cabinet may allot a small
holding to any applicant who served as a soldier during the Great War of 1914–1918
upon such terms and conditions as to payment or otherwise as it shall deem fit.

## PART IV

Miscellaneous

### 24.

Crops may be grown in accordance with instructions given by the Land
Settlement Officer.

### 25.

Each holder shall be liable to eviction if he or she—
(a)

fails to carry out the directions of the Land Settlement Officer;

(b)

fails to apply himself or herself assiduously to the cultivation of his or her
holding;

(c)

fails to pay instalments as they fall due without producing reasons
satisfactory to the Cabinet for his or her default;

(d)

is convicted of praedial or other larceny in circumstances which, in the
opinion of the Cabinet, make him or her undesirable as a holder;

(e)

becomes incapable of working his or her holding and is unable to make
arrangements satisfactory to the Cabinet for its proper working:

Provided, however, that the Cabinet may award any compensation as may be
considered in the circumstances meet.

### 26.

In the event of the death of the holder before the issue of his or her grant, his or
her lawful heirs shall have the right to carry on and fulfil the terms of the agreement and
shall enjoy all the rights and privileges thus inherited, and in the event of there being no
lawful heir the grant shall revert to the Government of Grenada.

## PART V

Books and Registers

### 27.

The Cabinet may, by notice in the Gazette, appoint officers to be called “Land
Settlement Officers” who shall be responsible for the general administration of Land
Settlement areas and who shall keep a register in which shall be recorded—
(a)

the number, area, and value of each small holding;

(b)

the name of the small holder;

(c)

the date of allotment;

(d)

the amount paid previous to allotment of the small holding;

(e)

the sums paid on account of instalments and the dates of such payments;

(f)

any other information which the Cabinet may from time to time require to be
recorded.

### 28.

After survey and division of the land into lots, the Land Settlement Officer shall
publish a notice in the Gazette, and in a newspaper inviting applications for leases of such
lots.

### 29.

A lease of any lot may be granted to each approved applicant upon such
conditions as the Minister shall deem fit.

### 30.

Such lease shall be granted for a term of seven years provided that the Cabinet
may, in its discretion, permit the lessee to purchase the freehold of the lot so leased to him
or her after the expiry of five years of such term or any time after the expiration of the
said term but during the lessee’s continuing tenure of the lot.

### 31.

The annual rent payable by the lessee of any lot shall be fixed by the Cabinet
which shall also determine what part, if any, thereof shall be applied towards the
reduction of the amount stated as the value of the lot at the time that any lease of the same
shall be effected for the purpose of arriving at the purchase price to be paid by any lessee
whose application for the sale to him or her of the leased premises shall be granted.

### 32.

On the expiration of any term for which a lease of any lot may be granted the
Cabinet may grant a new lease of the same lot to the lessee and the value to be placed on the
said lot in such new lease shall be the amount at which it was valued in the immediately
preceding lease less such part of the total rents paid by the lessee during such preceding
term as shall have been allocated towards reduction of the value stated in such
immediately preceding lease.

### 33.

An order for the forfeiture of any lot and of all rights in connection therewith may
be made by the Cabinet for just cause, and whether leasehold or freehold, and such other
order may be made as in each case be deemed just and in accordance with the proviso
contained in section 9(2) of the Act.

### 34.

Notwithstanding anything in these Regulations contained, the Cabinet may grant
a lease or other estate including the freehold of any lot to any person who may have
served with the forces or as a munition worker during any war in which Her Majesty may
have been or may be engaged in, upon such terms and conditions as to payment or
otherwise as the Cabinet shall deem fit.

### 35.

Regulations 10 to 23, inclusive, and regulations 25 and 26 of these Regulations
shall cease to have effect, as regards all applicants for lots in any Land Settlement area,
after the 3rd day of May, 1941.

### 36.

The allottee or tenant shall not—
(a)

trim or fell any trees or burn coals except with the written authority of the
Land Settlement Officer;

(b)

burn or permit to be burnt, remove or permit to be removed from the
settlement, bush, grass or any other vegetable matter suitable as a mulch;
provided that brambles or other prickly plants may be burnt by permission in
writing obtained from the Land Settlement Officer;

(c)

tether cattle on the lot except such animal is kept at picket or penned and the
manure produced is not removed from the settlement.

### 37.

Allotters or tenants shall keep around all boundary marks free from weeds; any
marks missing or damaged shall be replaced by the Land Settlement Officer at the
expense of the allotters or tenants concerned.

LAND SETTLEMENT (POINT SALINE) REGULATIONS
[Section 9.]

[15th January, 1935.]

---

### 1.

Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Land Settlement (Point Saline) Regulations.

### 2.

Application of Regulations
These Regulations shall apply only to the lands purchased at Point Saline for locating
small holders; the administration and disposal of such lands and of all details connected
therewith shall, subject to these Regulations and to such orders as may be given by the
Cabinet, be entrusted to the Director of Lands and Surveys.

### 3.

Survey
The lands intended for settlement shall be surveyed by the Director of Lands and
Surveys and divided into small holdings and a plan of the said lands made and kept at the
Lands and Surveys Department.

### 4.

Paths, etc.
In making the survey, reservations shall be made for paths and for an experimental and
nursery holding.

### 5.

Division of land
The plan of the survey shall show with denoting numbers the small holdings into
which the land is divided with the area of each such holding.

### 6.

Boundary marks
Every corner of each small holding shall be marked by hardwood posts or concrete
pillars, and the general outline by dragon’s blood or immortelle plants, which plants shall
be carefully tended by the holder.

### 7.

Price

The price of the land shall be fixed at fifteen dollars per acre.

### 8.

Deposit
Every small holder shall pay the purchase price of the holding by a deposit of twentyfive per cent and the remainder by nine annual instalments.

### 9.

Balance of payment
The deposit of twenty-five per cent shall be paid before the small holder is permitted
to occupy the holding and the balance shall be payable on an annuity basis with interest at
five per cent within ten years from the date of allocation.

### 10.

Full purchase price
A small holder may at any time pay the full purchase price of his or her holding.

### 11.

Conditions
The conditions of tenure of a small holding shall be as follows—
(a)

no holding may be alienated, let or encumbered without the consent of the
Cabinet;

(b)

the word “alienated” does not refer to or include an involuntary alienation in
the case of descent nor an alienation partly involuntary as in the case of a
devise by will;

(c)

the holding shall be cultivated in ground provisions. Permanent crops such
as limes may be grown in accordance with the advice of the Ministry of
Agriculture;

(d)

all paths passing through or along a holding shall be kept clean by the
holder.

### 12.

Failure to comply with conditions
On failure by a small holder of a holding to comply with any of the conditions of tenure
hereinbefore detailed the Chief Forestry and Lands Officer shall immediately report all the
facts of the case to the Minister, who shall inquire as fully as possible into all the
circumstances of the default, whereupon the Cabinet may order that the right to such a
holding shall be forfeited without any appeal to any Court, but subject to the proviso
contained in section 9(2) of the Act.

### 13.

Experimental holding
The general care and maintenance of the Experimental and nursery holding shall be
under the officer in charge of the Ministry of Agriculture.

### 14.

Grant to holder
On payment in full by a holder of the purchase money of a holding, he or she shall be
entitled to a grant of the same. Such grant shall be in a form approved by the Cabinet and
shall be recorded in the Land Registry Office free of all costs to the holder except the
stamp duty.

LAND SETTLEMENT DEVELOPMENT RULES
[Section 9. SRO 6 of 1972.]

[27th May, 1972.]
1\. Citation
These Rules may be cited as the Land Settlement Development Rules.
2\. Allotments
Allotments shall be made from time to time and in such manner as the Board shall
deem fit.
3\. Purchase of holding
An existing tenant shall have the first choice of purchasing the small holding within
which his or her cultivation falls, unless the Board shall decide that such tenant is
otherwise ineligible.
4\. Manner of payment
After an initial deposit of not less than ten per cent of the purchase price has been
paid, the remainder of the amount due on the allotment shall be paid in equal annual
instalments covering a maximum period of fifteen years, interest being payable at the rate
of five per cent per annum on the reducing balance.
5\. Purchase price of allotment
The price of each allotment shall in every case be determined by the Board and shall
be paid to the Ministry of Finance in accordance with the provisions of rule 4 of these
Rules.
6\. Failure to comply with provisions
In the event of an allottee failing to comply with the provisions of rule 4, the Minister
may, on recommendation of the Board, order the forfeiture of all rights of the allottee to
the lot.
7\. Conditions of tenure
(1) The conditions of tenure of small holdings shall be as follows—

(a)

If an allottee finds it necessary to leave the State before the lot has been paid
for in full, he or she shall inform the Secretary of the Board in writing of the
name of the person who is to be appointed his or her agent or representative
in his or her absence.

(b)

No small holding shall be alienated, let or incumbered during the period in
which any portion of the purchase price remains outstanding without the
consent in writing of the Minister.

(c)

No small holding shall be let for any period exceeding three years or sold or
incumbered without the consent in writing of Cabinet, such consent being
signified under the hand of the Cabinet Secretary and every letting, transfer
or encumbrance in contravention of this Rule shall be void.

(2) For the purpose of this Rule, the word “alienation” shall not refer to or include an
involuntary alienation as in the case of descent, devise by will or bankruptcy.
(3) No exclusive right shall vest in any person to any mineral resource or any spring,
stream, pond, well or other natural source of water in or flowing through any land.
8\. Procedure governing payment in full
On payment in full by an allottee of the purchase price of a holding, he or she shall be
entitled to a conveyance of the same. Such conveyance shall be in a form approved by the
Board without cost to the allottee, excluding stamp duty, and shall be recorded in the
Deeds and Land Registry.
9\. Allottee dying before issuance of conveyance
In the event of the death of the allottee before the issue of his or her conveyance, his or
her personal representative shall have the right to carry on and fulfil the terms of the
agreement and shall enjoy the rights and privileges thus inherited.
10\. Eviction
An allottee shall be liable to eviction if he or she—
(a)

fails to cultivate his or her holding in keeping with the directions of the
agricultural instructor for his or her area;

(b)

fails to pay his or her instalments as they fall due;

(c)

becomes incapable of working his or her holding and is unable to make
arrangements satisfactory to the Board for its proper working:

Provided, however, that the Board may award such compensation as may be
considered meet in the circumstances.
11\. Compensation
Upon forfeiture of his or her small holding an allottee shall be entitled to
compensation from the Board for any increase in value of his or her lot.
