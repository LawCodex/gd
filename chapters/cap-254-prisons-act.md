## CHAPTER 254

PRISONS ACT
An Act to provide for the regulation and management of prisons and prison officers.
[Act No. 11 of 1980 amended by Act No. 23 of 1988, Act No. 46 of 1991.]

[21st March, 1980.]

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Prisons Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act—
“civil prisoner” means a prisoner other than a criminal prisoner;
“Commissioner” means the Commissioner of Prisons appointed under section 6;
“criminal prisoner” means a prisoner duly committed to custody under a writ or
warrant or the order of a court or other lawful authority exercising criminal
jurisdiction including any court martial;
“first offender” means a person committed to prison for the first time;
“lock-up” means any house, building or enclosure declared to be a lock-up under
section 4;
“medical officer” means a medical officer assigned to duties in a prison by the
Chief Medical Officer under section 7;

“member of the subordinate staff” means a prison officer, below the rank of
Assistant Superintendent, having duties to instruct, supervise or employ prisoners or to
perform work in connection with prisons;
“Minister” means the Minister for the time being responsible for prisons;
“prison” means Richmond Hill Prison, the police cells at Hillsborough in
Carriacou, and any house, building or enclosure declared to be a prison under section
4;
“prison offence” means an offence committed by a prisoner against this Act or
prison rules, or prison standing orders;
“prison officer” means any person holding an office or performing a duty in or in
connection with a prison, but does not include any person appointed to perform
clerical duties only;
“prison rules” means the rules made under section 53;
“prisoner” means a person (whether convicted or not) undergoing detention in a
prison;
“prohibited article” means—
(a)

any intoxicating liquor, drug, tobacco, money, clothing, food, letter, or other
thing of any kind whatsoever that is likely to prejudice good order and prison
discipline;

(b)

any article the introduction or removal of which into or out of a prison or any
part thereof is prohibited by prison rules or standing orders;

“young prisoner” means a prisoner under the age of eighteen years.
Establishment and Administration of Prisons and Lock-Ups

### 3.

Richmond Hill Prison and police cells at Carriacou
(1) The prison situate at Richmond Hill known as Richmond Hill Prison shall be a
prison for the purposes of this Act.
(2) The police cells at Hillsborough in Carriacou shall be a prison for the purposes of
this Act.

### 4.

Declaration of prisons and lock-ups
(1) The Minister may, by Order published in the Gazette—
(a)

declare any house, building or enclosure or any part thereof to be a prison or
lock-up for the purposes of this Act and may, in such Order, specify the
name by which such prison or lock-up shall be known;

(b)

declare that a prison or lock-up shall cease to be a prison or lock-up and, as
from the date of the publication of such Order or such other date as may be
specified therein, such prison or lock-up shall cease to be a prison or lock-up
for the purposes of this Act.

(2) Every place used as a lock-up at the commencement of this Act shall be deemed
to be a lock-up for the purposes of this Act as if such place had been declared to be a
lock-up pursuant to subsection (1)(a).

### 5.

Responsibilities of the Commissioner
The general charge and administration of prisons and the control and direction of the
prison staff shall be the responsibility of the Commissioner who shall discharge such

responsibility in compliance with the directives of the Public Service Commission given
in accordance with the Constitution, in particular section 85 of the Constitution.
Appointment, Administration and Powers of Prison Officers and Staff

### 6.

Appointment of prison officers and staff
(1) In accordance with the Constitution, in particular section 85 thereof, the
Governor-General acting in accordance with the advice of the Public Service
Commission, shall have the power to appoint persons to hold or act in (including the
power to confirm appointments to) the following offices in the Prisons Department—
(a)

a Commissioner of Prisons, as Head of the Prisons Department;

(b)

a Superintendent of Prisons, as Deputy Head of the Prisons Department.

(2) The power to exercise disciplinary control over the persons referred to in
subsection (1)(a) and (b), and the power to remove such persons from office and the
power to grant leave to them shall vest in the Governor-General, acting in accordance
with the advice of the Public Service Commission, and on the conditions stipulated by
section 85 of the Constitution.
(3) In accordance with the Constitution, in particular section 84 thereof, the Public
Service Commission shall have the power to appoint persons to hold or act in (including
the power to confirm appointments to) the following offices in the Prisons Department—
(a)

an Assistant Superintendant of Prisons;

(b)

as many subordinate prison officers as may be necessary; and

(c)

as many clerical officers as may be necessary.

(4) The power to exercise disciplinary control over the persons referred to in
subsection (3)(a), (b) and (c) and the power to remove such persons from office and the
power to grant leave to them shall vest in the Public Service Commission in accordance
with the Constitution, particular section 84 thereof.
(5) All staff members serving in the Prisons Department on the coming into force of
this Act shall be deemed to have been appointed under the provisions of this section.
(6) All provisions of this Act regarding the power to appoint persons to hold or act in
(including the power to confirm appointments to) offices in the Prisons Department or the
power to exercise disciplinary control over persons holding or acting in such offices and
the power to remove such persons from such offices and the power to grant leave to such
persons shall be read subject to the Constitution, in particular sections 84, 85 and 91
thereof.

### 7.

Medical officers
The Chief Medical Officer shall assign in respect of each prison a medical officer who
shall have control generally of the health and medical welfare of the prisoners and the
sanitation of the prison, and such medical officer shall perform such other duties as may
be imposed on him or her by this Act or by prison rules.

### 8.

Standing orders
The Public Service Commission may issue standing orders for the governing of
members of the staff of the Prisons Department and prisoners.

### 9.

Power of interdiction

(1) The Public Service Commission may interdict from his or her duties any member
of the subordinate staff pending the result of any inquiry the Public Service Commission
may see fit to order.
(2) An officer so interdicted shall be paid such proportion of his or her salary, being
not less than one-half, as the Public Service Commission shall think fit.
(3) If the inquiry results in the exculpation of the officer he or she shall be entitled to
the full amount of his or her salary which he or she would have received if he or she had
not been interdicted but, if the inquiry results in any punishment other than dismissal, the
officer shall be allowed such salary as the Public Service Commission may think fit.

### 10.

Resignation of subordinate staff
(1) No member of the subordinate staff shall without the written permission of the
Public Service Commission resign or withdraw from prison duties unless he or she has
first given to the Public Service Commission at least one month’s notice in writing
expiring on the last day of the succeeding month of his or her intention to do so.
(2) A member of the subordinate staff who contravenes the provisions of
subsection (1) shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of
four hundred dollars and to imprisonment for two months.

### 11.

Power of Superintendent to hold inquiry into wrongdoing by staff
(1) If directed by the Public Service Commission in writing with consent of the Prime
Minister, the Superintendent or any other prison officer may hold an inquiry into the
conduct of any member of the subordinate staff who is alleged to have—
(a)

been absent from duty without good cause;

(b)

been asleep on duty;

(c)

been insurbordinate;

(d)

been late for duty;

(e)

disobeyed any lawful order given him or her by his or her superior in rank or
seniority, whether verbally or in writing;

(f)

committed any neglect of duty;

(g)

used personal violence or abusive, insulting or threatening language to any
member of the prison staff;

(h)

lost by neglect or wilfully destroyed, damaged or permitted injury to any
article of clothing, arms, tools, supplies or equipment in his or her charge;

(i)

presented himself or herself for duty when dirty or untidy in person or
clothing;

(j)

been under the influence of alcohol or drugs while on duty;

(k)

used unnecessary violence to a prisoner;

(l)

associated with a discharged prisoner without the written permission of the
Superintendent;

(m)

been unduly familiar with, or shown undue favour to, a prisoner;

(n)

communicated to another person any matter or thing connected with the
administration of the Prison Department;

(o)

committed an act, or conducted himself or herself in a manner, prejudicial to
prison discipline or likely to bring the prison service into disrepute;

(p)

committed any breach of prison rules or standing orders,

and if after such inquiry the Superintendent is satisfied that such member of the
subordinate staff has committed the wrongdoing alleged, he or she may award any of the
following punishments—
(i) reprimand,
(ii) severe reprimand,
(iii) forfeiture of good conduct pay or any benefit arising from service,
(iv) fine of not more than one-half of one month’s salary,
(v) suspension, stoppage or deferment of increment,
(vi) reduction in rank or grade,
(vii) dismissal:
Provided that in the case of conduct specified in—
(aa) paragraph (a) of this subsection, the punishment may include
forfeiture of pay for the period of absence;
(bb) paragraph (h) of this subsection, the punishment may include an order
to pay the value or part of the value of the property lost, destroyed,
damaged or injured to be enforced by deduction from such member’s
salary either at one time or by such instalments as may be specified in
the order.
(2) A member of the subordinate staff may, within two days of the date on which a
punishment was awarded under this section, appeal in writing to the Public Service
Commission against the punishment, and the Public Service Commission may allow the
appeal, or reduce, confirm or increase such punishment or substitute any other
punishment authorised by the foregoing subsection.

### 12.

Offences by prison staff
Every member of the prison staff and any person who is employed in any capacity
whatsoever in a prison who—
(a)

has any dealing, whereby he or she obtains or might expect to obtain at any
time either immediately or in the future any benefit of any nature
whatsoever, with a prisoner or with any person on behalf of a prisoner; or

(b)

assaults, threatens or insults any member of the prison staff,

shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of four hundred
dollars and to imprisonment for three months.

### 13.

Powers, etc., of prison officers
Every prison officer while acting lawfully in the execution of his or her duties as such
shall have, by virtue of his or her office, the same powers, immunities and privileges as a
police constable under the Police Act, Chapter 244.

### 14.

Use of weapons by prison officers
(1) A prison officer may use a weapon against any prisoner escaping or attempting to
escape:
Provided that resort shall not be had to the use of any weapon unless such officer
has reasonable grounds for believing that he or she cannot otherwise prevent the escape.
(2) A prison officer may use a weapon against any prisoner engaged in any combined
outbreak, or in any attempt to force or break open the outside door, or gate, or enclosure

wall, of the prison, and may continue to use such weapon so long as such combined
outbreak or attempt is being actually prosecuted.
(3) A prison officer may use a weapon against any prisoner using violence against
any person if such officer has reasonable grounds for believing that such person is in
danger of life or limb or that other grievous harm is likely to be caused to him or her.
(4) Before using any firearm against a prisoner under the authority of subsection (1)
the prison officer shall, if possible, give warning to the prisoner that he or she is about to
open fire on him or her.
(5) No member of the subordinate staff shall in the presence of his or her superior
officer use any firearm against a prisoner in the case of an attempt to escape or of a
combined outbreak, except under the orders of the superior officer.
(6) The use of firearms under this section shall, as far as possible, be to disable and
not to kill.
(7) A police officer for the time being on duty as an escort, or as a guard in or around
any prison or lock-up for the purpose of ensuring the safe custody of a prisoner or person
detained in a prison or lock-up, shall be deemed to have the same powers and obligations
as a prison officer under this section for the purpose of carrying out that duty.

### 15.

Inspection of prisons by, and annual report of, the Commissioner
The Commissioner shall as often as may be necessary visit and inspect all prisons and
shall make an annual report to the Minister on the administration and condition of the
prisons, the conduct and treatment of the prisoners and such other matters relating to the
prisons or to prisoners as he or she may think fit.

### 16.

Prison Officers’ Reward Fund
All pay which is forfeited by, and fines inflicted upon, members of the subordinate
staff under this Act shall be paid into a fund to be called the Prison Officers’ Reward
Fund and this fund shall be administered in accordance with the prison rules.
Custody and Removal of Prisoners and Persons

### 17.

Persons sentenced, etc., to be committed to prison
(1) Every person sentenced to imprisonment, unless the sentence is expressed by a
court to be suspended, shall be committed to and detained in a prison.
(2) A person remanded in custody may be committed to and detained in either a
prison or a lock-up.

### 18.

Prisoners, etc., deemed in legal custody
(1) Every prisoner whenever he or she is confined in a prison in which he or she is
lawfully confined, or whenever he or she is being taken to or from such prison, or is
working in the custody or under the control of any member of the prison staff or police
officer assigned to that duty beyond the limits of such prison, shall be deemed to be in the
custody of the person in charge of such prison.
(2) Every person whenever he or she is confined in a lock-up in which he or she is
lawfully confined, or whenever he or she is being taken to or from such lock-up, shall be
deemed to be in the legal custody of the person in charge of such lock-up.

### 19.

Detention of persons committed to prisons and lock-ups authorised

Persons in charge of prisons and lock-ups are hereby authorised and required to keep
and detain all persons duly committed to their custody by a court, Judge, magistrate,
coroner or other public officer lawfully exercising civil or criminal jurisdiction according
to the terms of any writ, warrant, or order by which such persons have been committed
until such persons are discharged by due process of law.

### 20.

Delivery of person on remand
Every person charged with an offence and remanded in custody to a prison or lock-up
by a court, Judge, magistrate or coroner shall be delivered to the person in charge of the
prison or lock-up as the case may be, together with the warrant of commitment, and the
person in charge of the prison or lock-up shall detain that person according to the terms of
the warrant, and shall cause such person to be delivered to such court, Judge, magistrate
or coroner, or shall discharge such person, at the time named in and according to the
terms of such warrant.

### 21.

Power of detention in civil cases
Every person arrested under a writ, warrant or order of a court having civil jurisdiction
shall be brought without delay before the court by which the writ, warrant, or order was
issued, and if such court is not then sitting such person shall be kept in custody in a prison
or lock-up until the next sitting of the court and shall be then brought before such court in
order that he or she may be dealt with according to law.

### 22.

Release of prisoners to be immediate
Every prisoner and person detained in a prison or lock-up shall be released
immediately upon his or her becoming entitled to release, whether by the expiration of his
or her term of sentence, or by pardon, by commutation, or by remission of sentence or by
other lawful means.

### 23.

Production of persons confined in prisons, etc., before the court
(1) Whenever the presence of a person confined in a prison or lock-up is required in
any court of civil or criminal jurisdiction, the court may issue an order addressed to the
person in charge of the prison or lock-up, as the case may be, requiring the production
before the court of such person in proper custody at the time and place named in the
order, and the person named in the order shall bring him or her up as directed and shall
provide for his or her safe custody during his or her absence from the prison or lock-up;
and the court may by endorsement on such order require the person named therein to be
again brought up at any time to which the matter, wherein such person’s presence is
required, may be adjourned.
(2) The Minister on being satisfied that the presence of a named prisoner or person
detained in a prison or lock-up is required at any place in the interest of justice, or for the
purpose of a public inquiry, may, in his or her discretion, by writing under his or her hand
order that such prisoner or person be taken to the place at the time specified in such order,
and the person in charge of the prison or lock-up shall cause the prisoner or the person so
named to be taken as directed and shall provide for his or her safe custody during his or
her absence from the prison or lock-up.

### 24.

Person taken out of prison under order deemed in legal custody
A person taken from a prison or lock-up under an order made under section 23 shall,
whilst outside that prison or lock-up, be deemed to be in legal custody.

### 25.

Prisoner of unsound mind may be removed to hospital

(1) Whenever a prisoner or person detained in a prison or lock-up appears to the
Commissioner on the certificate of the medical officer to be of unsound mind, the
Commissioner may, in his or her discretion, by order in writing setting forth the grounds
of belief that such prisoner or person detained is of unsound mind, direct his or her
removal to a mental hospital under the Mental Hospitals Act, Chapter 190, until the
Medical Superintendent of the mental hospital certifies that such prisoner or person has
ceased to require treatment in that hospital.
(2) Where the Medical Superintendent of a mental hospital has certified as set out in
subsection (1), if the person in respect of whom the certificate is given is—
(a)

a prisoner whose sentence has expired, the Commissioner shall by order in
writing direct that such prisoner be discharged;

(b)

a prisoner whose sentence has not expired, the Commissioner in his or her
discretion may by order in writing direct that either he or she be returned to
the prison whence he or she was removed there to serve the remainder of the
term to which he or she is liable, or that he or she be discharged; or

(c)

a person awaiting trial or remanded in custody, the Commissioner in his or
her discretion may by order in writing direct that such person be returned to
the prison or lock-up whence he or she was removed until he or she is dealt
with according to law.

### 26.

Removal of prisoner suffering from communicable disease
(1) Whenever a prisoner or person detained in a prison or lock-up appears to the
Commissioner on the certificate of any medical officer to be suffering from a
communicable disease, the Commissioner in his or her discretion may, in writing setting
forth the grounds of belief that the prisoner or such person detained is suffering from such
communicable disease, direct his or her removal to such hospital as may be specified in
the order there to be kept and treated until the medical officer in charge of the hospital
certifies that such prisoner or person has ceased to require treatment from the hospital.
(2) Where a certificate to the effect that a prisoner or person detained has ceased to
require institutional treatment has been given under subsection (1) then if any prisoner or
person in respect of whom the certificate is given is—
(a)

a prisoner and the term of his or her sentence has expired, the Commissioner
shall by order in writing direct that such prisoner be discharged;

(b)

a prisoner and the term of his or her sentence has not expired, the
Commissioner in his or her discretion may by order in writing direct either
that he or she be returned to the prison whence he or she was removed there
to serve the remainder of his or her sentence, or that he or she be discharged;
or

(c)

a person remanded in custody or awaiting trial, the Commissioner in his or
her discretion may by order in writing direct that such person shall be
returned to the prison or lock-up whence he or she was removed until he or
she is dealt with according to law.

(3) In this section, “medical officer” has the meaning assigned by section 2 of this
Act and the Medical Officers Act, Chapter 188.

### 27.

Removal of disabled prisoner
Where the Minister is satisfied that a prisoner is suffering from a permanent physical
injury or disability and that it is necessary for the purpose of ensuring the proper care of
the prisoner that he or she should be detained in an institution other than a prison, he or
she may in writing direct that the prisoner be removed to an institution specified in the

direction and be there detained for the remainder of the term to which he or she is liable
unless sooner discharged according to law.

### 28.

Removal of prisoner in case of serious illness
(1) In case of the serious illness, except unsoundness of mind, or any communicable
disease, of a prisoner or person detained in a prison or lock-up, there being no suitable
accommodation for such prisoner or person in the prison or lock-up, the Commissioner
on the certificate of any medical officer or in a case of urgent necessity on the certificate
of any medical practitioner (or, if no such medical officer or practitioner is available, the
Commissioner or, in his or her absence, any senior prison officer) may in writing direct
his or her removal to a public hospital.
(2) So long as a prisoner or person detained who has been removed to a public
hospital under the provisions of subsection (1) remains therein, a medical officer shall at
the end of each month transmit to the Commissioner or the person in charge of the lockup a certificate signed by him or her that it is in his or her opinion necessary that the
prisoner or person detained should remain in the hospital.
(3) As soon as, in the opinion of the medical officer in charge of a public hospital, it
is no longer necessary that a prisoner or person detained who has been removed to such
hospital should remain therein, he or she shall transmit to the Commissioner or person in
charge of the lock-up, as the case may be, a certificate stating that such necessity has
ceased and thereupon the Commissioner or person in charge of the lock-up shall forthwith
cause such prisoner or person detained to be brought back to the prison or lock-up if he or
she is still liable to be confined therein.
(4) The provisions of subsections (2) and (3) shall cease to apply to a prisoner or
person detained on and from the date on which he or she would be entitled to be released
from the prison or lock-up.
(5) In this section, “medical officer” has the meaning assigned by section 2 of this Act
and the Medical Officers Act, Chapter 188.

### 29.

Custody of prisoner, etc., in hospital
Where a prisoner or person detained in a lock-up is, by virtue of section 28, removed
from the prison or lock-up to a public hospital, the Commissioner shall take all practical
steps to prevent the escape of such prisoner or person, and while such prisoner or person
remains in the hospital he or she shall be deemed to be in legal custody:
Provided that where the prisoner or person detained would, but for the fact that he
or she is in the hospital, be entitled to be released from the prison or lock-up then, from
the date on which he or she would be so entitled to be released, he or she shall no longer
be deemed to be in legal custody and no steps shall be taken to prevent him or her from
leaving merely by reason of the fact that he or she had been a prisoner or person detained.

### 30.

Transfer of prisoners from one prison to another
The Commissioner may order the removal of all or any prisoners confined in one
prison to another prison and it shall not be necessary for the purposes of such order to
designate any prisoner by name, but it shall be sufficient to describe such prisoners by
reference to their sentence or by some other general description.

### 31.

Separation of prisoners
(1) No male prisoner shall be detained in the same part of a prison as any female
prisoner.
(2) No civil prisoner shall (so far as possible) be detained in the same part of a prison
as any criminal prisoner.

(3) No young prisoner shall (so far as possible) be detained in the same part of a
prison as any adult prisoner.

### 32.

Transfer of young offenders
(1) Where a person apparently under the age of sixteen years has been committed to
prison, the Commissioner may order that he or she be transferred to an Industrial School
and kept there as if he or she had been committed thereto by a court.
(2) In the foregoing subsection, “Industrial School” has the same meaning as in
section 3(1) of the Criminal Code, Chapter 72A.
Discipline and Control of Prisoners

### 33.

Employment of prisoners outside prison
All prisoners may, with the approval of the Commissioner, be taken beyond the limits
of a prison and put to such labour as may be prescribed by prison rules.

### 34.

Prisoners subject to Act and rules
Every prisoner shall be subject to the provisions of this Act and prison rules, and shall
also be subject to prison discipline during the whole of his or her imprisonment, whether
he or she is or is not within the limits of a prison.

### 35.

Punishment for prison offences
Where a prisoner commits any of the following prison offences namely—
(a)

mutiny or incitement to mutiny;

(b)

escape or attempt to escape;

(c)

taking part in any assault or attack on a member of the prison staff or on a
medical officer;

(d)

aggravated or repeated assault on any other prisoner;

(e)

wilful destruction of property;

(f)

wilfully causing to himself or herself any illness, injury or disability;

(g)

wilfully making any false or groundless accusation or complaint against a
member of the prison staff, the medical officer or another prisoner;

(h)

persistent repetition of any minor prison offence;

(i)

any act of misconduct or insurbordination;

(j)

abetting the commission of any major prison offence;

(k)

malingering or feigning illness;

(l)

disobeying an order of the Commissioner or any other officer, or any prison
rules or standing orders;

(m)

acting in any way prejudicial to good order and discipline,

then, if such prisoner is found guilty thereof after proper inquiry by the Superintendent or
an officer authorised by him or her, the officer hearing the inquiry may impose any one or
any combination of not more than two of the following punishments, namely—
(i) in the case of an offence specified in paragraphs (a), (c) or (d), lawful
corporal punishment not exceeding twelve strokes:

Provided that corporal punishment shall not be imposed without the approval
of the Minister,
(ii) reduction of diet to No. 1 punishment diet for any period not exceeding
fourteen consecutive days,
(iii) reduction of diet to No. 2 punishment diet for any period not exceeding
twenty-one consecutive days,
(iv) suspension or postponement of any or all privileges for any period not
exceeding ninety days,
(v) forfeiture of remission for any period not exceeding ninety days and, in the
case of an offence specified in paragraph (b), forfeiture of all remission
earned up to the date of such offence.

### 36.

Prisoner to be heard on his or her own behalf
No prisoner shall be punished until he or she has had an opportunity of hearing the
charge against him or her and making his or her defence.

### 37.

Entry of punishment in register
When any punishment is imposed upon a prisoner the Commissioner shall cause to be
entered, in a register to be kept for the purpose, a record of the punishment showing in
respect of each prisoner punished the name of the prisoner, the nature of his or her
offence and the nature and extent of his or her punishment.

### 38.

Appeal by prisoner to Commissioner
A prisoner, within forty-eight hours of the imposition of a punishment under section
35, may appeal in writing to the Commissioner who shall either allow the appeal, or
reduce, confirm or increase the punishment, as he or she considers just.

### 39.

Detention after date of release
A punishment lawfully imposed on a prisoner under this Act or prison rules may be
carried into effect notwithstanding that the carrying into effect thereof may necessitate
detention of the prisoner beyond the date on which he or she would otherwise be entitled
to be released:
Provided that the period of detention, calculated from the last hour of the day upon
which the prisoner would otherwise be entitled to be released, shall not exceed seven
days.

### 40.

Supervision of prisoner under sentence of death
Every prisoner under sentence of death shall be confined apart from other prisoners in
a special cell, and by day and by night shall be kept under constant supervision.

### 41.

Access to prisoners under sentence of death
Except with the written permission of the Commissioner, no person other than a
member of the prison staff, the medical officer and a minister of the religious
denomination to which the prisoner belongs shall have access to a prisoner under
sentence of death.
Membership of Trade Unions, etc., by Prison Officers Prohibited

### 42.

Prison Officers’ Welfare Association
(1) For the purpose of enabling prison officers to consider and bring to the notice of
the Commissioner and the Minister all matters affecting their general welfare and
efficiency, other than questions of discipline and any other matter affecting individuals,
there shall be established in accordance with rules to be made by the Minister an
organisation to be called the Prison Officers’ Welfare Association which shall act through
boards and a central committee as provided in the rules.
(2) The Association shall be entirely independent of, and unassociated with, any body
outside the Prison Department.

### 43.

Prohibition on prison officers becoming members of trade unions, etc.
(1) Subject to the provisions of the preceding section, it shall not be lawful for a
prison officer to be or become a member of a trade union or of any association which has
for its objects, or one of its objects, to control or influence the pay, pension or conditions
of service of any prison officer, or to promote feelings of ill-will and hostility between
different classes or races.
(2) If a prison officer becomes a member of a trade union or any such association he
or she shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of two
hundred dollars and he or she shall be dismissed from the prison service.
Miscellaneous

### 44.

Power to require released prisoner to notify address
(1) The Commissioner may, on or before the release of a prisoner, in writing direct
him or her to notify, within seven days of his or her release, his or her address to the
officer in charge of a police station in the district in which he or she is residing and
further to notify such officer within seven days of the occurrence of any change in his or
her address during such period, not exceeding five years after such release, as the
Commissioner in his or her direction shall specify.
(2) A direction given under this section shall be served on the prisoner before his or
her release from prison.
(3) A person who contravenes a direction given under subsection (1) or who
knowingly or recklessly notifies a false or incorrect address shall be guilty of an offence
and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of two hundred dollars and to imprisonment
for two months.

### 45.

Coroner to hold inquest on death of prisoner
It shall be the duty of the coroner having jurisdiction in the place where a prison is
situated to hold an inquest upon the body of a prisoner who dies in the prison whether
following sentence of death or otherwise.

### 46.

Prisoner not to be punished twice for same offence
Nothing in this Act or in prison rules shall be construed to exempt a prisoner from
being proceeded against in a court for any offence, but no prisoner shall be punished
twice for the same offence:
Provided that nothing in this section shall be construed as derogating from the
powers of punishment in section 35.

### 47.

Offence of escape, and effects on sentence

(1) A prisoner who escapes or attempts to escape from lawful custody shall be guilty
of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to imprisonment for twelve months, and
remission earned by such prisoner up to the date of the offence shall be forfeited.
(2) If a prisoner under sentence of imprisonment escapes, the currency of the sentence
which he or she is then undergoing shall be suspended from the day on which he or she
escapes to the day on which he or she is recaptured so that he or she shall be deemed not
to have served any part of the sentence during the period between the day on which he or
she escaped and the day on which he or she was recaptured.

### 48.

Prohibited articles
(1) A person who, without lawful authority—
(a)

brings, throws, conveys or causes to be conveyed or supplied to a prisoner or
introduces by any means into a prison, or hides or places for any prisoner or
prisoners any prohibited article; or

(b)

brings or throws or conveys, or attempts to bring, out of a prison any
prohibited article,

shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of four thousand
dollars and to imprisonment for one year.
(2) A person having committed or being in the act of committing an offence under
subsection (1) may be arrested without warrant by any other person and taken before a
magistrate to be dealt with according to law.
(3) A prison officer or police officer convicted of an offence under subsection (1)
shall, in addition to any other punishment, forfeit his or her office and any arrears of
salary due him or her and also any pension and gratuity to which he or she may be
entitled.

### 49.

Communication with prisoners forbidden
A person who, without leave or lawful excuse, distracts or speaks to or in any way
communicates with a prisoner at work without the walls of a prison or while going to or
returning from such work shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction,
to a fine of two hundred dollars and to imprisonment for three months.

### 50.

Assault, etc., on prison officer
A person who assaults, obstructs, or resists or aids or incites another person to assault,
obstruct, or resist a prison officer in the execution of his or her duty shall be guilty of an
offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand dollars and to
imprisonment for twelve months, and if the offender is a convicted prisoner he or she
shall be liable upon conviction on indictment to imprisonment for two years.

### 51.

Power of Minister to make rules, and saving of existing rules
(1) The Minister may make rules generally for the good management and governance
of prisons and prisoners and for carrying out the objects of this Act.
(2) Without prejudice to the generality of subsection (1), the Minister may make rules
governing—
(a)

the general conditions of the prisons;

(b)

the employment, classification, safe custody separation, treatment and
discipline of prisoners;

(c)

the medical examination, measuring, photographing, taking of fingerprints
and other records of prisoners;

(d)

the remission of sentences to be allowed to prisoners, including the
conditions under which remissions are to be granted;

(e)

the rehabilitation of a discharged prisoner including the supply to him or her
of money, food, clothing or the means of travelling to the town nearest his or
her place of abode;

(f)

the administration of the Prison Officers’ Reward Fund;

(g)

the conditions under which, and the manner in which, the sentence of death
shall be administered;

(h)

the conditions under which, and the manner in which, lawful corporal
punishment shall be administered;

(i)

the means and methods of restraining refractory prisoners;

(j)

the appointment, duties, powers and proceedings of Visiting Committees.

(3) All rules made under the provisions of the Prisons Act, 1965, which were in force
at the commencement of this Act, so far as consistent with the provisions of this Act shall
continue to have effect until revoked by rules made under this section.

PRISON OFFICERS (DISCIPLINE) RULES
[SRO 15 of 1980. Section 51.]

[16th January, 1981.]

---

### 1.

Citation
These Rules may be cited as the Prison Officers (Discipline) Rules.

### 2.

Application
The Code of Discipline set out in these Rules shall apply to prison officers below the
rank of Assistant Superintendent of Prisons.

## PART I

Code of Discipline

### 3.

Offences against prison discipline
An officer to whom these Rules apply commits an offence against prison discipline if
he or she is guilty of—
(a)

being absent from duty without good cause;

(b)

being asleep on duty;

(c)

being insubordinate;

(d)

being late for duty;

(e)

disobeying any lawful order given him or her by his or her superior in rank,
whether verbally or in writing;

(f)

committing any neglect of duty;

(g)

using personal violence or abusive, insulting, or threatening language to any
member of the prison staff;

(h)

losing by neglect, wilfully damaging or permitting injury to any article of
clothing, arms, tools, or supplies or equipment in his or her charge;

(i)

presenting himself or herself for duty dirty or untidy in person or clothing;

(j)

being under the influence of alcohol or drugs while on duty;

(k)

using unnecessary violence on any prisoner;

(l)

associating with any discharged prisoner without the written consent of the
Commissioner;

(m)

communicating to anyone any matter or thing connected with the
administration of the prisons;

(n)

being unduly familiar with, or showing undue favour to, any prisoner;

(o)

committing any breach of the Prison Rules or Standing Orders for the time
being in force;

(p)

committing any act or conducting himself or herself in a manner prejudicial
to prison discipline, or in any manner likely to bring the prison service into
disrepute.

## PART II

### 4.

Charge sheet
(1) A charge against an officer for any offence against discipline shall be entered on a
charge sheet as soon as possible after consideration of the statements on which the charge
is based.
(2) The charge sheet shall specify the provision of these Rules under which the charge
is made, and shall contain, such particulars as will leave the officer in no doubt as to the
precise nature of the allegations on which the charge is based.
(3) A copy of the charge sheet shall be handed to the officer accused as soon as
possible after the charge is made.
(4) The officer accused shall, within twenty-four hours of the receipt by him or her of
the charge sheet, state in writing upon the charge sheet and return it to the
Superintendent—
(a)

whether he or she admits or denies the charge;

(b)

if he or she so desires, his or her reply to the charge; and

(c)

the names of any witnesses whom he or she desires to call at the hearing of
the charge.

(5) The officer accused shall, if he or she so requests, be supplied with copies of all
documents or statements made supporting the charge.
(6) The Superintendent shall, as soon as possible, hear the charge, and shall take or
cause to be taken a full record of the proceedings; and the witnesses shall sign such parts
of the record as comprise evidence given by them.
(7) The officer accused shall be entitled at the hearing to hear the charge against him
or her and to have an opportunity of cross-examining the witnesses called in support of
the charge against him or her and of giving evidence, calling witnesses and making
representations in his or her defence.
(8) If the accused so desires he or she shall be allowed, in proceedings before the
Superintendent, to have a friend assist him or her in the presentation of his or her case, a
prison officer, selected by himself or herself, who shall be a member of the staff of the
prison at which the accused is serving.
(9) The friend of the officer accused may advise him or her on all points and suggest
the questions to be put to witnesses and may, if desired by the officer accused, examine or
cross-examine the witnesses and address the Superintendent on behalf of the officer
accused.
(10) Where the Superintendent determines the case, he or she shall either dismiss the
charge or, if he or she finds it proved, award any of the following punishments, namely—
(a)

reduction in rank or grade;

(b)

suspension, stoppage or deferment of increment;

(c)

forfeiture of good conduct pay or any benefit arising from service;

(d)

fine of not more than fifty dollars;

(e)

admonition;

(f)

reprimand;

(g)

severe reprimand;

(h)

dismissal:

Provided that in the case of contravention of rule 3(a) of these Rules the
punishment may also include forfeiture of pay for the period of absence:
Provided also that in the case of rule 3(h) of these Rules, the punishment may also
include an order to pay the value or part of the value of the property destroyed, lost or
injured, and such payment may be enforced by deductions from such officer’s salary
either at one time or by such instalments as may be ordered.
(11) A disciplinary award made by the Superintendent shall be shown to, and
initialled by, the officer on the charge sheet and the award shall be recorded by the Clerk
in the Officer’s record of service.
(12) Where a charge is dismissed by the Superintendent or dealt with by a caution
without a disciplinary award, no entry shall be made in the officer’s record of service.

### 5.

Appeal
An officer may, within twenty-four hours from the date of the determination of the
case by the Superintendent, appeal in writing to the Commissioner against the
Superintendent’s decision and shall hand this appeal to the Superintendent to be
forwarded to the Commissioner.

### 6.

Duties of the Superintendent
(1) The Superintendent shall forward to the Commissioner with the appeal the record
of the proceedings taken together with all statements taken into consideration during the
proceedings as well as his or her observations on the proceedings so taken. These shall be
initialled by the officer accused and copies of them shall be given to the officer on
request.
(2) The Commissioner may allow the appeal or reduce or confirm or increase such
punishment or make such other order as he or she may see fit.

### 7.

Proceedings as to decision
(1) When the decision of the Commissioner has been received by the Superintendent,
he or she shall supply the officer with a copy of the recommendations of the
Commissioner, as the case may be, conveying the decision.
(2) The decision of the Commissioner shall, subject to the provisions of the next
succeeding paragraph, be entered by the Clerk in the officer’s record of service.
(3) No entry shall be made in record of service in respect of a charge which is
dismissed or for which the Commissioner had administered a caution.

### 8.

Hearings
Any hearing or inquiry held under the provisions of these Rules may be adjourned
from time to time as may appear necessary.

PRISON OFFICERS WELFARE ASSOCIATION RULES
[SRO 3 of 1965 amended by SRO 21 of 1966. Section 42.]

[4th September, 1965.]
1\. Citation
These Rules may be cited as the Prison Officers Welfare Association Rules.
2\. Constitution and Objects
(1) There shall be established for the members of the subordinate staff of the Prison
Service an association to be called “the Prison Officers Welfare Association” (hereinafter
referred to as “the Association”), for the purpose of enabling members of the subordinate
staff to consider and bring to the notice of the Superintendent and the Minister matters
affecting their conditions of service, general welfare and efficiency, other than questions
of discipline or any other matter affecting individuals.
(2) The Association shall be entirely independent of and unassociated with any body
outside the Prison Department.
3\. Membership
The Association shall consist of all members for the time being of the Prison Service
who are below the rank of Assistant Superintendent, and the Association shall act through
Branch Boards and a Central Committee as hereinafter provided.
4\. Branch Boards
(1) There shall be two Branch Boards for the Prison Service, one for male and female
Prison Officers in receipt of salary not exceeding two thousand and sixty-four dollars per
annum to be known as the “Junior Branch Board”, and the other for officers below the
rank of Assistant Superintendent, to be known as the “Senior Branch Board”.
(2) The members of each Branch Board shall be elected as soon as may be after the
coming into operation of these Rules and thereafter in the month of January in each year

and shall hold office until the Branch Board for the succeeding year has been duly
constituted.
(3) The members of each Branch Board shall choose by ballot from among their
number a Chairperson and Secretary.
(4) The Chairperson shall preside at all meetings of the Branch Board and in addition
to his or her original vote shall have a second or casting vote. In the absence of the
Chairperson from a meeting of a Branch Board the members present at the meeting shall
appoint one of their member to preside as Chairperson and such appointed member shall
for that meeting have all the powers of the Chairperson.
(5) Each Branch Board shall hold its first meeting (hereinafter called “the annual
meeting”) as soon as may be after the coming into operation of these Rules and thereafter
in the month of February in each year. Other meetings may be held as often as the
circumstances may require.
(6) The quorum of a Branch Board at any meeting shall be three.
5\. Composition of Branch Boards
Each Branch Board shall consist of four members chosen from the ranks of the Prison
Service for which the Branch Board exists. Such members may be elected without regard
to the specific duties of the members.
6\. Central Committee
(1) There shall be constituted a Central Committee consisting of four members and
the members of each Branch Board shall at the annual meeting of the Branch Board, by
ballot, elect from among their number two members to be members of the Central
Committee.
(2) The Central Committee shall choose from among its members a Chairperson who
shall preside at all meetings of the Central Committee.
(3) The Central Committee shall at its first annual meeting appoint a fit and proper
person (a member of the Prison Service but not a member of the Committee) to be
Secretary for the purpose of attending and taking minutes of its meetings. The Secretary
shall not have the right to vote. The Central Committee may at any time require any
person appointed as Secretary to relinquish his or her appointment and may appoint
another person in his or her stead.
(4) The Central Committee shall hold at least one meeting in each year.
(5) Members of the Central Committee shall hold office until the Central Committee
for the succeeding year has been duly constituted.
7\. Elections to Branch Boards
(1) Every member of that part of the Prison Service for which a Branch Board has
been constituted shall have the right to vote at the election of members for that particular
Branch Board.
(2) All such elections shall be by secret ballot under the supervision of a
commissioned officer of the Prison Service.
(3) At any election under these Rules each voter may cast one vote and no more for
each of any number of candidates not exceeding the number to be elected.
(4) The Supervising Officer shall, if necessary, order a further ballot to decide which
of two or more persons for whom equal numbers of votes are cast at the first ballot are to
be elected and if such further ballot fails to resolve the issue he or she may arrange for it
to be settled by drawing lots.

8\. Functions of Branch Boards and Central Committee
(1) It shall be the duty of a Branch Board to consider and make representations with
regard to all matters affecting the conditions of service, general welfare and efficiency of
the members of the Prison Service for which that particular Branch Board has been
constituted.
(2) It shall be the duty of the Central Committee to consider and make
recommendations with regard to all matters affecting the conditions of service, general
welfare and efficiency of the Prison Service, including any matter which may be referred
to it by the Superintendent.
9\. Vacancies
(1) A Branch Board shall, at a time approved by the Superintendent summon a
meeting of the members of the Prison Service for which the Branch Board is constituted
for the purpose of filling vacancies amongst the members of such Branch Board.
(2) A Branch Board shall at its next convenient meeting and in the manner prescribed
by rule 6(1) above, fill any casual vacancy which may occur amongst the members of that
Branch Board on the Central Committee.
10\. Mode of making representation
Representations may be made by resolution or petition in writing submitted by the
Secretary to the Superintendent of Prisons or through the Superintendent to the Minister.
11\. Facilities for meeting
Every opportunity and facility shall be given to members of the Prison Service to
attend meetings of the Association and every attendance at a meeting shall, as regards
allowance and expenses be deemed to be an occasion of duty.

PRISONS (INDUSTRIES) RULES
[SRO 9 of 1964. Section 51.]

[9th May, 1964.]
1\. Citation
These Rules may be cited as the Prisons (Industries) Rules.
2\. Definitions
In these Rules—
“Minister” means the Minister of the Government charged with responsibility for
the administration of prisons;
“prison industry” means such industry as is or may, from time to time be engaged
in, with the approval of the Superintendent of Prisons, on confirmation by the
Minister, by inmates of the prisons.
3\. Administration by Superintendent
(1) The Superintendent of Prisons shall, at his or her discretion, continue all such
prison industries as exist at the date of the coming into operation of these Rules.
(2) The Superintendent shall not introduce any new industry or revive a dormant one
without the prior written approval of the Minister.
(3) No capital equipment, including tools, shall be purchased by the Superintendent
for use in connection with prison industry without the prior approval of the Minister:

Provided that no such approval will be required for executing repairs to existing
equipment or for the purchase of new equipment not exceeding the sum of ten dollars in
value.
4\. Keeping of accounts
(1) The Superintendent shall be responsible for keeping the accounts of prison
industry, which said accounts shall be kept at the Richmond Hill Prisons.
(2) The Superintendent shall have power to operate a separate current account at a
private commercial bank operating in the State and shall lodge into such account all
monies received on account of prison industry.
(3) The Superintendent shall, with the approval of the Permanent Secretary (Finance),
operate a savings account to be named the “Prisons Industry Savings Account”. In like
manner and with like authority the Superintendent may open a “Prisons Industries
Equipment Reserve Account” and shall place such sums therein as the Permanent
Secretary (Finance) may approve.
5\. Superintendent may transfer monies
(1) The Superintendent shall have power to transfer monies from the Current Account
to the Savings Account and to make proper payments and disbursements from either
account:
Provided that he or she shall not retain an amount in excess of five thousand dollars
in the Savings Account at any one time without the consent of the Permanent Secretary
(Finance).
(2) He or she shall not invest or cause to be invested except as is herein provided any
monies derived from prison industry except with the prior approval of the Permanent
Secretary (Finance).
6\. Returns
(1) The Superintendent shall prepare quarterly accounts of all prison industry
commencing on the thirty-first day of July, 1964, and thereafter, and shall forward copies
of such accounts to the Minister.
(2) The Superintendent shall prepare Annual Accounts for the period ending twelve
months from the date of coming into operation of these Rules, and annually thereafter.
Copies of such accounts shall be forwarded to the Minister.
(3) Such accounts shall be submitted to the Senior Auditor within thirty days of the
end of the period to which it relates:
Provided that the Minister may, in his or her discretion, extend the time for
submission. The audited accounts shall be included by the Superintendent in the Annual
Prisons Report to be laid before the House of Representatives.
7\. How surplus accounted for
(1) After the annual account has been verified by the Auditor, any surplus of revenue
over expenditure appearing therein shall be paid over without delay to the AccountantGeneral for the general uses of the State:
Provided that the Minister may allow a sum not exceeding five hundred dollars to
be carried forward.
(2) Where any surplus is paid into the Treasury as required by these Rules, it shall be
distributed in manner following, that is to say—
(a)

seventy per cent to the Consolidated Fund;

(b)

fifteen per cent to the Discharged Prisoners Welfare Fund;

(c)

ten per cent to the Prisoners Amenities Fund; and

(d)

five per cent to the Prison Officers Fund.

8\. Provision in Prison Estimates
Provision shall be made annually in the Prison Estimates for the distribution of the
surplus as set out in the preceding rule, but no payment may be made to any fund unless
in compliance with rules for such control as in the opinion of the Cabinet are adequate for
its protection and furtherance of its objectives.

Prisons (Prison School) Regulations
[Section 51.]

---

### 1.

Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Prisons (Prison School) Regulations.

### 2.

The prison school shall be under the direct control of the Chief Warder, and shall be
kept from 12 noon until 2 p.m. every day when the number of prisoners eligible for
instruction shall exceed twelve, otherwise the school shall be kept every alternate day. No
instruction shall be given on Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays.

### 3.

Instruction shall be given in reading, writing, and arithmetic to such convicts under
sentence from the High Court (provided such sentence is not less than three months) and
such prisoners under sentence of three months and upwards from the Magisterial Courts,
as shall be selected by the Superintendent for that purpose every school day.

### 4.

Not less than four and not more than eight prisoners shall be sent to school on any
school day.

### 5.

In the selection of prisoners to attend the prison school every precaution shall be taken
to avoid any appearance of favouritism, and the Superintendent shall take care that every
convict under sentence from the High Court of three months and upwards shall, unless
exempted by himself or herself or the medical officer, attend school and receive
instruction at least once a week.

### 6.

The books to be used in the prison school and the course of instruction to be followed
shall be those prescribed by the Ministry of Education for the Primary Schools of the
State:
Provided that the Superintendent with the authority of the Minister may introduce
other books and make any changes in the course of instruction which may be found
necessary or desirable.

### 7.

The Chief Warder as Schoolmaster shall record his or her proceedings daily in the
school journal in manner directed by the Prison Rules. He or she shall also keep a school
register, in form prescribed by the Superintendent, of all prisoners sent to him or her for
instruction, and he or she shall enter therein daily the progress or otherwise made by each
such prisoner under his or her tuition and a statement of his or her conduct at school.

### 8.

Instruction shall not be given to any prisoner over the age of forty years or to any
prisoner who being eligible can pass the fourth standard.

PRISONS (REHABILITATION OF DISCHARGED PRISONERS) RULES
[Section 51.]

[12th July, 1969.]
1\. Citation
These Rules may be cited as the Prisons (Rehabilitation of Discharged Prisoners)
Rules.
2\. Establishment of Committee
There is hereby established and constituted a committee to be called the Discharged
Prisoners Aid Committee (hereinafter referred to as “the Committee”).
3\. Membership of Committee
(1) The Committee shall consist of seven members all of whom shall be appointed by
the Minister for a period of one year from the date of appointment.
(2) The Minister shall appoint a Chairperson and a Secretary from among the
members of the Committee.
(3) Notwithstanding anything contained in paragraph (1) of this Rule, a member of
the Committee shall ipso facto cease to be a member thereof if he or she is absent from
three consecutive meetings of the Committee without leave from the Committee, or if he
or she is absent from Grenada for more than two months without such leave.

4\. Meetings and procedure
(1) The Committee shall meet at least once in every month, and may meet as often as
may be required for the transaction of business.
(2) Subject to the provisions of these Rules, the Committee shall have the power to
regulate its own proceedings.
(3) Any four members of the Committee, including the Chairperson shall form a
quorum.
(4) The Chairperson shall preside at all meetings of the Committee:
Provided that in the event of his or her being unable to attend a meeting the
members of the Committee present and constituting a quorum shall elect a chairperson to
preside at that meeting.
(5) In the event of an equality of votes on any question at a meeting, the chairperson
of the meeting shall have a second or casting vote in respect of that question.
(6) Decisions of the Committee may be signified under the hand of the chairperson or
the Secretary of the Committee.
(7) The validity of any proceeding of the Committee shall not be affected by any
vacancy in the membership thereof or by any defect in the appointment of a member
thereof.
5\. Duties of Committee
The Committee shall be responsible generally for the rehabilitation of discharged
prisoners for such period, and in such manner, as to the Committee seems fit, and for
administering the funds provided for assisting prisoners on discharge from prison.
6\. Funds of Committee
The funds of the Committee shall consist of—
(a)

profits derived from the Prisons Industries Account;

(b)

such sums of money as may be provided for the purpose by Parliament from
time to time; and

(c)

such sums of money as may be provided for the purpose from any other
source.

7\. Accounts and audit
(1) The Committee shall keep proper books of accounts to the satisfaction of the
Accountant-General.
(2) Such books of accounts and all monies, documents and things pertaining to the
Committee, in the custody or under the control of the Committee or any member thereof
shall, at all times, be subject to audit and inspection by the Director of Audit or the
Government chief audit officer, however called, as if the said books of accounts, monies,
documents and things were in the custody or under the control of the public officer in his
or her official capacity.
8\. Annual report
The Committee shall submit an annual report to the Superintendent of Prisons for
transmission to the Minister on the activities of the Committee during the preceding year.
The Committee may include in such report any advice and recommendations as the
Committee may consider appropriate.

PRISONS RULES
[SRO 14 of 1980 amended by SRO 12 of 2006. Section 51.]

[16th January, 1981.]

---

### 1.

Citation
These Rules may be cited as the Prisons Rules.

## PART I

Board of Review

### 2.

Board of Review
A Board of Review shall be appointed by the Minister.

### 3.

Review of sentences
The Board, shall at the prescribed intervals, or after such lesser intervals as
circumstances require, review the sentences of all long term prisoners including prisoners

detained during Her Majesty’s pleasure in the under-mentioned manner and in each case
tender appropriate advice to the Minister on the exercise of the prerogative of mercy—
(a)

Review of Fixed Sentences.—In the case of adults over twenty years of age,
the first review shall take place after 4 years of a sentence exceeding that
period has been served, and thereafter a further review of two-yearly
intervals;

(b)

Review of Intermediate Sentences.—In the case of a life sentence or
detention during Her Majesty’s pleasure, the first review shall take place
after the initial twelve months have been served. Thereafter a further review
shall take place after four years from the date of sentence, and then in fouryearly intervals except where a review has indicated that the case should
come up again after less than the usual period;

(c)

Review of Sentences of Young Persons.—In the case of young persons under
twenty years of age at the time of their offences, such young persons shall
have their cases reviewed annually whether their sentences are indeterminate
or for fixed terms.

### 4.

Long sentence convicts
The Superintendent shall bring to the notice of the Board all long sentence convicts
(both men and women) at the end of the fourth year of imprisonment and every fourth
year thereafter, describing the prisoner’s mental and bodily conditions with particular
reference to the effect of his or her imprisonment upon his or her health, his or her
demeanour and his or her attitude towards the offence and towards crime generally and
upon his or her conduct and industry and on any other points which might be of
assistance to the Board in considering his or her case.

### 5.

Assisting on discharge
(1) There shall be a fund provided in the estimates and from profits from the Prison
Industries Account, from which prisoners are assisted on discharge according to their
need and prospects of rehabilitation. It shall be administered by a Discharge Prisoners
Aid Committee consisting of seven persons appointed by the Minister annually for the
purpose.
(2) The Minister shall appoint a Chairperson and a Secretary from among the
members of the Discharged Prisoners Aid Committee.

## PART II

Staff

### 6.

Responsibility of Commissioner
The Commissioner shall strictly conform to all the laws and rules relating to the
Prisons and shall be responsible for the due observance of them by all members of the
staff.

### 7.

Supervision
The Commissioner shall observe the conduct of all officers and enforce on each of
them the due execution of his or her duties.

### 8.

Interview of prisoners

The Commissioner shall interview or cause to be interviewed all prisoners on
reception and prior to their discharge.

### 9.

Inspection of prisons
The Commissioner shall inspect the Prisons and all prisoners as often as possible
paying special attention to gangs working outside the Prisons in order to ensure that they
are industrious and that all orders respecting their safe custody and the application of
labour are duly enforced. He or she shall state in his or her Journal when such inspections
are made. He or she shall at intervals make surprise visits to the Prisons at night and
record in his or her Journal each such visit and the result thereof.

### 10.

Reports on locking-up
The Commissioner shall require a report to be made to him or her at the hour of
locking-up of the prisoners for the night, as to whether or not everything is correct.

### 11.

Medical attention
The Commissioner shall call the attention of the Medical Officer to any prisoner
whose state of mind or body appears to require attention, and shall, so far as practicable,
carry into effect any written recommendation made by the Medical Officer.

### 12.

Very ill prisoners
When a prisoner, is in the opinion of the Medical Officer, dangerously ill, the
Commissioner shall whenever practicable forthwith inform the relatives of such prisoner.

### 13.

Report on insanity
The Commissioner shall without delay report to the Minister any case of insanity or
apparent insanity occurring among the prisoners, or any case in which the Medical
Officer is of the opinion that the mental state of any prisoner is becoming impaired or
enfeebled by continued imprisonment, or that the life of any prisoner may be endangered
by further imprisonment, or that any sick prisoner may not survive his or her sentence or
is totally and permanently unfit for prison discipline.

### 14.

Death of prisoner
The Commissioner shall upon the death of a prisoner give immediate notice thereof to
the Coroner and other authorities as prescribed by the Coroner’s Act, Chapter 69, or other
law for the time being applicable. He or she shall also, when practicable, notify the
nearest relative of the deceased.

### 15.

Prohibition on conversations, etc.
The Commissioner shall not allow any person to view any prisoner or hold
conversation with a prisoner except in accordance with these Rules or other relevant
provisions.

### 16.

Prohibition on sketches, etc.
The Commissioner shall ensure that no person authorised to view the Prison makes
any sketch or takes any photograph thereof.

### 17.

Removal for improper conduct

The Commissioner shall have the power to remove from the Prison any person whose
conduct is improper or order his or her removal therefrom by any officer.

### 18.

Notice on prohibited articles
The Commissioner shall cause a notice to be affixed at the entrance to the Prison
cautioning persons against bringing prohibited articles, a list of which will be given in the
notice. Should he or she suspect any person of bringing any prohibited article to, or of
carrying out any property belonging to the Government from the Prison, he or she may
order such person to be searched.

### 19.

Abstract of Rules
The Commissioner shall cause an abstract of such of these Rules as relate to the
treatment, privileges, earnings, and remission of sentence of prisoners to be placed in a
conspicuous part of the Prison and he or she shall also ensure that those Rules are
explained to prisoners on admission.

### 20.

Keeping records
The Commissioner shall keep or cause to be kept all records, registers, journals,
accounts and other books necessary for the efficient administration of the Prison
Department.

### 21.

Checks on stores, etc.
The Commissioner shall arrange for a regular check of all stores, inventories, records,
work orders and accounts by officers of the Department.

### 22.

Information for Minister on visit
Whenever visits are made to the Prison by the Minister the Commissioner shall supply
him or her with full information regarding the Prison and shall give free access to all parts
of the Prison and to the prisoners therein.

### 23.

Fire precautions
The Commissioner shall see that precautions against fire are adopted, and that
appliances for the extinction of fire are at all times kept in good order and ready for use.
He or she shall take care that instructions are given as to the steps to be taken in case of
fire, and the prison officers concerned are acquainted with their duties on such occasions.

### 24.

Emergency cases
In cases of emergency not provided for in these Rules the Commissioner shall be
guided in taking action by the Rules and Standing Orders where the same are clearly not
inconsistent with any local law.

### 25.

Communication with Minister
The Commissioner shall communicate directly with the Minister on any matter
connected with the Prison Department.

### 26.

Delegation of duties during absence
The Commissioner may deputise any officer to act in his or her stead during his or her
absence and that officer shall in the Commissioner’s absence perform all the duties of the
Commissioner.

### 27.

Other delegation
The Commissioner shall, from time to time, delegate to the Superintendent, and
Assistant Superintendent such duties as he or she may think fit.

### 28.

Free medical attendance
The Commissioner shall be entitled to free medical attendance and medicines.

### 29.

Leave
The Commissioner shall be entitled to leave in accordance with the Civil Service
Regulations for the time being in force.

### 30.

Prohibition on sleeping
The Commissioner shall not allow any person unconnected with the Prisons to sleep
therein.

### 31.

Precautions regarding escape
The Commissioner shall take every precaution to guard against the escape of any
prisoner, and shall cause a daily examination to be made of all cells, bars, bolts, locks,
etc. In the event of an escape being effected, he or she shall immediately report it to the
Police.

### 32.

Inquiry into escape
The Commissioner shall inquire into the escape of any prisoner and the conduct of
prison officers in charge at the time.

### 33.

Corporal punishment
The Commissioner shall attend all corporal punishment and shall enter in his or her
Journal any orders which the Prison Medical Officer may have given on such occasion.
He or she shall make all necessary arrangements for the carrying out of all executions and
shall be present thereat.

### 34.

Assistance re-disturbance, etc.
In the event of any disturbance or insubordination among the prisoners which the
Superintendent and other prison officers are unable to subdue, the Commissioner shall
make immediate demand on the Police for assistance and shall also report the matter to
the Minister.

### 35.

Conduct of prison officers
The Superintendent shall observe the conduct of all prison officers and enforce on
each of them the due execution of his or her duties.

### 36.

Inspection
The Superintendent shall inspect the Prisons and all prisoners as often as possible
paying special attention to gangs working outside the Prison in order to ensure that they
are industrious and that all orders respecting their safe custody and the application of
labour are duly enforced. He or she shall state in his or her Journal when such inspections
are made. He or she shall at intervals make surprise visits to the Prison at night, and
record in his or her Journal each such visit and the result thereof.

### 37.

Disciplining offences
The Superintendent shall arrange that offences against Prison discipline are promptly
dealt with and shall, as soon as possible, investigate any complaints and attend to any
application from a prisoner and shall ensure that any prisoner desiring to see the
Commissioner shall have an opportunity of doing so.

### 38.

Cleanliness
The Superintendent shall enforce a high degree of cleanliness among the staff, and the
prisoners, and in every part of the Prisons.

### 39.

Free medical attendance
The Superintendent shall be entitled to free medical attendance and medicines.

### 40.

Leave
The Superintendent shall be entitled to leave in accordance with the Civil Service
Regulations for the time being in force.

### 41.

Corporal punishment
The Superintendent shall take care that no prisoner is subjected to any corporal,
cellular, or dietary punishment which the Prison Medical Officer has not certified that he
or she is capable of undergoing.

### 42.

Precautions regarding escape
The Superintendent shall take every precaution to guard against the escape of any
prisoner, and shall cause a daily examination to be made of all cells, bars, bolts, locks,
etc. In the event of an escape being effected, he or she shall immediately report it to the
Commissioner and the Police.

### 43.

Reading letters
The Superintendent shall read every letter addressed to, or written by a prisoner, and
in every instance where he or she may deem it necessary, to withhold any such letter, he
or she shall record the fact in his or her Journal:
Provided that the Superintendent may delegate the duty of reading any such letter to
any senior officer.

### 44.

Training of officers
The Superintendent shall be responsible for the training of subordinate officers of the
Prison and shall be assisted in these duties by the Chief Officer.

### 45.

Daily inspection
The Superintendent shall daily inspect every part of the Prisons and shall see every
prisoner once at least in every twenty-four hours, special attention being paid to those
undergoing punishment and those in the infirmary.

### 46.

Inspection of working gangs
The Superintendent shall as frequently as possible inspect gangs working outside the
Prison in order to ensure that discipline is being maintained and that a full day’s work is
being done.

### 47.

Surprise night visits
The Superintendent shall, at least once a week pay a surprise visit to the Prison at
night.

### 48.

Co-operation with teachers
The Superintendent shall co-operate with and facilitate the work of the teachers.

### 49.

Presence at corporal punishment
The Superintendent shall be present at all corporal punishment inflicted at the Prison
and shall promptly carry into effect any order or instruction of the Commissioner in
connection therewith.

### 50.

Regulation of duties
The Superintendent shall regulate the duties and hours of the staff in consultation with
the Chief Officer of the Prisons.

### 51.

Permission for absence
The Superintendent shall not be absent from the Prison during the prescribed hours of
attendance without the permission of the Commissioner.

### 52.

Enforcement of rules
The Superintendent shall carefully observe and carry out all Prison Rules, and orders
and instructions of the Commissioner and shall ensure that all concerned do the same.

### 53.

Examination of stores, etc.
The Assistant Superintendent shall carefully examine all requisitions for stores,
materials and tools before approving orders therefor so as to ensure that rigid economy is
practised.

### 54.

Supervision
The Assistant Superintendent shall observe the conduct of all officers and enforce on
each of them the due execution of his or her duties.

### 55.

Interviews
The Assistant Superintendent shall interview or cause to be interviewed all prisoners
on reception and prior to their discharge.

### 56.

Inspection
The Assistant Superintendent shall inspect the Prison and all prisoners as often as
possible paying special attention to gangs working outside the Prison in order to ensure
that they are industrious and that all orders respecting their safe custody and the
application of labour are duly enforced. He or she shall state in his or her Journal when
such inspections are made. He or she shall at intervals make surprise visits to the Prison at
night, and record in his or her Journal each such visits and the result thereof.

### 57.

Disciplining offences
The Assistant Superintendent shall arrange that offence against Prison discipline are
promptly dealt with and shall, as soon as possible, investigate any complaints and attend

to any application from a prisoner and shall ensure that any prisoner desiring to see the
Commissioner shall have an opportunity of doing so.

### 58.

Free medical attendance
The Assistant Superintendent shall be entitled to free medical attendance and
medicines.

### 59.

Leave
The Assistant Superintendent shall be entitled to leave in accordance with the Civil
Service Regulations for the time being in force.

### 60.

Corporal punishment
The Assistant Superintendent shall take care that no prisoner is subjected to any
corporal, cellular, or dietary punishment which the Prison Medical Officer has not
certified that he or she is capable of undergoing.

### 61.

Precaution against escape
The Assistant Superintendent shall take every precaution to guard against the escape
of any prisoner, and shall cause a daily examination to be made of all cells, bars, bolts,
locks, etc. In the event of an escape being effected, he or she shall immediately report it
to the Commissioner and the Police.

### 62.

Reading letters
The Assistant Superintendent shall read every letter addressed to, or written by, a
prisoner, and in every instance where he or she may deem it necessary, to withhold any
such letter, he or she shall record the fact in his or her Journal; provided that the
Superintendent may delegate the duty of reading any such letter to any senior prison
officer.

### 63.

Responsibility for stores, etc.
The Assistant Superintendent shall be responsible for all stores, clothing, etc., and for
all deficiencies in the same. He or she shall direct the issue of the proper supplies and
take care that no article of any description is issued until it is properly marked with the
Prison marks. He or she shall carefully study the requirements of the Prison for the
purpose of promoting economy and preventing waste.

### 64.

Training of officers
The Assistant Superintendent shall assist the Superintendent in the training of
subordinate officers of the Prison and shall be assisted in these duties by the Chief
Officer.

### 65.

Warrants of commitment
The Assistant Superintendent shall carefully check all warrants of commitment and
see that the dates of discharge of prisoners are accurately entered in the Discharge
Journals.

### 66.

Daily inspections

The Assistant Superintendent shall daily inspect every part of the Prison and shall see
every prisoner once at least in every twenty-four hours, special attention being paid to
those undergoing punishment and those in the infirmary.

### 67.

Inspection of gangs
The Assistant Superintendent shall as frequently as possible inspect gangs working
outside the Prison in order to ensure that discipline is being maintained and that a full
day’s work is being done.

### 68.

Compliance with medical instructions
The Assistant Superintendent shall take care that all written instructions of the
Medical Officer are carried out. Should these instructions interfere with the daily routine,
good order and discipline of the Prison, he or she shall at once confer with the Medical
Officer and report the matter to the Commissioner.

### 69.

Quantity of diets
The Assistant Superintendent shall frequently inspect the quality and quantity of the
diets and make sure that every prisoner receives the diet to which he or she is entitled.

### 70.

Presence at corporal punishment
The Assistant Superintendent shall be present at all corporal punishment inflicted at
the Prison and shall promptly carry into effect any order or instruction of the
Commissioner in connection therewith.

### 71.

Records
The Assistant Superintendent shall take care that all records, Journals, and other
documents are kept up to date and are carefully preserved.

### 72.

Permission for absence
The Assistant Superintendent shall not be absent from the Prison during the prescribed
hours of attendance without the permission of the Commissioner.

### 73.

Compliance with rules
The Assistant Superintendent shall carefully observe and carry out all Prison Rules,
and orders and instructions of the Commissioner, and shall ensure that all concerned do
the same.

### 74.

Sick bay
A suitable part of the Prison shall be equipped and furnished in a manner appropriate
for the medical care and treatment of sick prisoners and staffed by properly trained prison
officers.

### 75.

Medical care of prisoners
The Prison Medical Officer shall have the general care of the health of the prisoners
and shall attend all sick prisoners.

### 76.

Visit of medical officer

(1) The Medical Officer shall, subject to the provisions of paragraph (2), of this Rule
visit the prison every day and shall see every prisoner—
(a)

who is sick;

(b)

who is undergoing dietary punishment;

(c)

who is under sentence of death; or

(d)

to whom his or her attention is specially directed,

he or she shall also visit the Prison at any other time, if required to do so in any
emergency by the Commissioner.
(2) The Medical Officer may use his or her discretion as to his or her attendance at the
prison on Sundays, Christmas Day, Good Friday, Easter Sunday, or any public holiday.
He or she shall, however, so arrange that he or she can be called in the event of any
emergency.

### 77.

Free medical treatment
The Medical Officer shall attend free of charge to all prison officers who apply for
treatment at the prison, or whom it may be desirable for special reasons that he or she
should visit at their homes; provided that such officers reside within the city of St.
George’s.

### 78.

Medical examination on admission
The Medical Officer shall examine every prisoner on admission recording in his or her
Journal and in the prisoner’s record the state of health and the class of labour from among
the categories prescribed in Standing Orders, which each prisoner is capable of
performing.

### 79.

Medical examination before discharge
The Medical Officer shall examine every prisoner before discharge or removal to
another institution, and in each case of removal a certificate in the Penal Record shall be
signed by the Medical Officer. In the case of discharge the Medical Officer shall make
such special remarks therein as he or she thinks necessary.

### 80.

Immediate attendance
The Medical Officer shall attend as soon as possible on receiving information of the
illness of a prisoner.

### 81.

Assistance for operation
(1) The Medical Officer may at his or her discretion call into consultation any other
Government Medical Officer, and shall do so, if possible, before a serious operation is
performed upon a prisoner, unless in his or her opinion, it is essential for the operation to
be performed immediately.
(2) The Medical Officer shall keep a record of the occasions on which, in accordance
with this Rule, he or she consults another Government Medical Officer and of the
circumstances in which serious operations are performed without consultations.

### 82.

Report on serious cases
(1) The Medical Officer shall report to the Commissioner any matter which appears to
him or her to require the consideration of the Minister on Medical grounds, and the
Commissioner shall send such report to the Minister.

(2) The Medical Officer shall, when requested by the Commissioner, examine any
prisoner and shall forward a report in writing on such prisoner. Where the Commissioner
requests that the examination be carried out on a particular day the Medical Officer shall
examine the prisoner on that day.

### 83.

Effect of continuous imprisonment
Whenever the Medical Officer has reason to believe that a prisoner’s mental or
physical health is likely to be injuriously affected by continued imprisonment or that the
life of a prisoner will be endangered by imprisonment, or that a sick prisoner will not
survive his or her sentence, or is totally and permanently unfit for imprisonment, he or
she shall, without delay, report his or her opinion and the grounds thereof in writing to the
Commissioner who shall forward the relevant documents to the Minister.

### 84.

Report on diet, etc.
The Medical Officer may, if he or she thinks fit, report in writing to the Commissioner
on any prisoner and, where he or she considers it necessary on medical grounds to do so,
may make recommendations for the alteration of the diet or treatment of the prisoner, or
for the supply to him or her of additional clothing, bedding or other articles. The
Commissioner shall carry such recommendations into effect as far as is practicable.

### 85.

Reasons for special diet
Where the Medical Officer prescribes any diet or treatment not provided for in these
Rules, he or she shall report to the Commissioner in writing the reasons for such special
diet or treatment.

### 86.

Suicidal tendencies
The Medical Officer shall draw the attention of the Commissioner to any prisoner
whom he or she suspects of suicidal or homicidal tendencies in order that special
observation may be kept on such a prisoner, and the Commissioner shall without delay
direct that such prisoner be kept under observation in accordance with any directions of
the Medical Officer.

### 87.

Special observations
The Medical Officer shall keep under special observation every prisoner whose mental
condition appears to require it, and shall take such steps as may be necessary for his or
her segregation.

### 88.

Serious illness
The Medical Officer shall give notice to the Commissioner when any prisoner appears
to him or her to be seriously ill. The Commissioner shall forthwith inform the Chaplain or
Priest of the religious denomination to which the prisoner belongs and, also, the nearest
relative of the prisoner.

### 89.

Examination before corporal punishment
The Medical Officer shall examine every prisoner about to undergo corporal
punishment and shall be present during the administration of such punishment and he or
she may at any time order that such punishment shall not be inflicted or shall be stopped
if he or she is of the opinion that the infliction or further infliction of that punishment as
the case may be is likely to endanger the health of such prisoner.

### 90.

Medical care of capital offenders
The Medical Officer shall cause to be kept under observation a prisoner charged with
a capital offence and he or she shall keep a written record of the physical and mental
condition of that prisoner.

### 91.

Report on mental condition
The Medical Officer shall submit a report to the Attorney-General on the mental
condition of that prisoner at least three days before the date of the trial. If, from
symptoms observed while under observation in the prison, the Medical Officer is of the
opinion that the prisoner is insane, or if the Medical Officer is of the opinion that there is
a distinct history of periodical attacks of insanity followed by intervals of mental
clearness and that the prisoner has been enjoying a lucid interval while under observation
in the prison, the report shall embody such opinion.

### 92.

Presence of medical officer at executions
The Medical Officer shall be present at all executions and shall issue a certificate in
respect thereof in the form set out in the First Schedule to these Rules.

### 93.

Examination of defaulters
The Medical Officer shall examine all defaulters and record whether they are fit to
receive punishment, close confinement or corporal punishment, as the case may be.

### 94.

Books to be kept
The Medical Officer shall ensure that the following books are kept—
(a)

an Officer’s Medical Journal;

(b)

a Prisoner’s Medical Journal;

(c)

a Medical Minute Book;

(d)

a Hospital Case Book; and

(e)

a Statistical Book for compiling returns for the annual report.

### 95.

Inspection of food
The Medical Officer shall frequently inspect both the cooked and uncooked food of
the prisoners and shall report thereon to the Commissioner whenever he or she thinks fit.

### 96.

Signature for diets
The Medical Officer shall sign—
(a)

every order relating to the diet of a prisoner under treatment;

(b)

every order requiring any variation in the diet, clothing or treatment of a
prisoner;

(c)

every requisition for any article required in his or her department.

### 97.

Record of changes in diet, etc.
The Medical Officer shall enter on the medical history sheets of all prisoners under his
or her care any change in the diet or treatment.

### 98.

Infectious diseases

(1) The Medical Officer shall report all infectious diseases to—
(a)

the Chief Medical Officer, and

(b)

the Commissioner.

(2) The report shall state what precautions have been taken to prevent the spread of
the disease and shall give an opinion as to the source of the disease with any
recommendations that may be necessary.

### 99.

Compliance with Rules
The Medical Officer shall conform to these Rules, and shall, in the performance of his
or her duties, assist the Commissioner in the maintenance of discipline and the safe
custody of the prisoners.

### 100.

Annual report
The Medical Officer shall at the end of each year forward to the Commissioner his or
her annual report containing such statistics and other particulars as may be required.

### 101.

Advice on hygiene
The Medical Officer shall oversee and advise the Commissioner upon the hygiene of
the Prison and the prisoners, including arrangements for cleanliness, sanitation, lighting
and ventilation.

### 102.

Inspections
The Medical Officer shall inspect every part of the Prison at least once every three
months and report in writing on the sanitary arrangements to the Commissioner who shall
take what action he or she considers necessary, and shall forward the report with his or
her comments to the Minister.

### 103.

Use of soap
Arrangements shall be made for every prisoner to wash with soap daily at all proper
times and for men (unless excused or prohibited on medical grounds) to shave frequently
and have their hair cut as required.

### 104.

Care of hair
(1) The hair of a male prisoner may be cut as short as is necessary for good
appearance and the maintenance of health and cleanliness.
(2) The hair of a female prisoner shall not be cut without her consent, except by the
direction of the Medical Officer for the eradication of vermin, dirt or disease.

### 105.

Provision of toilet articles
Every prisoner shall be provided on admission with such toilet articles as are
necessary for cleansing his or her teeth and person and arrangements shall be made for
the replacement of these articles when necessary.

### 106.

Dentures, etc.
Artificial teeth and spectacles shall only be provided for any prisoner if payment is
made therefor on his or her behalf and if the Medical Officer certified them to be
necessary in the interest of the prisoner’s health or, in the case of spectacles, to enable
him or her to work; but that where the Commissioner is satisfied that the prisoner cannot

have the cost or some part thereof paid for on his or her behalf such artificial teeth or
spectacles may be supplied from such funds as may be available to meet their cost or the
outstanding part thereof.

### 107.

Duties of Chief Officer
The Chief Officer shall detail the duties of the staff and arrange that all work parties
are suitably supplied with labour.

### 108.

Visits by Chief Officer
The Chief Officer shall visit all work parties within the prison each day and shall visit
other work parties at such times as the Commissioner may require. He or she shall see
that the prisoners have sufficient work and are kept steadily at labour, and shall ensure
that every prisoner is assigned to a work party as soon as possible, and not more than
forty-eight hours after his or her reception.

### 109.

Keys and arms, etc.
The Chief Officer shall ensure that all keys and arms and ammunition are kept in the
proper place and in good condition.

### 110.

Instruction on use of firearms
The Chief Officer shall ensure that all members of the staff are properly instructed in
their duties and in the use of firearms.

### 111.

Inspection by Chief Officer
The Chief Officer shall frequently inspect every part of the Prison and see that the
cells are kept clean and in order and that all means of security are effective.

### 112.

Supervision of staff, etc.
The Chief Officer shall carefully observe the behaviour and general demeanour of the
staff and of all prisoners and shall report on his or her observations to the Superintendent
or Assistant Superintendent.

### 113.

Misconduct
Any neglect or misconduct observed by him or her shall be reported immediately.

### 114.

Lateness for duty
The Chief Officer shall report to the Superintendent or Assistant Superintendent any
prison officer reporting late for duty or the absence from duty without leave of any
member of the staff.

### 115.

Checking of muster
The Chief Officer or Assistant Chief Officer shall attend the checking of the muster at
all times and shall attend all duty mounting and dismounting parades of the staff.

### 116.

Safety, etc., of prisoners
The Chief Officer shall inform the Superintendent of anything likely to affect the
safety, security or health of the prisoners or of any matter in relation to the staff.

### 117.

Night visits
The Chief Officer shall visit the Prison and all posts at an uncertain hour of the night
not less than five times per month.

### 118.

Search of cells
The Chief Officer shall ensure that every prisoner and his or her cell are specially
searched once every fortnight at uncertain intervals and shall ensure that no prisoner has
prior knowledge of such search.

### 119.

Delegation of duties
The Assistant Superintendent may delegate to the Chief Officer such duties as the
Commissioner may from time to time authorise to be delegated.

### 120.

Exemplary conduct
The Chief Officer shall at all times set an example to his or her subordinates by his or
her conscientious performance of his or her duties.

### 121.

Duties of Matron
The Matron shall be under the immediate direction of the Commissioner. She shall be
entrusted with the care, safe custody and superintendence of the women prisoners, and the
keys of the Women’s Prison shall be kept by her.

### 122.

Supervision
The Matron shall exercise a close and constant personal supervision over the whole of
the Female Prison. She shall visit and inspect daily all parts of the Prison where female
prisoners are employed or confined, and shall give special attention to any female
prisoner who is sick or under restraint or in cellular confinement. Neither the Matron nor
any female prison officer shall quit the Prison during duty hours without the permission
of the Commissioner.

### 123.

Acquaintance with Rules
The Matron shall make herself fully acquainted with the Prison Rules and shall
enforce them so far as they apply to the Women prisoners and any officers under her.

### 124.

Permission for absence
The Matron shall not absent herself from the Prison while on duty at any time without
leave of the Commissioner, and during her absence she shall give charge to such other
woman officer as the Commissioner may direct. Such officer shall have all the powers
and perform the duties of the Matron.

### 125.

Night visits
The Matron shall make such visit or inspection during the night as the Commissioner
may direct.

### 126.

Attendance on Commissioner, etc.
The Matron, shall, unless unavoidably prevented attend the Commissioner and the
Prison Medical Officer in their visits to the Women’s Prison and she shall especially take
care that no male prison officer, visitor or prisoner enters the Women’s Prison unless

accompanied by herself or some other woman prison officer, with the approval of the
Commissioner.

### 127.

Responsibilities of Matron
The Matron shall be directly responsible in the Women’s Prison for the distribution of
the prisoner’s meals according to the prescribed scale of diet, and shall report to the
Assistant Superintendent whether the food is properly cooked and of sufficient quantity.

### 128.

Instructions in vocational training
The Matron shall give or cause to give instructions to women prisoners in sewing,
washing, or other approved labour, and shall be directly responsible under the
Commissioner for the enforcement of the proper tasks.

### 129.

Report on misconduct
The Matron shall report to the Superintendent any misconduct or breach of the Rules
by a woman prisoner and also touching anything in the discipline and condition of the
Women’s Prison.

### 130.

Rules
The Matron and every other woman prison officer shall be subject to the general rules
for subordinate officers so far as they apply to them.

### 131.

Stores, etc.
The Steward shall receive, examine and take particular account of all stores connected
with the victualling and clothing of the prisoners and all other stores committed to his or
her care, and shall be responsible for accounting therefor.

### 132.

Defects in quality
The Steward shall report immediately to the Assistant Superintendent any defect in
quality or any deficiency or irregularity in delivery of any of the prison stores and take his
or her direction thereon.

### 133.

Identity marks
The Steward shall ensure that no article that can be marked is issued from the store
without being marked in such a manner as will render it readily identifiable as
Government property.

### 134.

Compliance with Stores Regulations
The Steward shall observe and comply with the Stores Regulations made by the
Permanent Secretary (Finance) and any regulations amending or replacing the same.

### 135.

Requisition
Every prison officer shall forward through the Assistant Superintendent a requisition
for all articles and stores required in his or her department.

### 136.

Books, etc.
The Steward shall at all times have his or her books and supporting vouchers available
for inspection by the Commissioner, or anyone delegated by the Commissioner.

### 137.

Inventories
(1) The Steward shall keep correct inventories of all clothing, furniture, tools and
utensils in the Prison.
(2) Every inventory shall be signed by the person under whose immediate custody
such articles are kept.
(3) No alteration shall be made to such inventory without the written direction of the
Commissioner.

### 138.

Unnecessary expenditure
The Steward shall bring to the attention of the Assistant Superintendent any apparent
waste and unnecessary or improvident expenditure.

### 139.

Replacement of stores, etc.
(1) The Steward shall ensure that all unexpendable stores which have become
unserviceable shall be handed into the store in exchange for replacements.
(2) He or she shall ensure that unserviceable stores are returned to the store and kept
until a Board of Survey has recommended their disposal.

### 140.

Unserviceable articles
The Steward shall report to the Assistant Superintendent any case in which in his or
her opinion any articles have become unserviceable otherwise than through wear and tear.

### 141.

Produce from Prison farm
(1) The Steward shall receive all produce from the Prison Farm and shall check and
account for the quantity and the subsequent issue of such produce. He or she shall retain
all supporting vouchers and receipts.
(2) He or she shall ensure that all sales of produce are accounted for and that no
person receives more than he or she is charged for and that the accounts for such sales are
rendered to the individuals and departments concerned.

### 142.

Manufactured articles
(1) The Steward shall receive from the workshops all manufactured articles, and shall
check their quantity with the quantity of materials issued by him or her to be
manufactured.
(2) He or she shall ensure that manufactured articles are accounted for and that any
article sold is properly charged for according to the official price list.

### 143.

Responsibility for kitchen
The Kitchen Officer shall be in charge of the kitchen and where possible be a trained
cook.

### 144.

Cleanliness
The Kitchen Officer shall manage the cooking for the whole Prison and shall be
responsible for the cleanliness of the kitchen and of all the utensils in use.

### 145.

Safety of kitchen

The Kitchen Officer shall be responsible for the general good order and safety of the
kitchen and the stores therein and the discipline of the prisoners employed in the kitchen.

### 146.

Traffic in food
The Kitchen Officer shall take steps to ensure that no prisoner is able to traffic with
food, and shall forthwith report any suspicious circumstances to the Assistant
Superintendent through the Chief Officer.

### 147.

Prohibition on entry
The Kitchen Officer shall not allow officers and prisoners who are not working in the
kitchen or sent there on orders of a superior officer, to enter the kitchen.

### 148.

Measuring, etc., of meals
The Kitchen Officer shall be exact as possible in measuring and weighing the
provisions to be served out at each meal, and it shall be his or her duty to report forthwith
to the Assistant Superintendent any defect he or she may observe in the quantity or
quality of the articles of food.

### 149.

Proper care of food
The Kitchen Officer shall ensure that no food is so placed as to be easily accessible to
prisoners or prison officers until issued to them.

### 150.

Locking of cupboards, etc.
The Kitchen Officer shall ensure that all store cupboards, lockers, and receptacles are
kept locked and shall keep the keys in his or her possession at all times.

### 151.

Enforcement of prisoners in kitchen
The Kitchen Officer shall not employ in the kitchen or about the work of the kitchen
any prisoner who has not been detailed for such work by the Superintendent.

### 152.

Delivery of diets
The Kitchen Officer shall take steps to ensure that the diet for any prisoner is not
delivered by another prisoner unless such prisoner is accompanied by a prison officer.

### 153.

Reports to Chief Officer
The Kitchen Officer shall not attend to written or verbal requests by prisoners in
relation to diets, but shall at once report the matter to the Chief Officer.

### 154.

Care of stores, etc.
The Kitchen Officer shall ensure that no wood or other fuel is left in or near the stoves
or cooking places at night.

### 155.

Cleanliness in kitchen
(1) The Kitchen Officer shall ensure, that all prisoners employed as cooks or in the
handling of food are clean in person and that they bathe daily.
(2) He or she shall ensure that they are provided with clean clothing, a cook’s apron, a
hat and a sweat rag, and that they are worn.

(3) He or she shall inspect frequently the hands and fingernails of all prisoners
employed in the handling of food and shall ensure that their hands are kept clean at all
times during the handling and preparation of food.
(4) He or she shall not allow prisoners to keep clothing or articles in the kitchen.

### 156.

Medical fitness
The Kitchen Officer shall ensure that every man employed in the kitchen has been
passed as medically fit for such work by the Medical Officer.

### 157.

Covering of food
The Kitchen Officer shall ensure that all food awaiting preparation or issue is covered
with a clean cover.

### 158.

Searching of prisoners
The Kitchen Officer shall search all prisoners leaving the kitchen every time they do
so.

### 159.

Responsibility for bakehouse
The Master Baker shall be responsible for the general good order and safety of the
bakehouse and the discipline of the prisoners employed therein.

### 160.

Restriction on entry to bakehouse
The Master Baker shall take steps to ensure that no prison officer or prisoners enter
the bakehouse other than those employed therein, without his or her permission.

### 161.

Bread
The Master Baker shall bake all the bread required for the use of the Prison or for any
other departments and shall ensure that the meal and flour used is of good quality.

### 162.

Storage of bread
The Master Baker shall send all baked bread to the store room immediately upon its
removal from the oven.

### 163.

Unauthorised baking
The Master Baker shall take steps to ensure that no unauthorised baking or cooking of
bread is carried out in the bakehouse.

### 164.

Locking of cupboards
The Master Baker shall ensure that all stores cupboards are kept locked and shall
retain the keys on his or her person.

### 165.

Medical fitness
The Master Baker shall not employ any prisoner in the bakehouse who has not been
passed as medically fit for such work by the Medical Officer.

### 166.

Cleanliness of persons in bakehouse

The Master Baker shall ensure that all prisoners employed at the bakehouse are kept
clean in person and that they bathe daily.

### 167.

Providing of clean clothing
The Master Baker shall ensure that all prisoners employed in the bakehouse are
provided with clean clothing, a cook’s apron, a hat, and a sweat rag, and that they are
worn.

### 168.

Inspection of hands, etc.
The Master Baker shall inspect frequently the hands and finger nails of all prisoners
employed in the bakehouse and shall ensure that their hands are clean at all times during
the handling and preparation of bread.

### 169.

Prohibition of clothing
The Master Baker shall not allow any article of clothing or personal property to be
kept in the bakehouse.

### 170.

Empty flour bags
The Master Baker shall return to the store room all empty flour bags, which shall be
accounted for in the flour bag ledger by the Steward. Any deficiencies shall be charged
against the personal account of the officer concerned at the rate of not more than one
dollar per bag. This charge shall be in addition to any other punishment meted out under
any disciplinary proceedings.

### 171.

Wood or other fuel
The Master Baker shall ensure that no wood or other fuel is left in or near the ovens
during the night.

### 172.

Handing over bread
The Master Baker will hand over to the Steward all bread baked and sign the delivery
book.

### 173.

Schoolmaster
A schoolmaster may be seconded by the Chief Education Officer for duty in the Prison
Department.

### 174.

Duty to give instruction
The Schoolmaster shall give instruction at such hours as the Superintendent may from
time to time direct, and shall keep proper records of the time and duration of classes,
names of prisoners under instruction and their progress, and the course of instruction
pursued.

### 175.

Annual report, etc.
The Schoolmaster shall, at the end of each year, submit to the Chief Education
Officer, through the Commissioner, a report of the year’s work and a programme of work
for the ensuing year.

### 176.

Compliance with Rules, etc.

The Schoolmaster shall be requested to observe the usual rules and regulations which
are binding upon prison officers and he or she shall conduct his or her work under the
general guidance of the Superintendent.

### 177.

Responsibility for workshop
A prison officer assigned to have charge of a Workshop shall be responsible for its
general good order and for the discipline and instruction of the prisoners employed
therein.

### 178.

Manufactured articles
An officer-in-charge of a shop shall, immediately upon their completion, forward all
manufactured articles to the store.

### 179.

Orders for work
An officer-in-charge of a workshop shall not allow any work to be done without an
order in writing from the Assistant Superintendent.

### 180.

Use of materials
An officer-in-charge of a workshop shall be responsible for the economical use of all
materials used therein.

### 181.

Responsibility for materials
An officer-in-charge of a workshop or any work party engaged in manufacturing
articles shall be held responsible for all materials issued for use in that workshop or in the
manufacture of articles.

### 182.

Responsibility for tools, etc.
An officer-in-charge of a workshop or any work party shall be held responsible for all
tools, equipment and stores used in that workshop or by that party.

### 183.

Worn-out tools
An officer-in-charge of a workshop or any work party shall ensure that all worn out
tools are returned to the store in exchange for replacements.

### 184.

Qualifications for gatekeeper
No prison officer with less than five years service shall be detailed for duty as
gatekeeper.

### 185.

Ascertainment of identity
The gatekeeper may not open the main gate unless he or she has ascertained the
identity of the person seeking admission and that such person may properly be admitted.

### 186.

Prohibition on egress
The gatekeeper shall not without the sanction of the Superintendent permit to leave the
Prison any member of the subordinate staff who has entered upon his or her duties.

### 187.

Inner gate

The gatekeeper shall not allow any member of the subordinate staff to enter the inner
gate if such member is off duty and in civilian clothes.

### 188.

Prohibition on press
The gatekeeper shall not permit any member of the press to enter without the sanction
of the Commissioner.

### 189.

Record to be kept
The gatekeeper shall keep in the gate journal a record of all persons who pass through
the gate, including members of the staff. The record shall show the name of each person
and the time of entering or leaving the Prison.

### 190.

Record of latecomers
The gatekeeper shall keep in a book provided for that purpose a record of the names of
any officers reporting late for duty, and such officer shall sign his or her name against the
time he or she reports, and the gatekeeper shall bring all such records to the attention of
the Superintendent through the Chief Officer.

### 191.

Record of vehicles
The gatekeeper shall keep in a book provided for that purpose a record of all vehicles
which pass through the gate.

### 192.

Record of key safe
The gatekeeper shall be responsible for the key safe and shall ensure that keys issued
therefrom are issued only to the proper members of the staff authorised to receive them
and that every key is returned to the safe at the proper time.

### 193.

Arms and ammunition
The gatekeeper shall be responsible for all arms and ammunition entrusted to his or
her care.

### 194.

Conduct of gatekeeper
The gatekeeper shall be courteous and civil and at the same time firm with all
members of the public calling at the gate.

### 195.

Operation of gates
The gatekeeper shall ensure that the small gate for pedestrians, and the big gate for
vehicular traffic, are never open at the same time.

### 196.

Improper articles
The gatekeeper shall ensure that no articles are improperly brought into or taken out of
the Prison and for such purpose shall examine any article, vehicle or person entering or
leaving the Prison and, if necessary, detain such article, vehicle or person pending the
instructions of the Superintendent.

### 197.

Improper conduct

The gatekeeper shall ensure that all persons, including members of the staff, entering
or leaving the Prison, conduct themselves in a proper manner, and shall bring to the
attention of the Superintendent any case of improper conduct.

### 198.

Tally of prisoners
The gatekeeper shall keep a tally of all prisoners passing into or out of the Prison.
Gate passes must be signed by the Chief Officer.

### 199.

Responsibility of transport officer
The Transport Officer shall be in charge of any vehicle attached to the Prison for the
purpose of bringing supplies thereto and shall keep it in proper order and cleanliness. He
or she shall carry all supplies to and from the Prison or any other department, as from
time to time directed by the Assistant Superintendent; and shall be responsible for all
deficiencies in weight or measurement of any article received by him or her from
contractors. When not otherwise engaged the transport officer shall render such general
assistance as the Commissioner may direct.

### 200.

Patrols
The Night Duty Officer shall patrol the main building of the Prison and the front and
back yards during such hours and at such intervals as may from time to time be appointed
and shall record such visits by “pegging” the recording clock at each box in the proper
order.

### 201.

Vigilance
The Night Duty Officers shall vigilantly watch the premises and prisoners during the
night, in order to preserve security and silence and prevent fire. They shall not suffer any
prisoner to talk to them, unless it is absolutely necessary, and shall report to the
Superintendent the precise nature of any irregularity or extraordinary occurrence that may
come to their notice during the night.

### 202.

Familiarity with Act
Every prison officer shall acquaint himself or herself with the Prisons Act and with
these Rules and with any orders relating to his or her duty.

### 203.

Obedience to orders
Every prison officer shall strictly obey the orders of the Commissioner and of all
prison officers senior to him or her.

### 204.

Maintenance of order
(1) Every prison officer shall assist in maintaining order and discipline among
prisoners. Order and discipline shall be maintained with firmness.
(2) In the control of prisoners, officers shall seek to influence them through their own
example and leadership; they shall strive to acquire a moral influence over prisoners by
the conscientious discharge of their duties and by exhibiting a consistent example of
regard to truth and integrity even in the smallest matters.
(3) At all times the treatment of prisoners shall be such as to encourage their selfrespect and a sense of personal responsibility.
(4) No prison officer may engage in any wrangling or argument with prisoners.

### 205.

Conduct towards prisoners
(1) No prison officer may speak to a prisoner unnecessarily, nor by word, gesture or
demeanour may he or she do anything which may tend unnecessarily to irritate a prisoner.
(2) A prison officer shall address a prisoner by his or her surname only and shall not
allow any familiarity on the part of a prisoner.

### 206.

Private affairs
No prison officer may discuss his or her private affairs or those of other prison officers
with prisoners.

### 207.

Self-defence
No prison officer may strike a prisoner except in self-defence, or in the defence of
another, and in any such case no more force than is necessary shall be used.

### 208.

Notification of punishment
No prison officer may inflict any punishment of any kind upon any prisoner unless
ordered so to do by the Commissioner.

### 209.

Maintenance of order
(1) Every prison officer shall at all times maintain order and reasonable quietness
among prisoners and shall prevent them changing places or going to the door of a cell
other than their own unless ordered to do so.
(2) Every prison officer shall at all times carefully watch the prisoners and shall use
the utmost vigilance to promote industry. Officers in charge of work parties shall be
responsible for the work output of their parties.

### 210.

Discussions on discipline
No prison officer may on any account discuss any matter of discipline or prison
arrangements within the hearing of a prisoner.

### 211.

Report on misconduct
Every prison officer shall make an immediate report of any misconduct or disregard to
these Rules or of any Standing Orders.

### 212.

Taking charge of party of prisoners
A prison officer taking charge of such a party of prisoners shall—
(a)

immediately prior to taking charge of such a party count the number of
prisoners in the party and report in a loud voice to the prison officer from
whom he or she receives such party;

(b)

be responsible for the safe custody and proper conduct of the prisoners in
that party;

(c)

prevent prisoners in that party straggling, communicating with unauthorised
persons or obtaining possession of prohibited articles;

(d)

ensure that no prisoner in that party is out of sight unless the prisoner is in
the charge of another prison officer;

(e)

ensure that every prisoner in that party is searched upon leaving the prison
and on his or her return.

### 213.

Attention to cleanliness
Every prison officer shall pay strict attention to cleanliness and neatness of person and
dress and shall at all times when on duty wear the uniform provided by the prison.

### 214.

Resignations, etc.
When a prison officer resigns, retires or is dismissed, his or her uniform, equipment
and last issue of boots or shoes shall be delivered up at once and returned to the store. The
uniform, equipment and boots or shoes shall be inspected and any deficiencies or damage
not due to fair wear and tear shall be made good before final discharge out of any money
due to the officer. No officer shall sell or dispose in any way of any article or uniform or
equipment.

### 215.

Keys
No prison officer entrusted with keys shall take them out of the Prison unless specially
authorised in writing to do so, or leave them lying about or in locks, or lend them to
another prison officer or for any purpose permit such keys to be out of his or her
immediate possession. He or she shall when leaving the prison or on any occasion when
required to deliver them up, deliver his or her keys to the prison officer whose duty it is to
receive them.

### 216.

Visitors
No prison officer may be permitted to receive any visitors in the interior of the prison
without the permission of the Commissioner.

### 217.

Report on abuses, etc.
Every prison officer shall at once communicate to the Commissioner any abuses or
impropriety which may come to his or her knowledge.

### 218.

Sickness of prisoners
Every prison officer shall direct the attention of the Superintendent to any prisoner
(whether he or she complains or not) who appears to be ill or whose state of mind appears
to be deserving of special notice and care and the Superintendent shall without delay
bring such cases to the notice of the Medical Officer.

### 219.

Prohibition on gratuities, etc.
No prison officer may receive any fee, gratuity or other consideration in connection
with his or her duties.

### 220.

Prohibition on transactions
No prison officer may carry out any pecuniary or business transactions with or on
behalf of prisoners.

### 221.

Communication with ex-prisoners, etc.
No prison officer may knowingly communicate with an ex-prisoner or with friends
and relatives of any prisoner or ex-prisoner except with the permission in writing of the
Commissioner.

### 222.

Searches of prison officers

Every prison officer shall submit himself or herself to be searched in the Prison if
called upon to do so by the Commissioner.

### 223.

Unauthorised communication
No prison officer may, directly or indirectly, make any unauthorised communication
to any representative of the Press or other person with reference to matters which have
become known to him or her in the course of his or her duty.

### 224.

Publication prohibited
No prison officer may, without authority, publish any matters or make any public
pronouncement relating to the administration or inmates of the Prison.

### 225.

Quarters
Every prison officer shall occupy such quarters as may be assigned to him or her and
shall at any time vacate them if required to do so.

### 226.

Vacating quarters
On termination of a prison officer’s service he or she shall give up the quarters he or
she has occupied as soon as he or she is required to do so; and on the death of a prison
officer his or her family shall give up the quarters when required to do so.

### 227.

Sickness of officers
(1) Every prison officer who is unable to present himself or herself for duty by reason
of being sick or disabled shall immediately notify the Superintendent either by reporting
in person or by a message and shall apply for leave.
(2) Leave on account of sickness or disability shall only be granted to an officer on
the certificate of a Government Medical Officer.

### 228.

Injury on duty
(1) Where a prison officer sustains any injury in the course of his or her duty the
Chief Officer shall submit to the Superintendent a report of the circumstances of the
injury and of his or her opinion as to whether such injury was or was not caused or
contributed to by any default on the part of the prison officer.
(2) The Medical Officer shall also submit a report to the Superintendent which shall
state the degree of incapacity if any, sustained by the prison officer.

### 229.

Disputes, etc.
(1) Prison Officers shall not indulge in any disputes or wrangling in relation to their
duties or on any matter when on duty or within the precincts of the prison.
(2) Any question of duty on which prison officers cannot agree shall be referred to the
Chief Officer for his or her decision.

### 230.

Complaints by officers
Any complaint by one prison officer against another shall be made in writing through
the Chief Officer to the Superintendent and shall not be received unless delivered within
twelve hours of the occurrence.

### 231.

Frivolous, etc., complaints

Any complaint found to be frivolous or vexatious or actuated by a spirit of ill will,
malice or revenge shall be reported to the Superintendent.

### 232.

Complaints in writing
(1) A prison officer having any complaint to make connected with his or her duty
shall within forty-eight hours of the occurrence complain or state the same in writing for
the decision of the Superintendent; and if dissatisfied with the decision of the
Superintendent may appeal to the Commissioner.
(2) A prison officer who makes a complaint through an improper channel shall be
liable to disciplinary action.

### 233.

Private monetary transactions
No prison officer may have any private monetary transactions with any other prison
officer.

### 234.

Prohibition on trade, etc.
No prison officer may undertake any business, trade or other employment of any
nature whatsoever whether for gain or otherwise without the sanction in writing of the
Commissioner.

### 235.

Service generally
Every prison officer engaged to serve in the prison service shall be liable to serve in
any part of the State, in any prison or lock-up, or any place or building established by the
Minister as a Prison or lock-up.

### 236.

Probationary period
(1) Subject to the provisions hereinafter contained, a person joining the prison service
as a member of the subordinate staff shall be on probation for a period of two years. At
the expiration of that period a probationer if efficient, of good character and qualified as
hereinafter stated for further service, shall be confirmed in his or her appointment in the
service.
(2) During the probationary period aforesaid the services of a member of the
subordinate staff may be dispensed with at any time if the Commissioner considers that
he or she is not fitted, physically or otherwise, to perform the duties of his or her office or
that he or she is not likely to become an efficient or well conducted officer.
(3) A member of the subordinate staff whose services are dispensed with under this
Rule shall be entitled to receive one month’s notice of the termination of his or her
services or one month’s pay in lieu of such notice.
(4) If any member of the subordinate staff has knowingly and wilfully made any false
statements in connection with his or her application for appointment, he or she shall be
liable to summary dismissal by the Commissioner.

### 237.

Disqualification
No person shall be admitted into the prison service who has been convicted in any
court of any criminal offence or who has been dismissed from any Government
Department.

### 238.

Medical examination

Every candidate selected for appointment shall prior to appointment be medically
examined as to his or her physical fitness for service as a prison officer.

### 239.

Breach of rules
Every prison officer shall make his or her defence in writing to any charge of a breach
of the Rules made against him or her.

### 240.

Witnesses
(1) Every prison officer for the purposes of his or her defence may call as a witness
any person in the prison service whom he or she requires.
(2) It shall be the responsibility of such prison officer to see that his or her witnesses
are present at any disciplinary inquiry.

### 241.

Assistance
A prison officer may for the purposes of his or her defence be assisted by a friend
within the prison service.

### 242.

Interdiction
No prison officer who has been interdicted from duty may take part in any other
employment.

### 243.

Cleanliness of quarters
(1) All prison officers shall ensure that the grounds surrounding their quarters are kept
clean and tidy.
(2) All prison officers shall ensure that all rubbish and sweepings from their quarters
are deposited in receptacles ready for removal between the hours of 7 a.m. and 8 a.m.
daily.

### 244.

Restriction on political activity
All prison officers shall observe such restrictions on their political activities as may be
prescribed for them from time to time as members of the Prison Service.

### 245.

Serious pecuniary embarrassment
Serious pecuniary embarrassment shall be regarded as impairing the efficiency of a
prison officer and, if occasioned by imprudence or other blameworthy cause, shall be
regarded as so affecting the trustworthiness of the officer as to render him or her liable to
disciplinary action.

### 246.

Arrest of officer, etc.
A prison officer who fails to inform the Commissioner of his or her arrest on any
criminal charge or bankruptcy proceedings having been taken out against him or her shall
be liable to disciplinary action.

### 247.

Handing over at end of duty
Every prison officer on being relieved from any particular duty or transferred to
another duty shall indicate to the prison officer succeeding him or her on such duty all
matters of special importance connected therewith and any order which may have been
given him or her with respect to any particular matter or prisoner.

### 248.

Report on infectious disease
Every prison officer shall report to the prison officer in charge and the Medical Officer
the occurrence of any infectious disease in his or her household or in any house occupied
by him or her.

### 249.

Opening of cells at night
No prison officer may enter or open the door of the cell of any prisoner at night unless
accompanied by another officer and then only in case of illness or other emergency.

### 250.

Fines
Fines imposed on prison officers and deducted from their pay shall be placed on
deposit by the Commissioner in the Treasury to a separate account to be called the Prison
Officers’ Reward Fund Account which will be administered and distributed for the
reward of deserving officers in such manner as may from time to time be directed by the
Minister; but no officer shall have any claim on such fund as a matter of right.

### 251.

Grants from Reward Fund
Grants from the Prison Officers’ Reward Fund may be made by the Commissioner to
any prison officer in the following circumstances, that is to say—
(a)

long continued illness of an officer involving loss or suspension of pay;

(b)

loss of wife when the prison officer is put to the expense of arranging for
someone to take care of his or her children;

(c)

illness of a prison officer’s family, or of those dependent on him or her, or
any similar case of unusual expense caused by illness;

(d)

discharge with or without pension on grounds of ill health if there are any
circumstances by which unusual expenses have to be borne by the officer;

(e)

to the widow or children of an officer who has died in the service or, after
being retired on the ground of ill health; dies within six months of the date of
his or her retirement leaving a widow or children;

(f)

any unexpected balance of the fund may be applied to assist in the education
of any children of a prison officer who has died in the service, or to
establishing and maintaining officers’ reading rooms, or to some similar
purpose by which the general body of prison officers may be benefited;

(g)

to any officer who has been exceptionally capable and diligent; and who has
not committed any serious breach of discipline for a number of years. This
grant may be awarded in addition to his or her pension and gratuity from the
Fund as recognition for such service;

(h)

loans may be made to subordinate prison officers by the Commissioner.
Such loans shall not exceed one and a half times the monthly salary of the
borrower. These loans shall be free of interest and shall be repaid by
monthly instalments to be determined by the Commissioner, deducted from
the salary of the borrower by the Commissioner.

### 252.

Code of discipline
Subordinate prison officers shall be subject to and be governed by the Code of
Discipline set out in the Prison Officers (Discipline) Rules, and any amendments thereto.
(1) Leave will be granted to members of the Prison Service at the discretion of the
Commissioner as follows—

(a)

not exceeding thirty days per annum to members of the Prison Service with
less than five years service. Such leave may be accumulated for three years
so that ninety days leave may be granted to a member of the Prison Service
who has had no ordinary leave for thirty-six months;

(b)

not exceeding thirty-six days per annum to members of the Prison Service
with five years service and less than fifteen years service, such leave may be
accumulated for four years so that one hundred and forty-four days leave
may be granted to a member of the Prison Service who has had no ordinary
leave for forty-eight months;

(c)

not exceeding forty-five days per annum to members of the Prison Service
with fifteen years of service or over. Such leave may be accumulated for
four years so that one hundred and eighty days leave may be granted to a
member of the Prison Service who has had no ordinary leave for forty-eight
months.

(2) Notwithstanding anything contained in this Regulation a member of the Prison
Service holding the rank of Chief Officer may accumulate leave to a maximum of one
hundred and eighty days, whatever the length of his or her service.

### 253.

Sick leave
(1) When sick leave granted to any member of the Prison Service exceeds twentyeight days in any one year, the excess will be deducted from his or her ordinary leave
entitlement.
(2) (a) Subject to rule 252, three months leave shall be granted to married women
Prison Officers on account of pregnancy or for the purpose of maternity leave:
Provided that nothing in this Rule shall be deemed to deprive any married woman
Prison Officer of vacation leave for which she may be eligible under these Rules.
(b) The Commissioner may, in his or her discretion in any special case grant to a
married woman Prison Officer for maternity purpose (in addition to any vacation leave
for which she may be eligible) leave on full pay, to be known as special maternity leave.
(c) No sick leave may be granted in addition to leave set out in subparagraphs (a)
and (b) of this Rule and this provision applies to married woman Prison Officers only.
(3) All applications for leave under this Rule shall be made in writing through the
usual channel to the Commissioner.

### 254.

Medicines, etc.
(1) All medicines and supplies prescribed by the appropriate District Officer or the
Prison Medical Officer in the course of treatment of members of the Prison Service shall
be supplied from the Dispensary at the General Hospital or the District dispensaries free
of charge. If these medicines or supplies are not available at the General Hospital or
District dispensaries then they may be obtained from private chemists but the cost will be
borne by Government.
(2) The Medical Officer shall supply only those medicines or articles which in his or
her opinion are absolutely necessary for the treatment of those individuals concerned.

### 255.

Free hospital treatment
All members of the Prison Service shall be admitted to Hospital free of charge.

### 256.

Free dental treatment

All members of the Prison Service shall be provided with free dental treatment in
respect of extractions. In the case of other dental treatment fifty per cent of the cost will
be borne by Government.

### 257.

Free eye treatment
The Medical Officer shall recommend all members of the Prison Service suffering
from eye disease to be sent to a Government eye specialist for treatment. Cost to be borne
by Government.

### 258.

Supply of spectacles
All members of the Prison Service shall on the prescription of a Government Eye
Specialist be supplied with spectacles, cost to be borne by Government, but in the event
of their requesting frames of a more expensive type than those normally supplied by the
optician under the Government Contract they shall be required to pay the difference in
cost.

### 259.

Availability of treatment
Medical and Surgical Treatment will be limited to such treatment as is available in the
State.

## PART III

General Rules for the Treatment of Prisoners

### 260.

Application of Rules
These Rules apply to all classes of prisoners except in so far as they are inconsistent
with any other of the Prison Rules.

### 261.

Training of prisoners
The purpose of training and treatment of convicted prisoners shall be to establish in
them the will to lead a good and useful life on discharge, and fit them to do so.

### 262.

Special class of prisoners
(1) The Commissioner may set aside particular parts of the prison for the
accommodation of special classes of prisoners.
(2) The Commissioner may establish or set aside a part of the prison for the treatment
of selected prisoners or classes of prisoners in conditions of minimum security.

### 263.

Arrangements for training, etc.
(1) The Superintendent shall as soon as possible after the reception of a prisoner and
within 48 hours of this reception interview such prisoner and consider what arrangements
are to be made for his or her work and training.
(2) The Superintendent shall also classify the prisoners sentenced to imprisonment
having regard to their age, character, medical category and previous history.

### 264.

Rules for classification

In so far as possible to prevent contamination and to facilitate training, the
arrangements set out in the following paragraphs of this Rule shall so far as possible be
made in classifying prisoners—
(a)

prisoners of eighteen years and over who have not previously been in prison
on conviction shall be placed in the Star Class unless the Superintendent
considers that, in view of their record and character, they are likely to have
bad influence on others. The Superintendent may also place in the Star Class
a prisoner of eighteen years of age and over who has previously been in
prison on conviction if he or she is satisfied, having regard to the nature of
the previous offence, or to length of time since it was committed or to the
prisoner’s general record and character, that he or she is not likely to have a
bad influence on others;

(b)

prisoners under the age of eighteen years of age shall be placed in the young
prisoners’ class;

(c)

other prisoners shall be placed in the ordinary class.

### 265.

Transfer between classes
(1) The Superintendent may in his or her discretion at any time remove from the
Young Prisoners’ Class any prisoner whom he or she considers unsuitable by reason of
his or her character for that class and may place him or her in the Star Class.
(2) The Superintendent may, in his or her discretion at any time remove from the Star
Class to the Ordinary Class a prisoner whose character has shown him or her to be unfit
to associate with other prisoners of a Star Class.
(3) Arrangements shall be made to provide as far as possible for the effective
separation at all times of the three classes of convicted prisoners.

### 266.

System of privileges
The Commissioner may in the interest of good conduct and training, establish such
systems of privileges as may be appropriate for different classes of prisoners and different
methods of training.

### 267.

Orderlies, gardener, etc.
Prisoners employed as orderlies, gardeners, and in similar capacities and not under
constant supervision of an officer shall be selected by the Commissioner from the Star
Class or from prisoners who have completed not less than 12 months of their sentence.
Each prisoner so selected shall give his or her word of honour to the Commissioner to
obey all rules, to put his or her best effort into his or her work and to conduct himself or
herself properly in every way in return for the privilege of being trusted. While so
employed prisoners shall wear a red band around the right arm.

### 268.

Certification of sleeping accommodation
(1) No sleeping accommodation for prisoners shall be used unless it is certified by the
Medical Officer to be of such size, and lighted, ventilated and fitted in such manner as is
requisite for health, and when such accommodation is locked it shall be furnished with
the means of enabling prisoners at any time to communicate with an officer.
If the certificate of any cell, room or ward so used is cancelled, the accommodation
shall not be used for sleeping unless it is again certified. The certificate shall specify the
maximum number of prisoners to be accommodated at any one time in such a room or
ward and the number specified shall not be exceeded without the authority of the
Commissioner.

(2) Where sleeping accommodation is in separate cells, each prisoner shall occupy a
cell by himself or herself:
Provided that where it is necessary for special reasons, the Prison Medical Officer
may authorise accommodation of not more than three prisoners in a cell.

### 269.

Supplies for prisoner
Every prisoner shall be supplied with—
(a)

a bed;

(b)

a blanket;

(c)

a water container; and

(d)

a covered chamber or covered slop pail; but additional bedding may be
supplied on the recommendation of the Medical Officer.

### 270.

Lighting of cells
Cells shall be individually lighted so as to permit reading or working up to 9.00 p.m.

### 271.

Female areas
In a prison containing both male and female prisoners, the whole of the premises
allocated to the female prisoners shall be entirely separated and shall be secured by locks
different from those used in the male prison.

### 272.

Keys to female areas
The keys of the premises allocated to female prisoners shall be under the control of
women prison officers and no male officer shall enter the premises except on duty and in
the company of a female prison officer.

### 273.

Precautions against outbreak of fires
(1) All reasonable precautions shall be taken against the outbreak of fire.
(2) The Commissioner shall cause all fire fighting appliances and equipment to be
inspected twice every year, and shall carry out such fire drills as may be prescribed by the
regulations.
(3) A set of rules and instructions governing fire precautions and fire shall be kept
prominently posted in every section of the Prison.

### 274.

Committal forms
Before any prisoner is received into the prison the appropriate form of committal
properly filled in all its parts, shall be handed to the Reception Officer.

### 275.

Time for reception
Prisoners shall not be received at the prison earlier than 7.00 a.m. or later than 6.00
p.m. or on any Sunday, or public holiday, except with the special permission of the
Commissioner.

### 276.

Search before reception
(1) Every prisoner shall be searched when taken into custody by an officer on
reception into the prison, and at such subsequent times as are directed, and all
unauthorised articles shall be taken away from him or her.

(2) The searching of a prisoner shall be conducted in as seemly a manner as is
consistent with the necessity of discovering any concealed articles.
(3) A prisoner shall be searched only by officers of the sex as the prisoner.

### 277.

Custody of prisoner’s property
All money, clothing or other effects belonging to a prisoner which he or she is not
allowed to retain shall be placed in the custody of the Reception Officer, who shall keep
an inventory thereof which shall be signed by the prisoner.

### 278.

Excess clothing
A prisoner may not, save in exceptional circumstances, deliver to the Reception
Officer, more than one outfit of clothing. Any other clothing which he or she brings to the
prison shall be returned to the prisoner’s home or to such other place as the prisoner may
request.

### 279.

Clothing kept at prisoner’s risk
Where any clothing in excess of one outfit is received by the Reception Officer, such
clothing shall be kept at the prisoner’s risk.

### 280.

Bulky articles
Articles which in the opinion of the Assistant Superintendent are too bulky for storage
shall not be received.

### 281.

Signing for property
Any prisoner taking his or her clothes out for any reason shall sign for taking them out
and on his or her return such clothes be checked in and signed for by the officer and
prisoner.

### 282.

Particulars of prisoner
The name, age, height, weight, particular marks and such other measurements and
particulars as may be required in regard to a prisoner shall upon his or her reception and
from time to time be recorded in such a manner as the Assistant Superintendent may
determine.

### 283.

Photographs and fingerprints
A prisoner may be photographed and have his or her fingerprints taken on reception
and subsequently, but no copy of the photographs or fingerprints shall be given to any
person who is not authorised to receive it.

### 284.

Bath on reception
Every prisoner shall, unless exempted by the Assistant Superintendent or the Prison
Medical Officer, take a bath on reception.

### 285.

Examination by Medical Officer
Every prisoner shall, within twenty-four hours of his or her reception, be separately
examined by the Medical Officer, who shall record the state of health of the prisoner, the
prisoner’s labour category, and any other particular necessary:

Provided that where a prisoner is received late on Saturday or the day preceding a
public holiday such prisoner shall be examined on the day subsequent to the Sunday or
public holiday.

### 286.

Treatment of babies
A female prisoner may have her baby with her during the normal period of lactation
and longer (to a maximum of twelve months) if required in special circumstances, and the
baby may be supplied with clothing and necessaries at the public expense.

### 287.

Information on reception
Arrangements shall be made to ensure that every prisoner on reception is provided, in
his or her cell, or room or ward, with full information governing the treatment of
prisoners of his or her class and about any other regulations about which he or she should
have knowledge, including those relating to earnings and privilege, of making
complaints, to food, clothing, bedding, and other necessaries, and to disciplinary
requirements of the prison.

### 288.

Reading of information
The Superintendent shall personally or through such officer as he or she may appoint
ensure as soon as possible after reception, and in any case within twenty-four hours, that
every prisoner who can read has read the information as provided. Where a prisoner
cannot read or has difficulty in understanding the information so provided, it shall be so
explained to him or her that he or she may understand both his or her rights and his or her
obligations.

### 289.

Removal of prisoners
When prisoners are being removed to or from prison, they should be exposed to public
view as little as possible and proper safeguards shall be adopted to protect them from
insult or curiosity.

### 290.

Interview, etc.
Every prisoner shall before discharge or removal to another prison be interviewed by
the Assistant Superintendent.

### 291.

Medical examination
(1) Every prisoner shall, as short a time as is practicable before discharge, be
examined by the Prison Medical Officer.
(2) A prisoner shall not be removed unless the Prison Medical Officer certifies that he
or she is free from infection and fit to travel.
(3) A prisoner due for discharge who is suffering from an acute or dangerous illness
shall, unless he or she refuses to stay, not be sent out of the prison until in the opinion of
the Medical Officer it is safe to send him or her out.

### 292.

Return of clothing
On discharge of a prisoner, his or her own clothes shall be returned to him or her
unless it has been found necessary to destroy or otherwise dispose of them in which case
proper clothing shall be provided from public funds.

### 293.

Release

A prisoner shall be released on the day on which his or her sentence expires, or on the
previous day whenever the sentence expires on Sunday, Christmas Day, Good Friday, or
any public holiday.

### 294.

Deaths
The Medical Officer shall keep a record of the death of any prisoner which shall
include the following particulars—
(a)

the time at which the prisoner was taken ill;

(b)

the time at which the illness was first reported to the Medical Officer;

(c)

the time at which the prisoner was first seen by the Medical Officer;

(d)

the nature of the illness;

(e)

the time of death; and

(f)

any other comments which appear to the Medical Officer to be necessary.

### 295.

Notification to Coroner
Upon the death of a prisoner, the Commissioner shall give immediate notice thereof to
the Coroner having jurisdiction.

### 296.

Release of body for burial
Whenever the relatives of a prisoner who has died in prison of natural causes are
desirous of having the body of that prisoner delivered to them for burial they shall apply
to the Commissioner who, on a certificate from the Medical Officer that it is not injurious
to the public health to grant the request, may deliver the body to the relatives who shall
take it away in a decent manner.

### 297.

General discipline
The Rules in this section shall be applied, due allowance being made for the difference
of character and response to discipline of different types of prisoners, in accordance with
the following principles—
(a)

discipline and order shall be maintained with firmness but with no more
restriction than is required for safe custody and well ordered community life;

(b)

at all times the treatment of prisoners shall be such as to encourage their selfrespect and a sense of personal responsibility.

### 298.

Instructions to female prisoners
Female prisoners shall be given instructions in sewing, washing, first aid and child
welfare.

### 299.

Use of force
No officer may in dealing with prisoners use force unnecessarily and, when the
application of force to a prisoner is necessary no more force than is necessary shall be
used.

### 300.

Provocation of prisoner
No officer may act in a manner calculated to provoke a prisoner.

### 301.

Positions of responsibility for prisoners

No prisoner may be employed in any disciplinary capacity, but prisoners carefully
selected by the Superintendent may be given positions of responsibility and leadership
within such limits as the Superintendent may determine.

### 302.

Separating prisoners
(1) If at any time it appears to the Superintendent that it is desirable for the
maintenance of good order or discipline or in the interest of a prisoner that the prisoner
should not work in association with others, the Superintendent may arrange for that
prisoner to work temporarily in a cell and not in association with other prisoners.
(2) It shall be within the discretion of the Superintendent to arrange for that prisoner
again to work in association whenever he or she considers this desirable, and he or she
shall do so in any case if the Medical Officer so advises on medical grounds.

### 303.

Eligibility for good conduct
(1) Every prisoner sentenced to imprisonment, whether by one sentence or by
consecutive sentences, for a period exceeding one month may by good conduct and
industry become eligible for discharge when a portion of his or her sentence not
exceeding one-third of the whole sentence has yet to run:
Provided that nothing in this Rule shall authorise the reduction of any period of
imprisonment to a period less than thirty days.
(2) The minimum number of days to be served shall be ascertained by deduction from
the number of days comprised in the sentence one-third of that number to the nearest
whole number. The earliest day of release shall be the day after the minimum number of
days has been served.

### 304.

Date of discharge
The earliest day of discharge shall be entered on the record of each prisoner who is
eligible for remission not later than forty-eight hours after he or she has been admitted to
prison.

### 305.

Remission
All remission forfeited by a prisoner shall be calculated in days.

### 306.

Extra remission
Notwithstanding anything in these Rules, extra remission may be granted by the
Minister to any prisoner on the recommendation of the Commissioner for any special
service.

### 307.

Corporal punishment
The Commissioner shall be present at the execution of a sentence of corporal
punishment and shall enter in a book to be kept for that purpose the number of lashes or
strokes inflicted and any remission ordered by the Medical Officer.

### 308.

Punishment diets
The restricted punishment diets which may be awarded for commission of prison
offences shall be prescribed in the Second Schedule to these Rules.

### 309.

Confinement to cell

The Superintendent may, where it is necessary in the interests of discipline and good
order, cause any disorderly prisoner to be confined to his or her cell for such period as he
or she may think fit.

### 310.

Visits by medical officer
Every prisoner undergoing cellular confinement or subjected to Punishment Diet No. 1
shall be visited at least once a day by the Superintendent and the Medical Officer and if
he or she is undergoing cellular confinement he or she shall be visited by an officer
appointed for that purpose at intervals of not more than three hours during the day.

### 311.

Mechanical restraints
(1) Mechanical restraints shall not be used as a punishment, or for any purpose other
than safe custody during removal except on medical grounds by direction of the Medical
Officer or as hereinafter provided.
(2) When it appears to the Commissioner that it is necessary to place a prisoner under
mechanical restraint in order to prevent him or her injuring himself or herself or others or
damaging property, or creating a disturbance, the Commissioner may order him or her to
be placed under mechanical restraint and notice thereof shall forthwith be given to the
Medical Officer.
(3) The Medical Officer on receipt of the aforesaid notice shall forthwith inform the
Commissioner whether he or she concurs in the order, and if on medical grounds he or
she does not concur the Commissioner shall act in accordance with any recommendation
which he or she makes.
(4) No prisoner may be kept continuously under mechanical restraint longer than is
necessary, or for a longer period than twenty-four hours unless the Commissioner
considers that it is necessary to keep such prisoner under mechanical restraint for a longer
period. In every such case he or she shall notify the Medical Officer.
(5) Particulars of every case of mechanical restraint shall forthwith be recorded by the
Commissioner in his or her journal.
(6) No mechanical means of restraint may be used except of such patterns and in such
manner and under such conditions as laid down by these Rules.

### 312.

Type of restraints
The means of mechanical restraint which may be used in respect of male and female
prisoners shall be—
(a)

Handcuffs.—These may be used for restraining prisoners who become
violent. Handcuffs may be ordered to be placed either in front or behind the
body. Handcuffs shall, when ordered to be placed with the hands behind the
body, be moved to the front at meal times and at night. The handcuffs shall
be of regulation patterns;

(b)

Body-belt.—This may be used for the restraint of prisoners who become
violent, where the safety either of the prisoner or of other prisoners so
require. At meal times the left hand, or in the case of a left-handed prisoner
the right hand, shall be released. After the meal has been eaten the hand shall
again be secured. A body-belt should be of double leather sewn together,
fastened by a lock around the waist with steel wristlets at equal distances at
the sides to lock the wrists therein;

(c)

One-piece canvas suit or dress.—This may be used to prevent prisoners
wilfully destroying their clothing. The suit or dress shall be suitable canvas,
double sewn;

(d)

Straight-Jacket.—This may be used for the restraint of prisoners who
become violent where the safety either of the prisoner or other prisoners
requires such restraint. The jacket shall be made of double canvas sewn and
so constructed that the jacket straps up at the back. It shall contain two
pockets inside the jacket for securing the prisoner’s hands;

(e)

Ankle-Straps.—This means of restraint may only be used in respect of
women who may seriously injure themselves or others if not so restrained
and only on the recommendation of the Medical Officer. Ankle-straps shall
be of the approved type.

### 313.

Violent behaviour
The Commissioner may order any refractory or violent prisoner to be temporarily
confined in a special cell certified by the Medical Officer as fit for the purpose, but no
prisoner shall be confined in such cell as a punishment or after he or she has ceased to be
refractory or violent.

### 314.

Useful work
Every prisoner shall be required to engage in useful work for not more than eight
hours a day, of which so far as practicable at least six hours shall be spent in associated or
other work outside the cells:
Provided that the Medical Officer may excuse a prisoner from work on medical
grounds, and no prisoner shall be set to work unless he or she has been certified as fit for
that type of work by the Medical Officer.

### 315.

Authorisation for work
(1) No prisoner may be set to any type of work not authorised by the Superintendent.
(2) Except with the authority of the Commissioner, no prisoner may work in the
service of another prisoner or of an officer, or of a Government department other than the
prison, or for the private benefit of any person.
(3) No unnecessary work shall be done by prisoners on Sunday, Christmas Day, Good
Friday or any public holiday.

### 316.

Religious denomination
The religious denomination of every prisoner shall be ascertained and recorded on his
or her reception and he or she shall be treated as a member of the denomination then
recorded unless and until he or she satisfies the Commissioner that he or she has good
grounds for desiring the record to be altered.

### 317.

Visits by Priests on request
Where a prisoner is recorded as belonging to a religion or a religious denomination the
Superintendent shall, if the prisoner so requests, arrange for him or her, as far as possible,
to be visited by a minister of that religion or denomination, and every prisoner shall be
informed of this Rule on reception.

### 318.

Sunday services
Arrangements shall be made for the holding in the Prison on every Sunday for the
Ministers of Religions who are desirous of holding services in the Prison to visit the
Prison, at an hour convenient to the schedule of the Prison.

### 319.

Regular visits by Priests
Every visiting Priest or Minister shall see each prisoner of his or her denomination or
religion as soon as possible after admission of such prisoner, and shall, as far as is
practicable, visit him or her regularly at proper and reasonable times.

### 320.

Interview by Priests, etc.
A visiting Priest or Minister shall on each visit to the Prison interview each Prisoner of
his or her denomination or religion who is—
(a)

under sentence of death;

(b)

in hospital; or

(c)

undergoing punishment other than imprisonment under his or her sentence.

### 321.

Restrictions on proselytising
Every visiting Priest or Minister shall refrain from proselytising amongst prisoners of
other denominations or religions.

### 322.

Compliance with Rules
Every visiting Priest or Minister shall conform to these Rules and shall not convey
messages, letters or information to or from prisoners and their relations and friends or
give information to persons outside or make public any matter that may come to their
knowledge within the prison. Any literature or other article that a visiting Priest or
Minister may wish a prisoner to receive shall be given to the Superintendent at whose
direction that article may be issued.

### 323.

Religious literature
There shall so far as practicable be available for personal use of every prisoner such of
the Scriptures and books of religious observance and instruction recognised for his or her
denomination as are accepted by the Superintendent for use in Prison.

### 324.

Improvement of education
(1) Every prisoner shall be encouraged to improve his or her standard of education
and general knowledge and for this purpose the Superintendent shall, so far as is
practicable, arrange educational classes.
(2) Special attention shall be paid to the education of illiterate prisoners.
(3) Classes shall be arranged so that as little interference as possible may be caused
with working hours.

### 325.

Library
A library shall be provided in the Prison, and subject to such conditions as the
Superintendent determines every prisoner shall be allowed to have library books and to
exchange them as often as practicable.

### 326.

Teachers
Teachers shall be selected for work in the Prison by agreement between the Education
Officer and the Superintendent.

### 327.

Programme of work

Teachers so selected shall submit to the Superintendent in advance a programme of
work for each year, and shall give instructions in secular subjects to such persons as the
Superintendent may from time to time direct.

### 328.

Monthly reports
Every teacher shall make a monthly report in writing to the Superintendent as to the
conduct and progress of the prisoners taught by him or her and such reports shall be
forwarded through the Commissioner to the Education Officer.

### 329.

Good order, etc.
Every teacher shall be responsible for the preservation of good order and discipline
during classes.

### 330.

Records to be kept
Every teacher shall keep an account of all books and other instructional material
distributed to the prisoners.

### 331.

Family relationships
(1) So far as possible, attention shall be paid to the maintenance of such relations
between a prisoner and his or her family as are desirable in the best interests of both.
(2) The Superintendent may at any time communicate to the prisoner or his or her
family or friends any matter of importance to that prisoner.
(3) A prisoner shall be allowed to inform his or her family of his or her transfer to
another prison.

### 332.

Welfare
From the beginning of the sentence of every prisoner consideration shall be given, in
consultation with the Welfare Officer, to the future of the prisoner and the assistance to
be given him or her on and after his or her discharge.

### 333.

Information to relations
Upon the death, or serious illness, or certification as insane or mentally defective of a
prisoner or the sustaining by a prisoner of a serious accident, the Superintendent shall, if
the prisoner is married to a person whose address is known, at once inform the wife or
husband as the case may be, and otherwise shall at once inform the nearest relative whose
address is known and shall in any event inform any other person whom the prisoner has
requested to be so informed.

### 334.

Letters
Every prisoner shall be allowed to write and receive a letter on reception and
thereafter every month.

### 335.

Visits
Every prisoner shall be allowed to receive a visit one month after admission and
thereafter at intervals of one month.

### 336.

Period of visits
The periods of a visit to a convicted prisoner shall be fifteen minutes.

### 337.

Certain communications prohibited
No prisoner shall be allowed to write or receive letters or visits from any ex-prisoner
unless there is proven close relationship.

### 338.

Other restrictions
(1) Notwithstanding the provisions of these Rules, the Assistant Superintendent may
impose such restrictions upon and supervision over letters and visits as he or she
considers necessary for securing discipline and good order, for the prevention of crime
and criminal associations, and for the welfare of individuals.
(2) Except as provided in these Rules, no person shall be allowed to communicate
with a prisoner without special authority.
(3) Visits to a prisoner shall not, except in an emergency take place on Sunday or any
public holiday, and, except as provided in these Rules in the case of a legal adviser, shall
be in the sight and hearing of a prison officer.

### 339.

Deferment of letters
When a prisoner who becomes entitled to a letter or a visit under any of these Rules is
at the time undergoing confinement in a cell the letter or visit may, in the discretion of the
Assistant Superintendent be deferred until such confinement has expired.

### 340.

Letter in lieu of visit
The Assistant Superintendent may allow a prisoner who is entitled to a visit to write a
letter and receive a reply instead of such a visit.

### 341.

Christmas mail
Every prisoner may be allowed to write and receive a reply to a letter at Christmas and
this shall be in addition to his or her ordinary letter.

### 342.

Special letters
A prisoner may be allowed at the discretion of the Assistant Superintendent to have
special letters and visits under very urgent circumstances, but it shall be in the Assistant
Superintendent’s discretion whether such special visits or letter shall be additional to or in
advance of a normal letter or visit.

### 343.

Visits by Police
(1) A police constable may visit any prisoner who is willing to see him or her on
production of an order issued by or on behalf of the Commissioner of Police and any such
visit shall be additional to those allowed under the foregoing rules.
(2) A person committed to prison in default of the payment of a sum which in
pursuance of any conviction or order he or she is required to pay, shall be allowed to have
an interview with his or her friends on a weekday at any reasonable hour, or to
communicate by letter with them for the bona fide purpose of providing for a payment
which would procure his or her release from prison, and every such prisoner shall on his
or her reception be informed of this Rule.

### 344.

Access to legal practitioner
(1) Reasonable facilities shall be allowed for the legal adviser of a prisoner who is
party to legal proceedings, civil or criminal to interview the prisoner with reference to
those proceedings in the sight but not the hearing of a prison officer.

(2) The legal adviser of a prisoner may, with the permission of the Superintendent,
interview the prisoner with reference to any other legal business in the sight and hearing
of a prison officer.

### 345.

Additional visits
The visits for special purposes under the last two foregoing rules shall be additional to
the visits allowed under any other of the foregoing rules.

### 346.

Payment of fines
Where a person is committed to prison for non-payment of a sum adjudged to be paid
by the conviction of any court, then, on payment to the Commissioner of Prisons of any
sum in part satisfaction of the sum so adjudged to be paid, and of the charges for which
the prisoner is liable, the term of imprisonment shall be reduced by a number of days
bearing as nearly as possible the same proportion to the total number of days to which the
prisoner is sentenced as the sum paid bears to the sum for which he or she is liable.

### 347.

Time for payment of fines
There shall be no obligation on the part of the Commissioner to receive upon a
Sunday, Christmas Day, Good Friday or any public holiday, any penalty, fine or payment
tendered to obtain the release or reduction of the term of imprisonment of any prisoner.
Fines or part payment thereof will only be received between the hours of 9 a.m. and
3 p.m. from Monday to Friday, and 9 a.m. to 12 noon on Saturday.

### 348.

Quality of food
The food provided for prisoners shall be of a nutritional value adequate for health and
strength and of wholesome quality well prepared and served and reasonably varied and in
accordance with the dietary scale approved by the Minister.

### 349.

Only prison diet allowed
No prisoner is allowed to have any food other than the normal prison diet except on
medical grounds, or as laid down by these Rules.

### 350.

Quantity of food
Except as provided under these Rules for an offence against discipline or on the
written recommendation of the Medical Officer in the case of a prisoner who persistently
wastes his or her food, or on medical grounds by directions of the Medical Officer, no
prisoner may have less food than is provided in the normal prison diet.

### 351.

Prison outfit
Every prisoner shall be provided with an outfit of day and night clothing adequate for
health, and shall wear such clothing and no other.

### 352.

Suitability of outfit
The prison clothing provided shall, where necessary include suitable protective
clothing for use at work.

## PART IV

Special Rules for Particular Classes of Prisoners

### 353.

Interpretation
In the following rules—
“Untried prisoner” means a person who has not been convicted and who has been
committed to prison—
(a)

on remand;

(b)

on committal for trial; or

(c)

on detention under the Immigration Act, Chapter 145, or any other law
authorising his or her detention.

### 354.

Untried prisoners
(1) Untried prisoners shall so far as possible be kept apart from convicted prisoners.
(2) Restrictions on the association of untried prisoners shall be limited to what is
necessary to prevent contamination or conspiracy to defeat the ends of justice.

### 355.

Payment in lieu of tasks
An untried prisoner may on the payment of a small sum to be determined by the
Superintendent be relieved of the duty of cleaning his or her cell or apartment or from
other tasks and offices.

### 356.

Supply of own food
An untried prisoner may be supplied at his or her own expense or at the expense of
friends with food and drink (cocoa, tea, coffee or milk) subject to such conditions as the
Assistant Superintendent may determine.

### 357.

Examination of food
The Assistant Superintendent may cause any such food to be examined and may at any
time, for sufficient reason, withdraw such permission.

### 358.

Wearing of own clothing
(1) An untried prisoner may wear his or her own clothing and have necessary changes
of his or her own underclothing supplied from time to time:
Provided that—
(a)

the clothing is sufficient and suitable;

(b)

it is not required for the purposes of justice; and

(c)

it shall be disinfected if the Medical Officer so requires.

(2) An untried prisoner who does not wear his or her own clothes shall be required to
wear prison dress.
(3) The privilege of wearing his or her own clothing may be forfeited for the offence
of escaping or attempting to escape from custody.

### 359.

Restriction on hair cuts, etc.
(1) An untried prisoner shall not be required to have his or her hair cut or, if he or she
usually wears a beard, etc., to shave or be shaved unless the Medical Officer deems it
necessary for the purposes of health or cleanliness, and his or her hair shall not be cut
closer than is necessary for those purposes.
(2) An untried prisoner shall not be allowed to alter his or her appearance.

### 360.

Supply of books
(1) The Superintendent may on the application of an untried prisoner allow him or her
at his or her own expense to be supplied with books, newspapers, writing materials, or
other means of occupation except such as appear objectionable or inconsistent with good
order and discipline.
(2) The privilege allowed by this Rule may at any time be withdrawn by the
Superintendent on proof of any abuse thereof.

### 361.

Work
An untried prisoner shall not be required to work in the service or industries of the
prison, but may do so with his or her own consent.

### 362.

Visits
An untried prisoner may receive not more than two visits in any one week. Such visits
may be allowed on any day except Sunday and Public holidays between the hours of 9
a.m. and 3 p.m. Not more than three persons shall be allowed to visit at one time and the
duration of each visit shall not be longer than fifteen minutes.

### 363.

Other visits
An untried prisoner may with the permission of the Superintendent be allowed to be
visited by more than three persons or for a longer period than fifteen minutes if the
Superintendent is satisfied in each case that such visit is necessary for a special reason.

### 364.

Facilities for bail
Where an untried prisoner is in prison in default of bail, facilities shall be given him or
her for the purposes of providing bail, to have an interview on any weekday at a
reasonable hour, or to communicate with friends.

### 365.

Visits by legal practitioner
An untried prisoner may for the purposes of his or her defence receive a visit from a
registered medical practitioner selected by him or her or his or her friends or his or her
legal adviser, under the same conditions as apply to a visit by the legal adviser.

### 366.

Facilities for writing
(1) An untried prisoner shall be allowed all reasonable facilities, including the
provision of writing materials, for communicating by letter with his or her friends, or for
conducting correspondence or preparing notes in his or her defence.
(2) A confidential written communication prepared as instructions for the legal
adviser of an untried prisoner may be delivered personally to such legal adviser and shall
not be treated as a letter under these Rules unless the Superintendent has reason to
suppose that it contains matter not relating to such instructions.

### 367.

Privileges
The privileges allowed to untried prisoners by rules 354 to 366 shall be liable to
forfeiture in addition to any other punishment awarded in conformity with the general
rules but forfeiture shall not affect visits or letters required for the purposes of securing
bail or preparing a defence.

### 368.

Sale of articles prohibited

The sale, transfer, or attempted sale or transfer to any person by an untried prisoner of
an article allowed to be introduced into the prison for his or her use shall be an offence
under the general rules and upon proof of the offence, those rules shall apply as they
apply to an authorised article.

### 369.

Appellant prisoners
The following rules apply to prisoners who have appealed against conviction to a
Court having jurisdiction in appeals from the Court in which they were convicted
(hereinafter called “appellants”) but prisoners who are under sentence of death shall be
subject to the special rules.

### 370.

Own clothing to be worn
An appellant when absent from prison under any order permitting his or her presence
in a Court of Appeal shall wear his or her own clothing or, if his or her clothing cannot be
used, clothing different from prison dress.

### 371.

Visits
An appellant shall be granted the same privileges relating to ordinary visits as an
untried prisoner.

### 372.

Visit by legal practitioner
An appellant may, for the purposes of his or her appeal, receive a visit from a
registered medical practitioner selected by him or her or by his or her friends or legal
adviser, under the same conditions as apply to a visit by his or her legal adviser.

### 373.

Other visits
An appellant may, for the purpose of his or her appeal, receive a visit from any other
person.

### 374.

Writing materials
(1) Writing materials to such extent as the Superintendent considers reasonable shall
be furnished to an appellant who requires them for the purposes of his or her appeal.
(2) An appellant may write a letter to his or her legal adviser or other persons for the
purposes of his or her appeal.
(3) A confidential written communication prepared as instructions for the legal
adviser of an appellant may be delivered personally to such legal adviser and shall not be
treated as a letter under these Rules unless the Superintendent has reason to suppose that
it contains matters relating to matters other than such instructions.

### 375.

Prisoners awaiting sentence
A prisoner who has been convicted and has not applied for leave to appeal, or who has
not appealed, and who is awaiting sentence or remanded on further charges shall be
treated in all respects as a convicted prisoner:
Provided that such prisoner if he or she so desires, for the purpose of preparing any
representation to the Court before which he or she is to appear to be tried, sentenced or
otherwise dealt with, be granted any or all of the facilities which are accorded to
appellants under these Rules except in relation to ordinary visits.

### 376.

Search

A prisoner under sentence of death shall immediately on his or her reception be
specially searched and every article shall be taken from him or her which the
Superintendent deems it dangerous or inexpedient to leave in his or her possession.

### 377.

Confinement to separate cell
(1) A prisoner under sentence of death shall be confined in a separate cell and shall be
kept apart from all other prisoners.
(2) He or she shall be kept by day and by night in the constant charge of a prison
officer.
(3) He or she shall not be subject to the requirements of these Rules on the subject of
work.

### 378.

Visits
(1) A prisoner under sentence of death may be visited by such of his or her relations,
friends and legal advisers as he or she desires to see and as are authorised to visit him or
her by an order in writing from the Commissioner but shall not receive a visit from more
than two persons at the same time.
(2) The Minister of the prisoner’s religion or denomination shall have free access to
every such prisoner and may visit him or her frequently.
(3) No other person except a prison officer may have access to the prisoner except by
the permission of the Commissioner.
(4) All visits to prisoners under sentence of death shall take place in the sight and
hearing of a prison officer, notwithstanding that the visit is by the prisoner’s legal adviser
or in relation to an appeal.
(5) Such a prisoner shall be allowed special facilities to correspond with his or her
legal advisers, his or her friends and relatives.
(6) During the preparation for an execution and at the time of an execution no person
may enter the prison or place of execution who is not legally entitled to do so unless in
pursuance of an order in writing from the Commissioner.
(7) A prisoner under sentence of death shall not be allowed to make or write any
statement for publication.

### 379.

Application of rules
The following rules apply to civil prisoners, that is to say, persons detained in a prison
who are not serving a sentence, are not imprisoned in default of payment of a sum
adjudged to be paid on conviction, and are not the subject of special treatment under any
of the foregoing provisions in this part of these Rules.

### 380.

Association by civil prisoners
(1) Civil prisoners may associate among themselves at such times, and in such
manner as the Commissioner determines.
(2) Where owing to the small number of civil prisoners or otherwise suitable
arrangements for association of such prisoners cannot be made, such prisoner may if he or
she so desires, with the approval of the Commissioner, be allowed to associate with
untried prisoners at such times and in such manner as the Commissioner determines.

### 381.

Clothing

(1) A civil prisoner may, if he or she so desires, wear his or her own clothing and
have necessary changes of underclothing supplied from time to time:
Provided that—
(a)

the clothing is sufficient and suitable;

(b)

it shall be disinfected if the Medical Officer so requires.

(2) A civil prisoner who does not wear his or her own clothing may be required to
wear prison dress.
(3) The privilege of wearing his or her own clothing may be forfeited by a civil
prisoner for the offence of escaping or attempting to escape from custody.

### 382.

Remission
The rules relating to remission shall not apply to civil prisoners.

### 383.

Restriction on visits
(1) A civil prisoner may be visited during such hours and under such restrictions as
the Superintendent determines and may write one letter and receive one letter each week:
Provided that the Superintendent may, in a special case, for special reasons, allow
additional letters to such reasonable extent as he or she deems advisable.
(2) The privileges shall be liable to forfeiture under these Rules provided that
notwithstanding such forfeiture the writing and receiving of one letter and the receiving
of one visit in four weeks shall be allowed.

## PART V

Miscellaneous

### 384.

Petitions to Minister
(1) A prisoner shall, if he or she wishes to complain about his or her treatment or
condition, be permitted to forward a petition to the Minister.
(2) Under no circumstances shall a prisoner be allowed to adopt any method of
bringing forward any complaint except in accordance with regular procedure.

### 385.

Prohibition on certain communications
Prisoners shall not be allowed to write to the judge or magistrate before whom they
were tried, to the Attorney-General or to other officials except the Registrar of the High
Court and the Commissioner of Police.

### 386.

Fire
If any part of the Prison is found to be on fire, or in danger from fire, the
Commissioner of Prisons is to be at once communicated with by the prison officer for the
time being in charge of the Prison. In the absence of the Commissioner the
Superintendent shall be notified.

### 387.

Safety of prisoners
If a fire occurs in any building occupied by prisoners, or a place contiguous thereto,
safety to life is the first object to be attended to by Prison Officers. The safe custody of
prisoners and the steps for extinguishing the fire will be the next consideration. The

prisoners should be at once removed to such place as the Commissioner or prison officer
in charge shall direct, according to the circumstances.

### 388.

Removal of prisoners
If a fire occurs in the infirmary, the patients shall be removed to some part of the
Prison where they may be out of danger and provided for as circumstances will permit
under the instructions of the Medical Officer, who will be at once summoned on an
outbreak of fire.

### 389.

Fire at night
If there is an outbreak of fire in the prison at night, the yard bell should be
immediately rung by the sentry on duty (three loud quick strokes). Prison officers
employed at the Prison shall, on hearing the alarm, when off-duty, hurry to the prison
with all despatch. The keys will at once be distributed to all prison officers present and to
each prison officer as he or she enters. A prison officer will be stationed at the Main Gate
to let in the fire brigade and prison officials and others authorised by the Commissioner of
Prisons and he or she shall on no account whatever admit any unauthorised persons.

### 390.

Fire Drill
All prison officers shall be instructed in Fire Drill, and shall make themselves
acquainted with the position of the hydrants in the prison in which they are employed.

### 391.

Instructions on fire to certain prisoners
Well conducted prisoners shall be instructed in the duties expected of them in case of
fire. This party of prisoners will be at once unlocked when there is a fire, and taken to the
duties in which they will have been previously trained. These prisoners will all be located
in the same building and in consecutive cells.

### 392.

Buckets, etc., for fire
Buckets, always to be kept filled, some with water and some with sand, and fireextinguishers shall be kept ready for use in easily accessible positions in different parts of
the Male and Female Prisons.

### 393.

Fire appliances
All fire appliances shall be kept in an accessible and convenient place.

### 394.

Precautions during fire
During an outbreak of fire in a prison, after due attention has been paid to the lives and
safe custody of prisoners, the prison officers shall direct their efforts to the saving of
stores, records and other property.

### 395.

Fire Orders
A copy of the Fire Orders made by the Commissioner shall be posted in each prison to
which they apply.

RICHMOND HILL PRISON (VISITING COMMITTEE) RULES
[SRO 14 of 1989. Section 51.]

[23rd June, 1989.]

1\. Citation
These Rules may be cited as the Richmond Hill Prison (Visiting Committee) Rules.
2\. Visiting Committee
(1) The Minister shall appoint a Visiting Committee (hereinafter referred to as “the
Committee”) comprising of not more than five persons, who shall be appointed to serve
for a period of two years. A member shall be eligible for re-appointment.
(2) The Minister shall appoint one member to be Chairperson.
(3) A member of the Committee may resign his or her appointment by letter
addressed to the Chairperson, and the Chairperson may resign by letter addressed to the
Minister.
(4) The Committee shall visit the Prison at such times as they may consider
necessary, but at least one visit must be made each month.
(5) The Committee shall cooperate with the Commissioner of Prisons in promoting
the efficiency of the Prison and shall make inquiry into any matter specially referred to
them by the Minister.
(6) The Committee shall assist the Commissioner of Prisons with advice and
suggestions as to the employment, education and recreation of the prisoners.
(7) The Committee shall hear and investigate any application or complaint which any
prisoner may desire to make to them and, if necessary, report the same, with their
opinion, to the Minister. They shall have free access to all parts of the Prison to all Prison
records and to all prisoners and may see any such prisoners as they desire, either in their
cells or in a room out of sight and hearing of Prison Officers.
(8) The Committee shall, at the beginning of each year, make an Annual Report on
the work done by them for the previous year and shall submit this report to the Minister
not later than 31st March.
