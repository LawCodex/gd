## CHAPTER 152B

INTERNATIONAL INSURANCE ACT
An Act to make provision respecting offshore insurance business.
[Act No. 37 of 1996 amended by Act No. 4 of 2000, Act No. 2 of 2002, Act No. 1 of 2008.]

[3rd March, 1997.]

## PART I

Preliminary

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the International Insurance Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires—
“actuary” means a person who is a member of a professional organisation of
actuaries which the Minister has specified by order published in the Gazette;

“approved form” means such as the Minister approves for the purposes of this Act;
“approved securities” means securities located or effected in Grenada or elsewhere
and approved by the Minister for the purpose of section 7(1)(b);
“auditor” means a person who is a member of one of the professional bodies for the
time being approved by the Minister;
“Authority” means the Grenada Authority for the Regulation of the Financial
Institutions established under the Grenada Authority for the Regulation of Financial
Institutions Act;
“captive insurance business” means insurance business where the insured is a
parent or affiliated company of the registered insurer or is a person in respect of whom
the registered insurer is authorised by the Supervisor to provide insurance;
“category of business” means long-term business, general business, re-insurance
business or captive insurance business, as the case may be;
“chief executive” means, in relation to a registered insurer, an employee of such
insurer, who either alone or jointly with another, is, responsible under the immediate
authority of the insurance business of that insurer;
“company” means anybody corporate formed or incorporated in Grenada;
“conditions” includes restrictions;
“contract of insurance” includes any contract the effecting of which constitutes the
carrying on of insurance business;
“controller” means a person—
(a)

in accordance with whose directions or instructions the directors of a
registered insurer are accustomed to act; or

(b)

who either alone or with any associate or associates is entitled to exercise or
control the exercise of, one-third or more of the voting power at any general
meeting of a registered insurer;

“Court” means the High Court or a Judge thereof;
“director” means any person occupying the position of director by whatever name
called and includes a person in accordance with whose directions or instructions the
directors of a company are accustomed to act;
“domestic business” means insurance business where, whether the contract of
insurance is made in Grenada or elsewhere, the subject matter of the contract is
either—
(a)

property that at the time of making of the contract in, or in transit to or from
Grenada;

(b)

the life, safety, fidelity or insurable interest of an individual who at the time
of the making of the contract is ordinarily resident in Grenada; or

(c)

a risk of a company formed in Grenada that is not an international company
or a foreign company;

“insurance” includes assurance and re-insurance;
“insurance business” means the business of effecting and carrying our contracts—
(a)

protecting persons against loss or liability to loss in respect of risks to which
such persons may be exposed; or

to pay a sum of money or other thing of value upon the happening of an
event,

and includes “re-insurance business”;

“insurance manager” means a person, not being an employee of an insurer, who—

provides management services for one or more insurers; or

holds himself or herself out as a manager in relation to one or more insurers,
but does not include the keeping of insurance business accounts for a
registered insurer;

“insurer” means a person effecting or carrying on insurance business and except
where otherwise stated, includes each member of an association of underwriters;
“international company” means a company incorporated under the International
Companies Act, Chapter 152;
“International Companies Act” means the International Companies Act,

## Chapter 152;

“licensed insurer” means an insurer licensed under section 8 of this Act to carry on
offshore insurance business;
“long-term business” means insurance business of any of the following kinds,
namely—
(a)

affecting and carrying out contracts of insurance on human life or contracts
to pay annuities on human life;

(b)

effecting and carrying out contracts of insurance against risks of the persons
insured sustaining injury as the result of an accident, or of an accident of a
specified class, or dying as the result of an accident or of an accident of a
specified class, becoming incapacitated in consequence of disease, or disease
of a specified class, being contracts that are expressed to be in effect for a
period of not less than five years or without limit of time and either not
expressed to be terminable by the insurer before the expiration of five years
from the taking effect thereof or are expressed to be so terminable before the
expiration of that period only in special circumstances therein mentioned;

(c)

effecting and carrying out contracts of insurance, whether effected by the
issue of policies, bonds or endowment certificates or otherwise, hereby in
return for one or more premiums paid to the insurer a sum or a series of
sums is to become payable to the persons insured in the future, not being
contracts such as fall within either paragraph (a) or (b); and

(d)

any kind of insurance business declared by regulations to be long-term
business;

“long-term business fund” means a fund established pursuant to section 16(2) of
this Act;
“Minister” means the Minister of Finance;
“mutual company” means a company whose capital is owned by the policy-holders
of that company and includes a company which has agreed to purchase back or is in
the process of purchasing back its share capital at a fixed price and is recognised by
the Registrar as a mutual company;
“offshore insurance business” means the carrying on or the conducting whether
within or outside Grenada of any insurance business where each of the insured, the
person to whom the policy monies are payable and the owner of the policy or any one
or more of such persons—
(a)

is not domiciled in Grenada;

(b)

is not ordinarily resident in Grenada;

(c)

is not a company incorporated or registered in Grenada under any legislation
other than the International Companies Act, Chapter 152; or

(d)

is a trustee company;

“owner”, in relation to a policy, means the person who for the time being has the
legal title to the policy and includes an assignee;
“policy” means—

any policy or other contract of insurance and any policy, bond, certificate,
receipt or other instrument evidencing the contract with the insurer or
underwriter or any person named in a policy or the contract of insurance who
is liable to pay or contribute towards payment of the sum secured by the
policy or contract;

(b)

an instrument evidencing a contract to pay an annuity;

(c)

any policy or other contract in relation to insurance business of any class
under which there is for the time being an existing liability already accrued
or under which a liability may accrue;

“policy holder” means a person who for the time being is the legal holder of a
policy for securing a contract with an insurer, including an assignee, and—
(a)

in relation to such long-term business as consists in the granting of annuities
upon human life, includes an annuitant; and

(b)

in relation to insurance business of any kind, includes a person to whom,
under a policy, a benefit is due or a periodic payment is payable;

“prescribe” or “prescribed” means prescribe or prescribed in orders or regulations
under this Act;
“registered insurance manager” is a person registered under section 21 of this Act;
“Registrar” means the Registrar of Offshore Services appointed or designated
under section 3 of the Offshore Banking Act, Chapter 217A;
“regulations” means regulations made under section 39 of this Act;
“re-insurance business” means insurance business where the risk insured by a
person is a risk that person has accepted from an insurer;
“related company” has the meaning given by the Companies Act, Chapter 58A;
“solvency margin” shall have the meaning ascribed to it by section 18(1) of this
Act;
“statutory fund” means a fund established pursuant to section 16(1) of this Act;
“year” means a calendar year.

## PART II

Administration of Act

### 3.

Administration of Act
The Registrar shall be responsible for the administration of this Act and shall carry out
the duties and functions vested in him or her by or under this or any other enactment.

### 4.

Registrar’s powers of inspection, etc.
(1) For the purpose of ascertaining whether a registered insurer is complying with the
provisions of this Act the Registrar or any person authorised by him or her may inspect
any book, minute book, register or record required by or under this Act to be kept by the
registered insurer.

(2) A person who, except for the purposes of this Act, or when properly ordered to do
so by the Court, makes a record of, divulges or communicates to any other person, any
information which he or she has acquired by reason of such inspection, commits an
offence under this Act.
(3) A registered insurer or any official thereof shall, on being required by the
Registrar or person so authorised by the Registrar, produce any such book, register or
record.
(4) A registered insurer or any officer thereof shall not obstruct or hinder the
Registrar or person so authorised while exercising any of the powers conferred in him or
her by this section.
(5) For the purposes of this section, “Authority” means the Grenada Authority for the
Regulation of the Financial Institutions established under the Grenada Authority for the
Regulation of Financial Institutions Act, Chapter 125A.

### 5.

Licensing of International Insurance Companies
(1) No person, shall carry on or transact or hold himself or herself out as carrying on
or transacting any offshore insurance business from within Grenada unless that person
holds a valid licence issued under this Part of this Act and its memorandum and articles
of association have been consented to by the Minister.
(2) A person who contravenes this section commits an offence under this Act.

### 6.

Applications of licence
(1) Applications for licence to carry on an offshore insurance business shall be made
in writing on the approved form to the Minister and accompanied by—
(a)

a certified copy of the Act, charter, deed of settlement, memorandum of
association and articles of association of the body corporate, or other
document or documents by which the body corporate is constituted;

(b)

such fee as may be prescribed by regulations;

(c)

such evidence as the Minister may require—
(i) to show that the applicant has complied with its obligations under the
International Company Act, Chapter 152,
(ii) of the nature and charter of the applicant’s business,
(iii) of the financial standing of the applicant,
(iv) of the share-holding and management of the applicant, and
(v) of the ultimate beneficial ownership of the stocks or shares of the
applicant;

(d)

the address of its registered office in Grenada;

(e)

such references as the Supervisor may require; and

(f)

such other information as may be required by regulations.

(2) In addition to the documents, information and evidence which shall accompany an
application under subsection (1) of this section, an applicant shall if required to do so by
the Minister, furnish such additional documents, information, or evidence as the Minister
may require.
(3) An application made under this section must be submitted through the Executive
Director appointed under the Grenada Authority for the Regulation of Financial
Institutions Act, Chapter 125A.

### 7.

Refusal of registration
(1) The Minister shall not register any registration applicant under section 8 if the
applicant—

is not international company or a foreign company; or

does not satisfy the Minister that the amount paid up on its share capital—
(i) in a case where the applicant proposes to carry on long-term business
but no other category of business, is two million dollars or more,
(ii) in a case where the applicant proposes to carry on general business but
no other category of business, is three million dollars or more,
(iii) in a case where the applicant proposes to carry on re-insurance
business but no other category of business, is one million dollars or
more,
(iv) in a case where the applicant proposes to carry on captive insurance
business but no other category of business is a minimum of one
million three hundred and fifty thousand dollars or more,
(v) in a case where the applicant proposes to carry on more than one
category of business is the aggregate of the amounts required for each
category,
and subject to subsection (2) does not satisfy the Minister that the minimum
amount paid up on its share capital is maintained in approved securities;

(c)

does not satisfy the Minister that the controller, directors and chief
executive, if any, of the applicant are fit and proper persons to be associated
with insurance companies;

(d)

does not satisfy the Minister that it has, or has available, adequate
knowledge and expertise of the insurance business to be carried on;

(e)

does not satisfy the Minister that it has either—
(i) management in Grenada with adequate knowledge and expertise of
the insurance business to be carried on and has at least one director
resident in Grenada, or
(ii) appointed a registered insurance manager in Grenada with adequate
knowledge and experience of the insurance business to be carried on;

(f)

does not comply with the criteria contained in regulations;

(ff)

is not a fit and proper person to carry on insurance business; or

(g)

it is in the public interest to refuse the registration.

(2) The requirement to maintain minimum paid up share capital approved securities
shall not apply where the applicant does not propose to carry on any insurance business
other than re-insurance business or captive insurance business.
(3) It is a condition of registration that the licensee will not carry any activities from
within Grenada, otherwise than in connection with or for the purpose of the particular
offshore insurance business in respect of which it is registered.

### 8.

Registration
(1) In relation to every application for registration the Minister shall—

issue licence to the applicant authorising it to carry on, subject to such terms
and conditions as he or she thinks are appropriate, a category or categories of
insurance business;

refuse the application; or

(c)

defer the application and direct that such further enquiries as he or she
considers necessary be made.

(2) Where the Minister refuses an application he or she shall notify the applicant in
writing of the refusal but he or she shall not be required to give reasons therefor.
(3) Where an application is refused any application fee, less a prescribed
administration charge, shall be refunded.
(4) If any terms and conditions are imposed under subsection (1)(a) of this section,
the Minister may, from time to time, vary or revoke any of those terms or conditions or
impose any additional conditions.

### 9.

Renewal of licence
(1) The first licence shall be valid for twelve months from the date of the issue of the
licence and shall be renewable thereafter for further period of twelve months from each
anniversary of the date of registration upon due payment of the prescribed annual renewal
fee.
(2) Where application to renew the licence is not made before the date of expiry, or
the annual renewal fee is not paid prior to that date, a renewal licence may nevertheless
be issued, on payment of the prescribed penalties provided that—
(i) the licence has not been cancelled or revoked,
(ii) a renewal application is made and the annual renewal fee is paid, within one
month after the date of expiry of the licence, and
(iii) the Registrar is satisfied that there were good and sufficient reasons as to
why an application for renewal or payment of the renewal fee was not made
prior to the date of expiry of the licence.
(3) Every licence shall show on its face the date of expiry.
(4) Any director or officer of a registered insurer who knowingly permits the
company to continue to carry on its insurance business after the date of expiry of its
licence and before a current licence has been issued commits an offence under this Act.

### 10.

Cancellation, suspension of licence
(1) A licence may be cancelled or suspended or made subject to conditions by the
Minister at any time for such period as the Minister deems fit, where there has been any
change in the circumstances of the registered insurer (including any change in shareholding, management, or beneficial ownership) which would have caused the Minister to
have refused an application for registration if such changed circumstances had been part
of the original application or where the registered insurer—
(a)

fails to furnish any document, information or evidence which he or she has
been required to furnish;

(b)

fails to notify the Registrar of any material changes which have taken place,
as required by section 13;

(c)

has a winding-up order made against it;

(d)

fails to comply with any term or condition subject to which the licence was
issued;

(e)

is in breach of any duty or obligation imposed upon it by this Act or commits
an offence under this Act;

(f)

ceases to transact offshore insurance business from within Grenada and
requests that the licence be cancelled; or

(g)

conducts its affairs in a manner which is or might be prejudicial to the
interest of present or potential policy holders and the general public.

(2) Where the Minister intends to suspend or cancel or impose conditions on a licence
under subsection (1) he or she shall cause notice to be given to the registered insurer of
his or her intention and reasonable opportunity to show cause as to why the licence
should not be suspended or cancelled or made subject to conditions.
(3) Where the Supervisor has given notice to the registered insurer of his or her
intention to suspend or cancel or impose conditions on the licence under subsection (2)
the Minister, after considering any submissions and evidence by the registered insurer
may—
(a)

suspend or cancel or impose conditions on the licence;

(b)

alter his or her intention by suspending instead of cancelling the licence or
vice versa; or

(c)

not proceed with his or her intention to suspend or cancel or impose
conditions on the licence,

and he or she shall give notice to the registered insurer of his or her decision and such
decision shall take effect from such date as is specified by the Minister in the notice.
(4) Where the Minister gives notice to the registered insurer of his or her decision to
suspend or cancel or impose conditions on the licence under subsection (3) of this section,
the registered insurer shall return the licence of the Registrar forthwith.

### 11.

Appeal against cancellation, suspension or imposition of conditions on licence
(1) Any person who is aggrieved by the cancellation, suspension or imposition of
conditions on a licence may, within fourteen days of the giving of the notice under
section 10(3) of this Act, appeal against the cancellation, suspension or imposition of
conditions to a Judge in chambers whose decision thereon shall be final.
(2) Pending the determination of any appeal under subsection (1) of this section, the
Judge may at any time and from time to time, defer the cancellation, suspension or
imposition of conditions which is the subject of the appeal.

### 12.

Place of business
(1) A registered insurer shall transact business only through its registered office in
Grenada, through the office of a trustee company or company licensed to engage in
company management unless it applies for and receives the prior written permission of
the Minister to maintain a permanent establishment in Grenada and the Minister may
grant the permission upon such terms and conditions as he or she thinks fit.
(2) Unless otherwise approved the principal place of business of the registered insurer
shall be its registered office in Grenada, the address of the trustee company or company
licensed to engage in company management as the case may be.

### 13.

Notification of changes
A registered insurer who proposes to make an alteration in the Act, charter, deed of
settlement, memorandum of association, articles of association or other document by
which it was constituted a body corporate, must submit for the approval of the Registrar
particulars in writing of the proposed alteration.

## PART III

Records, Accounts and Long-Term Business

### 14.

Keeping of business records
(1) In this section—
“accounts” means profit and loss accounts and balance sheets, and includes notes
(other than directors’ reports) attached to, or intended to be read with, any of those
profit and loss accounts or balance sheets;
“business records” includes accounting, policy and claims records of the registered
insurer and such working papers and other documents as are necessary to explain the
methods and calculations by which its accounts are made up.
(2) A registered insurer shall keep and maintain—
(a)

such business record as correctly records and explain its transactions and
financial position;

(b)

its business records in such a manner as will enable true and fair accounts to
be prepared from time to time;

(c)

its business records in such a manner as will allow its accounts to be
conveniently and properly audited; and

(d)

its business records at its principal place of business in Grenada unless
otherwise authorised in writing by the Minister.

(3) Any registered insurer who fails to comply with this section commits an offence
under this Act.

### 15.

Annual accounts
(1) The provisions of the International Companies Act, Chapter 152, relating to
accounts and records except to the extent that they are inconsistent with this Act apply to
registered insurers.
(2) In this section, “annual accounts” means—
(a)

the revenue or fund account for the year;

(b)

the profit and loss account for the year or, in the case of a registered insurer
not trading for profit, income and expenditure account for the year; and

(c)

the balance sheet as at the end of the year in such form (being a form
calculated to enable comparisons to be made between the registered insurer’s
business for the year in respect of which the statements are prepared and the
registered insurer’s business for the year immediately preceding that year)
and shall contain such information, as may be prescribed.

(3) A copy of the registered insurer’s audited annual accounts shall be submitted to
the Registrar within twenty-one days after the date of the meeting at which the accounts
were approved by the board of directors and in any even not later than six months after
the close of the financial year to which they relate.
(4) Where the audited accounts are submitted later than six months after the close of
the financial year to which they relate a penalty to be prescribed shall be payable.
(5) Such statement, reports, certificate and information as may be required by
regulations made under this Act; or required by any other enactment to be annexed or
attached to the annual accounts for any purpose shall be produced to the Registrar at the
same time as the annual accounts are submitted.
(6) For the purpose of this section, an audit shall be carried out by a registered
company auditor approved for that purpose by the Minister.
(7) Any registered insurer who wilfully fails to comply with subsection (3) or (5) of
this section, commits an offence under this Act.

### 16.

Statutory and long-term business fund
(1) Subject to this section—

a registered insurer may establish and maintain one or more statutory funds,
under an appropriate name, in respect of such part or parts of the offshore
insurance business carried on by it, as the registered insurer so determines;

(b)

where a registered insurer has established a statutory fund, it shall determine
as soon as practicable thereafter what part or parts of its offshore insurance
business relate to that statutory fund;

(c)

a registered insurer shall not determine that a particular policy or policies
issued by it relate to a particular statutory fund unless the owner of the
policy or the person who upon the issue of the policy will become the owner
has consented in writing to the application of this section to the policy;

(d)

all amounts received by a registered insurer in respect of any part of its
offshore insurance business in respect of which it has established a statutory
fund, shall be carried to and become assets of that fund, including any
income arising from investment of the assets of that statutory fund;

(e)

the whole or any part of the assets of a statutory fund may, from time to
time, be applied or invested by a registered insurer in or towards the
payment of any premium or other fee or payment (each of whatsoever
nature) payable in order to effect or maintain, or in respect of effecting or
maintaining, re-insurance in respect of that statutory fund.

(2) Where a registered insurer transacts or carries on long-term insurance business,
the insurer shall establish a statutory fund to be known as the long-term business fund,
into which all receipts in respect of each subclass of such business shall be paid.
(3) No transfer of assets from a long-term business fund other than a transfer in
accordance with subsection (7) shall be effected without—
(i) the authority of a resolution of the directors of the registered insurer, and
(ii) the written consent of the actuary to the registered insurer.
(4) No registered insurer transacting or carrying on long-term business shall declare
or pay a dividend to any person other than a policy holder unless—
(a)

at a date within a year immediately preceding the declaration of the
dividend, the value of the assets in its long-term business fund as certified by
the actuary to the registered insurer, exceeds the extent (as so certified) of
the liabilities of the registered insurer’s long-term business; and

(b)

the amount of such dividend shall not be such as to cause the margin of
solvency of the registered insurer to fall below the amount required under
section 18.

(5) Every registered insurer which has established any statutory fund or long-term
business fund in respect of any part of its offshore business shall—
(a)

keep its business accounts in respect of such insurance business separate
from any business accounts kept in respect of any other insurance business;

(b)

maintain books of accounts and other records in such a manner that the
assets of any statutory or long-term business fund established pursuant to
this section and the liabilities referable to the offshore insurance business to
which any statutory or long-term business fund relates, can be readily
identified at any time.

(6) The assets of a statutory or long-term business fund shall not be available to meet
any liabilities or expenses of the registered insurer other than the liabilities or expenses

referable to that part of the insurance business to which the statutory or long-term
business fund relates.
(7) The restriction imposed by subsection (6) of this section shall not apply to so
much of those assets as are certified by the actuary to the insurer to exceed the extent (as
certified) of the liabilities of the registered insurer’s long-term business.
(8) A registered insurer shall not mortgage or charge any assets of any statutory or
long-term business fund other than with the consent of all owners of policies issued in
respect of the insurance business to which that statutory of long-term business fund relates.
(9) The assets of a statutory or long-term business fund shall be kept distinct and
separate from all other assets of the registered insurer; and where a registered insurer has
established a statutory or long-term business fund in accordance with this section—
(a)

no assets of the registered insurer other than those comprising and
constituting the statutory or long-term business fund shall be available to
meet any liabilities or expenses (each of whatever nature) any way referable
to the insurance business to which the statutory or long-term business fund
relates; and

(b)

no proceedings of any nature whatsoever seeking to recover from such
registered insurer any assets or amount of the statutory or long-term business
fund a value thereof shall be taken in any way against or in respect of such
registered insurer in any Court and no judgement of any Court shall issue
against any such registered insurer or be enforced in Grenada against such
registered insurer for any monies or assets other than those of the statutory or
long-term business fund.

(10) Any registered insurer who contravenes the provisions of this section commits an
offence under this Act.

### 17.

Transfer of long-term business
(1) Where it is proposed to carry out a scheme under which the whole or part of the
long-term business carried on by a registered insurer (“the transferor”) is to be transferred
to another registered insurer (“the transferee”) the transferor or transferee must seek the
approval of the Registrar.
(2) The Registrar may require a report on the terms of the scheme by an independent
actuary (not being an employee of, nor actuary to, either registered insurer) and must be
satisfied that the requirements of subsection (3) of this section have been complied with
or that in the circumstances compliance is unnecessary.
(3) The requirements referred to in subsection (2) of this section are—
(a)

that a notice has been published in such two newspapers circulating in
Grenada as the Registrar shall approve stating that the application has been
made and giving the address of the offices at which, and the period for
which, copies of the documents mentioned in paragraph (d) of this
subsection will be available as required by that paragraph;

(b)

that the statement—
(i) setting out the terms of the scheme, and
(ii) containing a summary of the report mentioned in subsection (2)
sufficient to indicate the opinion of the actuary on the likely effects of
the scheme on the long-term policy holders of the registered insurers
concerned, has been sent to each of those policy holders and to every
member of those registered insurers;

(c)

that a copy of the application and of the report mentioned in subsection (2)
of this section and of any statement sent out under paragraph (b) of this

subsection has been served on the Registrar and that a period of not less than
three weeks has elapsed since the date of such service; and
(d)

that copies of the application and of the report mentioned in subsection (2) of
this section have been open to inspection at the office in Grenada of the
registered insurers concerned or their representatives for a period of not less
than three weeks beginning with the date of the publication of a notice in
accordance with paragraph (a) of this subsection.

(4) Each of the registered insurers concerned shall furnish a copy of the application
and of the report mentioned in subsection (2) of this section to any person who asks for
one at any time before approval is given under this section.
(5) On any application under this section—
(a)

the Registrar; and

(b)

any person (including any employee of the transferor or the transferee who
alleges that he or she would be adversely affected by the carrying out of the
scheme),

and be entitled to be heard.
(6) The Registrar may approve the scheme if he or she is satisfied that the transferee
is, or immediately after the making of the Order will be authorised to carry on any longterm business to be transferred under the scheme.
(7) Where the Registrar approves a scheme under this section he or she may impose
conditions relating to any of the following matters—
(a)

the transfer to the transferee of the whole or any part of the undertaking and
of the property or liabilities of the transferor;

(b)

the allotting or appropriation by the transferee of any shares, debentures,
policies or other like interests in the transferee which under the scheme are
to be allotted or appropriated by the transferee to or for any person;

(c)

such incidental, consequential and supplementary matters as are necessary to
secure that the scheme shall be fully and effectively carried out.

(8) Any person who fails to comply with this section commits an offence under this
Act.
(9) In this section, “property” includes property, rights and powers of every
description; “liabilities” include duties, and “share” and “debenture” have the same
meaning as in the International Companies Act, Chapter 152.

### 18.

Solvency margins
(1) Subject to subsection (7) of this section every registered insurer shall ensure that
the value of its assets exceed the amount of its liabilities (“the margin of solvency”) by
such amount as may be prescribed by or determined in accordance with regulations made
for the purposes of this section.
(2) If the solvency margin of a registered insurer falls below such amount as may be
prescribed by or determined in accordance with regulations made for the purposes of this
section, the registered insurer shall at the request of the Registrar submit to him or her a
short-term financial scheme within thirty days of the request, or such longer periods as
the Registrar may permit and shall if the Registrar considers the scheme (or the scheme as
previously modified) inadequate, propose modifications to it.
(3) Where a registered insurer is required to maintain separate solvency margins in
respect of long-term and general business, subsection (2) of this section shall have effect
as if any reference to the solvency margin of the registered insurer is a reference to the
aggregate of those margins.

(4) A scheme submitted under subsection (2) of this section, may include proposals
for—

the appointment of a special manager acceptable to the Registrar; and

the discontinuance, either in whole or in part, of the registered insurer’s
business.

(5) A registered insurer shall give effect to any scheme accepted by the Registrar.
(6) If a registered insurer is unable or fails to submit a scheme which is acceptable to
the Registrar, the registered insurer shall be deemed for the purposes of the International
Company Act, Chapter 152, to be unable to pay its debts.
(7) This section shall not apply to a registered insurer who carries on only reinsurance business or captive insurance business.

### 19.

Solvency margins for re-insurer and captive insurer
Every registered insurer who carries on only re-insurance business or captive
insurance business shall if required by the Registrar in writing—
(a)

have such surplus of tangible assets or other assets as may be approved by
the Registrar over liabilities as may, from time to time, be prescribed by
regulations; and

(b)

demonstrate to the satisfaction of the Registrar its financial stability.

## PART IV

Regulation of Registered Insurers

### 20.

Advertising
(1) No advertising shall be used by a registered insurer which is likely to mislead, or
deceive prospective policy holders with respect to the registered insurer’s assets,
corporate structure, financial standing, age or relative position in the insurance business
or in any other material respect.
(2) Any person who contravenes this section commits an offence under this Act.

### 21.

Insurance managers
(1) No person shall act as an insurance manager for, or in relation to, a registered
insurer, unless that person—
(a)

is registered in a register to be kept by the Registrar in accordance with
regulations and has paid to the Registrar the prescribed registration fee; or

(b)

is exempted from registration by such regulation; and is either an
international company or a foreign company.

(2) An applicant for registration shall provide such documents and information as
may be required by the Registrar including, without limiting the generality of the
foregoing, such documents and information as the Registrar may require to satisfy
himself or herself that the applicant is a fit and proper person and has or has available
adequate knowledge and expertise so as to provide satisfactory insurance management
services in Grenada.
(3) Sections 22, 23 and 24 of this Act shall apply, with the necessary modifications in
relation to any person acting as an insurance manager as they apply to a registered
insurer.

(4) The Registrar may if he or she thinks it proper to do so—

refuse to register any applicant for registration; and

cancel any registration at any time.

(5) Where a person carries on a business which in any respect is or in the opinion of
the Registrar appears to be, of a similar character to the business carried on by an
insurance manager, that person upon a declaration made by the Registrar in writing shall
be subject to the provisions of this section and shall be deemed to be acting as an
insurance manager.
(6) A person contravenes subsection (1) if he or she wilfully—
(a)

takes or uses any style, title or description which consists of or includes the
expression “insurance manager” when he or she is not so registered; or

(b)

takes or uses any style, title or description falsely implying or otherwise
pretends, that he or she is registered.

### 22.

Management
(1) Subject to subsection (2) of this section every registered insurer shall—
(a)

have management in Grenada; or

(b)

appoint a registered insurance manager in Grenada with adequate knowledge
and experience of the insurance business carried on.

(2) A registered insurer who is authorised to carry on only captive insurance business
shall appoint a registered insurance manager in Grenada with adequate knowledge and
experience of the insurance business carried on or to be carried on, unless it is authorised
by the Minister to maintain a permanent establishment pursuant to section 12(1) of this
Act.

### 23.

Power to obtain information
(1) The Registrar may require a registered insurer to provide him or her, at specified
times or intervals, with information about specified matters relating to the insurer being,
if he or she so requires, information verified in a specified manner or provided in a
specified form.
(2) Where by virtue of subsection (1) the Registrar has required a registered insurer to
provided information about specified matters and the registered insurer has failed to
provide that information, the Registrar may require the registered insurer’s actuary,
auditor, insurance manager or banker to provide such information about those matters as
is within his or her knowledge.
(3) The Registrar may require a registered insurer to produce, at such time and place
as he or she may specify, such documents or information as he or she determines.
(4) Where by virtue of subsection (3) the Registrar has power to require the
production of any document from any registered insurer, the Registrar shall have the like
power to require the production of that document from any person who appears to him or
her to be in possession of it; but where any person from whom such production is
required claims a lien on any document produced by him or her, the production shall be
without prejudice to the lien.
(5) Any power conferred by virtue of subsection (3) or (4) to require a registered
insurer or other person to produce any document or information shall include a power to
take copies of it or extracts from it.
(6) In this section and in section 24, references to the Registrar shall include any
person appointed in writing by the Minister to carry out the duties of the Registrar.

(7) Any person who fails to supply information or to produce any document when
required to do so by the Registrar in accordance with this section, commits an offence
under this Act.
(8) The power conferred by this section and section 24 may be exercised in relation to
a registered insurer whose licence has been cancelled or suspended but shall not be
exercised after that registered insurer has ceased to have any liability in respect of any
contract of insurance which was entered into at a time when it was registered.

### 24.

Inspection and investigation
(1) The Minister may designate any qualified person as an Inspector to investigate the
affairs or any part of the affairs of a registered insurer and to inspect the registered
insurer’s business and accounting records and accounts and such other records as may
have been kept by the registered insurer in the course of its offshore insurance business, if
he or she has reasons to believe that the registered insurer is carrying on its business in a
manner prejudicial to the interest of its policy holders or of persons who may become
policy holders or that may have insufficient assets to cover its liabilities and claims by
policy holders, or either in Grenada or elsewhere it may be contravening any of the
provisions of this Act.
(2) It shall be the duty of the registered insurer under investigation and of all past and
prevent officers of the registered insurer and of any related company which has been
involved in the offshore insurance business transactions of the registered insurer, to
produce to the Inspector all books, records and documents relating to the registered
insurer or related company under investigation which are in their custody or control and
otherwise to give the Inspector all assistance in connection with the investigation which
they are reasonably able to give.
(3) The Inspector shall have every such power of entry and access as may be
necessary for the purposes of this section, and he or she may take possession of all such
books, accounts and documents as, and for so long as, may be necessary for the purposes
of his or her inspection and investigation.
(4) The Inspector shall exercise his or her right of entry and access under subsection (3)
during the normal office hours of the registered insurer, unless otherwise authorised by
the Court.
(5) The Inspector may take copies of all books, accounts and documents of the
registered insurer in his or her possession for the purposes of his or her inspection and
investigation under this section.
(6) The Minister, may if he or she thinks fit, order the registered insurer or any related
company whose officers are investigated under the provisions of this section to pay all or
any of the expenses properly incurred in connection with the investigation or in
connection with the proceedings instituted as a result of the investigation.
(7) Any person who—
(a)

wilfully obstructs the Inspector acting in the execution of his or her powers
under this section;

(b)

refuses or fails or neglects to produce any books, accounts, documents or
information required under this section; or

(c)

knowingly produces or causes or permits to be produced any book account,
document or information required under this section which he or she knows
to be false in a material particular,

commits an offence under this Act.
(8) The Inspector shall have the same power in respect of the supply of information
and production of documents as are given to the Registrar under section 23.

### 25.

Preservation of confidentiality
(1) No person who has acquired information in his or her capacity as—

director, officer, employee or agent of any licensee or former licensee;

counsel, attorney, consultant or auditor of the Authority or employee or
agent of such counsel, attorney, consultant or auditor;

(c)

counsel, attorney, consultant, auditor, accountant, receiver or liquidator of
any licensee or former licensee or employee or agent of such counsel,
attorney, consultant, auditor, accountant, receiver or liquidator;

(d)

auditor of any customer of any licensee or former licensee or employee or
agent of such auditor;

(e)

an Inspector under the provisions of this Act;

(f)

a director, officer, employee or agent of the Authority,

shall, without the express or implied consent of the customer concerned or of the Authority,
disclose to any person any such information relating to the identity, assets, liabilities,
transactions or accounts of a customer of a licensee or relating to any application by any
person under the provisions of this Act, as the case may be, except—
(i) for the purpose of the performance of his or her duties or the exercise
of his or her functions under this Act, if any,
(ii) for the purpose of the performance of his or her duties within the
scope of his or her employment,
(iii) when a licensee is lawfully required to make disclosure by any court
of competent jurisdiction in Grenada or under the provisions of any
law of Grenada,
(iv) for the purpose of enabling or assisting the Authority to exercise any
functions conferred upon it by any written law,
(v) to a person with a view to the institution of, or for the purpose of—
(a) criminal proceedings,
(b) disciplinary proceedings, whether within or outside of Grenada
relating to the exercise by a counsel, attorney, auditor, accountant,
valuer or actuary of his or her professional duties, or
(c) disciplinary proceedings relating to the discharge of his or her
functions by an employee of the Authority.
(2) Subsection (1) does not apply in any case where, in accordance with such conditions
as the Authority determines, information of such class or classes as the Authority may,
from time to time, approve is transmitted from a licensee o the head office or a branch or
subsidiary of that licensee outside of Grenada solely for the purposes of carrying out
collation, synthesis or processing of information on behalf of the licensee.
(3) In any civil proceedings where information is likely to be disclosed in relation to a
customer’s bank account, the proceedings may, if the court of its own motion or on the
application of a party to the proceedings so orders, be held in camera and the information
shall be confidential as between the court and the parties thereto.
(4) No person shall publish the name, address or photograph of any parties to the
proceedings referred to in subsection (3) or any information likely to lead to the
identification of the parties thereto either during the currency of the proceedings or after
they have been terminated.
(5) The Authority may provide information on the beneficial owners, directors,
officers and operations of a licensee (including any report produced pursuant to any
inspection or examination of the licensee) to the home supervisor or host supervisor, as

the case may be, which is responsible for regulating the head office of the licensee for the
purpose of consolidated supervision of the licensee.
(6) To facilitate regulatory co-operation within Grenada, the Authority may provide
information on the beneficial owners, directors, officers and operations of a licensee and
any of its affiliates to the domestic regulatory authorities specified in subsection (9).
(7) Nothing in this section—
(a)

prejudices or derogates from the rights and duties subsisting at common law
between a licensee and its customer;

(b)

prevents a licensee from providing upon a legitimate business request in the
normal course of business a general credit rating with respect to a customer;
or

(c)

prevents the Authority from sharing information relating to the identity,
assets, liabilities, transactions or accounts of a customer of a licensee with
the Financial Intelligence Unit where it is believed that a suspicious
transaction was not reported as required under the Money Laundering
(Prevention) Act, Chapter 197A, in accordance with the Anti-Money
Laundering Guidelines issued by the Supervisory Authority.

(8) A person who contravenes the provisions of subsection (1) commits an offence
and is liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of thirty thousand dollars and to
imprisonment for two years.
(9) The regulatory agencies for the purposes of subsection (6) are the Financial
Intelligence Unit, the Supervisory Authority and any other agency established by law
from time to time.
(10) For the purposes of this section—
(a)

“Financial Intelligence Unit” means the officers of the Royal Grenada Police
Force assigned to assist the Supervisory Authority in the execution of its
duties under the Money Laundering (Prevention) Act, Chapter 197A;

(b)

“Supervisory Authority” means the Supervisory Authority appointed by the
Minister of Finance pursuant to section 11 of the Money Laundering
(Prevention) Act, Chapter 197A.

### 26.

Winding-up of registered insurer
(1) The provisions of the International Companies Act, Chapter 152, shall apply to the
winding-up of a registered insurer registered under this Act, so far as they are applicable,
subject to the provision of this section and sections 27, 28 and 29.
(2) A petition for the winding-up of registered insurer shall not be presented, other
than by the Registrar except by leave of the Court.

### 27.

Winding-up on petition of Registrar
(1) The Registrar may present a petition for the winding-up of a registered insurer or
its business within Grenada on any ground provided by and in accordance with the
provisions of the International Companies Act on the grounds—
(a)

that the registered insurer has failed to satisfy an obligation to which it is or
was subject by virtue of this Act;

(b)

that the registered insurer being under an obligation imposed by this Act
with respect to the keeping of business and accounting records, had failed to
satisfy that obligation or to produce records kept in satisfaction of that
obligation and the Registrar is unable to ascertain its financial position.

(2) In any proceedings on a petition to wind up a registered insurer presented by the
Registrar under subsection (1) of this section evidence that such registered insurer was
insolvent at the close of the period to which the annual accounts of the registered insurer
last submitted under section 15 relate shall be evidence that the registered insurer
continues to be unable to pay its debts, unless the contrary is proved.

### 28.

Winding-up of insurer with long-term business
(1) No registered insurer which carries on long-term business shall, except with the
prior approval of the Registrar, be wound up voluntarily.
(2) Subject to subsection (3), in any winding-up—
(a)

the assets in a registered insurer’s long-term business fund or funds shall be
available only for meeting the liabilities and expenses of the registered
insurer attributable to such long-term business for which the particular fund
or funds were created;

(b)

the other assets of the registered insurer shall be available only for meeting
the liabilities and expenses of the registered insurer attributable to its other
business.

(3) Where the value of the assets mentioned in either of the paragraph of subsection (2)
exceeds the amount of the liabilities and expenses mentioned in those paragraphs, the
restriction imposed by that subsection shall not apply to so much of those assets as
represents the excess.
(4) Where under any enactment money or property is ordered to be repaid or restored
to a registered insurer or any sum is ordered to be contributed to its assets then, if, and in
so far as the wrongful act which is the reason for the making of the Order relates to assets
representing a fund or funds maintained by the registered insurer in respect of its longterm business, the Court shall in respects of its direction that the money, property or
contribution shall be treated for the purposes of this Act as assets of that fund or funds
and this Act shall have effect accordingly.

### 29.

Continuation of long-term business insurers in liquidation
(1) This section has effect in relation to the winding-up of a registered insurer
carrying on long-term business.
(2) The liquidator shall, unless the Court otherwise orders, carry on the long-term
business of the registered insurer with a view to its being transferred as a going concern
to another international company or foreign company and, in carrying on that business as
aforesaid, the liquidator may agree to the variation of any contracts of insurance in
existence at the commencement or the winding-up but shall not effect any new contracts
of insurance.
(3) If the liquidator is satisfied that the interest of the creditors in respect of liabilities
of the registered insurer attributable to its long-term business requires the appointment of
a special manager of the registered insurer’s long-term business, he or she may apply to
the Court, and the Court may on such application appoint a special manager of this
business to act during such time as the Court may direct, with such power as may be
granted to him or her by the Court.
(4) A special manager appointed under subsection (3) shall—
(a)

give such security and account in such manner as the Court may direct; and

(b)

receive such remuneration as may be fixed by the Court.

(5) The Court may, if it thinks fit and subject to conditions, if any, as it may
determine, reduce the amount of the contracts made by the registered insurer in the course
of carrying on its long-term business.

(6) The Court may, on the application of the liquidator, or a special manager appoint
under subsection (3), appoint an independent actuary to investigate the long-term
business of such company and to report to the liquidator, the special manager or the
Registrar as the case may be, on the desirability or otherwise of that business being
continued and on any reduction in the contracts made in the course of carrying on that
business that may be necessary for its successful continuation.

### 30.

Reduction of contracts as alternative to winding-up
In the case of an insurance company which has been proved to be unable to pay its
debts, the Court may, if it thinks fit, reduce the amount of the contracts of the company
on such terms and subject to such conditions as the Court thinks just, in place of making a
winding-up order.

## PART V

Taxation and Exemptions

### 31.

Profits and dividends
Notwithstanding anything contained in any other enactment—
(a)

no income tax, nor any other direct or indirect tax or impost, shall be levied
in Grenada upon the transactions, contracts securities or other dealings or
upon profits or gains of a registered insurer, or upon any premiums earned
by a registered insurer, or money paid to an insured by a registered insurer,
in respect of any offshore insurance business written in or from within
Grenada; and

(b)

no income tax, nor any other direct or indirect tax or impost, shall be levied
in Grenada in respect of any dividends or earnings, or upon any premiums
paid to a registered insurer or money paid to an insured by a registered
insurer or money paid to an insured by a registered insurer attributable to the
shares or securities of a registered insurer that are beneficially owned by
another registered insurer or by a person who is not a resident of Grenada.

### 32.

Exemption from stamp duty
Contracts of insurance executed for or in connection with the offshore insurance
business of a registered insurer under this Act shall be exempt from stamp duty.

### 33.

Exemption from currency and exchange control
(1) Any offshore insurance business conducted by a registered insurer in accordance
with this Act shall be exempt from any currency and exchange control restriction
pursuant to any enactment.
(2) No foreign exchange levy, as prescribed, shall be imposed or be payable in respect
of any offshore insurance business conducted by a registered insurer in accordance with
this Act.

### 34.

Power of exemption
(1) The Minister may of his or her own motion or pursuant to an application in
writing from a registered insurer exempt such registered insurer from all or any part of the
provisions of this Act or any regulations made under this Act and may impose such terms
and conditions as he or she thinks fit as a condition under which such exemption is
granted.

(2) An exemption granted under this subsection may be revoked or other terms and
conditions changed by the Minister at any time.
(3) In dealing with an application the Minister shall not be required to act judicially
and his or her decision in all cases shall be final.

### 35.

Exemption from taxes, etc.
For the avoidance of doubt it is hereby declared that the exemptions from taxes, duties
and levies specified in section 110 of the International Companies Act, Chapter 52, shall
apply to companies licensed under this Act.

## PART VI

Miscellaneous

### 36.

Indemnity
The Minister, and the Registrar shall not, nor shall any member, officer or person
acting pursuant to any authority conferred by the Minister, or the Registrar, as the case
may be, be liable to any action, suit or proceeding for, or in respect of, any act or matter
done or omitted to be done in good faith in the exercise, or purported exercise, of the
functions conferred by or under this Act or any regulations made thereunder.

### 37.

False statements
Any person who—
(a)

causes or permits to be included in any book or document provided or
produced to the Minister, the Registrar or to an Inspector appointed under
the provisions of section 24 a statement which he or she knows to be false in
a material particular; or

(b)

in purported compliance with any provision of this Act or a requirement
imposed under any such provisions, provides information which he or she
knows to be false in material particular or recklessly provided information
which is false in a material particular,

commits an offence under this Act.

### 38.

General penalty provisions
(1) Any person who commits an offence under the provisions, or any part of the
provisions of section 14, 20, 21 or 23 shall be liable, on summary conviction, if the
offender is an individual to a fine of twenty-thousand dollars or to a term of imprisonment
for one year, or to both such fine and imprisonment in respect of each such offence and if
the offender is not an individual to a fine of fifty thousand dollars.
(2) Any person who commits an offence under the provisions of section 4, 5, 9, 24 or 37
shall be liable, on summary conviction, if the offender is an individual, to a fine of fifty
thousand dollars or imprisonment for two years or to both such fine and imprisonment in
respect of each such offence and if the offender is not an individual to a fine not exceeding
one hundred thousand dollars.
(3) Any person who contravenes or fails or neglects to comply with any provisions of
this Act or regulations made thereunder, commits an offence under this Act and shall be
liable, on summary conviction, in a case where for which no specific penalty is otherwise
provided in this Act, if the offender is an individual, to a fine of twenty thousand dollars
or to imprisonment for six months, or to both such fine and imprisonment, or if the
offender is not an individual to a fine of twenty thousand dollars.

(4) Where any person is convicted of an offence under this Act and such offence is a
continuing one the offender shall be liable in addition to the penalty otherwise provided
for in this Act to an additional penalty of five thousand dollars for everyday on which the
offence has continued.
(5) Where any offence under this Act committed by a body corporate is proved to
have been committed with the consent or connivance of, or to be attributable to any
neglect on the part of any director, chief executive, manager, secretary or other similar
officer or servant of the body corporate or any person who was purporting to act in such
capacity, he or she as well as the body corporate, shall be deemed to have committed an
offence under this Act and liable to be proceeded against and punished accordingly.
(6) No person shall be persecuted for an offence under this Act without the consent of
the Director of Public Prosecutions.

### 39.

Regulations, etc.
(1) The Minister may from time to time make such regulations and orders as may be
deemed necessary or expedient for the purpose of giving full effect to the provisions of
this Act and for the due administration thereof.
(2) Without limiting the general powers conferred by subsection (1) of this section it
is hereby declared that any regulations may be made under this section for all or any of
the following purposes—
(a)

provisions prohibiting or restricting the issue or transfer of any share in a
registered insurer or any interest in such a share, to any specific person or
class of persons;

(b)

provisions restricting a registered insurer and any related company from
entering into transactions of any specified description with any person of any
specific class or description;
(i) the preparation, form and content of the annual accounts of a registered
insurer,

(c)

(ii) the preparation, form and consent of statement, reports and
information to be annexed to annual accounts,
(iii) provisions restricting the appointment of auditors to persons who have
effected indemnity insurance of such type and amounts as may be
specified;
(d)

the determination of the level and amount of assets and liabilities for the
purposes of the calculations of margins of solvency under this Act and
regulations made in exercise of this power may provide that, for any
specified purpose, assets or liabilities of any specific category or description
shall be left out of account or shall be taken into account only to a specified
extent;

(e)

the fixing of penalties for the contravention of any provisions of or
regulations made under this Act;

(f)

purposes connected with any of the matters specified in paragraphs (a) to (c).

### 40.

Procedure where none laid down
In the event that any act or step is required or permitted to be done under this Act, and
no form is prescribed or procedure laid down in this Act or regulations for doing the
same, application may be made to the Registrar for directions as to the manner in which
the same may be done and any act or step done or taken in accordance with his or her
directions shall be a valid performance of such act or step.

### 41.

Financial provisions
All fees and charges payable under this Act shall be paid to the Accountant-General.

### 42.

Commencement
This Act comes into force on the 3rd day of March, 1997.
