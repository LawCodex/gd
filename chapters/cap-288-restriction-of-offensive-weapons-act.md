## CHAPTER 288

RESTRICTION OF OFFENSIVE WEAPONS ACT
An Act to restrict the manufacture, importation and disposal of offensive weapons
and to prohibit the possession of offensive weapons in a public place without
lawful authority or reasonable excuse, and to provide for connected matters.
[Act No. 14 of 1961 amended by Act No. 38 of 1972.]

[9th December, 1972.]

## PART I

Restriction of Offensive Weapons

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Restriction of Offensive Weapons Act.

### 2.

Interpretation of Part

In this Part, “offensive weapon” means—
(a)

a knife having a blade that opens automatically by the application of
pressure to a button, spring or other device in or attached to the handle of the
knife;

(b)

a knife having a blade that is released from within its handle or sheath by the
force of gravity or by the application of centrifugal force and which, when
released, is locked in place by means of a button, spring, lever, or other
device;

(c)

a knife or sharpened instrument built into or forming part of a stick and
which is released from the stick by the manipulation of a button, spring,
knob or other device.

### 3.

Offences in connection with offensive weapons
A person who manufactures, imports, sells, offers for sale, hires, displays in any shop
window or elsewhere, lends or gives to any other person, or has in his or her possession
an offensive weapon shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, in
the case of a first offence to imprisonment for three months and to a fine of two thousand
dollars, and in the case of a second or subsequent offence to imprisonment for six months
and to a fine of five thousand dollars. A police officer may seize, forfeit and destroy an
offensive weapon wherever found.

## PART II

Prohibition of Offensive Weapons

### 4.

Interpretation of Part
In this Part—
“offensive weapon” means any article made or adapted for use for causing injury to
the person, or intended for that use by the person having it with him or her;
“public place” includes all premises and places to which the public at the material
time have, or are permitted to have, access whether on payment or otherwise.

### 5.

Prohibition on the possession of offensive weapon, without lawful authority or
reasonable excuse, in a public place
(1) Whoever without lawful authority or reasonable excuse, the proof whereof shall
lie on him or her, is in possession in any public place of an offensive weapon shall be
guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to imprisonment for six months
and to a fine of seven hundred and fifty dollars, and the court convicting him or her may
make an order for the forfeiture or disposal of the weapon in respect of which the offence
was committed.
(2) Notwithstanding subsection (1) a person having possession in a public place of a
firearm which he or she is licensed to possess, and permitted to carry in a public place,
under Part IV of the Firearms Act, Chapter 105, shall be deemed to have lawful authority
for the purposes of subsection (1) for so long as he or she does not contravene any
provision of the Firearms Act, Chapter 105.

### 6.

Power of police officer to arrest without warrant
A police officer may, without warrant, arrest a person whom he or she has reasonable
cause to believe is guilty of an offence under section 5(1) if the police officer is not
satisfied as to that person’s identity or place of residence or has reasonable cause to

believe that it is necessary to arrest him or her in order to prevent the commission by him
or her of any other offence in the course of committing which an offensive weapon might
be used.
