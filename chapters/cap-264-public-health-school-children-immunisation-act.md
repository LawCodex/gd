## CHAPTER 264

PUBLIC HEALTH (SCHOOL CHILDREN IMMUNISATION) ACT
An Act to require the immunisation of school children under thirteen years of age
against communicable diseases.
[Act No. 41 of 1980.]

[5th September, 1980.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Public Health (School Children Immunisation) Act.
2\. Interpretation
In this Act—
“certificate of immunisation” means document issued by a medical practitioner
certifying that he or she is satisfied that a child is immune with respect to a
communicable disease—
(a)

by reason of his or her having been immunised against such communicable
disease; or

(b)

by reason of his or her having suffered from a communicable disease
whereby immunity to such disease was derived;

“child” means an individual under the age of thirteen years;
“communicable disease” means disease specified in the Schedule;
“parent” includes guardian;
“pre-primary school”, “primary school” and “all-age school” have the same
meaning as in section 2 of the Education Act, Chapter 84.

3\. Certificate of immunisation required for school admission
Notwithstanding any law to the contrary, but subject to the provisions of this Act, no
child shall be admitted as a pupil to a pre-primary school, primary school, private school
or all-age school unless he or she produces to the Principal thereof a certificate of
immunisation with respect to every communicable disease, except—
(a)

where a child produces a certificate of a medical practitioner or a community
health nurse certifying that immunisation against any particular
communicable disease or communicable diseases is not advisable on medical
grounds; or

(b)

where a child has been exempted on religious grounds in accordance with
section 4.

4\. Procedure if parent objects on religious grounds to immunisation of child
(1) If a parent objects on religious grounds to the immunisation of a child, that parent
shall state the grounds of his or her objection in writing and give or send it to the
Principal of the school to which he or she seeks the admission of the child, and the
Principal shall forward the objection to the Minister responsible for education.
(2) The Minister responsible for education shall consider the objection and decide
whether or not the child shall be exempted from immunisation, and the decision of the
Minister shall be final.
5\. Power to amend Schedule
The Minister responsible for health may, from time to time, by Order, amend the
Schedule.
6\. School attendance may be suspended during period of epidemic or threat thereof
Where a communicable disease has become epidemic or when an epidemic of a
communicable disease is threatened, the Minister responsible for health may by Order
suspend from attendance at any pre-primary school, primary school or all-age school any
child that has not been immunised against such disease, and the suspension shall remain
in force until the Minister by further Order declares that the epidemic is ended or that the
threat of such epidemic is past.
