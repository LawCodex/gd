## CHAPTER 254A

PRIVATE HOSPITALS AND NURSING HOMES ACT
An Act to regulate private hospitals, and for related matters.
[Act No. 12 of 2002.]

[31st May, 2002.]

## PART I

Preliminary

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Private Hospitals and Nursing Homes Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires—
“approved home” means a house licensed for the reception of persons of unsound
mind under the Mental Hospitals Act, Chapter 190;
“Board” means the Private Hospitals Board established under section 3;
“Director” means the person appointed by the licensee under section 13 to be in
charge of the day-to-day running of a private hospital;
“house” means a building or other structure, whether permanent or temporary,
intended for human habitation; and, where one or more houses are occupied by the
same person, they are deemed to constitute a single house for the purposes of this Act;
“inspection team” means an inspection team appointed by the Minister under
section 17(3);
“licence” means a licence issued under section 5 to operate a private hospital;
“maternity home” means any house used or intended to be used for the reception,
treatment and confinement of pregnant women or for the care and treatment of women
within 14 days of childbirth;
“midwife” means a person who is certified as a midwife under the Midwives Act,

## Chapter 194 (repealed);

“nurse” means a person who is registered as a nurse under the Nurses Registration
Act, Chapter 214 (repealed);
“nurse midwife” means a person who is both a nurse and a midwife;
“nursing home” means a home for the residential care of the elderly;

“patient” means a person admitted to a private hospital for the purpose of
treatment;
“private hospital” means a house where persons suffering from any sickness, injury
or infirmity whether physical or mental are given medical or surgical treatment or
nursing care and includes an approved home, a maternity home, a convalescent home
and a nursing home but does not include a hospital or other establishment or
institution to which the Hospitals Act, Chapter 135 (repealed), pplies;
“treatment” means the maintenance, observation, nursing and medical care and
supervision of a patient.

## PART II

Administration of Private Hospitals and Nursing Homes

### 3.

Private Hospitals Board
(1) This section establishes a Private Hospitals Board to assist and advise the Minister
on matters relating to the administration of this Act.
(2) The Board consists of seven members appointed by the Minister as follows—
(a)

the Chief Medical Officer, as Chairperson;

(b)

the Director of Medical Services in the Ministry of Health;

(c)

two other officers in the Ministry of Health who are not medical
practitioners, one of whom is skilled in nursing administration and the other
in hospital administration;

(d)

the Chief Welfare Officer in the Ministry of Social Services.

(e)

two persons who are neither public officers nor actively engaged in the
management of a private hospital.

(3) The members appointed under subsection (2)(d) must be appointed for a period
not exceeding two years but are eligible for re-appointment.
(4) No member of the Board may have a financial interest in a private hospital.
(5) If the Chairperson is unable to perform the functions of the office owing to
absence from Grenada or inability for any other reason, the Minister may appoint a
member to act as Chairperson during the time the absence or inability continues.
(6) If a member of the Board appointed under subsection (2)(d) is unable to perform
the functions of the office owing to absence from Grenada or inability for any other
reason, the Minister may appoint some other person to act as a temporary member during
the time the absence or inability continues in the same manner as that in which the
original appointment was made.

### 4.

Procedure of the Board
(1) The Chairperson or other member presiding and three other members constitute a
quorum at meetings of the Board.
(2) The powers of the Board may be exercised notwithstanding any vacancy in its
number.
(3) The Board must meet at least quarterly, and, subject to this Act, may regulate the
procedure to be followed at its meetings.

### 5.

Licences for private hospitals

(1) The Minister may, subject to this Act, issue a licence for the use of any house as a
private hospital.
(2) No house shall be used by any person as a private hospital except under the
authority of a licence issued by the Minister under this Act.
(3) If a house is used as a private hospital in contravention of subsection (2), the
occupier and every person concerned in the management of the house or in the admission
thereto or treatment of any patient therein each commits an offence.

### 6.

Application for a licence
(1) An applicant for a licence must be an individual, but may represent a body
corporate or association of persons.
(2) Written application for a licence to operate a private hospital must be made to the
Minister and must contain the particulars specified in the Schedule. Each application must
be verified by the applicant under oath.
(3) Before issuing a licence, the Minister must be satisfied—
(a)

that the house, its location with regard to neighbouring premises and its
proposed facilities and equipment have been approved by an inspection team
as suitable for the purpose indicated in the application;

(b)

that the use of the house for the proposed purpose complies with any laws
relating to land use, design, fire safety, structural safety, electrical safety and
similar matters as indicated by reports from relevant departments of the
Government;

(c)

that the applicant is a fit and proper person to hold a licence and, in the case
of an application on behalf of a body corporate or association of persons,
that the body or association is a fit and proper body or association to operate
a private hospital.

(4) An application must be accompanied by the prescribed application fee and before
a licence is issued the prescribed licence fee must be paid.
(5) The Minister may by Order amend the Schedule by adding to, varying or deleting
any of the particulars.

### 7.

Classes of hospitals
(1) Every licence for a private hospital must specify the nature of the services that
may be provided at the hospital and, without limiting the foregoing, may be issued in
respect of one or more of—
(a)

a medical or surgical hospital;

(b)

a maternity hospital;

(c)

a medical, surgical and maternity hospital;

(d)

a home for the care and accommodation of convalescent or chronically ill
persons;

(e)

a hospital for the care of any specified class of patients suffering from any
designated or specified disease, disorder or illness;

(f)

a nursing home;

(g)

an approved home.

(2) Subsection (1) does not prevent the carrying out at a private hospital in a case of
emergency of any surgical treatment that in the opinion of a medical practitioner is
necessary; but if the treatment is not within the terms of the licence relating to the

hospital, a report must within 48 hours be made to the Board by the Director showing the
nature of the treatment and the names of the medical practitioners who recommended and
performed the same.
(3) The requirement for a licence under this Act for the use of a house as an approved
home is in addition to any licence requirement under the Mental Hospitals Act,

## Chapter 190.

### 8.

Administration of licences
(1) Every licence must state the maximum number of patients who may be
accommodated in the hospital at any one time and may be limited to any particular class
or classes of patients.
(2) Every licence remains in force for one year but may be renewed annually on
payment of the prescribed fee.
(3) An official register must be kept by the Chief Medical Officer in respect of all
private hospitals containing the prescribed information.

### 9.

Revocation of licence
(1) A licence may at any time be revoked by the Minister—
(a)

if the licensee or Director—
(i) is convicted of an offence against this Act,
(ii) wilfully neglects or refuses to comply with the provisions of this Act
or the regulations, or
(iii) obstructs, impedes or hinders any person carrying out any duties or
responsibilities under it; or

(b)

if, on the basis of a report from an inspection team, the Minister considers
that—
(i) the premises of the private hospital are unsanitary or without proper
fire protection, or
(ii) the private hospital is managed or conducted in a manner contrary to
the conditions of the licence or in such a manner that the revocation of
the licence is required in the public interest.

(2) Before a licence is revoked, the Minister must give notice to the licensee of the
ground or grounds on which it is proposed to revoke the licence and must afford the
licensee an opportunity of showing cause why the licence should not be revoked.

### 10.

Refusal of renewal of licence
(1) The Minister may refuse to renew the licence of any private hospital on the same
grounds on which the Minister may revoke a licence under section 9(1).
(2) If the renewal of a licence is refused, or if a licence has been revoked, the licence
must not be displayed in a manner that could induce a person to believe that it is still in
force, and every person who so displays a licence commits an offence.

### 11.

Death of licensee
(1) If a licence has been issued to a representative of a body corporate or association
of persons, and the representative dies during the currency of the licence, the body or
association must nominate another individual to hold the licence.

(2) If the Minister is satisfied that the nominee is a fit and proper person to hold a
licence, the Minister may transfer the licence to that person.
(3) Pending the transfer of a licence, the Minister may issue to such person as the
Minister thinks fit a temporary licence to operate the private hospital for such period not
exceeding six months and subject to such terms and conditions as the Minister thinks fit.

### 12.

Transfer of licence
On an application in writing signed by the licensee and by any person to whom it is
proposed that the licence be transferred, the Minister may, if satisfied as to the character
and fitness of such person, by endorsement on the licence or otherwise in writing, transfer
the licence to that person, and thereupon that person becomes the licensee of the private
hospital with the same rights and obligations as if the licence had been issued to that
person in the first instance.

### 13.

Directors of private hospitals
(1) Every private hospital must have at all times a Director appointed by the licensee.
(2) The Director must possess the qualifications prescribed by the Regulations.
(3) Subject to paragraph (5), no person may be appointed as the Director of a hospital
until his or her name and qualifications have been furnished to the Minister and the
Minister has approved of the appointment.
(4) If appropriately qualified, the licensee of a private hospital may also be the
Director, in which case the Minister’s approval is not required except as to the
qualifications.
(5) During the temporary absence, illness or incapacity of the Director, the licensee
may appoint as acting Director any other person qualified in accordance with this Act and
the Regulations, and any person so appointed—
(a)

will, while so acting, be considered for the purposes of this Act to be the
Director;

(b)

may not so act, whether under the same or successive appointments, for a
longer continuous period than four weeks.

### 14.

Medical practitioner to be in attendance
(1) Subject to subsection (2), there must be a medical practitioner in attendance at a
private hospital whenever there are patients in the hospital.
(2) The Minister may in writing grant exemption from the requirement in
subsection (1) to a private hospital that is a nursing home or an approved home.

### 15.

Register of patients
(1) The licensee of every private hospital must keep or cause to be kept a register of
the patients in which is entered—
(a)

the name, age, sex and usual place of abode of each patient, and the date of
his or her admission to the hospital;

(b)

each patient’s diagnosis;

(c)

the name of the medical practitioner attending each patient;

(d)

the date on which each patient leaves the hospital and, if transferred to
another hospital, the name of the other hospital, or, in the event of death of a
patient in hospital, the date of death; and

(e)

any other particulars prescribed by the Regulations.

(2) The particulars required by subsection (1) must be entered in the register as soon
as practicable after the occurrence of the act or event to which the entry relates.
(3) A person who knowingly makes a false entry in a register of patients commits an
offence.
(4) A licensee who fails to make an entry in the register as required by subsection (2)
commits an offence.

### 16.

Reporting of deaths
(1) The Director of a private hospital must within twenty-four hours of the death of
any patient in the hospital make a report of the death to the Chief Medical Officer, giving
the name of the patient, the cause of death, and the name of the attending medical
practitioner.
(2) The Director must within twenty-four hours report to the Coroner any death of a
patient occurring within six hours of admission or within twenty-four hours following
surgery, delivery or anaesthesia.
(3) A Director who contravenes subsection (1) or (2) commits an offence.

### 17.

Panel of inspectors
(1) The Minister may for the purposes of this Act appoint a panel of inspectors not
less than five nor more than seven inspectors, from the following categories of persons—
(a)

a member of the Medical Board established by section 4 of the Medical
Practitioners, Dentists and Veterinary Surgeons Registration Act,

## Chapter 189;

(b)

a retired nurse;

(c)

an experienced civil engineer;

(d)

an expert in environmental health;

(e)

a member of the Grenada Medical Association;

(f)

such other persons as the Minister considers fit to appoint to the panel.

(2) A member of the inspection panel must not be engaged in the management of, nor
have any financial interest in, a private hospital.
(3) The Minister must designate three members of the inspection panel to be an
inspection team whenever an inspection for purposes of this Act is to be carried out.
(4) The Minister must provide every member of an inspection team with a certificate
of appointment.
(5) If the Minister has reasonable grounds to believe or to suspect that any house is
used as a private hospital without being licensed, he or she may authorise an inspection
team, with such assistance as may be required, to enter and inspect the house and every
part of the house.

### 18.

Inspection of private hospitals
For the purposes of this Act and the Regulations, a private hospital, its registers and
other records, other than confidential case history notes, must at all times be open to
inspection or examination by the members of an inspection team, upon presentation of
their certificates of appointment.

### 19.

Frequency of inspection

(1) Every private hospital must be inspected and every aspect of its administration,
operations and management must be inspected at least once a year by an inspection team.
(2) In addition to the inspection referred to in subsection (1) an inspection team must
inspect a private hospital when so directed by the Minister.

### 20.

Reports to Minister
(1) An inspection team that inspects a private hospital under this Act must, subject to
subsection (2), forward a report to the Minister within 14 days of the completion of the
inspection.
(2) The report of an inspection conducted at the direction of the Minister under
section 19(2) must be forwarded to the Minister within twenty-four hours of the
completion of the inspection.

### 21.

Obstruction
A person who obstructs or prevents or attempts to obstruct or prevent any inspection
of a private hospital under this Act commits an offence.

## PART III

Offences and Penalties

### 22.

Unlawful operation of private hospital
(1) If a private hospital is operated contrary to the terms of the licence issued in
respect of it, the licensee and the Director each commits an offence.
(2) If a private hospital is used as such while it has no duly qualified Director, the
licensee commits an offence.
(3) If there is no medical practitioner in attendance at a private hospital as requested
by section 14, the licensee and Director each commits an offence.

### 23.

Unlawful treatment
If, except in the case of an emergency not exceeding seven days or such further time
as may be authorised by the Minister, a private hospital is used at any time for the
treatment of a greater number of patients than is permitted by the licence, or if a patient of
a class not permitted by the licence is admitted, the licensee and the Director each
commits an offence.

### 24.

Holding out as a hospital, etc.
(1) No person may hold out any house in which the person has an interest as a
hospital or nursing home unless the house has an appropriate licence under this Act.
(2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an offence.

### 25.

Structural alterations
(1) No structural alteration or addition to any private hospital may be made until a
plan of the proposed alteration or addition has been submitted to and approved by the
Minister on the basis of reports from relevant departments of the Government.
(2) If any alteration or addition is made in contravention of subsection (1), the
licensee of the private hospital commits an offence.

### 26.

Personal liability
If a person committing an offence against this Act is a body corporate, every director,
officer and person concerned in the management of the body corporate, is each guilty of
the same offence unless he or she proves that the act constituting the offence took place
without his or her knowledge or that he or she exercised all due diligence to prevent the
commission of the offence.

### 27.

Burden of proof
(1) In a prosecution for an offence under this Act, the burden of proving that a person
found in a house and there receiving medical treatment is not a patient within the meaning
of this Act is upon the person charged.
(2) In a prosecution for an offence under this Act, the burden of proving—
(a)

that a licence is in force, and its terms; and

(b)

that a person apparently having the charge, control or management of a
private hospital is the Director thereof within the meaning of this Act,

is upon the person charged.

### 28.

Penalties
A person who commits an offence under this Act is liable on summary conviction—
(a)

to a fine of twenty thousand dollars and to imprisonment for twelve months;

(b)

in the case of a continuing offence, to a fine of two thousand dollars for
every day during which the offence continues.

## PART IV

Miscellaneous

### 29.

Director deemed to be occupier
Without affecting section 16, the Director of a private hospital is deemed to be the
occupier of the house for the purpose of giving notice or information under the
Registration of Births and Deaths Act, Chapter 280, of the death of any person or of the
birth of any child in the private hospital.

### 30.

Regulations
(1) The Minister may make regulations—
(a)

prescribing forms in connection with the issue of licences under this Act;

(b)

prescribing fees for licences and inspections under this Act;

(c)

prescribing the information to be kept in the register under section 8(3);

(d)

prescribing the qualifications of Directors and staff of private hospitals;

(e)

prescribing the particulars to be entered in registers of patients;

(f)

providing for the management and administration of private hospitals;

(g)

regulating surgical operations in private hospitals;

(h)

regulating the accommodation to be provided in private hospitals;

(i)

prescribing any other matter, whether similar to the foregoing or not,
reasonably required to give this Act full force and effect.

(2) Regulations under this section may prescribe penalties for a contravention not
exceeding a fine of two thousand dollars and imprisonment for six months.

### 31.

Minister acts on advice of Board
The powers of the Minister under this Act, except for those in sections 3, 11(3), 17(2)
and 23, must be exercised on the advice of the Board.

### 32.

Commencement
This Act comes into force on the 31st day of May, 2002.

### 33.

Transitional
(1) If a house is used as a private hospital at the commencement of this Act, the
requirement for a licence under this Act is suspended in respect of the house for six
months or for any longer period, not exceeding twelve months in total, that the Minister
allows in writing.
(2) Other provisions of this Act apply to all private hospitals from the date of
commencement.

Schedule
PRIVATE HOSPITALS AND NURSING HOMES ACT
Particulars to be Contained in an Application for a Licence
[Section 6(2).]

1\.

The full name, place of abode, qualifications and occupation of the
applicant.

2\.

A statement of the estate or interest of the applicant in the house in respect of
which the licence is desired.

3\.

A description of the situation of the house.

4\.

A statement as to the class of hospital proposed to be operated.

5\.

A sketch plan of the house showing the location and the intended use of each
room.

6\.

A statement of the number of patients proposed to be admitted to the house
and to each room or apartment of the house.

7\.

The number of staff and the qualification of each member of the staff of the
proposed private hospital.

8\.

A statement of the sanitary arrangements, ventilation and water supply of the
house.

9\.

A full description of the fire escapes of the house and the facilities provided
for use in case of fire.

10\.

A description of the access arrangements for ambulances.

11\.

A statement as to the provisions to be made for diagnostic and
pharmaceutical services.

12\.

If it is proposed to offer services in surgery, gynaecology or obstetrics, a
statement as to the type of surgery, gynaecology or obstetrics to be

performed and as to the facilities and equipment which are to be provided in
the house for these purposes including facilities for anaesthesia.
