## CHAPTER 309

STAMP ACT
An Act relating to stamp duties.
[Amended by Act No. 6 of 1963, Act No. 14 of 1968, Act No. 23 of 1972, Act No. 20 of 1974, Act
No. 29 of 1974, Act No. 39 of 1975, Act No. 11 of 1978, Act No. 18 of 1982, Act No. 8 of 1986,
Act No. 32 of 1990, Act No. 4 of 1992, SRO 8 of 1992, SRO 18 of 1997, SRO 61 of 1997, SRO
29 of 2002, Act No. 7 of 2007, Act No. 17 of 2007, SRO 23 of 2009.]

[1st January, 1897.]

## PART I

Preliminary

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Stamp Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act, the word “instrument”, unless modified by the context, means every
written document.

## PART II

Imposition of Stamp Duties and General Regulations

### 3.

Levy and payment of stamp duty
There shall be levied and paid in respect of the various instruments mentioned in the
Schedule the duty respectively specified in relation thereto in the Schedule:
Provided that the Minister may, upon approval of Cabinet, exempt a person from the
provisions of this section.

### 4.

Duties under direction of Comptroller of Inland Revenue
The said duties shall be denominated and deemed stamp duties and shall be paid in
and denoted by stamps and not otherwise, and shall, subject to subsidiary legislation from
time to time made by the Minister, be administered under the direction of the Comptroller
of Inland Revenue.

### 5.

Facts and circumstances affecting duty to be set forth in instruments
All the facts and circumstances affecting the liability of an instrument to duty, or the
amount of the duty with which an instrument is chargeable, are to be fully and truly set
forth in the instrument; and every person who, with intent to defraud—
(a)

executes an instrument in which all the said facts and circumstances are not
fully and truly set forth; or

(b)

being employed or concerned in or about the preparation of an instrument,
neglects or omits fully and truly to set forth therein all the said facts and
circumstances,

shall be guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of one thousand dollars, and in default of
payment to imprisonment for one month.

### 6.

Power of Minister to amend the Schedule
The Minister may from time to time, by Order, amend the Schedule by increasing or
decreasing the amount of, or by annulling, any of the duties therein mentioned, or impose
duties on any instruments not mentioned in the Schedule, or alter the Schedule in such
other manner as he or she may deem advisable:
Provided that before any such Order shall come into operation it shall have been
approved by both the House of Representatives and the Senate, by resolution.

### 7.

Stamps, etc., to be provided
There shall be procured such stamps, dies, and other implements as may be directed by
the Minister for the purposes of this Act, and all sums which may be necessary to defray
the expenses of procuring them and for otherwise carrying out the provisions of this Act
shall be paid by the Comptroller of Inland Revenue on the warrant of the Minister.

### 8.

Stamps, etc., to be deposited with Comptroller of Inland Revenue
All such stamps, dies, and other implements shall be deposited with the Comptroller
of Inland Revenue or such other officer as the Minister may from time to time direct, who
shall be responsible for their safe keeping; and all stamps to be impressed shall be
impressed by the Comptroller of Inland Revenue in the presence of the Director of Audit
or of such other officer as the Minister may from time to time direct.

### 9.

Stamps to be impressed or adhesive
The stamps to be used under this Act shall be impressed or adhesive as the Minister
may, from time to time, direct.

### 10.

Duties may be made up by several stamps
The duties imposed by this Act may be made up by several stamps; and stamps of
greater value than is required may be used upon any instrument.

### 11.

Stamping of instruments relating to Crown or Government property
All instruments relating to property belonging to the Crown or the Government shall,
unless exempted, be charged with the same duty as an instrument of the same kind
relating to property belonging to a subject who is a citizen.

### 12.

All stamp duties to be paid according to this Act
All stamp duties which are now payable or which may at any time become payable
under any Act upon any instrument are to be paid and denoted according to the provisions
of this Act.

### 13.

Payment for stamps before issue
No person authorised to sell or supply stamps shall be required to sell or supply any
stamp unless it is previously paid for.

### 14.

Stamp vendors

The Postmaster-General, the district post officers, the Registrar of the Supreme Court,
and such other persons as may from time to time be licensed by the Minister, shall be
authorised vendors of stamps.

### 15.

Issue of stamps to vendors
The Comptroller of Inland Revenue shall from time to time upon the requisition of the
Postmaster-General or the Registrar of the Supreme Court, issue stamps to them and take
their receipts for them, and the Postmaster-General shall issue to the district post officers
and to the other authorised vendors such supplies of stamps as may be allowed for that
purpose by the regulations.

### 16.

Accounts to be kept by vendors and audited
The Postmaster-General, the post officers and the Registrar shall keep books in which
all transactions relating to the receipt and sale of stamps shall be duly entered, and the
books shall be audited at such times as are prescribed by the Minister. All sums received
by them for the sale of stamps shall be paid into the Treasury or the District Revenue
Office, as the case may be, daily, or at such other times as may be allowed by the
regulations.

### 17.

Registrar not to register, etc., any instrument until stamped
The Registrar shall not register, file, or issue any instrument which is required to be
stamped, until it has been duly stamped and the stamp cancelled in the manner herein
provided.

### 18.

Spoiled stamps
(a) The value of stamps issued under this Act which may be casually destroyed or
spoiled may be allowed for by the Comptroller of Inland Revenue, subject to a deduction
of ten per cent thereon, on proof being given to his or her satisfaction, by declaration or
otherwise, of the stamps having been so destroyed or spoiled.
(b) No public officer shall be liable for the value of any stamps casually destroyed or
spoiled while in his or her custody, upon his or her producing satisfactory proof as
aforesaid.
(c) All such spoiled stamps shall be produced and given up to the Comptroller of
Inland Revenue.

### 19.

Fees payable for the public use to be paid in stamps
(1) All fees payable for the public uses of Grenada in a court or in any public office
shall be collected by means of stamps and not otherwise.
(2) When any such fee is payable in respect of an instrument, the stamp denoting the
fee shall be affixed to the instrument or to such other instrument as the Minister may
require to be used for the purpose; and when such fee is payable otherwise than in respect
of an instrument, the stamp denoting the fee shall be affixed to such instrument as the
Minister may require to be used for the purpose.
(3) This section is subject to the express provisions of the Supreme Court Fees Act,

## Chapter 315.

## PART III

Special Rules as to the Stamping of Instruments

### 20.

Cancellation of stamps
An instrument, the duty on which is required or permitted by law to be denoted by an
adhesive stamp, shall not be deemed duly stamped unless the person required by law to
stamp it cancels the stamps thereon by writing or otherwise indelibly marking on or
across them his or her name or initials, or the name or initials of the person for whom he
or she is acting, together with the true date of his or her cancelling them, so that the
stamps may be effectually cancelled and rendered incapable of being used for any other
instrument, or unless it is otherwise proved that the stamps appearing on the instrument
were affixed thereto at the proper time.

### 21.

Cancellation of stamps by Comptroller of Inland Revenue and public officers
When by this Act the Comptroller of Inland Revenue or any public officer is required
to affix any adhesive stamp, he or she shall cancel it in the manner herein provided for
cancellation of stamps.

### 22.

Stamping of certificate of barrister or solicitor
Every certificate of the admission of a barrister or solicitor to practise in Grenada shall
be stamped as is required by this Act.

### 23.

Stamping of bills and notes drawn in Grenada
The adhesive stamps denoting the duty on a bill of exchange or promissory note drawn
or made in Grenada, whether payable in or outside Grenada, shall be affixed and
cancelled by the person by whom the bill or note is signed, before he or she delivers it out
of his or her possession, custody, or power:
Provided as follows—
(a)

if at the time when any promissory note comes into the hands of any bona
fide holder there are not affixed thereto the proper adhesive stamps, it shall
be competent for the holder, before in any manner negotiating or paying the
note, to affix thereto the proper adhesive stamps and to cancel the same, and
upon his or her so doing the note shall be deemed duly stamped, and as valid
and available as if the stamp had been affixed and cancelled by the person by
whom the note was signed;

(b)

nothing in the foregoing proviso shall relieve any person from any fine or
penalty incurred by him or her for not cancelling an adhesive stamp.

### 24.

Stamping same when drawn outside Grenada
The adhesive stamps denoting the duty on any bill of exchange or promissory note
drawn or made outside Grenada shall be affixed and cancelled by the person into whose
hands the bill or note comes in Grenada before he or she presents for payment or
endorses, transfers, or in any manner negotiates or pays the bill or note.
Provisos for the protection of bona fide holders:
Provided as follows—
(a)

if at the time any bill of exchange or promissory note comes into the hands
of any bona fide holder thereof there are affixed thereto the proper adhesive
stamps effectually obliterated and purporting and appearing to be duly
cancelled, the stamps shall, so far as relates to the holder, be deemed to be
duly cancelled, although they may not appear to have been so affixed or
cancelled by the proper person;

(b)

if at the time when any bill of exchange or promissory note comes into the
hands of any bona fide holder thereof there are affixed thereto the proper

adhesive stamps not duly cancelled, it shall be competent for the holder to
cancel the stamps as if he or she were the person by whom they were
affixed, and, upon his or her so doing, the bill or note shall be deemed duly
stamped and as valid and available as if the stamps had been duly cancelled
by the person by whom they were affixed;
(c)

if any bill of exchange liable only to the duty of ten cents is presented for
payment unstamped, the person to whom it is so presented may affix thereto
the proper adhesive stamp and cancel it as if he or she had been the drawer
of the bill, and may upon so doing pay the sum in the bill mentioned and
charge the duty in account against the person by whom the bill was drawn,
or deduct the duty from the said sum, and the bill is, so far as respects the
duty, to be deemed good and valid.

But the foregoing provisos are not to relieve any person from any penalty he or she
may have incurred in relation to any such bill or note.

### 25.

Bills and notes purporting to be drawn, etc., outside Grenada to be deemed to
have been so drawn, etc.
A bill of exchange or promissory note purporting to be drawn or made outside
Grenada is, for the purposes of this Act, to be deemed to have been so drawn or made,
although it may in fact have been drawn or made within Grenada.

### 26.

Stamping bills drawn in sets
When a bill of exchange is drawn in a set according to the custom of merchants, and
one of the set is duly stamped, the other or others of the set shall, unless issued or in some
manner negotiated apart from the duly stamped bill, be exempt from duty; and, upon
proof of loss or destruction of a duly stamped bill forming one of a set, any other bill of
the set which has not been issued or in any manner negotiated apart from the lost or
destroyed bill may, although unstamped, be admitted in evidence to prove the contents of
the lost or destroyed bill.

### 27.

How conveyance in consideration of a debt, etc., to be charged
Where any property purchased after the commencement of this Act is conveyed to a
person in consideration, wholly or in part, of any debt due to him or her, or subject either
certainly or contingently to the payment or transfer of any money, whether being or
constituting a charge or encumbrance upon the property or not, the debt or money is to be
deemed the whole or part, as the case may be, of the consideration in respect whereof the
conveyance is chargeable with ad valorem duty.

### 28.

Stamping bills of lading
The adhesive stamps denoting the duty on a bill of lading shall be affixed and
cancelled by the person who makes or executes it, before he or she delivers it out of his or
her possession, custody, or power.

### 29.

Stamping charter parties
All charter parties or agreements in writing as to the letting or hiring of any vessel
sailing from Grenada shall be liable to the duty specified in the Schedule, and the
adhesive stamps denoting the duty shall be affixed and cancelled by the person by whom
the instrument is last executed in Grenada, or by whose execution it is completed as a
binding contract:

Provided that if any such charter party or agreement be not duly stamped the
Comptroller of Inland Revenue may stamp it if brought to him or her within seven days
after its execution, on payment of the duty and a penalty of fifteen dollars.

### 30.

Stamping notarial acts
The adhesive stamp denoting the duty upon a notarial act and upon the protest by a
notary public of a bill of exchange or promissory note shall be cancelled by the notary.

### 31.

Stamping receipts; and “receipt” defined
(1) The adhesive stamp denoting the duty upon a receipt shall be cancelled by the
person by whom the receipt is given before he or she delivers it out of his or her hands.
The stamp duty on a receipt may be paid by means either of an impressed or an adhesive
stamp.
(2) For the purposes of this Act, the word “receipt” includes any note, memorandum
or writing whereby any money amounting to five dollars or upwards, or any bill of
exchange or promissory note for money amounting to five dollars or upwards, is
acknowledged or expressed to have been received or deposited or paid, or whereby any
debt or demand, or any part of a debt or demand, of the amount of five dollars or
upwards, is acknowledged to have been settled, satisfied, or discharged, or which
signifies or imports any such acknowledgment, and whether the same is or is not signed
with the name of any person.

### 32.

Receipts to be given
Where a person receives payment, whether in whole or in part, for goods or services
or in or towards satisfaction of any debt or demand in respect of any transaction
undertaken for profit or of any transaction in the nature of commerce, and the payment is
of an amount of five dollars or upwards, that person shall at the time of receiving
payment, give a receipt to the person making the payment, whether the receipt is
requested or not.

### 33.

Receipt to be demanded
When a person makes payment, whether in whole or in part, for goods or services or
in or towards satisfaction of any debt or demand in respect of any transaction whether or
not undertaken for profit or of any transaction in the nature of commerce, and the
payment is of an amount of five dollars or upwards, that person shall at the time of
making payment, demand a receipt from the person receiving the payment.

### 34.

Offences in relation to receipts
(1) If a person—
(a)

gives a receipt liable to duty and it is not duly stamped;

(b)

fails to give a receipt in accordance with section 32;

(c)

in any case where a receipt would be liable to duty neglects or refuses to
give a receipt duly stamped; or

(d)

upon a payment of an amount of five dollars or upwards gives a receipt for a
sum not amounting to five dollars, or separates the amount paid with intent
to evade the duty,

he or she shall be guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of one thousand dollars, and, if
he or she holds a licence to carry on commerce, the licence may be suspended for six
months. On a third conviction under this section, whether for a similar offence or not, in
addition to any fine the licence shall be suspended for one year.

(2) If a person—
(a)

accepts a receipt which is liable to duty and is not duly stamped;

(b)

fails to demand a receipt in accordance with section 33; or

(c)

upon making a payment of an amount of five dollars or upwards accepts a
receipt for a sum not amounting to five dollars, or agrees to the separation of
the amount to be paid with intent to evade the duty,

he or she shall be guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of one thousand dollars.

### 35.

Exemptions
(1) The Minister may, from time to time, by Order, declare that the provisions of
section 32 or 33 shall not apply to persons who receive payments or make payments in
respect of those services or of those classes of transactions specified in the order.
(2) The Minister may, from time to time, permit persons conducting a certain business
or transaction or class of business or transaction, in lieu of stamping receipts, to pay a
commuted fee annually, which fee shall be due and payable on the first working day of
January each year.
(3) A notice to the effect that the conduct of the business, or transaction, or class of
business or class of transaction, as the case may be, specifying the same, is exempt from
the stamping of receipts shall be displayed in a prominent position within the place where
the business or transaction occurs, and notwithstanding the provisions of section 33 a
purchaser shall incur no liability under this Act.
(4) The Minister may, from time to time, by Order, fix the commuted fee payable
under subsection (2).

### 36.

Stamping in other cases
In all cases not otherwise specifically provided for in this Act, an instrument shall be
written on material duly stamped or shall be stamped by the person by whom or by whose
direction it was prepared, on or before its final completion or execution.

## PART IV

Stamping of Instruments after Execution and by Agreement

### 37.

Stamping after execution under penalty
Except where express provision to the contrary is made by this or any other Act, an
unstamped or insufficiently stamped instrument may be stamped by the Comptroller of
Inland Revenue after its execution, on payment to the Comptroller of Inland Revenue of
the unpaid duty and a penalty of one hundred dollars:
Provided that whenever it is made to appear to the satisfaction of the Comptroller of
Inland Revenue, by declaration or otherwise, that the omission to duly stamp an
instrument has not been willful or with an intent to defraud, and the instrument is brought
to the Comptroller of Inland Revenue to be stamped within twelve months after its first
execution, the Comptroller of Inland Revenue may, with the previous sanction of the
Minister, remit the whole or part of the penalty payable on stamping the instrument.

### 38.

Unstamped instrument inadmissible as evidence
No instrument made liable by an Act to any duty shall be pleaded or given in evidence
in a court unless it is duly stamped, except as is hereinafter provided.

### 39.

Production of unstamped instrument
(1) Upon the production of an instrument as evidence in an action or proceeding in a
court or in Judge’s chambers, it shall be the duty of the Registrar or clerk of the court to
call the attention of the court to any omission or insufficiency of stamp upon it, and it
shall not be received in evidence or otherwise used in the action or proceeding until the
stamp duty imposed by law thereon, together with a penalty of two hundred and fifty
dollars, is paid.
(2) The presiding Judge or magistrate may, if he or she thinks fit, allow an adjournment
to enable the person producing the instrument to have it stamped by the Comptroller of
Inland Revenue in the manner hereinbefore provided.
(3) The Registrar or clerk of the court shall, upon payment to him or her of the stamp
duty on the instrument, and of the penalty imposed by law, give a receipt for the amount
of the duty and penalty; and thereupon the instrument shall (saving all just exceptions on
other grounds) be admissible in evidence; and the Comptroller of Inland Revenue shall,
upon production to him or her of the receipt, cause the instrument to be stamped with the
stamp equivalent to the duty and penalty so paid.
The Registrar or clerk of the court shall forthwith pay the said duty and penalty into
the Treasury.
(4) The decision of the presiding Judge or magistrate as to the necessity or
insufficiency of a stamp upon an instrument, or as to the amount payable as stamp duty
thereon, shall be final.
(5) No instrument shall in any criminal proceeding be inadmissible in evidence for
want of a stamp.

### 40.

Payment of penalties to be denoted by stamps on instruments
The payment of a penalty for stamping an instrument after its execution shall be
denoted on it by stamps, and the word “Penalty” shall be written across the face of each
of the stamps by the officer cancelling them.

### 41.

Composition for duty on cheques, etc.
(1) The Permanent Secretary (Finance) may enter into an agreement with a banker for
the composition, in accordance with the following provisions of this section, of the stamp
duty chargeable under the heading in the Schedule “Bill of Exchange, Cheque or Order –
payable on demand or at sight or on presentation or within three days after date or sight”
on instruments—
(a)

drawn on the banker by customers of his or her on forms supplied by him or
her;

(b)

drawn by the banker on himself or herself or another banker, as may be
specified in the agreement.

(2) An agreement under subsection (1) shall require the banker to furnish the Permanent
Secretary (Finance) with periodical accounts in respect of the instruments to which the
agreement relates containing particulars—
(a)

of forms that have been supplied by him or her with a view to there being
completed and issued as such instruments by his or her customers, and of
such forms supplied but returned unused or spoilt; and

(b)

of such instruments issued by him or her,

and may contain such other terms and conditions as the Permanent Secretary (Finance)
thinks proper.

(3) Where an agreement under subsection (1) has been made, an instrument to which
the agreement relates and which bears such indication thereon of the payment of stamp
duty as the Permanent Secretary (Finance) may require shall not be chargeable with
stamp duty; but the banker shall pay to him, on demand, such sums as would, but for this
section, have been chargeable by way of stamp duty on such instruments during the
period to which the account required by subsection (2) relates, it being assumed that the
number of such instruments issued by the customers of the banker is equal to the number
of forms supplied less the number of forms returned unused or spoilt.
(4) If a banker fails to deliver an account as required by such an agreement, or to pay
the whole or any part of the duty payable when so demanded, he or she shall be guilty of
an offence and liable to a fine of two hundred and fifty dollars for each day during which
the default has continued and he or she shall also be liable to pay to the Permanent
Secretary (Finance), in addition to the duty so payable, interest at the rate of five per cent
per annum from the date on which it was so demanded.
(5) In this section, “banker” includes all persons who carry on the business of banking
in Grenada.

## PART V

Miscellaneous

### 42.

Power of Minister to remit penalties
The Minister may, if he or she thinks fit, at any time within twelve months after the
first execution of an unstamped or insufficiently stamped instrument, remit the whole or
part of the penalty payable on the stamping of it.

### 43.

Offence of neglecting to stamp instrument
Whoever, being required by law to stamp an instrument and to cancel the stamps
thereon, wilfully neglects or refuses duly and effectually to do so in the manner herein
provided shall be guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of five hundred dollars.

### 44.

Offence of making or altering unstamped instrument liable to duty with intent
to defraud
Whoever knowingly, wilfully, and with intent to defraud makes or alters any
instrument or writing liable to stamp duty without the same being duly stamped shall be
guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of one thousand dollars.

### 44A.

Police officer, etc., may prosecute
Any police officer, price control inspector or revenue collector may prosecute and
conduct any information or other proceedings under this Act in respect of any offence or
penalty.

### 45.

Offence of practising any fraudulent act not specially provided for
Whoever does or is concerned in any fraudulent act or device (not specially otherwise
provided for by this Act) with intent to evade the duty imposed by this Act shall be guilty
of an offence and liable to a fine of three thousand dollars.

### 46.

Licence to issue unstamped bank notes; offence of issuing unstamped notes
without licence

Every bank having power to issue notes payable to bearer on demand shall obtain a
licence from the Comptroller of Inland Revenue, upon payment of the sum mentioned in
the Schedule, authorising the issue of unstamped notes, which licence shall be in force for
one year from the date thereof. Every manager or other person for the time being in
charge of any bank not duly licensed to issue unstamped notes who shall issue or cause or
permit to be issued any note not duly stamped shall, for every offence, be liable to a fine
of three thousand dollars.

### 47.

Recovery of fines
All fines imposed by this Act may be recovered on behalf of the Government by the
Comptroller of Inland Revenue, or some person authorised by him, before a court of
summary jurisdiction:
Provided that the Minister may award any portion of a fine, when recovered, to such
persons who shall have aided in the prosecution as he or she may think fit.

### 48.

Using stamps already used, etc.
Whoever fraudulently uses a stamp which has been cut, torn or removed from an
instrument, or fraudulently alters an instrument with intent to use a stamp on it, or
fraudulently cancels a stamp by writing on it or on an instrument to which it is affixed,
shall be guilty of an offence and liable to imprisonment for two years.

### 49.

Forging, etc., stamps, dies, etc.
(1) Whoever forges or counterfeits or causes or procures to be forged or counterfeited
any stamp or die which shall have been provided, made or used in pursuance of this Act
shall be guilty of an offence and liable to imprisonment for three years.
(2) Any stamp, die, plate, instrument or materials found in the possession of any
person in contravention of subsection (1) may be seized and shall be forfeited.

### 50.

Regulations
The Minister may make regulations regarding the distribution, sale, use, and
application of stamps, the granting of licences to sell stamps, the commission to be paid
to authorised vendors of stamps, and generally for better carrying out the purposes of this

STAMP REGULATIONS
[Section 50. SRO 23 of 1964, SRO 9 of 1972.]

[15th August, 1953.]
1\. Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Stamp Regulations.
2\. Licensed Stamp Vendors
Stamp Vendors licensed by the Cabinet under the provisions of section 14 of the Act
shall be entitled to receive a commission at the rate of five per cent on the value of stamps
purchased by them from the Postmaster-General. Such commission shall be paid to the
vendors in stamps by the Postmaster-General upon proof being given to his or her
satisfaction that they are and have been for the three months next preceding the date of
application for purchase of stamps bona fide and regularly engaged in the sale of stamps
to the public. The nature of the proof produced shall be recorded by the PostmasterGeneral for the information of the Auditor-General in checking the issue of stamps
representing the commission paid.
3\. Supply of Stamps to Vendors
No stamp of a denomination above seventy-five cents in value shall be issued to
licensed vendors of stamps under section 15 of the Act.
4\. Insufficiently stamped instruments
Any unstamped or insufficiently stamped instrument (other than a promissory note,
bill of exchange, charter party, or like instrument) which has been executed at any place
out of the State of Grenada may be stamped by the Registrar of the High Court, at any
time within two months after it has been first received in the said Island, on payment of
unpaid duty only.
