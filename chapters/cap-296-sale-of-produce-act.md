## CHAPTER 296

SALE OF PRODUCE ACT
An Act relating to the sale of produce.
[Amended by SRO 45 of 1961, Act No. 35 of 1964, Act No. 30 of 1965, SRO 7 of 1967.]

[1st June, 1954.]

## PART I

Introductory

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Sale of Produce Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act—
“agricultural officer” means an agricultural officer of the Ministry of Agriculture
not below the rank of Agricultural Assistant;
“cocoa” means cocoa beans, whether in pod or pulp or separated from the pulp, and
whether cured or uncured, ripe or unripe;
“copra” means the dried kernel of the coconut;
“cured cocoa” means cocoa beans, polished or unpolished, which have been
properly fermented for a period of at least eight days followed by drying for the
purpose of being bagged for export;
“cured nutmeg” means a nutmeg which has been thoroughly dried in such a
manner that when the shell is cracked the kernel is sound at the base and at the top,
does not yield to pressure and which, when cut, is free from interior mould;
“district” means a magisterial district;
“immature nutmeg” means a nutmeg which has been picked or severed from the
tree upon which it was growing before the pod has opened and the nutmeg and mace
within become visible;
“licensable produce” includes—
(a)

lime juice, lime oil, cocoa, copra, coffee, cotton, and such other articles of
produce as the Minister may, from time to time, by Order, direct to be
included in such term; and

(b)

for the purposes of section 29 and of Part V of this Act, nutmegs;

“licensed dealer” means a person licensed under this Act to deal in licensable
produce;
“licensed premises” means the premises specified in the licence issued to a licensed
dealer;
“licensed seller” means a person to whom a produce seller’s book has been issued;
“Licensing Board” means a Licensing Board constituted under section 4 and the
Cocoa Board established by section 5 of the Grenada Cocoa Association Act,

## Chapter 128;

“Licensing District” means Licensing District as provided in section 4(1);
“licensing session” means a licensing session as constituted by or under the
provisions of the Liquor Dealers’ Licences Act, Chapter 174.
“nutmeg” means nutmeg, whether in the pod or separated from the pod, and
whether cured or uncured, mature or immature, and includes mace;
“owner” includes the tenant or person in actual possession of, and the manager or
other person having chief authority on, any plantation or lands;
“partially dried cocoa” means cocoa which is neither wet cocoa nor cured cocoa;
“peasant proprietor” means a person who is owner or occupier of not more than ten
acres of land, and who is an agriculturist, labourer, domestic or menial employee,
artificer, handicraftsman, or otherwise engaged in manual labour;
“produce inspector” means an officer appointed under the provisions of section 49;

“purchase” includes the taking of licensable produce in exchange for other goods
or on account of any claim of indebtedness; also the delivery of any licensable
produce under an agreement to purchase the same in certain eventualities; and also the
completion of any contract for the sale or purchase of licensable produce, nothing
further being required to vest the property and possession in the purchaser;
“revenue officer” includes an inland revenue officer;
“soaked nutmeg” means a nutmeg in its green or uncured condition which has been
immersed in water or other fluid, or to which water or other fluid has been applied (not
amounting to an immersion or the application in any manner of water or other fluid so
that a nutmeg to which it is applied shall become wet, and not including such light
sprinkling with water as may be necessary and sufficient to enable mace to be properly
removed from any nutmeg);
“washed cocoa” means unfermented or partly fermented cocoa which has been
immersed in water or some other liquid, or to which water or some other liquid or
substance has been added, for the purpose of removing pulp from the bean;
“wet cocoa” means cocoa beans which have been extracted from the pods and
which are covered or partly covered with pulp and do not show any signs of
fermentation.

## PART II

Licences and Certificates

### 3.

Produce Dealers’ licences
(1) It shall not be lawful for any person to carry on the business of a dealer in any
description of licensable produce without having obtained a licence to deal in such
licensable produce in the form and manner and subject to the conditions prescribed by
this Act.
(2) A licence to deal in licensable produce shall be in Form 1 in the First Schedule.
(3) A licence to deal in licensable produce shall not be issued except on the
production of a certificate granted by a Licensing Board.
(4) The certificate for a licence to deal in licensable produce shall be in Form 2 in the
First Schedule.
(5) A certificate for a licence shall not be granted except by the Licensing Board of
the Licensing District in which the premises sought to be licensed are situate.
(6) All provisions of the Liquor Dealers’ Licences Act which relate to the making and
hearing of applications for certificates thereunder shall, so far as practicable, apply
(subject to the provisions of this Act) to the making and hearing of applications for
certificates for licences under this Act, and certificates granted hereunder shall be of the
same force and duration as if granted under the Liquor Dealers’ Licences Act, Chapter
174\.
(7) An application to deal in licensable produce shall be in Form 3 in the First
Schedule.
(8) Any person shall be at liberty to oppose the grant of a certificate provided that
written notice of such intended opposition stating in general terms the grounds of such
opposition shall have been served on the chairperson of the Board and on the applicant
not less than seven days before the day fixed for the hearing of the application.
(9) A produce inspector and a police officer shall be present at each licensing session
and, if the applicant has been convicted of any offence involving dishonesty or of any

offence under this Act, it shall be their duty to give evidence of the same to the Licensing
Board.
(10) A licensed dealer who keeps or stores licensable produce in any premises other
than those described in his or her licence or indicated by him or her in his or her licensed
dealer’s book shall be guilty of an offence against this Act.

### 4.

Licensing Districts and constitution of Licensing Boards
(1) For the purposes of this Act there shall be six Licensing Districts, namely—
(a)

the parish of Saint George;

(b)

the parish of Saint David;

(c)

the parish of Saint Andrew;

(d)

the parish of Saint Patrick;

(e)

the parishes of Saint Mark and Saint John; and

(f)

the district of Carriacou, consisting of the islands of Carriacou and Petit
Martinique, and the neighbouring islets lying to the North of the parallel of
twelve degrees and twenty minutes of North latitude that are included within
Grenada.

(2) For each of the said Licensing Districts there shall be established a Licensing
Board consisting of the magistrate whose jurisdiction extends over the whole or the
greater part of the Licensing District and two other persons to be appointed by the
Minister.
(3) The quorum of a Licensing Board shall be two members including the magistrate
aforesaid and the magistrate shall be the chairperson of the Board.
(4) In the case of equality of votes in a question at a session the chairperson shall have
a second or casting vote.
(5) If for any cause less than two members of a Licensing Board are able to attend at
the time and place appointed for a session the power of the Board to adjourn may be
exercised by the member present.
(6) Each member of a Licensing Board appointed by the Minister shall, subject to the
terms of his or her appointment, hold office for one year.

### 5.

Conditions precedent to granting of certificate
(1) No certificate for a licence to deal in licensable produce shall be granted to an
applicant unless the Licensing Board is satisfied—
(a)

that the applicant, if an individual, is of the age of twenty-one years or
upwards and is of good character, or, if a company, is duly constituted under
the Companies Act, Chapter 58A;

(b)

that the applicant is not disqualified from holding a licence; and

(c)

that the premises sought to be licensed are considered suitable for the
purpose by an agricultural officer and, in particular, that they are situate
either near to a public road in a town or village or in a collection of houses
not less than five in number and near to one another.

(2) No certificate for a licence shall be granted unless the applicant therefor produces
to the Licensing Board a testimonial of good character signed by—
(a)

two landowners each of whom shall be in possession or occupation of not
less than ten acres of cultivated land, and who are approved by the Licensing
Board; or

(b)

two Justices of the Peace.

(3) No certificate for a licence shall be granted if the Licensing Board is of the
opinion that—
(a)

the application is not made in good faith but is made for the benefit of some
other person;

(b)

the applicant is married to, or residing in the same house with, a person who
is disqualified from holding a licence:
Provided that the Licensing Board may grant a certificate for a licence to an
applicant if it is satisfied that the events referred to in this paragraph took
place subsequent to such disqualification, or that it is just and equitable in
the circumstances to grant such application;

(c)

the applicant (if formerly licensed) had at any time during the year
immediately preceding the application employed, as manager of his or her
business as a licensed dealer, any person to whom a certificate has been
previously refused; or

(d)

for any other good and sufficient reason such certificate should not be
granted to the applicant.

(4) Any applicant, or any person objecting to the granting of a certificate, who shall
be dissatisfied with the decision of a Licensing Board, shall have a right of appeal to a
Judge of the High Court; and in such event, the provisions relating to appeals from
magistrates contained in the Magistrates Judgements (Appeals) Act, Chapter 178, shall
apply to such appeals, and the Licensing Board shall state in writing the reasons for its
decision.

### 6.

Issue of licences
(1) The Permanent Secretary (Finance) or the officer in charge of any district revenue
office shall, upon the production of a certificate granted by a Licensing Board and upon
payment of the fee therefor as prescribed in the Second Schedule, issue the applicable
licence to the person named and with respect to the premises specified in such certificate:
Provided that, if any objection shall have been raised to the granting of such
certificate and a notice of appeal thereon shall have been given to the Permanent
Secretary (Finance) he or she or, as the case may be, the revenue officer may refuse to
issue the licence pending such appeal.
(2) The Minister may authorise the remission or refund of the whole or part of any fee
if he or she is satisfied that it is just and equitable to do so.

### 7.

Effect of licence
(1) Subject to the provisions of section 18 a licence shall extend only to the premises
described therein.
(2) Two or more persons carrying on business in partnership shall not be required to
take out more than one licence in respect of the same premises:
Provided that the Permanent Secretary (Finance) and the several Licensing Boards
shall, in relation to licences under this Act, have and exercise all the like powers and
discretion for authorising transfers of ownership of licences and changes or removals of
licensed premises which are vested in the Permanent Secretary (Finance) and the Boards
by the Liquor Dealers’ Licences Act, Chapter 174.

### 8.

Power to refuse licence after two convictions

No licence shall be issued to any person who has been convicted of two offences
under this Act until the expiration of a period of two years from the date of the latter of
the two offences aforesaid:
Provided that in any case where circumstances of an exceptional nature are shown
to obtain the Licensing Board shall have power to authorise the issue of a licence
notwithstanding anything contained in this section.

## PART III

Conduct of Business

### 9.

Signboards
(1) Every licensed dealer shall cause to be painted in letters at least three inches in
length and one inch in width, in white upon a black ground or in black upon a white
ground publicly visible and legible upon a board placed over the entrance door of his or
her trading premises, his or her name or trade name together with the words “Licensed to
deal in licensable produce”.
(2) Every such dealer shall preserve and keep such name or names and words so
painted visible and legible during all the time that he or she continues licensed.
(3) Any person contravening the provisions of this section, and any person who puts
or has any such letters as aforesaid upon his or her premises falsely importing that he or
she is a licensed dealer, shall be guilty of an offence against this Act.

### 10.

Produce seller’s book
(1) Every person when offering licensable produce for sale to a licensed dealer shall
produce a valid book, bearing a serial number and with consecutively numbered pages,
which pages shall also bear the serial number of the book, to be called a “produce seller’s
book” in which shall be entered by the licensed dealer the particulars and nature of the
transaction.
(2) A produce seller’s book shall be issued by the Permanent Secretary (Finance) to
any applicant who pays the prescribed fee of twenty-five cents and who, at the time of
application, makes an affidavit in Form 4 in the First Schedule which shall include details
of—
(a)

the area and nature of the applicant’s cultivation; and

(b)

the estimated annual production of licensable produce:

Provided that where the applicant is a licensed dealer no such affidavit shall be
necessary where such dealer does not himself or herself own or occupy any land where
licensable produce is cultivated.
(3) The particulars contained in the affidavit required under subsection (2) shall be
entered by the Permanent Secretary (Finance) on the inside cover of the produce seller’s
book and in any proceedings for an offence under this Act the production of any such
book with the particulars entered as aforesaid shall be prima facie evidence of the
particulars entered in the said affidavit.
(4) Where there is any increase or decrease by sale, purchase, lease, devolution or
otherwise in the area and nature of the seller’s cultivation in licensable produce he or she
shall, within twenty-eight days of the change, apply to the Permanent Secretary (Finance)
to amend the affidavit upon which his or her seller’s book was issued whereupon the
particulars recorded in the inside cover of his or her seller’s book shall be amended
accordingly. Any seller who fails or neglects to comply with the provisions of this
subsection shall be guilty of an offence against this Act.

(5) Every produce seller’s book shall be in Form 5 in the First Schedule or in such
other form as, from time to time, may be by Order prescribed by the Minister. No new
book shall be supplied to any produce seller unless the existing book is returned to the
Permanent Secretary (Finance) or unless—
(a)

the seller has sworn an affidavit to the effect that such book has been lost or
destroyed, or has otherwise satisfied the Permanent Secretary (Finance) that
such is the case;

(b)

has paid a replacement fee of fifty cents for the new book; and

(c)

the Permanent Secretary (Finance) has sent a circular by registered post to
each licensed dealer informing him or her of the registered number of the
lost book, of its cancellation and of the illegality of recording further
transactions therein, and the registered number of the new book. The
registered receipt therefor shall be deemed sufficient notice to the licensed
dealer for the purposes of this section.

(6) A revenue officer, police officer, produce inspector or any person appointed by
the Permanent Secretary (Finance) may demand the production of any produce seller’s
book and may inspect such book and make a copy of any entry therein:
Provided that where such officer, inspector or person has reasonable grounds for
suspecting that an offence has been committed under this Act he or she may retain the
book as evidence.
(7) Where such book has been retained under the provisions of the preceding
subsection the officer, inspector or person retaining the book shall cause a new book to be
issued in place thereof.
(8) In addition to the powers conferred by subsections (6) and (7) the Permanent
Secretary (Finance) may, at any time, by Notice published in the Gazette, do all or any of
the following acts or things—
(a)

declare all or any produce seller’s books to be cancelled from the date
specified in the notice;

(b)

require all or any produce sellers to apply for and obtain a new produce
seller’s book;

(c)

require all or any produce sellers to surrender their produce seller’s book or
books to a specified person at a specified time and place.

(9) A person who—
(a)

fails or neglects to produce or surrender such book when lawfully required to
do so; or

(b)

makes use of any produce seller’s book not his or her own or not issued by
the Permanent Secretary (Finance) or of more than one book; or

(c)

refuses to allow a copy of any entry in such book to be taken,

shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of five thousand
dollars and to imprisonment for twelve months:
Provided that where a produce seller is also a licensed dealer or where he or she
owns more than one property, he or she may be permitted the use of any number of
produce seller’s books if in the opinion of the Permanent Secretary (Finance) such are
reasonably required by him or her.
(10) A licensed dealer who purchases produce from any person who fails to produce a
valid produce seller’s book as required by this section shall be guilty of an offence and
liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of ten thousand dollars and to imprisonment for
twelve months.

(11) The owner of land on which licensable produce is being grown shall not by
reason of the fact that that person is a licensed dealer be exempt from the provisions of
this Act with regard to licensable produce grown on such land, and such produce received
at his or her licensed premises shall be deemed to have been sold or purchased within the
meaning of this section.
(12) The Permanent Secretary (Finance) shall keep a register in Form 6 in the First
Schedule of produce seller’s books issued.

### 11.

Power of Permanent Secretary (Finance) to require verification of particulars
(1) The Permanent Secretary (Finance) may, by writing under his or her hand, require
an agricultural officer to enter and inspect the lands of any licensed seller where he or she
has reason to believe that the affidavit of such licensed seller is not reasonably accurate.
(2) It shall be lawful for an agricultural officer required by the Permanent Secretary
(Finance) as aforesaid to enter at any reasonable time of the day any land specified by the
Permanent Secretary (Finance) for the purpose of ascertaining or verifying the particulars
mentioned in section 10(2).
(3) In deciding whether or not the particulars contained in an affidavit required by
section 10(2) are reasonably accurate, the Permanent Secretary (Finance) shall be guided
by the report of the agricultural officer who inspected the lands to which the affidavit
related, and by any report submitted by or on behalf of the licensed seller.
(4) If the Permanent Secretary (Finance) is not satisfied that the particulars are
reasonably accurate he or she shall require the produce seller to deliver up his or her
produce seller’s book and shall cancel the same and inform the produce seller of the
cancellation.
(5) A new produce seller’s book shall not be issued to any person whose book has
been retained and cancelled unless such person—
(a)

produces an affidavit containing particulars which, in the opinion of the
Permanent Secretary (Finance), are reasonably accurate; and

(b)

pays the prescribed fee of twenty-five cents.

(6) A person who—
(a)

fails to deliver up his or her produce seller’s book after being duly required
to do so by the Permanent Secretary (Finance) unless he or she satisfies the
magistrate that the book has been lost or destroyed; or

(b)

obstructs or hinders any agricultural officer in the execution of his or her
duties under this Act,

shall be guilty of an offence against this Act.

### 12.

Illegal sale of licensable produce
(1) A person who sells or offers for sale to a licensed dealer licensable produce other
than produce grown on the lands described in the produce seller’s book of such person
shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of ten thousand
dollars and to imprisonment for twelve months.
(2) Whenever the Permanent Secretary (Finance) has reason to believe that a breach
of this section has been committed he or she may by writing under his or her hand require
an agricultural officer to enter and inspect the lands of such person.

### 13.

Scales and weights
(1) Every licensed dealer shall keep at his or her trading premises scales and weights
capable of weighing up to one hundred pounds.

(2) Every licensed dealer who fails to keep such scales and weights on his or her said
premises shall be guilty of an offence against this Act.

### 14.

Produce dealer’s book
(1) Every licensed dealer shall keep at his or her licensed premises a book with
consecutively numbered pages to be called a “produce dealer’s book” in the Form 7 in the
First Schedule.
(2) A licensed dealer shall preserve each such book for at least two years after the
date of the last entry therein.

### 15.

Entries in produce dealer’s book
(1) Subject to the provisions of section 18, immediately upon the receipt or disposal
of any licensable produce a licensed dealer shall cause the following entries to be made in
the produce dealer’s book kept by him or her—
(a)

upon receipt—
(i) the date and time of receipt,
(ii) the name and address of the person from whom the licensable produce
is actually received,
(iii) the name of the vendor,
(iv) the number and description of the produce seller’s book,
(v) the description of the licensable produce received,
(vi) the weight, measure or quantity of such licensable produce,
(vii) the amount paid for such licensable produce;

(b)

upon disposal—
(i) the date of disposal,
(ii) the name and address of the purchaser or the place to which, and the
ship by which, exported as the case may be,
(iii) the description of the licensable produce disposed of,
(iv) the weight, measure or quantity of such licensable produce.

(2) A licensed dealer—
(a)

who fails or neglects to keep on his or her licensed premises a produce
dealer’s book;

(b)

who fails or neglects to cause to be made the entries required by this section;

(c)

in whose book there shall be found any entry false in any particular; or

(d)

whose stock of licensable produce does not correspond with the amount
entered in such book,

shall be guilty of an offence against this Act.
(3) Every licensed dealer shall also, at the time of making the entries in the produce
seller’s book as required by this section, enter in the produce seller’s book the particulars
required by section 10.
(4) At the end of each month every licensed dealer shall equate wet cocoa, if any, to
cured cocoa, and shall in every case balance his or her books and carry forward his or her
stocks on hand. For the purpose of equating wet cocoa to cured cocoa the shrinkage
allowance shall be deemed to be sixty per cent.

(5) Every entry in the produce dealer’s book shall be recorded on a separate line in the
book.
(6) Where an offence for which a licensed dealer is convicted or liable to be convicted
under this section has in fact been committed by some agent or employee or other person
acting for such licensed dealer, the agent, employee or other person shall be liable to the
same penalty as if he or she were a licensed dealer.

### 16.

Production of produce dealer’s book for inspection
(1) The Permanent Secretary (Finance) may at any time require the production of, and
inspect, the produce dealer’s book of a licensed dealer.
(2) If a person having the custody or control of any such book neglects to produce it
for inspection when lawfully required so to do, he or she shall be guilty of an offence
against this Act.

### 17.

Power of entry and inspection
(1) A revenue officer, police officer, or produce inspector, or any person appointed by
the Permanent Secretary (Finance) for the purpose, may enter the licensed premises of
any licensed dealer and—
(a)

call for and inspect the scales and weights;

(b)

call for and inspect the books required to be kept under this Act;

(c)

take a copy of any entry in such books;

(d)

if he or she has reasonable grounds for suspecting that an offence under this
Act has been committed, retain the said books as evidence;

(e)

examine the licensable produce in the possession of such dealer and the
place where it is stored;

(f)

weigh and compare all such licensable produce as may be in stock with the
entries in such books; and

(g)

open and examine any crate, barrel or other container shipped by a licensed
dealer which contains or is suspected to contain licensable produce, and for
this purpose go on board and search any ship and may require the master to
produce any documents relating to any cargo which is being carried on such
ship.

(2) A licensed dealer who—
(a)

fails or neglects to produce such scales and weights;

(b)

fails or neglects to produce such books;

(c)

refuses to allow a copy of any entry in such books to be taken;

(d)

obstructs or resists the taking of any such copy;

(e)

fails or neglects to give and provide such assistance as may be necessary for
the purpose of checking the licensable produce in his or her possession; or

(f)

obstructs or resists any such examination of licensable produce in his or her
possession, or the opening and examination of any such crate, barrel or other
container shipped by him or her,

shall be guilty of an offence against this Act.
(3) Where a licensed dealer’s book is retained under the provisions of subsection (1)
the person retaining the book shall cause a new book to be issued to the licensed dealer.

### 18.

Restrictions as to dealing
(1) A person who, on a Sunday, or at any place other than his or her licensed
premises, or at any time except between the hours of 6 a.m. and 6 p.m. of any day—
(a)

sells or offers for sale licensable produce to a licensed dealer; or

(b)

purchases, or delivers or takes delivery of licensable produce,

shall, subject to the provisions hereinafter appearing, be guilty of an offence against this
Act.
(2) Nothing in this section shall be construed to prevent—
(a)

the delivery of licensable produce at any time on a Sunday by any shipper or
consignor to a steamer receiving the same as cargo;

(b)

the sale or delivery, between the hours of 6 a.m. and 6 p.m., on any
weekday, of any licensable produce to one licensed dealer at the licensed
premises of another licensed dealer;

(c)

the purchase or delivery, between the hours of 6 a.m. and 6 p.m., on any
weekday, of any licensable produce, from one licensed dealer at the licensed
premises of another licensed dealer;

(d)

the owner of land whereon any licensable produce was grown from selling,
offering for sale or delivering between the hours of 6 a.m. and 6 p.m. of any
weekday, such licensable produce (not being wet cocoa) to a licensed dealer
on such land or other land of the owner whereon such produce was cured,
dried or stored;

(e)

a elicensed daler from purchasing or taking delivery of such produce
between the hours of 6 a.m. and 6 p.m. on any weekday, on the land
whereon it was grown or on other land of the owner whereon it was cured,
dried or stored; or

(f)

the delivery of wet cocoa between the hours of 6 a.m. and 6 p.m. on any day
prescribed for the sale, purchase or delivery of wet cocoa, at any premises
belonging to him or her and approved by an inspector for the purpose of
curing wet cocoa, the provisions of section 22 notwithstanding.

(3) Entry of all licensable produce purchased, delivered or shipped under the
provisions of subsection (2) shall be made by such licensed dealer on the day on which
such licensable produce was purchased, delivered or shipped.
(4) Nothing in this section contained shall be construed to prevent the sale by a person
of licensable produce not exceeding twenty pounds in weight to a person requiring the
same for domestic or personal use and not trading or dealing in the same:
Provided that a sale under the provisions of this subsection shall be entered, in the
produce seller’s book or the produce dealer’s book, as the case may be, as soon as
delivery of the licensable produce has been effected.

### 19.

Produce dealer’s and produce seller’s books valid for three years
Subject to the provisions of this Act every produce dealer’s book and every produce
seller’s book shall be valid for a period of three years from the date of issue thereof, and a
person who makes any entry in any such book on a date later than three years from the
date of issue thereof shall be guilty of an offence against this Act.

### 20.

Offences by persons not being licensed dealers
(1) A person who, not being a licensed dealer—
(a)

purchases any licensable produce; or

(b)

receives into his or her possession or custody any licensable produce,

shall, subject to the exemptions hereinafter appearing, be guilty of an offence against this
Act.
(2) The provisions of this section shall not extend to—
(a)

the purchase or receipt by the owner of any plantation, from any tenant or
contractor in the occupation of any land part of such plantation, of the
produce of such land;

(b)

the purchase or sale, for seed, of licensable produce by the owner of any
plantation or land;

(c)

the purchase of licensable produce taken or sold under the process of any
court;

(d)

the receipt of licensable produce by a ship’s agent or consignee or carrier for
hire;

(e)

the receipt of licensable produce as a free gift;

(f)

the receipt of licensable produce under any contract for the supply thereof to
any public institution in Grenada;

(g)

the sale to the Ministry of Agriculture of licensable produce for the use of
that Ministry;

(h)

the purchase by, or sale to, any person for domestic purposes of any
licensable produce not exceeding one hundred dollars in value by a licensed
dealer;

(i)

the purchase or receipt of any cotton wool by or from any chemist or
druggist or any mercer or linen draper for medicinal or domestic purposes;

(j)

the receipt or custody of licensable produce by an agent with the written
authority of the owner or manager of the plantation or land from which the
said produce has been derived:
Provided that such agent shall, at the time of receipt of the licensable
produce, have with him or her the produce seller’s book issued to such
owner or manager under section 10;

(k)

the receipt of any licensable produce by any bank, merchant or other person
with whom the same is hypothecated;

(l)

the export for private purposes of licensable produce by any person not
trading or dealing in the same.

(3) The onus of proving to the satisfaction of a magistrate that a transaction falls
within the last preceding subsection shall be upon the person alleging the same.
(4) A revenue officer or police officer or any person appointed by the Permanent
Secretary (Finance) may seize any licensable produce in respect of which he or she has
reasonable grounds for believing that an offence against any of the provisions of this Act
has been committed, and for that purpose it shall be lawful for him or her to enter any
licensed premises or any house or building forming part of or contiguous to such licensed
premises where he or she reasonably suspects such produce to be.
(5) Any produce seized under this section shall be produced in evidence at the trial
and, on conviction of the offender, the same shall be forfeited and sold and the proceeds
shall be paid into the Consolidated Fund.

### 21.

Dealing in washed, or partially dried, or improperly fermented cocoa

If a licensed dealer sells or purchases washed, or partially dried, or improperly
fermented, cocoa, he or she and, in the case of a purchase, the seller also shall be guilty of
an offence against this Act.

### 22.

Dealing in wet cocoa
(1) It shall not be lawful for a licensed dealer to purchase wet cocoa unless he or she
shall have provided at his or her licensed premises means for fermenting and drying such
wet cocoa to the satisfaction of the Chief Technical Officer (Extension) or a person acting
under the authority of a Licensing Board.
(2) A person who—
(a)

sells or offers for sale wet cocoa to a licensed dealer; or

(b)

being a licensed dealer purchases, delivers or takes delivery of wet cocoa,

on any day of the week other than on Friday or such other day as may by Order be
prescribed by the Minister shall be guilty of an offence against this Act.
(3) The Minister may by Order prescribe the days on which wet cocoa may be sold.
(4) Nothing in this section shall apply to or prevent the sale on any day of wet cocoa
by the Ministry of Agriculture or the purchase of wet cocoa from that Ministry.

### 23.

Dealing in unripe cocoa
(1) A person who reaps, purchases or sells unripe cocoa shall be guilty of an offence
against this Act.
(2) A produce inspector may enter upon and inspect any land or premises where such
offences are likely to be committed.

### 24.

Power to reject unsatisfactory cocoa for export
(1) A produce inspector may reject as being unfit for export any cured cocoa which,
on examination, he or she finds to be—
(a)

not thoroughly dried; or

(b)

not thoroughly fermented,

or to contain—
(c)

foreign matter;

(d)

unsound or subgrade cocoa;

(e)

cocoa shell; or

(f)

more than four per cent by count of mouldy, insect-damaged, flat or
germinated beans, or slaty beans, or smoky beans.

(2) An exporter who is aggrieved by a decision made by a produce inspector under
subsection (1) may appeal to a panel comprising three arbitrators appointed by the
Minister, and the decision of the panel shall be final.
(3) In subsection (1)—
“flat beans” are cocoa beans from which the cotyledons are absent;
“foreign matter” means any material other than cocoa beans;
“germinated beans” are cocoa beans the testa or seed coat of which has been
pierced, split or broken, by the growth of the seed germ;
“insect-damaged beans” are cocoa beans the internal parts of which contain insects
or larvae or are visibly damaged by any type of insect;

“mouldy beans” are cocoa beans in the internal parts of which any visible mould or
fungus is present;
“slaty beans” are cocoa beans showing, when cut lengthwise through the centre, a
slaty colour on one-half or more of the cut surface;
“smoky beans” are cocoa beans which smell or taste of, or show signs of
contamination by, smoke.

### 25.

Refusing to answer or making false answer
(1) A person who, when offering for sale licensable produce to a licensed dealer,
refuses to answer, or makes any false answer to, any question put to him or her by such
dealer or his or her employee for the purpose of ascertaining—
(a)

his or her name and abode;

(b)

the particular situation of the land from which such licensable produce has
been reaped; or

(c)

whether he or she is the owner, tenant or contractor in occupation of any
such land or the employee or agent of any such owner, tenant or contractor,

shall be guilty of an offence against this Act.
(2) A licensed dealer to whom any licensable produce is offered for sale or his or her
employee, or any person authorised by such dealer, may—
(a)

apprehend any person acting in contravention of the provisions of the last
preceding subsection;

(b)

take such person to the police station of the district for the purpose of being
detained there, unless he or she is given bail to appear before a magistrate;

(c)

retain the licensable produce to be dealt with as the magistrate shall direct.

(3) On the ownership of the licensable produce being proved to his or her satisfaction,
the magistrate shall order it to be delivered to the owner.
(4) If the magistrate is not satisfied as to the ownership of the produce, he or she shall
order it to be sold at the expiration of seven days, and the proceeds thereof to be paid to
the person whom the magistrate considers to be lawfully entitled thereto:
Provided that the magistrate may order the sale at any time before the expiration of
seven days if, in his or her opinion, the produce is likely to perish or decrease in value.

### 26.

Purchasing from children under twelve years of age
A person who purchases licensable produce from any person whom he or she knows
or has reasonable cause for believing to be under the age of twelve years shall be guilty of
an offence against this Act.

### 27.

Power of entry where illegality suspected
(1) If a revenue officer, police officer or produce inspector has reasonable grounds for
believing that licensable produce is in or upon any premises contrary to the provisions of
this Act, it shall be lawful for him or her to enter such premises and to call upon the
owner or the person in possession of the premises to weigh and account for the licensable
produce found therein, and if he or she considers the account not satisfactory, he or she
may summon the owner or person in possession before a magistrate; and, if the defendant
does not give an account to the satisfaction of the magistrate as to how he or she came by
the licensable produce he or she shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary
conviction, to a fine of five thousand dollars and to imprisonment for twelve months.

(2) For the purposes of this section, the term “premises” shall include a private
residence.

### 28.

False representation as to quality of produce to the prejudice of purchaser
A person who shall sell, deliver or export, or who shall attempt to sell, deliver or
export, in consideration of an advance or for value received, to the prejudice of the
purchaser, any bag or package purporting to contain licensable produce which does not in
fact contain produce of the substance or quality described by such person shall, unless he
or she shall prove that he or she acted without intent to defraud, be guilty of an offence
against this Act.

### 29.

Trespassing upon land where produce ripening
A person found trespassing on any land, where licensable produce is growing and
ripening, and failing to give an account to the satisfaction of the Magistrate as to the
reason for his or her presence on the land, shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine
of two hundred dollars.

## PART IV

Special Provisions Relating to Nutmegs

### 30.

Improper storage of uncured nutmegs an offence
A person who stores or keeps uncured nutmegs in a bag, box or other receptacle or
who stores or keeps uncured nutmegs on any premises heaped or spread to a depth greater
than that of three inches shall be guilty of an offence against this Act.

### 31.

Possession, sale or purchase of improperly treated nutmegs an offence
Whoever—
(a)

soaks any nutmegs or has in his or her possession any nutmegs which he or
she knows or would, with the exercise of reasonable diligence, know to have
been soaked;

(b)

picks or gathers immature nutmegs or offers to purchase any nutmegs which
he or she knows or would, with the exercise of reasonable diligence, know to
have been soaked or to be immature; or

(c)

sells or offers for sale, purchases or offers to purchase, any nutmegs which
he or she knows or would, with reasonable diligence, know to have been
soaked or to be immature,

shall be guilty of an offence against this Act.

### 32.

Spreading of uncured nutmegs on concrete surface for purpose of drying an
offence
A person who—
(a)

spreads any uncured nutmegs on the earth or on a stone or concrete or road
surface for the purpose of drying them;

(b)

keeps, places or stores uncured nutmegs in unventilated premises; or

(c)

cracks any uncured nutmegs,

shall be guilty of an offence against this Act.

## PART V

Sales by Peasant Proprietors and Procedure

### 33.

Sales by peasant proprietors illegal in certain cases
(1) The full amount of the price of licensable produce sold to a licensed dealer by a
peasant proprietor shall be paid in cash, and every payment made to a peasant proprietor
by a licensed dealer in respect of such sale by the delivery to him or her of goods or
otherwise than in cash shall be illegal, null and void:
Provided that when a peasant proprietor is indebted to a licensed dealer for cash
lent, the price payable in respect of the licensable produce may, if the parties so agree, be
retained by the licensed dealer on account of such indebtedness.
(2) Every peasant proprietor shall be entitled to recover from a licensed dealer the
whole or so much of the price of licensable produce sold to the licensed dealer as shall
not have been actually paid to him or her in cash or retained for a debt for cash lent as
aforesaid.
(3) In any action, suit or other proceeding brought or commenced by a peasant
proprietor against a licensed dealer for the recovery of any sum of money due for or on
account of the price of licensable produce, the defendant shall not be allowed to make any
set-off, nor to claim any reduction of the plaintiff’s demand, by reason or in respect of
any goods sold, delivered or supplied to such peasant proprietor at any shop kept by or
belonging to such licensed dealer, or in the profits of which such licensed dealer shall
have any share or interest:
Provided that the defendant may be allowed to make a set-off, or claim a reduction
of the plaintiff’s demand, by reason or in respect of cash lent.
(4) No licensed dealer shall have or be entitled to maintain any suit or action against
any peasant proprietor for or in respect of any goods sold, delivered or supplied to any
such peasant proprietor, as or on account of the price of licensable produce.
(5) A licensed dealer who shall by himself or herself or by the agency of any other
person directly or indirectly make any payment contrary to the provisions of this section
shall be guilty of an offence against this Act.

### 34.

Time of entry
The power of entry conferred by this Act may be exercised—
(a)

at any time between 6 a.m. and 6 p.m. of any day;

(b)

at any time the premises are open; or

(c)

if a revenue officer, police officer or produce inspector has reasonable
grounds for believing that an offence against any provision of this Act has
been committed, at any time during the day or night and on all days
including Sundays and public holidays.

### 35.

Power to arrest person conveying produce
(1) It shall be lawful for—
(a)

any owner, manager, overseer, or agent of any land whereon any kind of
licensable produce is cultivated, without warrant, to apprehend and detain
any person having in his or her possession or carrying or conveying any such
kind of produce in and upon any such land or in or upon any land, road or
way immediately adjoining such first-mentioned land;

(b)

a revenue officer or police officer—

(i) without warrant, to apprehend and detain any person, not being the
owner of any land whereon any kind of licensable produce is
cultivated, having in his or her possession, or who shall be found
anywhere carrying or conveying, any such kind of produce, and
(ii) to stop and examine any horse or other beast of burden or any motor
vehicle, cart, waggon or other conveyance, in order to ascertain
whether any such kind of produce is being conveyed thereon or
therein,
which such owner, manager, overseer, agent, revenue officer or police officer may have
reasonable cause to suspect to be stolen or unlawfully obtained.
(2) If a person liable to arrest under the provisions of this section escapes from any
person attempting to arrest him or her or after he or she has been arrested, or lets fall or
throws away such produce from his or her possession or abandons the possession of any
such produce, it shall be lawful for a magistrate upon application to issue his or her
warrant for the arrest of such person, and upon his or her arrest such person shall be
deemed to be the person apprehended and detained within the meaning of this section.
(3) The application for a warrant under the last preceding subsection may be made by
any of the persons named in subsection (1) who has actually made or attempted to make
the arrest.
(4) On the hearing of any case under this section it shall not be incumbent on the
prosecutor to prove that the person having in his or her possession or found carrying or
conveying any such produce as aforesaid is not the owner, but the onus of proving
ownership shall be on the defendant.

### 36.

Procedure on arrest
(1) Immediately upon the apprehension or detention of a person under the powers in
the preceding section contained, such person shall, together with all produce so suspected
to be stolen or unlawfully obtained, and all beasts of burden and conveyances used in
carrying and conveying the same, be taken before a magistrate to be dealt with according
to law, or to the nearest police station.
(2) In the latter case, the officer in charge of such station may if he or she thinks fit
take bail, with one sufficient surety, from such person by recognisance to any amount not
exceeding one thousand dollars, conditioned for his or her appearance before a magistrate
in the usual course.

### 37.

Not satisfying magistrate as to possession
(1) If a person so taken before a magistrate does not make it appear to the satisfaction
of the magistrate by what lawful means he or she came in possession of any such
produce, he or she shall be liable on summary conviction to imprisonment for six months
and the magistrate may direct that the licensable produce be returned to the owner thereof
or be forfeited.
(2) If the offender be a male not above the age of sixteen years he or she shall be
liable, in lieu of imprisonment, to be ordered to be caned.

### 38.

Prosecution of offences
All offences under this Act, and under any regulation made under this Act, shall be
prosecuted, heard and determined, and all penalties shall be recovered, on summary
conviction, before a magistrate.

### 39.

Penalty for stealing, etc., licensable produce

A person convicted of the theft of licensable produce, or of being accessory to the
theft thereof, or being the receiver of stolen licensable produce knowing the same to have
been stolen shall, notwithstanding anything contained in the Criminal Code Act, Chapter
72A, be liable on summary conviction to a fine of five thousand dollars and to
imprisonment for twelve months.

### 40.

Forfeiture of licence and cancellation
(1) If a person licensed as a dealer under this Act is convicted of an offence against
this Act which the magistrate shall determine to be an offence involving dishonesty, the
magistrate, in addition to any other penalty which he or she may be authorised to inflict,
may order the licence of the offender to be forfeited and cancelled.
(2) If any person licensed as a dealer under this Act is convicted of a third offence
under this Act the magistrate may, in addition to any other penalty which he or she may
be authorised to inflict, order that the licence of the offender be forfeited and cancelled.
(3) The magistrate shall cause notice of the forfeiture and cancellation of a licence,
and of the date of the order of such forfeiture and cancellation, to be published in the
Gazette.
(4) Production of a copy of the Gazette containing a notice of such forfeiture and
cancellation shall be received as prima facie evidence thereof in all courts.

### 41.

Liability of partners, agents, etc.
Where a person licensed as a dealer under this Act is absent from Grenada, the
attorney, manager, agent or clerk in charge of the licensed premises shall be deemed to be
the person so licensed under this Act.

### 42.

General penalty
A person who commits an offence against this Act for which no specific penalty is
provided, shall be liable to a fine of three thousand dollars and to imprisonment for six
months.

### 43.

Onus of proof
The burden of proving that any licensable produce, bought, sold, exposed for sale, or
in the possession of any person, is not bought, sold, exposed for sale, or in the possession
of that person in contravention of the provisions of this Act shall lie on the person buying,
selling or exposing for sale or having in his or her possession such licensable produce.

### 44.

Application of this Act to any agricultural product
The Minister may, from time to time by Order published in the Gazette, direct that this
Act shall apply to any other agricultural product.

### 45.

Court fees
No fees shall be charged to a complainant in respect of any summons or other process
of the court under the provisions of this Act:
Provided that the court may order the defendant to pay costs in a case where judgement is given against him or her.

### 46.

Application of proceeds of sale by order of court
All moneys received under this Act for licensable produce sold by process of any
court or by order of a magistrate, if not successfully claimed within one month after the

sale, shall be paid into the Treasury; and if not successfully claimed within twelve
months, shall then form part of the Consolidated Fund.

### 47.

Rewards
A magistrate may award to any person giving information which has led to a
conviction under this Act any sum not exceeding one hundred dollars:
Provided that any such sum shall not exceed half the value of the fine recovered.

### 48.

Power to amend First Schedule
The Minister may by Order revoke, amend, vary or add to the Forms set forth in the
First Schedule and substitute other Forms therefor.

### 49.

Appointment of produce inspectors
The Minister may appoint a produce inspector or produce inspectors whose duty it
shall be to enforce and to carry out the provisions of this Act and regulations made
thereunder.

### 50.

Limitation of time in respect of proceedings
All proceedings for the recovery of any penalties or forfeiture or for the prosecution of
any offence under this Act shall be commenced within six months after the offence has
been committed.

### 51.

Regulations and power to amend Second Schedule
(1) The Minister may make regulations prescribing the duties and power of the
produce inspector or produce inspectors appointed under this Act and generally for the
further, better, or more convenient carrying out of any of the provisions or purposes of
this Act.
(2) Regulations may be made by the Minister under this section amending the licence
fees listed in the Second Schedule, but such regulations shall be of no force or effect until
they have been approved by resolution of the House of Representatives and published in
the Gazette.
