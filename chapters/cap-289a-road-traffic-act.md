## PART I

Preliminary

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Road Traffic Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires—
“agricultural tractor” means a tractor used exclusively for agricultural purposes
under the authority of a licence issued by the Licensing Authority under this Act;
“agricultural trailer” means a trailer used exclusively for agricultural purposes
under the authority of a licence issued by the Licensing Authority under this Act;
“authorised officer” means a person appointed pursuant to the provisions of section
3A;
“Board” means the Grenada Transport Board established by section 3;
“Chief Technical Officer” means the Chief Technical Officer in the Ministry of
Communications and Works;
“combination” means a group consisting of a motor vehicle connected to one or
more other vehicles;
“commercial vehicle” means a goods vehicle available for hire or reward and not
used by the owner for the transport of his or her own goods exclusively;
“cycle” means motor cycle;
“demerit point” means a point recorded pursuant to section 50 against the name of
a driver of a motor vehicle as part of a penalty on his or her conviction for an offence
under this Act;

“diplomat” means a head of mission or any member of the staff of a head of
mission certified to the Licensing Authority, by or on behalf of the Minister
responsible for foreign affairs, to be a diplomat;
“driver” includes any person actually driving or riding a motor vehicle at any given
time and any person in charge thereof for the purpose of driving or riding at any time
when the same is stationary on any road;
“driving” includes being in charge of a motor vehicle for the purpose of driving or
riding at any time when the motor vehicle is stationary on any road;
“driving licence” means a licence issued in accordance with Part IV and kept in the
form specified in section 42;
“freight passenger vehicle” means a motor vehicle constructed for the purpose of
carrying both passengers and goods and which is approved by the Licensing Authority
as suitable for that purpose;
“goods” includes—
(a)

animals, whether such animals are dead or alive; or

(b)

a container, whether such container is empty or full;

“goods vehicle” means a motor vehicle which is so constructed or adapted as to
show that its primary purpose is the carriage or haulage of goods, merchandise or
other loads;
“head of mission” means an Ambassador, High Commissioner or other person, by
whatever title called, accredited by a Sovereign power or by an intergovernmental
institution and recognised as such in Grenada by the Government of Grenada;
“hiring car” or “taxi” means a public service vehicle having seating
accommodation for not more than thirteen passengers and which is of a type approved
by the Licensing Authority as suitable for the ordinary purposes of a taxi;
“Inspector” means any person appointed under the provisions of this Act to carry
out the inspection, examination and making of reports on the condition of vehicles or
for other purposes of this Act;
“Licensing Authority” means the authority appointed in accordance with the
provisions of section 4 for the registration, licensing and control of motor vehicles;
“loader” means a person who—
(a)

loads a vehicle or combination;

(b)

supervise the loading of a vehicle or combination;

(c)

manages to control an activity in paragraph (a) or (b);

“load of a vehicle or combination” means—
(a)

all goods, passengers and drivers in or on the vehicle or combination;

(b)

all fuel, water, lubricants and readily removable equipment necessary for
normal operation of the vehicle;

(c)

any personal items of the driver of the vehicle;

“Maximum Gross Weight” or “MGW”, in reference to a goods vehicle, means the
tare together with the load including the weight of the driver and loaders, and in
reference to a motor omnibus means the tare together with the weight of the driver,
conductor and number of passengers for which the vehicle is licensed; and for the
purposes of this definition the weight of each person to be carried shall be taken as
126 pounds;
“MGW” means Maximum Gross Weight;

“Minister” means the Minister responsible for Communications and Works;
“minor risk breach” means—
(a)

a breach of a mass requirement where the subject matter of the breach is less
than a hundred and five per cent of the MGW, which is the MGW plus an
additional per cent less than five per cent, rounded to the nearest—
(i) 0.01 tonne, or
(ii) 0.5 tonne, whichever is the greatest;

(b)

a breach of a dimension where—
(i) the subject matter of the breach is less than a projection of 40
millimetres measured from a side of the maximum permissible
dimension limit as prescribed,
(ii) the load projects from both sides of the vehicle and the length of the
projection from one side is greater than the length of the projection
from the one side is greater than the projection from the other side, in
which instance, the breach is categorised by reference to the longer
projection, or
(iii) the overall length of the vehicle or combination with or without load,
is not more than 0.35 metre over the maximum permissible dimension
limit as prescribed;

(c)

a breach of load restraint requirement where the loss or shifting of a load—
(i) has not occurred,
(ii) is not imminent, and
(iii) is assessed by the authorised officer not to involve an appreciable risk
of harm to public safety; road and road infrastructure and amenity for
the environment, where it occurs;

“motor cycle” means a motor vehicle with less than four wheels and which has a
horizontal bar type steering mechanism;
“motor lorry” means a goods vehicle which is itself constructed to carry goods,
merchandise or other loads;
“motor omnibus” means a public service vehicle other than one licensed as a
“hiring car” or “taxi”, and includes a light motor omnibus having seating
accommodation for not more than seventeen passengers, a heavy motor omnibus
having seating accommodation for more than seventeen passengers and a freight
passenger vehicle;
“motor vehicle” means any mechanically propelled vehicle manufactured or
intended or adapted for use on roads;
“night” means the period from half an hour after sunset until half an hour before
sunrise;
“overtaking” includes one vehicle passing or attempting to pass any other vehicle
that is proceeding in the same direction as that of the vehicle so passing or attempting
to pass the other vehicle;
“owner”—
(a)

in the case of a motor vehicle that is registered, means the person in whose
name the vehicle is registered and includes the person who has custody or
control of the motor vehicle;

(b)

in the case of a motor vehicle that is not registered, includes the person in
actual charge or possession of the vehicle in Grenada:

Provided that, in the case of a motor vehicle which is the subject of a hire purchase
agreement, it means the person in possession of the vehicle under that agreement; and
Provided further that whenever the registered owner of a motor vehicle is absent
from Grenada, the person in actual charge or possession of the vehicle in Grenada shall
be deemed to be the owner thereof;
“parking” means causing or permitting a vehicle, whether occupied or not, to stand
on a road, except—
(a)

while the vehicle is being loaded or unloaded;

(b)

while passengers are entering or leaving the vehicle;

(c)

in compliance with the directions of a member of the Police Force or of a
traffic sign or with the requirements of traffic regulations;

(d)

while temporarily stopped by reason of traffic conditions; or

(e)

while stopped owing to mechanical breakdown or lack of fuel;

“parking place” means—
(a)

a place designated as such by the Minister; or

(b)

a place where motor vehicles of any particular class or description may wait;

“private motor car” means any motor vehicle other than a goods vehicle or a public
service vehicle;
“public safety” means the safety of persons or property, including the safety of—
(a)

the drivers and passengers in vehicles and combinations;

(b)

persons in or in the vicinity of, or likely to be in or in the vicinity of roads
infrastructure and public places; and

(c)

vehicles and comination and heavy loads on or in them;

“public service vehicle” means a motor vehicle used for carrying passengers for
hire or reward, whether at separate and distinct fares for their respective places or not;
“quadbike” means a vehicle with four wheels which has a steering mechanism
similar to that of a motor cycle;
“responsible person”, in relation to a vehicle or combination, means—
(a)

the owner of the vehicle;

(b)

the driver of the vehicle;

(c)

the registered operator in respect of the vehicle; or

(d)

the person with responsibility for carrying or moving the vehicle;

“risk breach” means, in respect of a load, a minor risk breach, a substantial risk
breach or a severe risk breach, respectively;
“road” means an area that is open to, or used by the public and developed for, or
has as one of its main uses, the driving, riding or parking of a vehicle or other traffic;
“road infrastructure ” means—
(a)

a road, including its surface or pavement;

(b)

anything under or supporting a road or its surface or pavement and
maintained by the State or an agency of the State;

(c)

any bridge, tunnel, causeway, road ferry, ford or other work or structure
forming part of a road system or supporting a road;

(d)

any bridge or other work or structure located above, in or on a road and
maintained by the State or an agency of the State; or

(e)

anything as may be prescribed by the Minister;

“road-related area ” means—
(a)

an area that divides a road;

(b)

a foot path or nature strip adjacent to a road;

(c)

an area that is open to the public and is designed for use by cyclists or
animals;

(d)

an area that is not a road that is open to or used by the public for driving,
riding or parking of vehicles;

(e)

a shoulder of a road;

(f)

any other area that is open to or used by the public and designated a road
pursuant to the provisions of the Physical Planning and Development
Control Act, Chapter 241A;

“road transport” means the transport of goods or passengers or both involving the
use of a road or road infrastructure by means of a vehicle or combination of vehicle
and trucks;
“safe location” means a location that a police officer acting upon the advice of an
authorised officer, believes poses a reduced risk or no appreciable risk to public
safety, the environment, road, wall infrastructure or related area, or public amenity;
“severe risk breach” means—
(a)

a breach of a mass requirement where the subject matter of the breach is a
hundred and twenty per cent or more of the MGW, which is the MGW plus
an additional twenty per cent or more, rounded to the nearest 0.1 tonne;

(b)

a breach of a dimension requirement as prescribed where—
(i) the projection from a side of the vehicle is eighty millimetres or over,
measured from a side of the vehicle or combination, and where the
load projects from the vehicle on both sides, the breach shall be
categorised by the longer projection,
(ii) the height, with or without load, is 0.6 metre or more over the
maximum permissible limit,
(iii) the overall length of the vehicle or combination, with or without load,
is 0.6 metre or more over the maximum permissible dimension limit
as prescribed; and

(c)

the loss or shifting of a load has occurred and is assessed by an authorised
officer to involve an appreciable risk of harm to public safety, road and road
infrastructure, public amenity or the environment;

“substantial risk breach” means—
(a)

a breach of a mass requirement where the subject matter of the breach is
equal to or greater than a hundred and five per cent of the MGW, that being
the MGW plus an additional per cent greater than five per cent, rounded to
the nearest tonne; or

(b)

a breach of a dimension requirement where—
(i) the projection from a side of the vehicle is equal to but not greater
than eighty millimetres measured from a side of the vehicle or
combination, and the load projects from the vehicle on both sides of

the vehicle; in which instance the breach is categorised by the longer
projection,
(ii) the height, with or without load, is fifty millimetres over the
maximum permissible dimension limit as prescribed,
(iii) the overall length of the vehicle or combination, with or without load,
exceeds thirty-five metres over the maximum permissible dimension
limit; or
(c)

a loss or shifting of a load which in the opinion of the authorised officer—
(i) has already occurred or is imminent and is assessed by the authorised
officer not to involve an appreciable risk of harm to public safety,
road and road infrastructure, public amenity or the environment,
(ii) even if it is not occurring or imminent and is assessed by the
authorised officer to involve an appreciable risk of harm to public
safety, road and road infrastructure, public amenity or the
environment;

“taxi” means “hiring car”;
“ton” means the Imperial ton of 2,240 lbs.;
“tractor” means any motor vehicle constructed for the purpose of hauling trailers
whether or not it is itself capable of carrying passengers or goods but does not include
a goods vehicle or motor lorry so constructed except when such goods vehicle or
motor lorry is hauling a trailer;
“traffic” includes vehicles of every description, pedestrians and all animals being
ridden, driven or led;
“traffic sign” means any object or device (whether fixed or portable) for conveying
to traffic on a road or to any particular class of traffic, warnings, information,
requirements, restrictions or prohibitions of any description and a reference to the
erection or placing of a traffic sign includes a reference to the displace thereof in any
manner whatsoever (whether or not involving fixing or placing);
“trailer” means a vehicle that—
(a)

is built to be towed or is towed by a motor;

(b)

is not capable of being propelled in the course of normal use on a road
without being towed by a motor vehicle;

“vehicle” means any description of vehicles on wheels used in the transportation of
goods or passengers.

### 3.

Grenada Transport Board
(1) There is hereby established for the purposes of this Act a body called the Grenada
Transport Board (hereinafter referred to as the Board) consisting of the Chief Technical
Officer as chairperson, the Commissioner of Police, the Officer-in-Charge of the Traffic
Department of the Police Force, a representative of the Attorney-General from the
Ministry of Legal Affairs, a representative of insurance companies appointed by the
Minister, and not less than three but no more than five other persons appointed by the
Minister, at least two of which such other persons shall ordinarily represent the interests
of owners of public service vehicles.
In the absence of the chairperson the members present shall elect one of their members
to preside as chairperson of the meeting. The Board may act by any three of their
members and may so act notwithstanding any vacancy in the numbers constituting the
Board. The Board shall have the power to regulate their procedure.

(2) The Permanent Secretary in the Ministry of Communications and Works shall be
the Secretary to the Board and, subject to the directives of the chairperson, shall be
responsible for co-ordinating the activities of the Board.
(3) The Board shall act in an advisory capacity and shall advise the Minister on all
matters pertaining to road transport and traffic and in particular as to the following
matters—
(a)

transport rates, fares, tolls, dues or other charges;

(b)

licence duties and fees in respect of motor and other vehicles;

(c)

customs and excise duties in respect of transport vehicles and fuel therefor;

(d)

the needs of any area or areas in relation to traffic (including the provision of
adequate, suitable and efficient services and the elimination of unnecessary
or unremunerative services) and the co-ordination of all forms of passenger
and goods transport;

(e)

road safety;

(f)

the regulation and control of traffic;

(g)

any other matter affecting traffic or transport that the Minister may refer to
the Board;

(h)

regulating road transport.

(4) The Board shall hear and determine any appeal submitted by any aggrieved person
against any order or decision of the Licensing Authority, or of a Licensing Officer, and
the Board’s decision shall be final and conclusive.
(5) No rates, fares, tolls, dues or other charges charged by public service vehicles
shall be increased except with the prior written approval of the Minister advised by the
Board under subsection (3).
(6) Any owner, driver or conductor of any public service vehicle, or any other person
on their behalf, who knowingly demands, collects, or attempts to collect any increase in
the rate, fare, toll, due or other charge charged by such vehicle when such owner, driver,
conductor or other person knows or ought to know that such increase has not had the
prior written approval of the Minister advised by the Board under subsection (3) commits
an offence.
(7) The Board shall perform such other duties as are assigned to it by this Act or
regulations made hereunder.

### 3A.

Appointment of authorised officer
(1) The Grenada Transport Board may, by instrument in writing, appoint persons to
be authorised officers for the purposes of this Act.
(2) An authorised officer appointed pursuant to subsection (1), shall be accountable to
the Permanent Secretary in the Ministry of Communications and Works and shall have
such powers as may be prescribed.
(3) The Grenada Transport Board shall issue an authorised officer with an
identification card.
(4) An identification card issued pursuant to subsection (3), shall contain a
photograph of the authorised officer, the name of the Grenada Transport Board; and—
(a)

the name and signature of the officer; and

(b)

a unique number that has been issued to the officer by the Grenada Transport
Board.

(5) An authorised officer who is exercising or about to exercise a power pursuant to
the provisions of this Act, shall be required to comply with a request by the owner or
driver of the vehicle concerned to identify himself or herself by producing his or her
identification.

### 4.

Licensing Authority, Licensing Officers and Inspectors
(1) The Commissioner of Police shall be the Licensing Authority and shall as such be
charged with responsibility for the registration, licensing and inspection of all motor
vehicles, the issue of driving licences, and such other matters as are assigned to him or
her by this Act or any regulations made hereunder.
(2) The Licensing Authority may appoint Licensing Officers and Inspectors to serve
in various localities to ensure the proper carrying out of the provisions of this Act and
regulations made hereunder.
(3) All Licensing Officers and Inspectors shall be under the direction of the Licensing
Authority.
(4) All licences and certificates of registration shall be issued by Licensing Officers
on behalf of the Licensing Authority.
PART IA
Inspection of Loads of Vehicle or Combination

### 4A.

Establishment of protocols and guidelines
In determining the necessary procedures for the operation of the provisions of this
Part, the Permanent Secretary in the Ministry of Communications and Works and the
Commissioner of Police shall establish such protocols and guidelines that are deemed
necessary for the effective implementation of the provisions of the Act.

### 4B.

Duty of responsible person
A responsible person shall—
(a)

exercise reasonable care in order to ensure that a vehicle or combination for
which he or she is responsible conforms to the mass, dimension or load
restraint requirement prescribed in respect of that vehicle;

(b)

shall not drive or cause to be driven along a road, any vehicle or combination
that contravenes the mass, dimension or load restraint requirement
prescribed in respect of that vehicle.

### 4C.

Categories of risk breaches
For the purposes of this Act, a breach of a maximum mass, dimension or load restraint
requirement as prescribed or as registered in respect of a vehicle or combination may be
categorised as follows—
(a)

a minor risk breach;

(b)

a substantial risk breach;

(c)

a severe risk breach.

### 4D.

Power of police officer to stop vehicle and measure load

(1) A police officer, acting on the advice and expertise of an authorised officer, shall
stop a vehicle or combination, which he or she believes is the subject matter of a risk
breach and shall—
(a)

require the driver of the vehicle or combination to—
(i) produce a written record of the actual MGW in respect of the vehicle
or combination, or
(ii) provide such information in relation to where and by whom the
vehicle or combination was loaded;

(b)

require that the MGW of the vehicle be measured in accordance with the
provisions of section 119;

(c)

subject to subsection (2), and acting on the advice and expertise of an
authorised officer, direct the responsible person of the vehicle or
combination not to proceed on its destination, but to go to a safe location
until the specified breaches of mass, dimension or load restraint
requirements have been rectified.

(2) Where pursuant to subsection (1) the police officer, acting on the advice and
expertise of an authorised officer, believes that the vehicle or combination commits a
minor risk breach, the police officer shall issue a police notice pursuant to section 97, for
an amount specified in the Third Schedule in respect of the breach, and shall, where he or
she does not give a direction pursuant to subsection (1)(c), allow the vehicle or
combination and load to proceed on its destination.
(3) Where pursuant to subsection (1), the police officer, acting on the advice and
expertise of an authorised officer, believes that the vehicle or combination commits a
substantial risk breach, the police officer shall—
(a)

issue a notice to the owner of the vehicle in accordance with section 97, for
an amount specified in the Third Schedule in respect of that breach specified
for that offence; and

(b)

drive away or tow away, or require the driver to drive away or tow away, the
vehicle in accordance with the provisions of section 30.

(4) In addition to the penalties stipulated under subsection (3), the police officer may
also charge the driver of the vehicle or combination with an offence pursuant to the
provisions of section 57.
(5) Where pursuant to subsection (1), the police officer, acting on the advice and
expertise of an authorised officer, believes that the driver of a vehicle or combination
commits a severe risk breach, the police officer shall—
(a)

drive away or tow away, or require the driver to drive away or tow away, the
vehicle in accordance with the provisions of section 30; and

(b)

charge the driver of the vehicle for an offence pursuant to section 56.

### 4E.

Duty and liability of loader
(1) A loader of a vehicle or combination shall exercise reasonable care in order to
ensure that the vehicle or combination is loaded in a manner that conforms to the mass,
dimension and load restraint requirement allowed in respect of that vehicle.
(2) The loader of a vehicle or combination who fails to comply with the provisions of
subsection (1) commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine not
exceeding twelve thousand dollars or to a term of imprisonment not exceeding three
years.

### 4F.

Duty of loader to keep records

(1) The loader of a vehicle or combination shall keep a record of—
(a)

the weight of a load in respect of the vehicle or combination;

(b)

the date the load was placed in the vehicle or combination;

(c)

the instrument or method used to measure the load of the vehicle or
combination; and

(d)

the name of the operator of the measuring instrument, if applicable.

(2) The loader of a vehicle or combination who fails to comply with subsection (1)
commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding three
thousand dollars or to a term of imprisonment not exceeding three months.

## PART II

Registration of Motor Vehicles

### 5.

Motor vehicle registers
(1) The Licensing Authority shall keep registers of motor vehicles in the prescribed
forms and separate registers shall be kept by the Licensing Officer in any locality as
directed by the Licensing Authority.
(2) Every Licensing Officer shall forward to the Licensing Authority a copy of every
entry made in his or her local register.
(3) The Licensing Authority shall supply to any person applying for a copy of the
entries relating to any specified vehicle a certificate extract of these entries on payment
by the applicant of the relevant fee set out in Part III of the First Schedule.

### 6.

Registration of motor vehicles
(1) No person shall, in any place, use or keep for use or, being the owner, permit any
other person to use or keep for use any motor vehicle except a vehicle exempted from
registration, unless it is registered under this Act and has affixed thereto the prescribed
identification mark in the prescribed manner, and any motor vehicle tax regarding its
registration required to be paid in accordance with any law has been paid.
(2) No person shall be convicted of an offence against this section if he or she proves
that he or she has taken all reasonable steps to comply with its requirements, or that the
vehicle was in use on a road on its way for the purpose of being registered or of being
examined or inspected under section 7 for the purpose of being registered.
(3) Application for the registration of a motor vehicle shall be made by the owner on
the prescribed form, and he or she shall pay the relevant fee set out in Part III of the First
Schedule.
(4) The Licensing Officer to whom application is made shall, upon being satisfied
that this Act and the Motor Vehicles Insurance (Third Party Risks) Act, Chapter 202, and
regulations respectively made thereunder, have been complied with, forthwith enter
particulars of the vehicle in the register, and shall assign to the vehicle a registered
identification mark or registration mark, comprising a registration letter or registration
letters and a registration number or registration numbers or a registration figure or
registration figures, the letter or letters or one of the letters as the case may be
representing the class of such vehicle or the parish in which the applicant resides.
(5) A certified extract or copy of the entry in the register, in the prescribed form, shall
be given to the owner of the vehicle, and such certificate of registration shall be regarded
as prima facie evidence that the vehicle to which it refers has been registered.

(6) If a motor vehicle does not bear on it an identification mark as provided by this
section, this fact shall be regarded as prima facie evidence that the vehicle has not been
registered, and any member of the Police Force may detain such a vehicle until enquiries
have been made.
(7) The onus of proving that a vehicle has been registered rests upon the owner of the
vehicle.
(8) The owner of a motor vehicle shall forward his or her certificate of registration for
amendment, on notifying the Licensing Authority of any circumstance or event which
affects the accuracy of the entries in the register. A Licensing Officer may at any time call
upon any owner of a motor vehicle to furnish all information for the verification of the
entries in the register relating to the vehicle.
(9) Whenever the registered owner of a motor vehicle is about to absent himself or
herself from the State for at least three months leaving his or her registered vehicle in the
State, he or she shall notify the Licensing Authority in writing of the name and address of
the person, if any, in whose charge or possession the vehicle will be left.
(10) No person shall be registered as the owner of a motor vehicle unless he or she is
at least eighteen years of age.

### 7.

Examination of motor vehicles
(1) Before registering any motor vehicle or trailer the Licensing Officer shall verify
all the particulars entered on the application form and may, if he or she deems it
necessary, send any vehicle to be examined or inspected by an Inspector for the purpose
of ensuring that it is of the construction and in the condition necessary for the purpose for
which it is sought to register the vehicle; and the applicant shall pay the relevant
examination or inspection fee set out in Part III of the First Schedule.
(2) Before registering any goods vehicle, freight passenger vehicle, motor omnibus or
trailer, the Licensing Officer shall ascertain, in the manner prescribed, the maximum
gross weight or the maximum number of passengers which the vehicle may be authorised
to carry, and such maximum gross weight or maximum number of passengers shall be
entered in the register and in the certificate of registration.
(3) If any owner of a motor vehicle or trailer is aggrieved at any decision of an
Inspector under this section, he or she shall be permitted to appeal to the Licensing
Authority, who may uphold, vary or reverse the decision of the Inspector as the Licensing
Authority sees fit.

### 8.

Cancellation of registration
(1) The registration of a motor vehicle shall remain valid so long as the vehicle is kept
in use, and shall only be cancelled if the Licensing Authority is satisfied that the vehicle
has been destroyed or rendered permanently unserviceable or permanently removed from
the State, or if it has not been relicensed for two consecutive years.
(2) The owner of any motor vehicle which has been destroyed or rendered
permanently unserviceable or has been permanently removed from the State shall notify
the Licensing Authority of that fact within three months of the happening of the event. A
person who contravenes this subsection commits an offence.
(3) A prosecution for an offence under subsection (2) may be instituted at any time
within one year from the commission of the offence.

### 9.

Exemptions from need for registration
(1) The following classes of motor vehicles shall be exempted from the need for
registration—

(a)

new motor vehicles in the possession of manufacturers or dealers in motor
vehicles, subject to the provisions as to dealers’ general licences as appear
hereafter;

(b)

private motor cars and motor cycles brought into the State by visitors for
their own use for a period not exceeding three months, subject to conditions
as may be prescribed;

(c)

any motor vehicle which is the property of or used by the Governor-General
and Prime Minister for their official duties.

(2) No fees on registration shall be charged in respect of the following—
(a)

any motor vehicle which is the property of the Government, or any statutory
local government authority;

(b)

any motor vehicle specially exempted by an Order of the Minister of Finance
on account of its being owned by a public body or of its being used for any
charitable, philanthropic or other public purpose;

(c)

any motor vehicle the property of or used by a head of mission or other
diplomat for his or her official or personal duties.

(3) Notwithstanding subsections (1) and (2) the exemption from payment of the
registration fee in respect of a vehicle shall cease whenever the condition justifying the
exemption ceases to apply.

### 10.

Registration on change of use
Where the owner of a motor vehicle which is registered for use for a particular
purpose intends to use that vehicle for some other purpose he or she shall, before using
that vehicle for that other purpose, apply to the Licensing Authority for registration of the
vehicle for use for that other purpose; and the application shall be dealt with as if the
vehicle had not previously been registered but the fee payable for such a registration shall
be the fee for an amendment of a register set out in Part III of the First Schedule.

### 11.

Change of ownership
(1) On the change of ownership of a motor vehicle otherwise than by death—
(a)

the motor vehicle shall not be used for more than one month after the change
of ownership unless the new owner is registered as the owner thereof;

(b)

the registered owner and the new owner shall, within three weeks after the
change of ownership, make a written application signed by both of them to
the Licensing Authority giving the name and address of the new owner and
the date of change of ownership and the application shall be accompanied by
the certificate of registration and the relevant fee set out in Part III of the
First Schedule; however where the new owner satisfies the Licensing Officer
that the registered owner is out of the State an application by the new owner
accompanied by a note of change of ownership issued by the registered
owner and the said certificate and fee shall suffice. The Licensing Officer
shall thereupon by endorsement of the certificate of registration and entry in
the register substitute the name of the new owner for that of the registered
owner and shall date and initial the substitution and from such date the new
owner shall for all purposes be deemed to be the registered owner of the
motor vehicle described in the relevant entry in the register and in the
certificate of registration:

Provided that where the ownership in a vehicle is transferred under any legal or
judicial process the application aforesaid may be made by the new owner alone subject to
the other provisions of this section being complied with.

(2) In the case of death of the registered owner the legal personal representative of the
deceased in the State shall be deemed to be the owner for the purposes of a transfer, and
in the absence of any legal personal representative in the State the person into whose
possession the motor vehicle shall lawfully come shall, for the purposes of a transfer, be
deemed to be the registered owner:
Provided that, such personal representative or other person as aforesaid shall notify
the Licensing Authority of the death of the registered owner within one month of the
happening of the event.
(3) A Licensing Officer may issue a new certificate of registration instead of
endorsing an original certificate.

## PART III

Licensing of Vehicles

### 12.

Using vehicle without prescribed licence
(1) In addition to any penalty for an offence provided for in this Act—
(a)

the owner of any motor vehicle or trailer or bicycle keeps for use or allows
to be kept for use, or uses or allows to be used, on a road such vehicle or
trailer or bicycle without having obtained, or otherwise fails or omits to
obtain, the relevant licence prescribed in respect of that motor vehicle, trailer
or bicycle;

(b)

any motor vehicle, trailer or bicycle is used upon any road for a purpose
which is not authorised by the licence issued in respect thereof or in
contravention of any of the provisions of this Act; or

(c)

any goods vehicle, public service vehicle or trailer is used upon a road being
loaded so as to exceed the maximum gross weight or the number of
passengers authorised by the licence or, in the case of an unlicensed vehicle,
specified in the register of motor vehicles in respect of that vehicle,

the owner, driver or other person in charge of the vehicle each commits an offence and
shall each be liable on summary conviction to a fine of five thousand dollars or to
imprisonment for three months or to both such fine and imprisonment.
(2) Where the driver of any vehicle is charged with an offence under subsection (1),
the Magistrate may order that a summons be issued against any person alleged by the
driver to be the owner of the vehicle, thus making the alleged owner the co-defendant in
the case; and the Magistrate may, after hearing the evidence and witnesses of all parties,
make such order in regard to penalty and costs as to the Magistrate seems just.
(3) Every vehicle shall, in all proceedings under this Act, be deemed to be kept for
use, until the contrary is proved by the owner of such vehicle.
(4) No motor vehicle shall be used as a taxi which is not licensed as such under this
Act; and if this subsection is contravened, the owner and the driver of the motor vehicle
each commits an offence.
(5) Upon any conviction for an offence under subsection (1)(a) or (4), the Magistrate,
in addition to any other penalty which may be inflicted, shall order the owner of the
motor vehicle, trailer or bicycle to pay into court to the credit of the Accountant-General
the amount of the relevant licence duty which would have been payable if the licence had
been taken out at the proper time, and in default of payment of the amount, the owner
shall be liable to imprisonment for three months; but no such order shall be made if the
Magistrate is satisfied that the licence duty was paid subsequent to commission.

(6) Where a Magistrate has made an order under subsection (5) in respect of a goods
vehicle or trailer, the Magistrate shall, unless he or she is satisfied that the vehicle has
been inspected in accordance with any regulations made under this Act, further order the
owner of the vehicle to produce it within forty-eight hours to the Licensing Authority for
inspection; and the Licensing Authority shall, if satisfied that the amount of the licence
duty has been paid and that the vehicle complies with all provisions of this Act and the
regulations made hereunder, issue the appropriate licence.
(7) A person shall not be convicted for an offence under this section if he or she
proves that he or she has not had a reasonable opportunity of obtaining a licence for the
motor vehicle, trailer or bicycle and that the vehicle, or trailer was being driven for the
purpose of obtaining a licence, or that the contravention was without his or her
knowledge and that he or she had taken responsible steps to avoid such contravention.
(8) Any owner who contravenes an order made under subsection (6) shall be liable on
summary conviction to a fine of one thousand five hundred dollars or to imprisonment for
six months, or to both such fine and imprisonment.

### 13.

Licensing Authority may permit goods vehicle to be used for the conveyance of
persons
(1) Notwithstanding section 12 the Licensing Authority may, in his or her discretion
but so as to not constitute a risk breach, grant permission to enable a goods vehicle or
trailer to be used for the conveyance, either generally or on particular journeys, of such
number and classes of passengers, without hire or reward, and of such number of other
persons employed for the purpose of loading or unloading the goods, merchandise or
other loads carried on the goods vehicle or trailer as he or she may think fit, subject to
such conditions as he or she may impose.
(2) Whenever permission is granted under subsection (1), a licence issued by the
Licensing Authority in a form to be determined by him or her and containing the
conditions subject to which permission has been granted shall be issued to the licensee of
the goods vehicle or trailer. One such condition shall require that there shall be adequate
insurance coverage in respect of the passengers permitted to be carried.
(3) A licence issued under this section for the conveyance of passengers shall be valid
for a period not exceeding twelve months from the date of issue.
(4) The Licensing Authority may cancel or suspend any licence issued under this
section if in his or her opinion any of the conditions attached to the issue of the licence
has not been complied with by the licensee.

### 14.

Restrictions on licensing and using of certain motor vehicles and trailers,
including tinted vehicles
(1) Except as provided in this section, no motor vehicle exceeding the maximum
gross weight as prescribed or of a mass as to cause harm or damage to a road, road
infrastructure or a road-related area, nor shall a licence be issued in respect of any such
vehicle.
(2) All the tyres of every motor vehicle or trailer used or to be used upon any road
shall be fitted with pneumatic tyres and no motor vehicle not so fitted shall be used or
licensed to be used on any road.
(3) The Licensing Authority may, in consultation with the Chief Technical Officer,
grant a special licence to the owner of a tractor, goods vehicle or trailer for conveying an
indivisible load of heavy machinery or other articles in excess of the maximum gross
weight as prescribed by order, subject to such conditions as may be specified in the
licence, and on payment of the prescribed fee.
(3A) Notwithstanding anything contained in subsection (1)(a), the Licensing
Authority may, after consultation with the Chief Technical Officer, grant a special licence

to the owner of a vehicle which exceeds the maximum gross weight of ten tons if he is
satisfied that it is in the public interest to do so, subject to such conditions as may be
specified in the licence and the payment of the prescribed fee.
(3B) Where a special licence is issued under subsection (3A), the Licensing Authority
shall also issue an identification mark to the owner of the vehicle which identification
mark must conform to and be affixed in accordance with regulations 7 and 8 of the Motor
Vehicles and Road Traffic Regulations, respectively.
(3C) The Licensing Authority may revoke a special licence granted under
subsection (3A) if there is a breach of any of the conditions subject to which it was
granted.
(3D) Subsections (3A) to (3C) apply to vehicles which—
(a)

exceed the maximum gross weight of ten tons;

(b)

are unlicensed; and

(c)

were imported on of after 1st July, 1997.

(4) The Licensing Authority may, in consultation with the Chief Technical Officer,
grant for a period of one year a special licence to the owner of any machine used for
industrial purposes to enable such machine to be moved from one place to another,
subject to such conditions as may be specified in the licence, and on payment of the
prescribed fee.
(5) Notwithstanding the issue of any licence under subsections (3) and (4) no fee shall
be imposed by the Licensing Authority in respect of—
(a)

road rollers; or

(b)

motor vehicles using roads which are not maintained at the public expense
but in any such case the person or authority at whose expense such roads are
maintained may charge a fee as he or she thinks fit.

(6) Any person to whom a licence has been granted under this section who fails to
observe any of the conditions specified in the licence commits an offence and shall be
liable on summary conviction to a fine of five hundred dollars.
(7) It shall be a summary offence to use a motor vehicle upon a road in violation of
the provisions of this section.
(8) In a prosecution under subsection (1)(b) evidence deriving from the use of light
metres and such other scientific devices as may be prescribed by the Minister may be
admitted by the court if the court is satisfied that such device was in proper working
condition at the relevant time.

### 14A.

Restriction on licensing and use of heavily tinted vehicles
(1) No motor vehicle may be used on a road, nor shall a licence be issued in respect of
it, if the windscreen is tinted, treated or darkened across more than twenty per cent of its
surface from the top, or if any other window is tinted, treated or darkened so as to restrict
the amount of light entering the vehicle by more than sixty-five per cent unless permitted
in writing by the Licensing Authority.
(2) For the purpose of subsection (1), “treated” includes coated wholly or partially
with reflective material and “darkened” includes having curtains or blinds drawn on the
inside of the window.
(3) In a prosecution under this section evidence from the use of a scientific device as
prescribed by the Minister may be admitted by the Court if the Court is satisfied that the
device was in proper working order at the relevant time.
(4) This section comes into operation twelve months after the coming into operation
of the Road Traffic (Amendment) Act, 2001.

### 15.

Applications for licence
Applications for the licensing of a motor vehicle, trailer or bicycle or for subsequent
re-renewals of a licence therefor shall be made in the prescribed form.

### 16.

Conditions to be satisfied before licence is issued
(1) A Licensing Officer shall not issue a motor vehicle or trailer licence until the
motor vehicle or trailer has been registered, or renew a motor vehicle or trailer licence
unless he or she is satisfied that the motor vehicle or trailer does not differ in any respect
from the particulars in the register and also that the Motor Vehicles Insurance (Third
Party Risks) Act, Chapter 202, and this Act, and the regulations respectively made
thereunder, have been complied with and that any fine imposed on the owner of the motor
vehicle or trailer for an offence under this Act or the regulations has been paid (unless the
conviction is the subject of an appeal or review).
(2) The decision of the Licensing Authority regarding the class or type to which any
vehicle conforms or under which it should be registered and regarding the licence that
may be issued in respect of any such vehicle shall be conclusive.

### 17.

Alteration in conditions on which licence is issued
(1) Where the registered owner of a motor vehicle or trailer applies under section 10
for the registration of that motor vehicle or trailer for a purpose other than that for which
it is registered, he or she shall, at the time of making the application, apply for the vehicle
or trailer to be licensed for that other purpose; and when, after the issue of a licence, a
vehicle or trailer is registered for a use other than that for which it is registered or is so
altered that a higher fee or a fee of a different class is required, the licence shall become
void, and the owner of the vehicle shall apply for a new licence appropriate to the new
registration.
(2) A new licence shall not be issued under subsection (1) until the old licence has
been surrendered and there shall be deducted from the fee on the new licence a sum equal
to the fee which would have been payable on the old licence if issued on the same day as
the new licence, provided that the minimum fee on the new licence shall be ten dollars.
(3) Before issuing a new licence under this section the Licensing Officer shall satisfy
himself or herself that the Motor Vehicles Insurance (Third Party Risks) Act,

## Chapter 202, has been complied with in respect of such new purposes.

(4) Where a new licence is issued under this section, the Licensing Officer shall
amend the register and issue a new certificate of registration.

### 18.

Cancellation or refusal of licence
(1) A Licensing Officer may cancel or suspend or refuse to issue or renew a motor
vehicle, trailer or bicycle licence if an Inspector certifies in writing that the motor vehicle,
trailer or bicycle is so constructed or is in such a condition as to cause harm or damage to
a road, road infrastructure or a road-related area and as to be a source of danger to any
person travelling in or on the vehicle, trailer or bicycle or to other users of the road, road
infrastructure or a road-related area, or to be injurious to the roads themselves, road, road
infrastructure or a road-related area or that the vehicle, trailer or bicycle fails to satisfy the
requirements of this Act or regulations made hereunder or if any fine imposed on the
owner of the motor vehicle, trailer or bicycle for an offence under this Act or the
regulations has remained unpaid for twenty-one days since the date it was imposed
(unless the conviction is the subject of an appeal or review).
(2) Any owner of a motor vehicle, trailer or bicycle in respect of which the licence is
cancelled or suspended or for which the issue or renewal of a licence is refused may
appeal against the decision of the Licensing Officer to the Licensing Authority and the

Licensing Authority may, if he or she thinks fit, direct the Licensing Officer in writing to
withhold, grant or issue the licence applied for.

### 19.

Transfer of ownership
On the change or transfer of the ownership of a motor vehicle, trailer or bicycle, the
Licensing Officer shall, on payment of the prescribed fee, duly amend the register, the
certificate of registration, and the licence, and it shall not be necessary to issue a new
licence.

### 20.

Duplicate licences
(1) If a motor vehicle, trailer or bicycle licence is lost, defaced, mutilated, or rendered
illegible, the Licensing Officer may issue a duplicate licence on payment of the
prescribed fee.
(2) If any lost licence is subsequently found, the holder of the duplicate licence shall
forthwith deliver up to the Licensing Officer the duplicate licence, and any person who
knowingly retains or has in his or her possession both an original and a duplicate licence
commits an offence.

### 21.

Licence fees
(1) Every motor vehicle, trailer and bicycle licence issued shall be in the prescribed
form, and the fees payable annually for such licences shall be as set out in Part I of the
First Schedule:
Provided that in respect of goods vehicles and vehicles issued special licences the
fees payable shall be prescribed on the basis of maximum gross weight.
(2) No licence fee shall be payable in respect of any vehicle which is exempt from
registration or from payment of the registration fee under section 9 once the conditions
justifying the exemption continue.
(3) The Minister of Finance may exempt from payment of licence fees or duties any
agricultural tractor used solely for the purpose of agriculture, and any other vehicle
which, in the opinion of the said Minister, is so constructed as to show that its primary
purpose is for use in agriculture or industry, but any vehicle so exempted shall be used
subject to the conditions specified in the instrument of exemption.
(4) (a) Subject to paragraph (b) of this subsection, licences shall be issued yearly for
motor vehicles, trailers and bicycles. The date of payment for licence fees for motor
vehicles and trailers shall be determined by the number of digits in the registration
numbers of the registration mark of the motor vehicle or trailer as set out in the First
Column of the Second Schedule and paid within the time specified in the Second Column
of that Schedule whether in Part I or II of that Schedule as the case may be. The licence
shall continue in force from the date of issue until the day preceding the date specified in
the Second Column of the Second Schedule as being the date for the next annual payment
of the fee regarding that licence:
Provided that after the licence fee for a motor vehicle or trailer has been paid at the
date of the first registration, the licence fee for that motor vehicle or trailer shall as from
the immediately following calendar year be payable, as regards the time of payment, in
accordance with the number of digits in the registration numbers of the registration mark
of the motor vehicle or trailer as set out in the Second Schedule notwithstanding that in
the said immediately following calendar year the day preceding the anniversary date of
the first registration has not yet arrived; but where the licence fee is thus required to be
paid in the said immediately following calendar year at least two months before the day
preceding the anniversary date of the first registration, the licence fee payable in the said
immediately following calendar year shall be fifty per cent of the annual licence fee
payable in respect of that motor vehicle or trailer.

(b) When a motor vehicle or trailer is registered for the first time the licence fee
therefor shall be paid on the date of registration:
Provided that notwithstanding that on account of paragraph (i) of this subsection the
annual licence fee for a motor vehicle or trailer newly registered might next have been
payable within the same calendar year as the date of first registration, the licence issued
on the date of first registration shall be designed to endure and shall endure until the
immediately following calendar year and no additional licence fee shall be payable during
or in respect of the calendar year in which the vehicle or trailer was first registered.
(c) Any person who fails to pay his or her annual motor vehicle or trailer licence
fee within the prescribed time shall pay an additional late fee of fifty dollars.
(d) Where the owner of any motor vehicle which has been duly licensed under this
Act desires to take out in respect of such motor vehicle a licence for which a different fee
is payable, he or she shall, if such fee is higher than the fee already paid in respect of such
motor vehicle, be required to pay only the difference between such higher fee and the fee
already paid.

### 22.

Dealer’s general licence
(1) A Licensing Officer may issue to a dealer in, or manufacturer or repairer of, motor
vehicles, trailers or bicycles, who applies in the prescribed form, a dealer’s general
licence which, subject to sections 23 to 28 and to any other conditions which may from
time to time be prescribed may authorise the use of any number of motor vehicles, trailers
or bicycles the property of the dealer or manufacturer, or in the possession of the repairer.
(2) The fee payable for a dealer’s general licence shall be payable annually and shall
be such as may be prescribed by the Minister.

### 23.

Uses of dealer’s general licence
(1) A dealer’s general licence in the prescribed form may authorise the use of any
motor vehicle or trailer, the property of the dealer, which is being tested by the dealer or
his or her employee, or is being tested or used by the dealer, his or her employee or a
prospective purchaser for the purpose of effecting a sale. It shall not authorise the carriage
of passengers or goods for fee or reward, or the carriage of any passenger not engaged in
testing the vehicle or inspecting the vehicle with a view to purchase, or the use of the
vehicle for any purpose other than those specified in this section.
(2) Notwithstanding subsection (1) a dealer’s general licence shall not authorise the
use of a motor vehicle or trailer on a Sunday or Bank Holiday or between 6.00 p.m. and
8.00 a.m.

### 24.

Register of dealer’s general licence
Motor vehicles and trailers used under the authority of a dealer’s general licence shall
not be registered in the manner described in section 6 but the Licensing Officer shall keep
a separate register in the prescribed form of all such licences issued by him or her.

### 25.

Cancellation of dealer’s general licence
(1) A Licensing Officer may at any time cancel a dealer’s general licence for a breach
of any of the provisions of this Act or of any regulations made hereunder.
(2) The Licensing Officer shall give notice of the cancellation to the person to whom
the licence was issued.
(3) Any person whose licence has been cancelled under this section or to whom a
dealer’s general licence has been refused may appeal to the Licensing Authority, and the

Licensing Authority may order that the cancellation shall be confirmed or that the licence
be restored or issued.

### 26.

Dealer’s general licence not transferable
No dealer’s general licence shall be used for any purpose not provided for in this Act
nor shall it be transferred or assigned to any other person, without the authority of the
Licensing Officer.

### 27.

Dealer to accompany vehicle when used
No motor vehicle or trailer shall be used on any road, road infrastructure or a roadrelated area under the authority of a dealer’s general licence unless the holder of the
licence, or a person authorised by him or her in writing, accompanies such vehicle.

### 28.

Record of vehicles used under authority of dealer’s general licence
(1) The holder of a dealer’s general licence shall keep, in the prescribed form, a
record of every motor vehicle or trailer which is used under the authority of the licence,
and shall produce the record for inspection when requested by any member of the Police
Force.
(2) The record referred to in subsection (1) shall contain details of the use of the
motor vehicle or trailer, the names of the users and the purposes for which the motor
vehicle or trailer was used, together with any other information as may be prescribed.

### 29.

International certificates and temporary licences for motor vehicles
(1) The owner of any motor vehicle in respect of which an international certificate for
a motor vehicle has been issued elsewhere than in the State in accordance with any
International Convention relating to the international circulation of motor vehicles which
applies to the State may, whilst the certificate remains valid, and subject to any prescribed
conditions, be exempted from any requirement to register the motor vehicle, and also may
be exempted for ninety days from any requirement to hold a licence in respect of the
motor vehicle.
(2) Where a motor vehicle is brought into the State for the purpose of being used by
the owner during a visit, and the owner of the motor vehicle does not hold an
international certificate the Licensing Authority may issue a temporary licence to use the
motor vehicle without registration or payment of any licence fee for not more than three
months and subject to any prescribed conditions.
(3) No motor vehicle in respect of which an international certificate or temporary
licence has been issued shall be used in the State for purposes of hire or trade unless the
owner has complied with the Motor Vehicles Insurance (Third Party Risks) Act,

## Chapter 202.

(4) Where a person in the State desires to use a motor vehicle in any other country
which adheres to any International Convention as aforesaid, he or she may apply to the
Licensing Authority in the prescribed manner for an international certificate in respect of
the motor vehicle.
(5) The Licensing Authority shall have power, subject to any prescribed conditions, to
issue international certificates for motor vehicles in accordance with any International
Convention Before such a certificate is issued the applicant shall satisfy the Licensing
Authority that the motor vehicle complies with the requirements of the Convention, and
for such purpose the Licensing Authority may require an Inspector to examine the motor
vehicle and report thereon.
(6) The Licensing Authority shall keep a record of all international certificates for
motor vehicles and temporary licences issued by him or her, and of all motor vehicles

being used in the State under the authority of international certificates issued in other
countries.

### 30.

Power to tow away vehicle
(1) If—
(a)

a motor vehicle is being used on a road when there is not in force in relation
to the use of the vehicle a policy of insurance, or security in respect of third
party risks, which complies with the requirements of the Motor Vehicles
Insurance (Third Party Risks) Act, Chapter 202;

(b)

a motor vehicles is being used on a road, road infrastructure or a road related
area so as to constitute—
(i) a substantial risk breach or a severe risk breach, or
(ii) a danger to other users of the road, road infrastructure or a road related
area; and

(c)

a motor vehicle is parked or left on a road or at a place in such a manner as
to cause danger or obstruction to other traffic on the road contrary to this
Act,

a police officer or a person authorised by a police officer may drive away or tow away the
vehicle, or require the driver of it to drive it, to premises under the control of the Police
Force.
(2) The owner or person in charge of a motor vehicle which is removed under
subsection (1) may recover the vehicle if—
(a)

in the case of a vehicle removed pursuant to paragraph (b) − the insurance
premium is paid or security deposited and a police document, cover note or
other evidence is produced to the officer in charge of the premises to which
the vehicle was removed;

(b)

if the vehicle was towed to the premises by or on the authority of a police
officer – a towing fee of one hundred and twenty-five dollars is paid to the
officer in charge of the premises to which the vehicle was removed;

(c)

in every case – a parking fee of twenty-five dollars for each day or part
thereof during which the vehicle is kept at the premises to which the vehicle
was removed is paid to the officer in charge of the premises.

(3) Neither a police officer, a person assisting a police officer nor the State is liable
for any motor vehicle towed or driven under this section or for any damage sustained by a
vehicle during or as a result of the towing, driving or parking of the vehicle unless such
damage is caused intentionally or negligently.
(4) No person, except a police officer or a person authorised by a police officer, may
remove, attempt to remove or in any way interfere with a motor vehicle taken to premises
under the control of the Police Force pursuant to this section.
(5) If a motor vehicle towed or driven under this section remains under the control of
the Police Force for more than three months, application may be made by the
Commissioner of Police by summons to the High Court, to be heard inter partes, for the
forfeiture of the vehicle.
(6) A person who contravenes subsection (4) commits an offence and is liable on
summary conviction to a fine of five thousand dollars and to imprisonment for one
month.
(7) For the purpose of this section—
“motor vehicle” includes a trailer;

“premises under the control of the Police Force” means a police station and any
other premises designated by the Commissioner of Police by notice published in the
Gazette.

## PART IV

Driving Licences

### 31.

Driving without licence
(1) Subject to section 35, no person shall drive a motor vehicle of any class or
description on a road unless he or she is the holder of a driving licence authorising him or
her to drive a motor vehicle of that class or description.
(2) No person shall employ or permit any person to drive a motor vehicle of any class
or description on a road unless the person he or she employs or permits to drive the motor
vehicle is the holder of a valid driving licence authorising him or her to drive a motor
vehicle of that class or description.
(3) Any person who contravenes this section commits an offence and shall be liable
on summary conviction—
(a)

if the contravention is in relation to a motor vehicle other than a public
service vehicle or a goods vehicle, to a fine of one thousand dollars and to
imprisonment for six months;

(b)

if the contravention is in relation to a public service vehicle or a goods
vehicle, to a fine of four thousand dollars and imprisonment for eighteen
months.

(4) Where a person is convicted under subsection (3) the Magistrate may, if the
vehicle used in connection with the offence is a public service vehicle, in addition to the
penalty imposed thereunder cancel the licence of that vehicle to operate as a public
service vehicle.
(5) The cancellation of a licence under subsection (3) and under section 62B(5) by the
Magistrate who imposes the penalty shall be for a period not exceeding two years.

### 32.

Minimum age for driving
(1) No person under the age of seventeen years shall be qualified to obtain a driving
licence to drive a motor cycle and no person under the age of eighteen years shall be
qualified to obtain a driving licence to drive any other class of motor vehicle.
(2) No person shall drive on a road, a motor vehicle of any class or description if he
or she is under the age prescribed by subsection (1) for obtaining a driving licence to
drive a motor vehicle of that class or description.

### 33.

Restrictions on driving licences for omnibuses
No driving licence shall be issued or endorsed as valid for a public service vehicle or
for a goods vehicle to an applicant who is under the age of twenty-five years and unless
he or she passes such special test as may be prescribed by the Licensing Authority.

### 34.

Driving when under prescribed age
(1) A person who drives a motor vehicle on a road while he or she is under the
minimum age prescribed for the driving of a motor vehicle of the class he or she is
driving commits an offence and shall be liable on summary conviction, in the case of a
first offence, to a fine of two hundred dollars or to imprisonment for three months, or to

both such fine and imprisonment and, in the case of a second or subsequent conviction, to
a fine of one thousand dollars or to imprisonment for twelve months, or to both such fine
and imprisonment.
(2) A person who causes or permits another person to drive a motor vehicle on a road
while the latter person is under the minimum age prescribed for the driving of a motor
vehicle of the class he or she is driving commits an offence and shall be liable on
summary conviction to a fine of one thousand dollars or to imprisonment for twelve
months, or to both such fine and imprisonment.

### 35.

Learner’s permit
(1) A person who is not the holder of a driving licence may, for the purpose of
learning to drive, drive a motor vehicle of a particular class on a road, if he or she holds a
learner’s permit.
(2) A learner’s permit is obtainable, subject to this Act, by applying to the Licensing
Authority and on payment of the relevant fee set out in Part II of the First Schedule.
(3) Where the Licensing Authority grants a learner’s permit to any person he or she
may set out in the permit such conditions and restrictions as he or she thinks necessary for
the safety of the public.
(4) Without prejudice to the generality of subsection (3), the Licensing Authority
may, in a learner’s permit, set out the times during which the holder of the permit may not
drive on roads specified in the permit.
(5) The holder of a learner’s permit shall not drive a motor vehicle of any class or
description unless—
(a)

he or she is under the supervision of a driver who—
(i) holds a driving licence for a vehicle of that class or description and
who has been the holder of such a licence for a period of not less than
three years, and
(ii) is present with him or her in the vehicle while he or she is driving it;
and

(b)

there is clearly displayed in a conspicuous position on the front and on the
rear of the vehicle, as directed by the Licensing Authority, the letter “L” in
red on a white background.

(6) Notwithstanding anything contained in this section, the holder of a learner’s
permit shall not drive and shall not be permitted to drive a vehicle while it is being used
to draw a trailer or convey passengers.
(7) Subsection (5)(a) does not apply to the holder of a learner’s permit who—
(a)

is undergoing a driving test; or

(b)

is driving a vehicle constructed or adapted to carry only one person.

(8) A person who is learning to drive and contravenes any of the conditions set out in
a learner’s permit commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine of
one thousand dollars or to imprisonment for twelve months, or to both such fine and
imprisonment and in addition the court may cancel the learner’s permit.
(9) A learner’s permit is valid for the six months and is, upon payment of the
prescribed fee, renewable for a further period of six months.

### 36.

Liability of instructor and person learning to drive to be co-extensive

Where a person who is learning to drive is charged with an offence under this Act, the
driver who is present with him or her in the vehicle by reason of section 35(5)(a) is also
chargeable with the same offence and both are equally punishable.

### 37.

Driving tests
(1) The Licensing Authority may require an applicant for a driving licence to produce
a certificate, signed by a person approved by the Licensing Authority, that the applicant
has passed a driving test showing that he or she is competent to drive.
(2) Driving tests may be conducted by examiners authorised by the Licensing
Authority.
(3) Subject to this Act, the nature of a driving test shall be at the discretion of the
Licensing Authority or the examiner appointed by him or her.
(4) For the purpose of this section, motor vehicles are divided into the classes
specified in section 40(2).
(5) The test that a person is required to pass before a driving licence is granted to him
or her authorising him or her to drive a motor vehicle of a particular class or description,
is a test carried out on a vehicle of that class or description, and the person conducting the
test shall satisfy himself or herself that the person taking the test—
(a)

is conversant with the rules of the road, road signs and road signals;

(b)

is competent to drive without danger to, and with due consideration for,
other users of the road; and

(c)

is able to comply with any other requirements specified for the purpose in
the regulations.

(6) Where an applicant requires a driving licence to drive a vehicle of a particular
class or description, the examiner shall, after satisfying himself or herself by means of
such tests as he or she considers suitable that the applicant is qualified to drive a vehicle
of that particular class or description, issue to the applicant a certificate of competence on
which there is clearly set out all the restrictions subject to which that certificate is issued.
(7) The Licensing Authority shall clearly set out in a driving licence the restrictions
referred to in subsection (6).
(8) For the avoidance of doubt, it is hereby provided that a person shall not be entitled
to obtain or be issued a driving licence to drive a vehicle of a particular class or
description only because he or she is or has been the holder of a licence to drive a vehicle
of another class or description.

### 38.

Applicant for driving licence or learner’s permit required to be medically fit
(1) Subject to subsection (3), the Licensing Authority shall not issue or renew a
driving licence or a learner’s permit to any applicant unless he or she is satisfied that the
applicant is medically fit to be in charge of a motor vehicle within the context of
subsection (4).
(2) Subject to the discretion of a court at the time of sentencing, a person who has
been convicted of an offence involving the use of a motor vehicle in the conveyance of
stolen goods shall not hold a driving licence or learner’s permit within two years after the
date of conviction.
(3) Notwithstanding subsection (1), the Licensing Authority may issue or renew a
driving licence or a learner’s permit to any person to drive a particular motor vehicle on
being satisfied that that motor vehicle is so equipped and that the motor vehicle can be
efficiently controlled by that person.

(4) An applicant for a driving licence or a learner’s permit may be required by the
Licensing Authority to produce a medical certificate signed by a medical practitioner,
stating that the applicant’s vision, hearing and other physical and mental fitness are
adequate for the applicant to hold the licence or permit for which he or she applies. A
person who fails to produce such a medical certificate when he or she is required to do so
shall be entitled to apply again for the licence or permit after six months of his or her
previous application but shall on such later application be still subject to producing the
required medical certificate.
(5) Where the Licensing Authority refuses to issue or renew to an applicant a driving
licence or a learner’s permit under this section, and persists in refusing to do so even after
the applicant produces the required medical certificate, the applicant may appeal against
the decision of the Licensing Authority to a Magistrate.

### 39.

Application for driving licences
(1) An application for a driving licence or a learner’s permit shall be made to the
Licensing Authority in the prescribed form.
(2) Any person who, in or in connection with an application for a driving licence or a
learner’s permit, makes a statement that to his or her knowledge is false commits an
offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine of one thousand dollars or to
imprisonment for twelve months or to both such fine and imprisonment.

Driving test fees
(1) Every person who presents himself or herself for a driving test shall pay, in

Form of driving licence
(1) A driving licence—
(a)

shall be in the form prescribed by regulations; and

(b)

is not valid unless signed by the holder thereof.

(2) Every applicant for a driving licence shall, at the time of his or her making
application, supply three recent photographs of himself or herself one of which shall be
affixed to the driving licence.

### 43.

Driving licence fees
There shall be payable, on the issue or renewal of a driving licence, the fee set out in

## Part II of the First Schedule.

### 44.

Production of driving licences
(1) The driver of a motor vehicle on a road shall, on being required by a member of
the Police Force, produce his or her driving licence for examination.
(2) Any person who fails to comply with subsection (1) commits an offence and shall
be liable on summary conviction to a fine of five hundred dollars or to imprisonment for
six months or to both such fine and imprisonment.
(3) Notwithstanding subsection (2), a person may not be convicted under this section
if, within forty-eight hours of being required to produce his or her driving licence, he or
she produces his or her driving licence at the police station nearest to his or her address or
at the police station specified by the police officer at the time its production was required.

### 45.

Register of driving licences
(1) The Licensing Authority shall keep a record of all driving licences issued and the
record shall contain—
(a)

the name and address of the holder;

(b)

the date of issue of the driving licence;

(c)

the class of vehicle or classes of vehicles to be driven by the holder; and

(d)

a record of all convictions of the holder in respect of traffic offences.

(2) The contents of the record referred to in subsection (1) shall in all proceedings be
prima facie evidence of all the facts contained therein.

### 46.

Duration of driving licence
(1) Subject to this section, an existing licence, unless previously suspended, cancelled
or revoked by a competent authority—

(a)

expires on the anniversary date thereof;

(b)

when renewed, expires on the first or third birthday, as the case may be, of
the applicant after such renewal.

(2) On the first issue or renewal of a driving licence after the commencement of the
Road Traffic (Amendment) Act, 2002, the fee payable for a driving licence will be
calculated pro rata on the number of months to the next anniversary of the date of birth of
the applicant.

### 47.

Duplicate driving licences
(1) Where a driving licence is lost, defaced, mutilated or rendered illegible, the
Licensing Authority, if satisfied of those matters, may issue a replacement licence on
payment of the relevant fee set out in Part III of the First Schedule without requiring the
applicant to undergo a further driving test.
(2) The Licensing Authority shall record on a replacement driving licence issued
pursuant to subsection (1) any endorsements made in the register of driving licences in
respect of the driving licence that has been lost, defaced, mutilated or rendered illegible.
(3) If any lost driving licence is subsequently found, the holder of the new driving
licence shall forthwith deliver up to the Licensing Authority such new licence and any
person who knowingly retains or has in his or her possession both an original and a new
licence commits an offence.

### 48.

Restriction on the issue of new driving licence after expiration of former licence
(1) On or after the expiration of a driving licence by effluxion of time and on
application by the holder thereof for the issue of a new driving licence the Licensing
Officer shall, subject to section 38, issue a new driving licence to the applicant without
subscribing thereon any endorsements other than those made within the three years
preceding the application.
(2) The Licensing Authority may suspend or refuse to renew a driving licence or
learner’s permit where the holder thereof or an applicant therefor has, within the last three
years prior to such suspension or refusal, been convicted of—
(a)

manslaughter arising out of the use of any motor vehicle;

(b)

any offence under section 55, 56 or 58; or

(c)

repeated offences under any one or more of the sections of this Act other
than the sections referred to in paragraph (b) or repeated offences under any
one or more of the regulations made under this Act,

and upon the recommendation of the court before which the holder or applicant was
convicted and upon certification from the court of such conviction.

### 49.

Cancelling or suspending driving licence or learner’s licence for medical
unfitness
(1) The Licensing Authority may suspend or cancel a driving licence or a learner’s
permit if the holder thereof fails, within one month of being required to do so, to comply
with a requirement from the Licensing Authority that the said holder produce to the
Licensing Authority a medical certificate, signed by a medical practitioner, stating that
the applicant’s vision, hearing and other physical and mental fitness are adequate for the
said holder to continue holding the licence.
(2) A person who fails to timeously produce a medical certificate when he or she is
required to do so under subsection (1) may produce same within six months of being
required to do so. If he or she so within this six months produces the medical certificate to

the Licensing Authority, he or she shall thereupon be entitled to have his or her licence
restored at once by the Licensing Authority.
(3) Where the Licensing Authority suspends or cancels a licence under subsection (1),
or fails or refuses to restore a licence under subsection (2), the holder or previous holder
thereof may appeal against the decision of the Licensing Authority to a Magistrate.

### 50.

Demerit points
(1) Where a person has been convicted under this Act or the regulations for an
offence listed in the first column of the Third Schedule, being an offence created by or
under the enactment specified in the second column of that Schedule the Licensing 4
Authority shall record in respect of that person the number of demerit points set out
opposite that offence in the third column of that Schedule.
(2) Where a person appeals against a conviction for an offence referred to in
subsection (1)—
(a)

no demerit points shall be recorded in respect of that conviction unless the
conviction is confirmed on appeal; and

(b)

if demerit points have been recorded in respect of the conviction before an
appeal is entered, the points recorded in consequence thereof shall be
removed until the appeal is heard or otherwise disposed of.

(3) Where a person is convicted of two or more offences arising out of the same
circumstances the demerit points to be recorded shall be those—
(a)

relating to the offence in respect of which the greatest number of points are
imposed; or

(b)

for only any one of the convictions where equal points are imposed for the
various offences.

(4) No demerit point shall be recorded against a person where—
(a)

that person pays a fixed penalty in accordance with a notice given him or her
under Part VIII, or

(b)

a court disqualifies that person under provisions of this Act or regulations
made hereunder enabling the court to do so on convicting him or her of
specified offences.

### 51.

Expunging demerit points
(1) Where a period of one year has elapsed after the entry of demerit points on the
record of a person, the Licensing Authority shall cause those points to be expunged from
the record if no further demerit points have been recorded against him or her during that
period.
(2) Where, under section 53(2) to (5) or by an Order of a court, a driving licence has
been suspended for a period of three months or more and the period of suspension has
expired, the Licensing Authority shall cause all demerit points to be expunged from the
record of the holder of the driving licence.

### 52.

Notifying persons of demerit points
Where more than ten but less than fourteen demerit points have accumulated in the
record of any person, the Licensing Authority shall cause a notice to be sent to that person
informing him or her of the number of demerit points accumulated.

### 53.

Suspending driving licence for accumulating too many demerit points

(1) Where fourteen or more but less than eighteen demerit points have accumulated in
the record of a person, the Licensing Authority may require that person to appear before
him or her for an interview and to furnish any information, evidence or material requested
to show cause why his or her driving licence should not be suspended.
(2) Where a person fails to comply with subsection (1), the Licensing Authority may,
after serving on that person ten days notice of his or her failure to comply with that
subsection, suspend the driving licence of that person until the subsection has been
complied with.
(3) The Licensing Authority may suspend the driving licence of any person who has
appeared before him or her in compliance with subsection (1) for a period not exceeding
two months if he or she is of the opinion that that person has failed to show cause why his
or her driving licence should not be suspended.
(4) If, within a period of one year, eighteen or more demerit points have accumulated
in the record of any person, the Licensing Authority shall suspend the driving licence of
that person for a period of two months.
(5) Where, in a period exceeding one year, twenty-five or more demerit points have
accumulated in the record of a person, the Licensing Authority shall suspend the driving
licence of that person for a period of six weeks.
(6) Where a driving licence is suspended under this section the period of suspension
shall be concurrent with the unexpired portion of any other suspension.
(7) A person whose driving licence has been suspended by the Licensing Authority
under this section may, within fourteen days of the decision of the Licensing Authority,
appeal to the High Court against that decision and the decision of that Court shall be final.
(8) A person who drives a motor vehicle while his or her driving licence is suspended
under this section commits an offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine
of one thousand dollars or to imprisonment for twelve months or to both such fine and
imprisonment and, in addition, his or her driving licence shall be suspended for a further
period of six months.

### 54.

Visitors driving licences and international driving licences
(1) The Licensing Authority may grant a temporary driving licence for a period not
exceeding three months to any applicant for the same when it is shown to his or her
satisfaction that the applicant is a bona fide visitor to the State and is competent to drive.
A temporary driving licence may be granted with or without the taking of a driving test
on the part of the applicant or without his or her being required to supply photographs but
shall be subject to the conditions stipulated on the licence and to the payment of the
prescribed fee.
(2) The holder of an international driving licence issued elsewhere than in the State in
accordance with any International Convention with respect to the international circulation
of motor vehicles to which the State adheres shall, whilst the licence remains valid, be
exempted from any requirement to hold a driving licence under this Act; but he or she
shall only be entitled to drive in the State a motor vehicle of the class or classes for which
he or she holds an authorisation to drive in the country in which his or her international
driving licence was issued and he or she shall pay the prescribed fee.
(3) Where a person in the State desires to drive a motor vehicle in any other country
which adheres to any International Convention as aforesaid, he or she shall apply to the
Licensing Authority in the prescribed manner for an international driving licence. The
applicant must comply with requirements of the Convention and shall, in addition to any
other particulars as may be required by the Licensing Authority, satisfy the latter that he
or she holds a valid driving licence issued in the State and that he is ordinarily resident in
the State. The Licensing Authority has the power to issue international driving licences in
accordance with any International Convention as aforesaid on payment of the he or she

fee. Such licences shall, unless suspended, remain in effect for twelve months from the
date of issue.
(4) The holder of a driving licence issued in any of the other member states of the
Caribbean Community and Common Market (CARICOM) shall, while such licence
remains valid, be exempted from any requirement to hold a driving licence issued under
this Act to drive in the State:
Provided that such driver shall only be entitled to drive in the State a motor vehicle
of the class or classes for which he or she holds an authorisation to drive in the other
CARICOM member state in which his or her driving licence was issued and he or she
shall pay the prescribed fee:
Provided further that the exemption under this section only applies to a person who is
ordinarily resident in Grenada and who at the time when the person was issued a licence
in a CARICOM State had been resident in that State for a continuous period exceeding
six months.
(5) The Licensing Authority shall keep a register of all holders in the State who hold
valid international driving licences issued elsewhere and of all holders of international
driving licences and temporary licences issued by him or her.

## PART V

Driving and Other Offences and General Conditions Relating to the Use of Roads

### 55.

Causing death by reckless or dangerous driving
(1) Any person who causes the death of another person by the driving of a motor
vehicle on a road recklessly, or at a speed or in a manner dangerous to the public, having
regard to all the circumstances of the case including the nature, condition and use of the
road and the amount of traffic that was actually on the road at the time or might
reasonably have been expected to be on the road at the time, commits an offence and shall
be liable on conviction on indictment to imprisonment for a term of ten years and shall be
disqualified by the court from the date of the conviction for a period of not less than
seven years from holding or obtaining a driving licence.
(2) Where, upon the trial of a person for an offence under subsection (1), the jury is
not satisfied that he or she is guilty of that offence but is satisfied that he or she did drive
the motor vehicle on the road recklessly, or at a speed or in a manner dangerous to the
public, having regard to all the circumstances of the case, including the nature, condition
and use of the road and the amount of the traffic that was actually on the road at the time
or might reasonably have been expected to be on the road at the time, it may find him or
her guilty of such reckless or dangerous driving. The person convicted under this
subsection shall be liable to a fine of seven thousand dollars or to imprisonment for five
years or to both such fine and imprisonment, and shall be disqualified by the court from
the date of the conviction for a period of not less than five years from holding or
obtaining a driving licence.

### 56.

Reckless or dangerous driving
(1) Any person who drives a motor vehicle on a road recklessly, or at a speed or in a
manner dangerous to the public, having regard to all the circumstances of the case
including the nature, condition and use of the road and the amount of traffic that was
actually on the road at the time or might reasonably have been expected to be on the road
at the time, commits an offence and shall be liable on summary conviction—
(a)

in the case of a first conviction, to a fine of two thousand dollars or to
imprisonment for twelve months, or to both such fine and imprisonment;

(b)

in the case of a second or subsequent conviction, to a fine of three thousand
five hundred dollars or to imprisonment for two years, or to both such fine
and imprisonment.

(2) The court shall order particulars of any conviction under this section or section 62
to be endorsed on any driving licence held by the person convicted.
(3) When a person is convicted of an offence under this section—
(a)

on the first conviction he or she may be disqualified by the court from
holding or obtaining a driving licence for such period not exceeding six
months from the date of the conviction as the court may order;

(b)

on the second conviction he or she shall be disqualified by the court from
holding or obtaining a driving licence for such period being not less than six
months and not more than three years from the date of the conviction as the
court orders;

(c)

on the third or subsequent conviction he or she shall be disqualified by the
court from holding or obtaining a driving licence for such period being not
less than five years from the date of the conviction as the court orders.

(4) A member of the Police Force may arrest, without warrant, the driver of any
motor vehicle who, within the view of that member of the Police Force, commits an
offence under this section if—
(a)

the driver refuses to give his or her name and address when requested to do
so;

(b)

the member of the Police Force has reason to believe that the name or
address given is false; or

(c)

the motor vehicle does not bear a registration number plate.

### 57.

Careless driving: driving without due care and attention
(1) Any person who drives a motor vehicle on a road without due care and attention,
or without reasonable consideration for other persons using the road, commits an offence
and shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine of one thousand dollars or to
imprisonment for six months or to both such fine and imprisonment.
(2) On a second or subsequent conviction for an offence under subsection (1), a
person may be disqualified by the court from holding or obtaining a driving licence, for
such period as the court orders from the date of the conviction.
(3) For the avoidance of doubt it is hereby provided that a person drives a motor
vehicle without reasonable consideration for other persons using the road if, inter alia, he
or she—
(a)

so drives the vehicle on a road that he or she deliberately and maliciously
splashes dirt or water on pedestrians when he or she could reasonably have
avoided doing so; or

(b)

drives the vehicle on a road when that vehicle is so loaded that it spills its
load on the road so that the spillage constitutes a danger to other users of the
road.

### 58.

Speeding
(1) A person who drives a motor vehicle of a class or description specified in the
Fourth Schedule at a speed greater than the speed specified in the Fourth Schedule or in
an Order made under section 59(1) as being the maximum speed for a vehicle of that
class or description on the road on which it is being driven, commits an offence and shall

be liable on summary conviction to a fine of one thousand dollars or to imprisonment for
twelve months, or to both such fine and imprisonment.
(2) Subject to subsection (4), a person charged with an offence under this section may
not be liable to be convicted of the offence on the evidence of only one witness to the
effect that in the opinion of that witness the person charged was driving the vehicle at a
speed in contravention of subsection (1).
(3) Any person who aids, abets, counsels or procures another person, whether or not
this latter person is employed by the former person to drive a motor vehicle on a road, to
commit an offence under this section commits an offence and shall be liable on summary
conviction to a fine of one thousand dollars or to imprisonment for twelve months, or to
both such fine and imprisonment.
(4) In a prosecution under this section evidence deriving from the use of radars and
speed guns and such other scientific devices as may be prescribed by the Minister may be
admitted by the court if the court is satisfied that such device was in proper working
condition at the relevant time. Where a person is prosecuted under this section and
evidence is admitted by the court under this subsection a person may be convicted of the
offence on such evidence and the evidence of only one witness to the effect that in the
opinion of that witness the person charged was driving the vehicle at a speed in
contravention of subsection (1).

### 59.

Experimental temporary adjustments to maximum speed by Order
(1) The Minister may, by Order, make temporary adjustments of an experimental
nature in respect of the maximum speed for any class or description of vehicle on any
road.
(2) Before the Minister makes an Order under subsection (1), he or she shall—
(a)

by notice published in the Gazette on at least three occasions over a period
of one month, inform the public of his or her intention to make the Order;
and

(b)

specify the new maximum speed in the notice and the days on which and the
times during which it will be in operation.

(3) An Order made under this section is subject to negative resolution of Parliament
and remains in operation for such period as may be specified in the Order, or, if no period
is specified in the Order, until such time as the Order is revoked.

### 60.

Speed limit may be varied or revoked by regulations
The Minister may, by regulations published in the Gazette, vary, amend or revoke any
of the provisions of the Fourth Schedule, and in particular he or she may by such
regulations prohibit the driving of motor vehicles on any road or over any bridge at a
speed greater than that specified in such regulations.

### 61.

Special provisions regarding racing
(1) A person who promotes or takes part in a race or trial of speed between cycles on
a road commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine of seven hundred
and fifty dollars or to imprisonment for three months, or to both such fine and
imprisonment unless the race or trial is authorised and is conducted in accordance with
any conditions imposed by or under regulations under this section.
(2) The Minister may by regulations authorise, or provide for authorising, for the
purposes of subsection (1), the holding on a road of races or trials of speed, in cases as
may be prescribed and subject to conditions as may be imposed by or under the
regulations.

(3) Regulations under this section may—
(a)

prescribe the procedure to be followed, and the particulars to be given, in
connection with applications for authorisation under the regulations; and

(b)

make different provision for different classes or descriptions of race or trial.

(4) Without prejudice to any other powers exercisable in that behalf, the
Commissioner of Police may give directions with respect to the movement of, or the route
to be followed by, vehicular traffic, during any period as may be necessary or expedient
to prevent or mitigate congestion or obstruction of traffic or danger to or from traffic, in
consequence of the holding of a race or trial of speed authorised by or under regulations
under subsection (2), including a direction that any road or part of a road specified in the
direction shall be closed during any period to vehicles of a class so specified.

### 62.

Drunken driving: driving while under influence of alcoholic liquor or drugs
(1) Any person who drives, attempts to drive, or is in charge of, a motor vehicle on a
road while under the influence of alcoholic liquor or drugs to such an extent as to be unfit
to drive or to be incapable of having proper control of the vehicle commits an offence and
shall be liable on summary conviction—
(a)

to a fine of five thousand dollars or to imprisonment for twelve months, or to
both such fine and imprisonment in the case of a first conviction; and

(b)

to a fine of ten thousand dollars or to imprisonment for two years or to both
such fine and imprisonment in the case of a second or subsequent conviction.

(2) A person convicted of an offence under this section shall, unless the court for any
special reason thinks fit to order otherwise, be disqualified by the court from the date of
the conviction for a period of not less than one year from holding or obtaining a driving
licence:
Provided that on a second conviction under this section, a person shall be
disqualified by the court from the date of the conviction for a period of not less than five
years from holding or obtaining a driving licence.
(3) For the purposes of this section a person is unfit to drive if his or her ability to
drive properly is for the time being impaired.
(4) Any member of the Police Force may arrest without warrant any person whom he
or she suspects on reasonable grounds of committing an offence under this section.

### 62A.

Drinking alcohol while driving
Any person who drinks, or attempts to drink, any alcoholic beverage while driving a
motor vehicle on a road commits an offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to
a fine of five hundred dollars.

### 62B.

Prohibition from driving public service vehicles
(1) No person shall, after the commencement of this Act, drive a public service
vehicle if he has been convicted of an offence involving carnal knowledge under Part V
of the Criminal Code, Chapter 72A.
(2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an offence and shall be liable
on summary conviction to a fine of five thousand dollars or imprisonment for six months,
or both.
(3) No owner of a vehicle licensed to operate as a public service vehicle shall
knowingly cause or permit a person convicted under subsection (1) to drive that vehicle.
(4) The owner of a public service vehicle who knowingly causes or permits a person
who has been convicted under subsection (1) to drive his vehicle commits an offence and

shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine of five thousand dollars or imprisonment
for six months, or both.
(5) Where a person is convicted under subsection (3), the Magistrate may, in addition
to the penalty imposed thereunder cancel the licence of the vehicle, used in connection
with the offence under subsection (3), to operate as a public service vehicle.

### 63.

Using vehicle without consent of the owner
(1) A person who uses or takes and drives away or attempts to use or take and drive
away a motor vehicle without the consent of its owner or other lawful authority or,
knowing a motor vehicle to have been so taken, uses it or drives it or allows himself or
herself to be carried in or on it without the consent of its owner or other lawful authority,
commits an offence and shall be liable—
(a)

on conviction on indictment, to a fine of two thousand five hundred dollars
or to imprisonment for a term of two years, or to both such fine and
imprisonment; or

(b)

on summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand dollars or to imprisonment
for nine months, or to both such fine and imprisonment.

(2) It is a defence to a charge brought under subsection (1) for the accused to establish
that at the time he or she committed the act in respect of which he or she is charged he or
she reasonably believed—
(a)

he or she had lawful authority to use or drive away the motor vehicle;

(b)

the owner would, in the circumstances of the case, have given his or her
consent if his or her consent had been requested; or

(c)

the vehicle was being driven with the consent of its owner or other lawful
authority.

(3) Where, on a trial on indictment for stealing a motor vehicle, the jury is of the
opinion that the accused is not guilty of stealing the motor vehicle but is guilty of an
offence under this section, it may find him or her guilty of an offence under this section
and thereupon he or she shall be liable to be punished accordingly.
(4) A member of the Police Force may arrest without a warrant any person reasonably
suspected by him or her of having committed, or of attempting to commit, an offence
under this section.
(5) Where a person is convicted under this section the court may, in addition to any
punishment it imposes, or without imposing any punishment, order the person convicted
to pay to the owner of the motor vehicle—
(a)

an amount as in the opinion of the court would have been charged if the
motor vehicle had been hired by the person convicted;

(b)

an amount as in the opinion of the court represents fair compensation for any
damage done to the motor vehicle; or

(c)

both the amounts referred to in paragraphs (a) and (b) of this subsection.

An award under this subsection shall not be a bar to a civil suit, but shall be considered in
ordering damages in a civil suit.

### 64.

Interfering with vehicle
Any person who, without the permission of the owner, gets on or into a motor vehicle
or attempts to manipulate any of the levers, the starter, brakes, controls or machinery of a
motor vehicle or in any way interferes with or damages a motor vehicle while that vehicle
is on a road or parking place, commits an offence and shall be liable on summary
conviction to a fine of one thousand dollars or to imprisonment for six months, or to both

such fine and imprisonment but a person shall not be convicted under this section where
that motor vehicle is obstructing his or her own vehicle or that of any other person, and he
or she does the act complained of for the purpose of clearing the obstruction.

### 65.

Endangering vehicle, etc.
Any person who maliciously throws any object at a motor vehicle or trailer, or at any
person in or on a motor vehicle or trailer, or places any objects in any road whereby
damage to any motor vehicle or trailer may be occasioned commits an offence and shall
be liable on summary conviction to a fine of one thousand dollars or to imprisonment for
six months, or to both such fine and imprisonment.

### 66.

Using vehicle in defective condition
Any person who uses or drives or permits to be used or driven any motor vehicle or
trailer on a road in such defective condition as regards its mechanical fittings or,
otherwise so as to be a source of danger to its occupants or the public when he or she
knows or ought to have known that it was so defective, commits an offence and shall be
liable on summary conviction to a fine of one thousand dollars or to imprisonment for six
months, or to both such fine and imprisonment.

### 67.

Leaving vehicle in dangerous position
(1) The owner or a person in charge of a motor vehicle, whether in working condition
or not, shall not cause or permit that vehicle or any trailer drawn thereby to remain
stationary on any road in such a position, in such condition or in such circumstances as is
likely to cause danger or obstruction to other traffic using the road.
(2) The owner or a person in charge of a motor vehicle or trailer referred to in
subsection (1) shall take all requisite steps by lighting or otherwise to indicate its
presence and position on the road and take immediate steps to have it removed as near as
possible to the left hand side of the road.
(3) Any member of the Police Force or the Chief Technical Officer or any person
authorised by the Chief Technical Officer may require the owner, driver or other person
in charge of any motor vehicle or trailer referred to in this section to remove that vehicle
or cause it to be removed, and a requirement to remove a motor vehicle may include a
requirement that the motor vehicle shall not be removed to any specified road or to any
particular position on such specified road.
(4) The Commissioner of Police or the Chief Technical Officer or any person
authorised by either of them may remove and impound a motor vehicle or trailer if the
owner, driver or other person in charge of it fails to remove it after being required to do
so under subsection (3) or cannot be found after reasonable steps have been taken
unsuccessfully to ascertain his or her whereabouts and the removal of the motor vehicle
or trailer is necessary.
(5) When a motor vehicle or trailer has been removed pursuant to subsection (4), no
person other than one specifically authorised to do so by the Commissioner of Police
shall recover that vehicle or trailer unless he or she is the owner or an agent of the owner,
and has paid any cost incurred in so removing it as certified in writing by a member of the
Police Force, has paid such poundage fee as hereby may be fixed by the Commissioner of
Police by Order, and has paid any other legal charge or fine where appropriate.
(6) Section 30(4) to (7) inclusive shall, as they apply to that section, apply also to this
section mutatis mutandis.

### 68.

Excessive noise from vehicle or equipment in it

(1) Any person who, without the written permission of the Commissioner of Police,
drives or permits to be driven a motor vehicle on a road when that vehicle emits excessive
noise from its engine, exhaust, radio, tape, tapedeck or any other part thereof or
equipment attached thereto, whether such equipment is built into the vehicle or not, and
whether for the reproduction of music, speech, other sound or otherwise, commits an
offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine of one thousand dollars, or to
imprisonment for six months, or to both such fine and imprisonment.
(2) For the purposes of subsection (1), a noise is excessive when it exceeds the
prescribed level on an instrument used for the purpose of measuring noise levels.
(3) Evidence for the purposes of subsection (1) which is derived from or based on an
instrument referred to in subsection (2) shall be admissible in any court of law.

### 68A.

Prohibition on amplification equipment
(1) Any person who, for the purpose of amplification of music or sound or otherwise,
installs, or causes to be installed, or operates or causes to be operated, on a public service
vehicle any amplifier, equaliser or speaker or other like equipment with a capacity
exceeding 20 watts, commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine of
one thousand dollars and to imprisonment for six months.
(2) A police officer in uniform may seize any equipment installed in a public service
vehicle which he reasonably believes to be in excess of the capacity specified in
subsection (1) and may produce it in court as evidence of an offence against that
subsection. If any person is convicted of an offence in connection with the equipment the
court may order it to be forfeited to the State, in addition to, or instead of any other
penalty for the offence.

### 69.

Warning to be given of intended prosecution
(1) Subject to subsection (2) a person may not be convicted of an offence under
section 56, 57, 58 or 62 unless—
(a)

he or she was warned at the time the offence was committed that the
question of prosecuting him or her for an offence under the relevant section
would be considered;

(b)

within fourteen days of the commission of the offence a summons in respect
of the offence was served on him or her;

(c)

within fourteen days of the commission of the offence a notice of the
intended prosecution specifying the nature of the alleged offence and the
time and place where it is alleged to have been committed was served on or
sent by registered post to him or her or the person in whose name the vehicle
was registered at the time of the commission of the offence; or

(d)

he was given a notice under Part VIII in respect of the offence and he or she
did not pay the fixed penalty relating thereto in accordance with the
requirements of the said Part VIII.

(2) Failure to comply with subsection (1) shall not be a bar to the conviction of the
accused in any case where the court is satisfied that—
(a)

neither the name and address of the accused nor the name and address of the
registered owner of the motor vehicle could with reasonable diligence have
been ascertained in time for the summons or notice to have been served on
or sent as aforesaid to the accused or the owner of the motor vehicle; or

(b)

the accused by his or her own conduct contributed to the failure to comply
with that subsection.

(3) The requirements of this section shall in every case be deemed to have been
complied with unless and until the contrary is proved.

### 70.

Restrictions on carriage of persons on motor cycles
(1) It shall be unlawful for more than one person in addition to the driver to be carried
on a two-wheeled motor cycle, or for any such one person to be carried otherwise than
sitting astride the cycle on a proper seat securely fixed to the cycle behind the driver’s
seat.
(2) If a person is carried on a cycle in contravention of subsection (1), the driver of
the cycle commits an offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine of five
hundred dollars or to imprisonment for one month, or to both such fine and
imprisonment.

### 71.

Restrictions on carriage of persons on bicycles
(1) It shall be unlawful for more than one person to be carried on a road on a bicycle
not propelled by mechanical power unless it is constructed or adapted for the carriage of
more than one person.
(2) If any person is carried on a bicycle in contravention of subsection (1), each of the
persons carried commits an offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine of
five hundred dollars or to imprisonment for three months, or to both such fine and
imprisonment.
(3) In this section, references to a person carried on a bicycle include references to a
person riding the bicycle.

### 72.

Restrictions on cyclists riding abreast or holding on to other moving vehicles
(1) It shall be unlawful for more than two persons to ride abreast on a road upon
motor cycles, or bicycles not propelled by mechanical power.
(2) A person shall not be convicted under subsection (1) in respect of the overtaking
of two persons so riding and in the absence of other traffic on the road at the point of
overtaking.
(3) It shall be unlawful for any person while riding a motor cycle, or a bicycle not
propelled by mechanical power, on a road—
(a)

to hold on to any other moving vehicle;

(b)

to ride such motor cycle or bicycle without at least one hand being on the
handle bars of such motor cycle or bicycle.

(4) Any person who contravenes this section shall be liable on summary conviction to
a fine of five hundred dollars or to imprisonment for three months, or to both such fine
and imprisonment.

### 73.

Cyclists and pedestrians to comply with directions to stop
Where a member of the Police Force in uniform engaged in the regulation of vehicular
traffic on a road gives a traffic control signal to enable any vehicle to proceed in an
indicated direction, any cyclist or pedestrian who proceeds against the flow of traffic
consequent upon such signal or loiters in the road or otherwise impedes any vehicle from
proceeding in the direction indicated commits an offence and shall be liable on summary
conviction to a fine of two hundred and fifty dollars or to imprisonment for one month, or
to both such fine and imprisonment.

### 74.

Protective head gear

(1) Subject to this section, a person must not drive or ride on a motor cycle unless
wearing protective head gear consisting of a helmet designed to protect the head of a
person driving or riding on a motor cycle.
(2) Protective head gear worn for the purposes of subsection (1) must have such
characteristics as the Minister prescribes by Order.
(3) This section comes into operation six months after the commencement of the
Road Traffic (Amendment) Act, 2001.

### 74A.

Seat belts
(1) It is an offence for a person to drive or cause to be driven on a public road a motor
vehicle to which this section applies unless it is fitted with seat belts on every front seat,
of a type and fitted in a manner approved by the Licensing Authority.
(2) No person shall drive or sit in the front of a vehicle to which this section applies
unless he or she is wearing a seat belt while the vehicle is in motion.
(3) This section applies to all private motor cars, public service vehicles and goods
vehicles.
(4) No person may import a vehicle to which this section applies unless it is fitted
with seat belts which comply with subsection (1).
(5) A person who contravenes this section commits an offence and is liable on
summary conviction to a fine of a thousand dollars and to imprisonment for three months.
(6) This section comes into operation one year after the commencement of the Road
Traffic (Amendment) Act, 2001.

### 75.

Duty to stop in case of accident
(1) A person riding, driving or in charge of any vehicle of any description shall, if,
owing to the presence of the vehicle on a road, an accident occurs whereby personal
injury is caused to any person or damage is caused to any vehicle—
(a)

immediately stop; and

(b)

if required to do so by any person having reasonable grounds for so
requiring, give—
(i) his or her name and address,
(ii) the name and address of the owner of the vehicle,
(iii) the registration number of the vehicle,
(iv) the name of the insurance company with which the vehicle is insured,
and
(v) the names of all persons in the vehicle at the time of the accident; and

(c)

report the accident at a police station as soon as reasonably practicable but
before the expiration of a period of 24 hours after its occurrence.

(2) Subsection (1)(c) shall not apply if a report is made, at the time and place of the
accident, to a member of the Police Force.
(3) Any person who contravenes or fails to comply with this section commits an
offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine of two thousand dollars or to
imprisonment for six months, or to both such fine and imprisonment.
(4) A person shall not be convicted under this section if, through pressing emergency,
he or she so had to help attend to the personal injury caused by the accident that he or she
could not reasonably have been expected to comply with this section.

### 76.

Power of police to test or cause vehicle to be tested after accident
(1) Where an accident occurs owing to the presence of a vehicle on a road, a member
of the Police Force may inspect and test the vehicle or cause it to be inspected and tested
by an Inspector and may, for this purpose, require that the vehicle shall not be driven or
taken away from the scene of the accident until it has been inspected and tested.
(2) Any person who—
(a)

obstructs an Inspector or a member of the Police Force in the performance of
his or her duty under this section;

(b)

contravenes or fails to comply with this section; or

(c)

fails to comply with a requirement by a member of the Police Force made
under subsection (1),

commits an offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine of five hundred
dollars or to imprisonment for three months, or to both such fine and imprisonment.

### 77.

Provisions regarding parking, no-entry, one-way traffic and bus-stops
(1) If any person parks or leaves a motor vehicle at a place or in a manner or at a time
prohibited by Order made by the Commissioner of Police and published in the Gazette
and at least one local newspaper, he or she commits an offence.
(2) No person shall drive a motor vehicle into or on a road into which vehicles are
forbidden to enter from the direction into which such entry is forbidden as indicated by a
“no entry” sign marked, fixed or placed in accordance with an Order made by the
Commissioner of Police and published in the Gazette and at least one local newspaper.
(3) No person shall drive a motor vehicle on a road or in a lane in a road in a direction
opposite to that in which vehicles are required to be driven in accordance with Orders
made by the Commissioner of Police and published in the Gazette and at least one local
newspaper requiring that vehicles on that road be driven in only one direction.
(4) It shall be unlawful for a motor omnibus to take on or set down passengers on a
road in any town or city or any other place prescribed by Order made by the
Commissioner of Police and published in the Gazette and at least one local newspaper
except at places on that road designated by the Order for such purpose indicated by the
sign “Bus-Stop”. The driver and the conductor of a motor omnibus shall both be liable if
the motor omnibus is used in contravention of this subsection.
(5) Whenever this section refers to an Order made by the Commissioner of Police, the
Commissioner of Police is by virtue of this section empowered to make such Order,
without prejudice to any other provision of this Act also empowering him or her to do so:
Provided that where the Commissioner of Police is by virtue of this section
empowered to make such an Order no such Order shall be valid unless it secures the prior
written approval of the Minister and is stated to have secured such approval.
(6) Any person who violates any provision of this section or of any Order made by
the Commissioner of Police under this section commits an offence and shall be liable on
summary conviction to a fine of one thousand five hundred dollars or to imprisonment for
twelve months or to both such fine and imprisonment.

## PART VI

Traffic Signs

### 78.

Traffic signs on or near a road

(1) Subject to subsection (2) the Licensing Authority may cause or permit traffic signs
to be placed, erected or otherwise marked on or near any road, and may authorise any
traffic signs so placed, erected or otherwise marked before the coming into force of this
Act to be retained.
(2) If the Licensing Authority causes or permits any traffic sign to be placed, erected
or otherwise marked, or authorises the retention of any traffic sign, on or near any road in
respect of which an Order of the Minister made under section 81 is in force, the
provisions of the Order made by the Minister regarding the subject matter of such traffic
sign shall be suspended during the continuance in force of such traffic sign.
(3) The size, colour and type of any traffic sign may be prescribed, and any traffic
sign shall be of the prescribed type and substantially of the prescribed size and colour
and, where the size, colour and type thereof are not prescribed, shall be of a character
authorised by the Licensing Authority.
(4) Every traffic sign erected, placed, marked or retained on or near any road shall,
unless the contrary is proved, be deemed—
(a)

to have been lawfully erected, placed, marked or retained;

(b)

to be of the prescribed type and substantially of the prescribed size and
colour, where the size, colour and type of the same have been prescribed,
and in other cases to be of a character authorised by the Licensing Authority
under this section.

(5) (a) Notwithstanding anything contained in subsection (4), after the
commencement of this Act no person other than the Licensing Authority or any person
acting under his or her directions may, except with the general or special permission in
writing of the Licensing Authority—
(i) erect, place or mark any traffic sign on or near any road, or
(ii) retain any traffic sign erected, placed or marked, or caused to be erected,
placed or marked by him or her on or near any road.
(b) Without prejudice to the operation of section 79(1), nothing in this subsection
shall apply to any traffic sign—
(i) which the owner of any land erects, places, marks or retains, or causes to be
erected, placed, marked or retained, on or near any private road which is
situated on such land, or
(ii) which any person erects, places, marks or retains, or causes to be erected,
placed, marked or retained, on or near any road in pursuance of any authority
conferred upon him or her by or under any law of the State other than this
Act.
(c) For the purposes of this subsection—
“owner”, in relation to any land, includes any person in lawful occupation of the
land; and
“private road” means any privately owned street, road or open space to which the
public is granted access conditionally.

### 78A.

Light signals
(1) The Licensing Authority may install or cause to be installed traffic signs
consisting of light signals for the purpose of regulating traffic at such places as it
considers appropriate.
(2) The light signals may be mechanical or electronic devices and shall consist of
size, number and type as may be prescribed.

(3) The light signals shall have such significance or commands as my be prescribed
and all drivers of motor vehicles shall obey such light signals in the same manner as other
traffic signs.

### 79.

Traffic signs on land
(1) The Licensing Authority may, by notice in writing, require the owner or occupier
of any land on which there is a traffic sign, or any object which so closely resembles a
traffic sign that it might reasonably be taken to be such a sign, to remove it, and if any
person fails to comply with such a notice, the Licensing Authority may himself or herself
effect or authorise the removal, causing as little damage as possible, and may, except
where the sign was erected, placed, marked or retained by the Licensing Authority,
recover in a court of competent jurisdiction as a civil debt from the person so in default
the expenses incurred by him or her in doing so.
(2) The Licensing Authority or any person authorised by him or her may, on any land
near to or adjoining a road, cut branches of trees, or other vegetation, or remove any other
thing which obscures or is likely to cause damage to a traffic sign; but the Licensing
Authority or any person authorised by him or her shall not exercise any powers under this
section until after the expiration of three days’ written notice given to the owner of the
land or posted up conspicuously thereon, stating his or her intention to exercise the
relevant power.
(3) The Licensing Authority or any person authorised by him or her may enter any
land near to or adjoining a road—
(a)

for the purpose of exercising any power conferred by this section upon the
Licensing Authority; or

(b)

for the purpose of replacing or maintaining traffic signs.

(4) The Licensing Authority or any person authorised by him or her shall do as little
damage as possible in executing any work authorised by this section.
(5) (a) If any person who owns or has interest in any property suffers pecuniary loss
by reason of any damage to the property resulting from the exercise by the Licensing
Authority of the powers conferred upon him or her by section 78 or this section,
compensation shall be payable to the person in respect of the loss.
(b) If any difference arises between any person claiming compensation and the
Licensing Authority as to whether the person is entitled to compensation or as to the
amount due, the difference shall on the written application of the person or the Attorney
General be determined—
(i) by a Magistrate if the amount claimed does not exceed nine thousand dollars,
or
(ii) by a Judge of the High Court if the amount claimed exceeds nine thousand
dollars,
as if the Magistrate or Judge were a single arbitrator appointed pursuant to the Arbitration
Act, and the said Act shall apply accordingly.
(6) Compensation payable under this section shall be defrayed out of monies provided
for the purpose by Parliament.

### 80.

Penalties for violating sections 78 and 79
Any person who—
(a)

unlawfully places, erects, marks or, retains, or causes to be placed, erected,
marked or retained, any traffic sign on or near any road;

(b)

removes, defaces, damages, alters or obscures or in any way interferes with,
any traffic sign lawfully placed, erected, marked or retained on or near any
road; or

(c)

being the driver of a vehicle, fails to comply with a traffic sign placed,
erected or otherwise marked on or near any road by the Licensing Authority,

commits an offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine of five hundred
dollars or to imprisonment for three months, or to both such fine and imprisonment.

### 81.

Parking of vehicles and one-way roads
(1) The Minister may by Order—
(a)

prohibit or regulate the parking of vehicles on any road;

(b)

prohibit the driving of any vehicle on any specified road otherwise than in a
specified direction;

(c)

specify the route to be travelled by public service vehicles; or

(d)

prohibit vehicles of a particular class from travelling on certain roads or
routes under conditions specified in the Order.

(2) An Order made under this section may prescribe in respect of any contravention
thereof a penalty not exceeding a fine of five hundred dollars or imprisonment for three
months or both such fine and imprisonment on summary conviction.
(3) Where any provision in an Order made by the Minister under this section conflicts
with any Order made or directions given under section 82 to meet the exigencies of the
special occasion, the operation of the provisions of the Order made by the Minister under
this section shall be suspended during the continuance in force of the Order or directions
made or given under section 82 as the case may be.
(4) Where any provision in any Order made by the Commissioner of Police under
section 77 conflicts with any provision in any Order or regulations made by the Minister
under this Act, the operation of such provisions in such Order or regulations made by the
Minister shall be suspended during the continuance in force of the provision in the Order
made by the Commissioner of Police.
(5) The Minister may by Order published in the Gazette zone the routes that are
travelled for regular hire or reward by public service vehicles and require public service
vehicles to travel such zoned routes for regular hire or reward in accordance with such
Order. When such an Order is in force, the driver of a public service vehicle governed
thereby shall display prominently at both the front and back of the vehicle a sign with
such letters or numbers or both letters and numbers as may be prescribed in the Order
clearly distinctly and conspicuously identifying the route which the vehicle is required to
travel. A requirement that a route be travelled includes a duty to begin and end the
journey at such points as may be stipulated in such Order.
(6) A requirement imposed by an Order referred to in subsection (5) that a public
service vehicle be used for hire or reward on or along a route shall not prevent that
vehicle from travelling other routes if it does so when it is not on duty or when it is on
tour or on charter. But in either of these events the driver shall display prominently at
both the front and back of the vehicle a sign with clearly visible and legible letters saying
that the vehicle is “Not on Duty” or “On Tour” or “On Charter” as the case may be.
(7) An Order referred to in subsections (5) and (6) may provide that any person who
contravenes such Order shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine of one thousand
dollars or to imprisonment for twelve months, or to both such fine and imprisonment.

### 82.

Special Orders may be made by Commissioner of Police

(1) The Commissioner of Police may by notice published in the Gazette and at least
any one local newspaper make Orders or give directions concerning the lane to be kept by
persons driving or riding any vehicle or animal on any road and as to the manner in which
vehicles of any description shall be parked or drawn up while waiting on any road, and as
to the route to be observed by all vehicles, horses and persons, and for preventing
obstructions on any road, in any case where because of some special occasion such road
is liable to be thronged or obstructed; or concerning the regulating of traffic in the
neighbourhood of Parliament, the Courts of Law, public offices, churches, theatres and
other such special places of public resort.
(2) The Commissioner of Police may, without making Orders or giving directions
referred to in subsection (1), place, erect, mark or otherwise issue a notice on or near any
road concerning the matters referred to in subsection (1).
(3) Any person who contravenes or fails to comply with Orders made or directions
given under subsection (1) or notices issued under subsection (2) commits an offence and
shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine of five hundred dollars or to imprisonment
for three months, or to both such fine and imprisonment.
(4) Any member of the Police Force may apprehend without warrant, any person who
commits an offence under this section within his or her view if the person refuses to give
his or her name and address or gives a name or address which the member of the Police
Force has reasonable grounds for believing to be false.

### 83.

Drivers to comply with traffic directions
(1) Where a member of the Police Force in uniform is for the time being engaged in
the regulation of traffic on a road where a traffic sign, being a sign of the prescribed size,
colour and type or of another character authorised by the Licensing Authority under
section 78, and being a sign for regulating the movement of traffic or indicating the route
to be followed by traffic, or when a notice of the Commissioner of Police under section
82 has been lawfully placed, erected, marked or otherwise issued or retained on or near a
road, any person driving or propelling a vehicle or any person riding or driving any
animal—
(a)

who neglects or refuses to stop the vehicle or animal or to make the vehicle
or animal proceed in or keep to a particular lane of traffic when directed or
signalled to do so by the member of the Police Force in the execution of his
or her duty; or

(b)

who fails to comply with the indication given by the traffic sign or notice,
when the member of the Police Force does not otherwise direct,

commits an offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine of five hundred
dollars or to imprisonment for three months or to both such fine and imprisonment.
(2) The Commissioner of Police may, without making an Order or giving directions
or issuing a notice under section 82, place, erect or mark a traffic sign of the
specifications referred to in section 78 or some other special traffic sign prepared by him
or her, or may authorise a member of the Police Force in uniform to regulate the
movement of traffic or to indicate the route to be followed by traffic or to regulate the
parking of vehicles; and any person driving or propelling a vehicle or any person riding or
driving any animal—
(a)

who neglects or refuses to stop the vehicle or animal or to make the vehicle
or animal proceed in or keep to a particular lane of traffic when directed or
signalled to do so by the member of the Police Force in the execution of his
or her duty; or

(b)

who fails to comply with the indication given by the traffic sign or notice,
when the member of the Police Force does not otherwise direct,

commits an offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine of five hundred
dollars or to imprisonment for three months, or to both such fine and imprisonment.

### 84.

Bus-stops
(1) Any person who parks any vehicle on a road so that any part of the vehicle or any
load carried thereon is within any bus-stop as designated under section 77 commits an
offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine of five hundred dollars or to
imprisonment for one month, or to both such fine and imprisonment.
(2) For the purposes of this section, “parking” includes causing or permitting a
vehicle, whether occupied or not, to stand on a road—
(a)

while the vehicle is being loaded or unloaded; or

(b)

while passengers are entering or leaving the vehicle.

## PART VII

Legal Proceedings, Suspension, Cancellation, Endorsement of Driving Licences

### 85.

Procedure on failure of jury to agree on alternative verdict
(1) Where, upon the trial of a person for an offence under section 55, the jury finds
the accused not guilty of the offence charged, but fails to agree on the alternative verdict
provided for in subsection (2) of that section, a charge based on the same facts for an
offence under section 56 or 62 shall not be preferred against the accused without the
written consent of the Director of Public Prosecutions.
(2) Where, pursuant to subsection (1), a person is charged with an offence under
section 56 or 62 he or she may be convicted of the offence notwithstanding that section
69 has not been complied with in respect of the offence.

### 86.

Power to proceed on charge of careless driving on hearing of charge of
dangerous driving or drunken driving
(1) Where, upon the trial of a person for an offence under section 55, 56 or 62 the
court is of the opinion that the offence is not proved, then, without prejudice to any other
powers the court possesses, it may at any time during the hearing or immediately
thereafter—
(a)

grant permission for a charge for an offence under section 57 to be preferred
against the accused; or

(b)

direct or allow such charge under section 57 to be preferred forthwith against
the accused and, subject to subsection (2), may thereupon proceed with the
charge.

(2) In any case referred to in subsection (1) the accused or his or her solicitor or
counsel shall be informed of the new charge and shall be given an opportunity, whether
by way of cross-examination of any witness whose evidence has already been given
against the accused or otherwise, of answering the new charge, and the court may adjourn
the hearing if it considers that the accused is prejudiced in his or her defence by reason of
the new charge being so preferred.
(3) Where—
(a)

a charge to which section 69 relates has been brought against an accused and
that section has been complied with; or

(b)

a charge has been brought against an accused under section 55, in which
case section 69 does not apply,

the accused may be convicted on a charge preferred pursuant to subsection (1),
notwithstanding that that subsection has not been satisfied in respect of the charge so
preferred.

### 87.

Power of court before which a person is convicted in connection with the
driving of a motor vehicle
(1) The court before which a person is convicted of any offence in connection with
the driving of a motor vehicle may, in addition to any other penalty provided for such
offence, order that the offender be disqualified for a stated period or permanently from
holding or obtaining a driving licence either generally or limited to the driving of a motor
vehicle of any particular class, and where the offender holds a driving licence the court
shall require him or her to produce the licence and the court shall endorse upon the
licence particulars of any Order the court makes under this section together with the
particulars of the conviction.
(2) A person who is disqualified under this Act from holding or obtaining a driving
licence may, after the expiration of six months from the date of disqualification, apply to
the court by which he or she was disqualified to remove the disqualification and the court
may, as it thinks proper, having regard to the character of the person disqualified and his
or her conduct subsequent to the disqualification, the nature of the offence and any other
circumstances of the case, either by Order remove the disqualification as from the date
specified in the Order or refuse the application.
(3) Where an application under subsection (2) is refused, a further application shall
not be entertained within six months of the date of refusal.
(4) Particulars of a conviction or disqualification or of a removal of a disqualification
under this Act shall be furnished by the court to the Licensing Authority.
(5) A person who by virtue of an Order of a court under this section is disqualified
from holding or obtaining a driving licence may appeal against the Order in the same
manner as against a conviction, and the court may, if it thinks fit pending the appeal,
suspend the operation of the Order.
(6) The provisions of this section regarding the disqualifying of a person and the
endorsing of his or her driving licence shall be subject to other provisions on these
matters of disqualifying and endorsing specifically contained in this Act.

### 88.

Production of driving licence for endorsement
(1) Any person who is convicted of an offence in connection with the driving of a
motor vehicle shall, if he or she holds a driving licence, produce it within such time as the
court directs for the purpose of endorsement thereon of the particulars of the conviction if
the court so directs.
(2) Any person who contravenes or fails to comply with this section commits an
offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine of five hundred dollars or to
imprisonment for three months, or to both such fine and imprisonment.

### 89.

Custody of driving licence when suspended or cancelled
(1) Where a court orders the suspension or cancellation of a driving licence or
disqualifies a person from obtaining a driving licence, the owner of that suspended or
cancelled driving licence shall surrender it to the court within seven days of the making of
the Order and the court shall keep same in its custody while such Order remains in force,
subject to subsection (3).
(2) At the expiration of the period of suspension of a driving licence the court shall
return the licence to the holder on demand.

(3) Where the disqualification to which a person has become subject is limited to the
driving of a motor vehicle of a particular class or description, the court shall upon
receiving the driving licence, indicate thereon the class or description of vehicle that the
holder of the driving licence is disqualified from driving and thereupon return the licence
to the holder.

### 90.

Power of court or Licensing Authority to suspend driving licence pending
determination of certain charges
(1) Where a person is charged before a court with an offence under section 55, 56 or
62 the court or the Licensing Authority may order the suspension of the driving licence or
the learner’s licence of that person pending the determination of the charge.
(2) A driving licence or learner’s licence suspended under subsection (1) shall, upon
being suspended, be surrendered by the holder thereof to the court before which he or she
is charged or the Licensing Authority as the case may be.
(3) Any person who contravenes subsection (2) commits an offence and shall be
liable on summary conviction to a fine of five hundred dollars or to imprisonment for
three months or to both such fine and imprisonment.

### 91.

Effect of suspension of driving licence or disqualification
(1) A driving licence suspended by a court or by the Licensing Authority, as the case
may be, is of no effect during the period of suspension and a person whose licence is
suspended, or who has been disqualified by the court from obtaining a driving licence,
shall not be competent to hold a driving licence during the period of suspension or the
period of disqualification as the case may be.
(2) Where a person drives a motor vehicle on a road while his or her driving licence
regarding that vehicle or class or description of vehicle is suspended, the provisions of
section 31 shall apply as if that person were driving without the required licence.

### 92.

Liability of person disqualified from holding or obtaining driving licence
Any person disqualified under this Part from obtaining a driving licence who—
(a)

applies for or obtains a driving licence while he or she is disqualified;

(b)

drives a motor vehicle on a road while he or she is disqualified; or

(c)

if the disqualification is limited to the driving of a motor vehicle of a
particular class or description, drives on a road a motor vehicle of that class
or description,

commits an offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine of one thousand
dollars or to imprisonment for twelve months or to both such fine and imprisonment.

### 93.

Non-disclosure of suspension or cancellation of driving licence
Any person whose driving licence has been suspended or cancelled who subsequently
applies for or obtains a driving licence during such suspension or cancellation without
disclosing the fact that his or her licence has been so suspended or cancelled commits an
offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine of one thousand dollars or to
imprisonment for twelve months or to both such fine and imprisonment and in addition
thereto any licence so obtained shall be of no effect.

### 94.

Non-disclosure of endorsement
Where—
(a)

a driving licence has been ordered to be endorsed; and

(b)

the holder of the driving licence is not yet entitled to a driving licence free
from endorsement pursuant to section 96,

if the holder applies for or obtains a driving licence without stating in the application
particulars of the endorsement, he or she commits an offence and shall be liable on
summary conviction to a fine of one thousand dollars or to imprisonment for twelve
months, or to both such fine and imprisonment and in addition thereto any licence so
obtained shall be of no effect.

### 95.

Particulars of endorsement to be copied on new driving licence
Subject to sections 48(1) and 96, any particulars, endorsed on a driving licence
previously held by a person must be copied on any new driving licence issued to him or
her.

### 96.

Issue of new licence free from endorsement after certain period
(1) A person in respect of whom an order has been made under this Part requiring that
an endorsement be made on any driving licence held by him or her, who, during a
continuous period of three years or upwards since the order was made, has had no other
such order made against him or her, shall be entitled on application to the Licensing
Authority, subject to payment of such fee as the Minister by Order prescribes and the
surrender of his or her current licence, to have issued to him or her a new driving licence
free from endorsement.
(2) For the purposes of subsection (1), in reckoning the period of three years since the
making of an order requiring an endorsement, any period during which the person was by
virtue of the order disqualified from holding or obtaining a driving licence shall be
excluded.
(3) Subject to this section, a person who, by an order of a court made pursuant to this
Act, is disqualified from holding or obtaining a driving licence may make application to
the court that made the order, for removal of the disqualification, in the same manner as
against a conviction, and on any such application being made, the court may, after having
regard to—
(a)

the character of the person disqualified;

(b)

his or her conduct subsequent to the making of the order;

(c)

the nature of the offence; and

(d)

any other circumstances of the case,

order the removal of the disqualification as from the date specified in the order, or refuse
the application. An order restoring a driving licence which has been cancelled may
likewise be granted on application by the person whose driving licence has been
cancelled if he or she applies, as the case may be, to the court which ordered the
cancellation or to the court in the district in which the office of the Licensing Authority is
situate when it was the Licensing Authority which ordered the cancellation.
(4) An application may not be made under subsection (3) for an Order for the removal
of a disqualification before the expiration of—
(a)

six months from the date of the ordering of the disqualification, if the
disqualification was ordered for a period less than a year;

(b)

one-half of the period of the disqualification, from the date of the ordering of
the disqualification if the disqualification was made for a period less than six
years but not less than one year; and

(c)

three years, in any other case.

(5) In determining the expiration of the period after which a person may apply under
subsection (4) for removal of a disqualification, any time after the conviction during
which the disqualification was suspended or he or she was further disqualified shall be
disregarded.
(6) Where an application made pursuant to subsection (3) for the removal of a
disqualification is refused, a further such application shall not be entertained if made
within three months after the date of the refusal.
(7) The court shall order particulars of any order it makes for the removal of a
disqualification to be endorsed on the driving licence, if any, previously held by the
applicant for the order; and it may also order the applicant to pay the whole or any part of
the costs of the application.
(8) An application may not be made under subsection (3) for an order for the removal
of a cancellation before the expiration of two years from the date of the ordering of the
cancellation. Where an application for the removal of a cancellation is refused, a further
application shall not be entertained if made within six months after the date of the refusal.

## PART VIII

Fixed Penalty

### 97.

Special meanings
In this Part—
“fixed penalty” means the penalty prescribed under section 101;
“notice” means a document in the form set out in the Fifth Schedule offering the
opportunity for the discharge of any liability to conviction for the offence specified
therein by payment of a fixed penalty in accordance with this Part;
“offence” means an offence listed in the second column of the Third Schedule,
being an offence created by or under the enactment specified in the third column of
that schedule, and attracting the fixed penalty set out in the fifth column of that
schedule.

### 98.

Member of Police Force may give or affix notice
(1) Where a member of the Police Force of or above such rank as may from time to
time be prescribed by the Commissioner of Police has reason to believe that an offence to
which this Part relates has been or is being committed with or in respect of a motor
vehicle, it shall be lawful for him or her to give to the driver of that vehicle a notice
charging him or her with the commission of such offence, and notifying him or her that a
complaint will be made against him or her in respect thereof and requiring him or her
either to pay the fixed penalty within the time specified in the notice or to appear at the
court specified in the notice on the day and at the hour stated therein to answer the said
complaint:
Provided that no more than one offence may be charged in any such notice:
Provided further that no more than one notice may be given in respect of a vehicle
regarding any one same set of circumstances.
(2) Subject to this section, where a member of the Police Force of or above such rank
as may from time to time be prescribed by the Commissioner of Police has reason to
believe that an offence to which this Part relates is being or has been committed with or in
respect of a motor vehicle, he or she may affix the notice referred to in subsection (1) to
any part of the vehicle where the notice may easily be seen.

(3) A notice affixed under subsection (2) shall for the purposes of this Act be deemed
to have been given to the person liable for the offence, and notwithstanding any law to the
contrary, the registered owner of a vehicle shall, for the purposes of the proceedings
instituted by the notice, be presumed to be the person liable for the offence.
(4) Where a member of the Police Force of or above such rank as may from time to
time be prescribed by the Commissioner of Police intends to proceed under subsection (1)
or (2), the notice referred to in those subsections shall be given or affixed on the occasion
when he or she considered that he or she had reason to believe that the offence was
committed.
(5) The member of the Police Force of or above such rank as may from time to time
be prescribed by the Commissioner of Police proceeding under this section shall send a
duplicate of the notice referred to in subsections (1) and (2) to the Magistrate of the
district in which the offence is alleged to have been committed, and the duplicate shall be
deemed to be a complaint laid before the Magistrate:
Provided that no such reminder need be served on or sent to the driver or owner of
the vehicle concerned where the notice was served personally upon the said driver or
owner as the case may be.
(6) The Member of the Police Force of or above such rank as may from time to time
be prescribed by the Commissioner of Police proceeding under this section shall serve
upon or send to both the driver and the owner of the vehicle concerned a written reminder
in such form as may hereby be prescribed by the Commissioner of Police stating that the
notice referred to in this section was affixed to the vehicle under this section. The
reminder shall be served or sent within seven days of the notice being affixed to a vehicle.
Evidence that such reminder was left at or posted to the last known residential address of
the driver and the owner shall suffice to show that such reminder was served or sent:
Provided that no such reminder need be served on or sent to the driver or owner of
the vehicle concerned where the notice was served personally upon the said driver or
owner as the case may be.
(7) The reference in subsection (2) to the affixing of a notice to any part of a vehicle
includes putting the notice inside the vehicle wherever possible.
(8) For the avoidance of doubt it is hereby declared that where an offence to which
this Part relates has been or is being committed, nothing in this Part shall be construed as
creating a mandatory requirement for a member of the Police Force, within the
contemplation of this section, to give the driver of the vehicle concerned a notice referred
to in subsection (1) instead of proceeding by way of summons before a court of law.
(9) Where an offence to which this section applies has been or is being committed by
any person, a member of the Police Force, within the contemplation of this section, in
making a determination as to whether to issue a notice under subsection (1) to the person
alleged to have committed the offence or to proceed by way of summons before a court of
summary jurisdiction, shall have regard to any or all of the following—
(a)

the nature of the offence;

(b)

the frequency with which the offence is committed generally;

(c)

the circumstances under which the offence was committed;

(d)

the conduct of the person who is alleged to have committed the offence;

(e)

the public interest.

### 99.

Particulars to be specified in notice
A notice given or affixed under section 98 shall be signed by the member of the Police
Force proceeding under that section and shall specify—
(a)

the date, time and place of the giving or affixing of the notice;

(b)

the section of the enactment creating the offence alleged, and such
particulars of the offence as are required under the Criminal Procedure Code,

## Chapter 72B;

(c)

the time within which the fixed penalty may be paid in accordance with
section 100(2);

(d)

the amount of the fixed penalty;

(e)

the clerk of the Magistrate’s Court to whom, and the address at or to which,
the fixed penalty may be paid or remitted.

### 100.

Payment of fixed penalty precludes prosecution
(1) Where a notice has been given under section 98, the driver or the registered owner
of the vehicle, as the case may be, may pay the fixed penalty in accordance with the
notice.
(2) The time within which a fixed penalty is payable is thirty-one (31) days from the
date of the notice, and where payment reaches the clerk of the Magistrate’s Court after
that time it shall be returned to the sender.
(3) Where the fixed penalty is paid in accordance with the notice, no person shall then
be liable to be convicted for the offence in respect of which the notice was given and the
proceedings instituted by the notice shall be deemed to have been dismissed.

### 101.

Amount of fixed penalty payable
(1) The fixed penalty for an offence shall be the amount specified in the fifth column
of the Third Schedule for that offence.
(2) Notwithstanding anything to the contrary contained in this Act or any other law,
where in respect of an offence attracting a fixed penalty a person is served with a notice
under section 98 requiring him or her to pay the fixed penalty or to appear at the court
specified, but he or she does not pay the fixed penalty and instead is proceeded against in
court, if he or she is convicted of the offence and the court decides to impose a fine, that
fine shall not be less than the sum that is the fixed penalty attaching to that offence by this
section and the Third Schedule.

### 102.

Payment of fixed penalty
(1) Payment of the fixed penalty shall be made to the clerk of the Magistrate’s Court
as stated in the notice given or affixed pursuant to section 98, and shall be dealt with in
the same manner as payment of a fine imposed for an offence under the Criminal
Procedure Code, Chapter 2.
(2) Payment of the fixed penalty shall be accompanied by the notice which shall be
completed by the driver or the registered owner, as the case may be, in the manner
prescribed.
(3) The clerk of the Magistrate’s Court to whom a fixed penalty has been paid shall in
writing inform the respective divisional headquarters of the Police Force as may hereby
be indicated by the Commissioner of Police in such form as may hereby be prescribed by
the Commissioner of Police.

### 103.

Certificate of payment or non-payment of fixed penalty
In any proceedings, a certificate that payment of the fixed penalty was or was not
made to the clerk of the Magistrate’s Court by the date specified in the certificate shall, if
the certificate purports to be signed by such clerk be sufficient evidence of the facts
stated, unless the contrary is proved.

### 104.

Consequence of failure to pay or to appear
(1) Where a fixed penalty is not paid within the time specified in the notice to which
section 98 refers, being the time within which it should be paid in accordance with
section 100(2), proceedings in respect of the offence specified in the notice shall
thereafter proceed in the manner prescribed by the Criminal Procedure Code, Chapter 2:
Provided that where a notice under Part VIII of this Act has been given to a person
regarding an offence and he or she does not pay the fixed penalty relating thereto in
accordance with the requirements of the said Part VIII the said notice shall be deemed to
be the information or complaint required by section 71 of the Code to institute
proceedings.
(2) No such proceedings as referred to in subsection (1) shall be instituted unless the
member of the Police Force proceeding under section 98 serves upon or sends to both the
driver and the owner of the vehicle concerned the written reminder referred to in
section 98(6).

### 105.

Notice not to be removed or interfered with
(1) A notice affixed to a vehicle under section 98(2) shall not be removed or
interfered with except by or under the authority of—
(a)

the driver or the person in charge of that vehicle; or

(b)

the person liable for the offence in question.

(2) Any person who, in contravention of subsection (1), removes a notice from a
vehicle commits an offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine of five
hundred dollars or to imprisonment for three months, or to both such fine and
imprisonment.

### 106.

Amendment and replacement of Third Schedule
(1) The Minister may from time to time by regulation—
(a)

add any offence to the Third Schedule;

(b)

remove any offence from the Third Schedule; or

(c)

replace the Third Schedule in whole or in part.

(2) In subsection (1)(a), “offence” means an offence against any enactment relating to
the use of vehicles on roads which is punishable on summary conviction.

### 107.

Special regulations
The Minister may by regulations make provisions respecting any matter incidental to
the operation of sections 97 to 106, and in particular for prescribing—
(a)

the form of notice to be given pursuant to section 98; and

(b)

the duties of the clerk of the Magistrate’s Court and the information to be
supplied to such clerk by the person on whom the fixed penalty is imposed.

## PART IX

Miscellaneous

### 108.

Fees

(1) Unless otherwise provided in this Act, the fees specified in the First Schedule
shall be payable annually to the Accountant-General on account of the general revenue of
the State.
(2) The Minister may by regulations amend, vary, suspend or revoke the provisions of
the First Schedule.
(3) Regulations made under subsection (2) shall be subject to affirmative resolution.

### 109.

Fraudulent imitation, etc., of documents
Any person who—
(a)

fraudulently imitates, alters, mutilates destroys; or

(b)

fraudulently uses or lends or allows to be used by any other person,

any identification mark, motor vehicle licence, certificate of registration or driving
licence issued or purported to have been issued under this Act, or certificate of insurance
of a motor vehicle, commits an offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to a
fine of one thousand dollars or to imprisonment for twelve months, or to both such fine
and imprisonment.

### 110.

Appointment of stands for public service vehicles etc.
(1) The Minister may by notice published in the Gazette designate stands and parking
places for use exclusively by public service vehicles and taxis plying or standing for hire.
(2) The driver of a public service vehicle or taxi who causes that public service
vehicle or taxi to ply or stand for hire on a road or place not designated under subsection
(1) for the use of a vehicle of that kind commits an offence and shall be liable on
summary conviction to a fine of five hundred dollars or to imprisonment for three months
or to both such fine and imprisonment.

### 111.

Penalty for offence not specifically provided for
(1) Any person who commits an offence under this Act or the regulations or an Order
made hereunder for which no penalty is specifically provided shall be liable on summary
conviction to a fine of two thousand dollars or to imprisonment for six months.
(2) Except where a particular provision in this Act specifically varies a fine or term of
imprisonment according to whether it is the offender’s first or second or third or
subsequent conviction, no more than a half of the maximum penalty provided for by this
Act regarding an offence shall be imposed upon an offender except in the case of that
offender’s third or subsequent conviction for that same particular offence and where a
person is a body corporate, to a fine of not less than fifteen thousand dollars.

### 112.

Failure to give certain information
(1) A driver or conductor of a motor vehicle who commits an offence under this Act
or regulations or Orders made hereunder, or who is alleged by a member of the Police
Force to have committed such an offence, who—
refuses to give his or her name and address, or gives a false name or address,
on being required by a member of the Police Force to give his or her name
and address,
commits an offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine of five hundred
dollars or to imprisonment for three months, or to both such fine and imprisonment.
(2) It shall be the duty of the owner of a motor vehicle to give to a member of the
Police Force such information as is possessed by the owner which may lead to the
identification and apprehension of the driver or conductor of that vehicle if that vehicle is

alleged by that member of the Police Force to have been involved in an offence under this
Act or regulations or Orders made hereunder if asked by a member of the Police Force to
do so. If the owner fails or refuses to give such information within four days of being
requested by a member of the Police Force to do so he or she commits an offence and
shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine of one thousand dollars or to
imprisonment for twelve months, or to both such fine and imprisonment.

### 113.

General power of arrest
(1) Subject to subsections (2) and (3), a member of the Police Force may arrest
without a warrant any person who within his or her view commits an offence under this
Act or any regulations, Orders or rules made pursuant to this Act or any person who such
member of the Police Force reasonably believes he or she saw committing such offence.
(2) The power of arrest conferred by subsection (1) may be exercised only in the
following cases—
(a)

in the case of a driver or conductor, where the driver or conductor, on being
requested to give his or her name and address or to produce his or her licence
for examination, refuses to do so;

(aa) in the case of an offence under section 83 of failing to comply with a traffic
direction given by a police officer in uniform;
(ab) in the case of any offence involving obstruction of a road by a motor vehicle
if the driver of the vehicle refuses to move the vehicle when requested to do
so by a police officer in uniform;
(b)

in any other case, where a person upon request refuses to give his or her
name or address; or

(c)

where there are reasonable grounds for suspecting that a person has given a
false name or address.

(3) Subject to subsection (4), any member of the Police Force may arrest without
warrant any person whom he or she sees driving or suspects on reasonable grounds to
have been driving a motor vehicle and whom he or she knows has never been issued a
licence.
(4) This section does not apply to a person who has failed or refused to produce his or
her licence when requested to do so by a member of the Police Force.

### 113A.

Prerequisite for arrests generally
No police officer may arrest any person unless he or she is wearing full police uniform
or suitably identifies himself or herself to the person whom he or she intends to arrest.

### 114.

Giving false statement
Any person who makes any statement which to his or her knowledge is false or in any
material respect misleading in connection with—
(a)

an application for a motor vehicle licence;

(b)

an application for the registration of a motor vehicle;

(c)

an application for a driving licence;

(d)

the endorsement of a driving licence;

(e)

any change or correction in a motor vehicle licence;

(f)

any change or correction in the registration of a motor vehicle; or

(g)

the giving of any information lawfully demanded or required under this Act,
the regulations or an Order made under this Act,

commits an offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine of one thousand
dollars or to imprisonment for twelve months, or to both such fine and imprisonment.

### 115.

Duplicate certificates, licences etc.
Any person or authority having power under this Act to issue any certificate, licence
or other document shall, on proof to his or her satisfaction that the certificate, licence or
other document has been lost, destroyed, defaced, mutilated or rendered illegible, and on
payment of the prescribed fee, issue a duplicate of the certificate, licence or other
document to the person entitled thereto.

### 116.

Liability of driver and owner of vehicle
Where an act committed by means of a motor vehicle is in contravention of this Act or
regulations or Orders made under this Act, the driver of the motor vehicle at the time the
act was committed and the owner of the vehicle shall both be held liable for the
contravention unless—
(a)

in the case of the driver, the offence was not due to any act, omission,
neglect or default on his or her part; and

(b)

in the case of the owner, the act was committed without his or her consent
and—
(i) was not due to any act or omission on his or her part, and
(ii) he had taken all reasonable precautions to prevent the commission
thereof.

### 117.

Power of entry
(1) Any person authorised in writing by the Licensing Authority may at all reasonable
times enter any premises for the purpose of—
(a)

ascertaining whether any vehicle is being kept on such premises for use in
contravention of this Act or regulations or Orders made under this Act; and

(b)

inspecting any vehicle on the premises.

(2) A person authorised in writing by the Licensing Authority may seize any vehicle
or part thereof or any equipment or accessory of a vehicle which the person reasonably
believes is required as evidence of an offence under this Act or the regulations and retain
the same until the final disposal of the proceedings relating to the offence. If any vehicle
or thing is seized under this subsection, the person seizing it must give a written receipt to
the owner of it and must return the vehicle or thing to the owner on the final disposal of
the proceedings unless a court otherwise orders pursuant to this Act.

### 118.

Motor vehicle deemed to be kept for use
Every motor vehicle and trailer shall, in all proceedings under this Act, be deemed,
until the contrary is proved, to be kept by the owner thereof for use.

### 119.

Power to require motor vehicles licensed according to maximum gross weight
to be weighed
(1) The responsible person of a load of a vehicle or combination shall—
(a)

on the request of a police officer, satisfy the police officer that the load does
not contravene the licence, this Act or any regulation or Order;

(b)

where required by the police officer referred to in paragraph (a), drive the
vehicle or combination to the nearest weighbridge, or other machine for
weighing vehicles as authorised under the National Metrology Act,

## Chapter 205B, for the purpose of weighing the load.

(2) Any person who, upon being required pursuant to subsection (1) to proceed to the
nearest weighbridge, fails to do so commits an offence and shall be liable on summary
conviction to a fine of fifty thousand dollars if the person is a body corporate or
otherwise, a fine of five thousand dollars or to imprisonment for three months, or to both
such fine and imprisonment.
(3) The Licensing Authority, or any person authorised by the Licensing Authority, as
a precondition to granting or renewing a licence in respect of a motor vehicle or trailer,
may require such motor vehicle or trailer to be weighed by and at the expense of the
owner in the presence of a person authorised by the Licensing Authority.

### 120.

Non-application of section 58 to vehicles when used for certain purposes
Section 58 does not apply to a motor vehicle while it is being used—
(a)

by a member of the Fire Service for the purpose of proceeding to a fire in
answer to a fire alarm;

(b)

by a member of the Police Force while in the execution of his or her duty; or

(c)

as an ambulance,

if the observance of that section would be likely to hinder such use of the vehicle.

### 121.

General application
(1) This Act applies to persons in the service of the State, subject to section 120.
(2) Without prejudice to provisions of this Act specially saying that provisions of this
Act regarding motor vehicles shall apply to motor cycles, provisions of this Act regarding
motor vehicles shall apply generally to motor cycles, unless the contrary is specifically
expressed or is clearly required by the context.
(3) Notwithstanding provisions in this Act or regulations made hereunder or
subsidiary legislation made under the Motor Vehicles and Road Traffic Act prohibiting
the parking of vehicles in any place or area, goods vehicles and trucks shall be allowed a
reasonable time, not being ordinarily more than twenty minutes in any hour, to load and
offload goods wherever this is necessary in any place or area including a no-parking place
or area.

### 122.

Regulations
(1) The Minister may make regulations respecting—
(a)

the prescribing of anything that is by this Act required or authorised to be
prescribed; unless some other person or authority is specifically required or
authorised by this Act to do the prescribing; or unless prescribed by this Act
itself;

(b)

the forms to be used under this Act;

of vehicle;

(g)

the inspection of any kind of vehicle and the time, place and manner of

(v) the taking up and setting down of passengers,
(vi) the carriage of goods or luggage,
(vii) the maximum hours of employment of drivers and conductors,
(viii) the issue, cancellation and suspension of licences of conductors, and

and limited permission to operate car rental services and the

(r)

the removal from roads, and safe custody of, vehicles which have broken

(s)

safety, control and regulation of traffic and the use of vehicles or any class or

(2) The Commissioner of Police may make regulations respecting—
(a)

other than the Minister conflicts with subsidiary legislation made under this Act by the
Minister, the subsidiary legislation made by the Minister shall prevail.

### 123.

General power of delegation
Where any power is by any provision of this Act conferred upon the Licensing
Authority, or the Commissioner of Police, he may delegate such power to any member of
the Police Force.

### 124.

Reward regarding fines and penalties collected
Where any fine or penalty is imposed and collected under this Act—
(a)

seventy per cent thereof shall be placed on deposit in the Treasury in the
consolidated Police Reward Fund; and

(b)

thirty per cent thereof shall be placed on deposit in the Treasury in the Police
Reward Fund established by section 46 of the Police Act, Chapter 244.

### 125.

Repeal
The Motor Vehicles and Road Traffic Act, Chapter 194, is hereby repealed.

### 126.

Effect of existing regulations
(1) The Motor Vehicles and Road Traffic Regulations, SROs 151 to 158, 1958
Revised Laws, and all other regulations, Orders or directions made, or purported to have

been made, by whomsoever, under or otherwise having effect under the Motor Vehicles
and Road Traffic Act, Chapter 194, and in force or purporting to be in force at the
commencement of this Act shall be deemed to have been duly made under this Act and
shall remain in force until otherwise provided but such existing subsidiary legislation
shall be construed with such modifications and adaptations as may be necessary to bring
it into conformity with this Act.
(2) Where any matter that falls to be prescribed or otherwise provided for under this
Act by subsidiary legislation made by any functionary, authority or person, is prescribed
or otherwise provided for by or under subsidiary legislation made or purported to be
made under the Motor Vehicles and Road Traffic Act by any functionary, authority or
person, that prescription or provision shall, as from the commencement of this Road
Traffic Act, Chapter 289A, have effect as if it had been duly made under this Road traffic
Act, Chapter 289A, by the proper functionary, authority or person until otherwise
prescribed or provided but such existing prescription or provision shall be construed with
such modifications and adaptations as may be necessary to bring it into conformity with
this Act.
(3) Any reference in any existing law to Motor Vehicles and Road Traffic
Regulations, Motor Vehicles and Road Traffic Orders, or Motor Vehicles and Road
Traffic directions as mentioned in subsection (1) may be cited respectively as the Road
Traffic Regulations, Road Traffic Orders and Road Traffic directions.

### 127.

Commencement
This Act shall come into force on such day as the Governor-General may appoint by
Proclamation published in the Gazette.

ROAD TRAFFIC (LIGHT SIGNALS) REGULATIONS

[SRO 1 of 1997.]

[3rd January, 1997.]
1\. Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Road Traffic (Light Signals) Regulations.
2\. Definition
In these Regulations, “traffic sign” means any object or device (whether fixed or
portable) for conveying to traffic on a road or to any particular class of traffic, warnings,
information, requirement, restrictions or prohibitions of any description and a reference to
the erection or placing of a traffic sign includes a reference to the display thereof in any
manner whatsoever (whether or not involving fixing or placing).
3\. Erection of light signals
The Licensing Authority may install or cause to be installed light signals, whether
mechanically or electronically operated, for the control of vehicular traffic, and the
Authority may cause to be placed or painted on the carriage way, a stop line or a
directional sign to be used in conjunction with such light signals.
4\. Light signals
(1) The light signals to be used to control vehicular traffic shall be of the number,
colour, size and type prescribed by subregulation (2) or (3).
(2) The number, colour, size and type of light signals for the purposes of these
Regulations shall be as follows—
(a)

three lights shall be used, one red, one amber, and one green;

(b)

the lamps showing the lights referred to in paragraph (a) shall be arranged
vertically, the lamp showing a red light being uppermost and that showing a
green light the lowermost;

(c)

each lamp shall be separately illuminated and the effective diametre of the
lens thereof shall be not less than seven and one-half inches nor more than
eight and one-half inches;

(d)

the height of the centre of the green lens from the surface of the carriageway
in the immediate vicinity shall be, in the case of signals placed at the side of
the carriageway or on a street refuge, not less than six and one-half feet nor
more than eleven and one half feet and in the case of signals placed
elsewhere and over the carriageway not less than sixteen feet nor more than
twenty feet;

(e)

the centres of the lenses shall be not more than fourteen inches;

(f)

the sequence of the light signal show for the purpose of controlling vehicular
traffic shall be as follows—
(i) red,
(ii) green,
(iii) amber.

(3) The light signals prescribed by this subregulation as follows—
(a)

two lamps each showing an intermittent red light shall be used;

(b)

the lamp shall be arranged horizontally so that there is a distance of not less
than nineteen and one-half inches nor more than twenty-eight inches
between the centre of the lenses of the lamp;

(c)

each lamp shall be separately illuminated and the effective diametre of the
lens thereof shall be not less than seven and one-half inches nor more than
eight and one-half inches;

(d)

when the signal is operated, each lamp shall show its intermittent red light at
a rate of flashing of not less than fifty-four nor more than sixty-six flashes
per minute, and in such a Manner that the light of one lamp is always shown
at a time when the light of the other lamp is not shown;

(e)

the height of the centre of each lens from the surface of the carriageway in
the immediate vicinity shall be, in the case of the carriageway or on a street
refuge, not less than six and one-half feet nor more than eleven and one-half
feet and, in the case of signals placed elsewhere and over the carriageway,
not less than sixteen feet nor more than twenty feet.

(4) Light signals prescribed by this Regulation shall be of the colour and type shown
in the diagram in the Schedule.
5\. Variations
(1) A lens of the size and colour shown in the diagram in the Schedule which when
illuminated shows a green arrow—
(a)

may be added to or substituted for, a lens showing the green light in the light
signal referred to in regulation 4(2) in any of the methods shown in the
diagrams in the Schedule; or

(b)

may be affixed to the light signal, referred to in regulation 4(2) or to those
signals as altered in accordance with paragraph (a) in any of the methods
shown in the diagram in the Schedule.

(2) The direction in which the arrow in the diagram in the Schedule points may be
varied so as to be—
(a)

a direction that lies straight upright; or

(b)

a direction that lies at any angle between ninety degrees either to the left or
to the right or the upright direction.

(3) The direction in which any arrow in any of the diagrams in the Schedule points
may be varied so as to be—
(a)

a direction that lies straight upright; or

(b)

a direction that lies between the direction in the diagram showing the arrow
and the upright direction.

(4) Where a lens showing a green light is varied in accordance with this Regulation,
the associated lens showing red and amber lights, may be varied with red and amber
arrows respectively, in which case subregulations (1) to (3) of the regulation shall apply,
mutatis mutandis.
6\. Portable light signals
(1) Portable light signals may be used for the control of vehicular traffic—
(a)

where, by reason of roadworks being in progress or for some other reason,
the width of the carriageway is temporarily restricted so that only one line of
traffic is possible; or

(b)

during the progress of temporary schemes of traffic control, the signals are in
the control of the Police.

(2) Where portable lights being used pursuant to subregulation (1)—
(a)

two lamps showing coloured lights shall be used, one red and the other
green;

(b)

the lamps showing the coloured lights shall be arranged vertically, the lamp
showing the red light being uppermost;

(c)

each lamp shall be separately illuminated and the effective diametre of the
lens thereof shall be not less than seven and one half inches nor more than
eight and one-half inches;

(d)

the light of the centre of the green lens from the surface of the carriageway
in the immediate vicinity shall be not less than five feet nor more than eleven
and one-half feet;

(e)

the red and green lights shall not be shown together and one shall follow the
other without any appreciable interval of time.

7\. Significance of light signals
(1) The significance of the light signals prescribed by regulation 4 or 6 shall be as
follows—
(a)

a red signal is a prohibition that vehicular traffic shall not be permitted to
proceed beyond the stop line on the carriageway provided in conjunction
with the signals or, if that line is not for the time being visible or there is no
such line, beyond the signals;

(b)

a green signal indicates that vehicular traffic may be permitted to pass the
signal and proceed straight on or to the left or to the right;

(c)

an amber signal is a prohibition that vehicular traffic shall not be permitted
to proceed beyond the stop line or, if that line is not for the time being
visible or there is no such line, beyond the signals, except in the case of any
vehicle which, when the signal first appears is so close to the line or signals,
that it cannot safely be stopped before passing the line or signals.

(2) The significance of the light signals prescribed by regulation 4(2), as varied in
accordance with regulation 5, is as follows—
(a)

subject as provided in subregulation (2)(d) a red signal is a prohibition that
vehicular traffic shall not proceed beyond the stop line on the carriageway
provided in conjunction with the signals or, if the stop line is not for the time
being visible, beyond the signals;

(b)

a green signal (where a green signal is provided) indicates that vehicular
traffic may pass the signals and proceed straight on or to the left or to the
right;

(c)

an illuminated green arrow indicates that vehicular traffic may be permitted
to pass the signals and proceed in the direction indicated by the arrow
notwithstanding any other indication given by the signals;

(d)

an amber signal is a prohibition that vehicular traffic shall not be permitted
to proceed beyond the stop line, or, if the stop line is not visible, beyond the
signal, except in the case of any vehicle which when the signal first appears
is so close to the said line or signals that it cannot safely be stopped before
passing the line or signals.

(3) Vehicular traffic passing light signals shall be driven or propelled with due regard
to the safety of other users of the road and subject to the direction of any member of the
Police Force in uniform, engaged in the regulation of traffic.
(4) The significance of the light signals prescribed by regulation 4(3) is that the
intermittent red lights convey the prohibition that vehicular traffic shall not be permitted
to proceed beyond the stop line on the carriageway provided in conjunction with the
signals or, if the line is not for the time being visible, beyond the signals; except in the
case of any vehicle which, when the lights first begin to flash, is so close to the line or
signals that it cannot safely be stopped before passing the line or signals.
(5) For the purposes of this Regulation and regulation 3 the expression “stop line”
means the traffic sign placed or painted on the carriageway to be used in conjunction with
light signals.
8\. Light signals for pedestrians
(1) Light signals for conveying to pedestrians the information mentioned in
subregulation (3) may be used facing across the carriageway in conjunction with the light
signals prescribed by regulation 4(2) if those light signals as varied in accordance with
regulation 5; and if so used, shall be of the size, colour and type shown in the Schedule.
(2) The height of the lower edge of the container enclosing light signals from the
surface of the carriageway in the immediate vicinity shall be not less than six and onehalf feet nor more than eight feet and shall be so designed that—
(a)

the red figure shown in the Schedule (in this Regulation referred to as “the
red signal”) can be internally illuminated by a steady light;

(b)

a green figure shown in the Schedule (in this Regulation referred to as “the
green signal”) can be internally illuminated by a steady light or by a flashing
light, flashing at a rate of not less than fifty-four nor more than one hundred
and twenty-five flashes per minute; and

(c)

when one signal is illuminated the other signal is not illuminated.

(3) The period during which, in the interest of safety—
(a)

pedestrians should not cross the carriageway, shall be shown by the red
signal during such time as it is illuminated;

(b)

pedestrians should cross the carriageway, shall be shown by the green signal
during such time as it is illuminated by the steady light;

(c)

pedestrians should not start to cross the carriageway, shall be shown by the
green signal during such time as it is illuminated by the flashing light.

9\. Prohibition regarding light signals
No light signals, other than light signals erected or placed by the Licensing Authority,
shall be erected or placed on or near any road.
10\. Offence
(1) All drivers of motor vehicles shall obey the light signal in the same manner as a
traffic sign.
(2) A driver of a motor vehicle who disobeys or fails to comply with a light signal
commits an offence and shall be punishable under section 111 of the Road Traffic Act,

ROAD TRAFFIC (BUS ROUTES) REGULATIONS
[SRO 11 of 2003.]

[14th April, 2003.]
1\. Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Road Traffic (Bus Routes) Regulations.
2\. Definitions
In these Regulations—
“bus route” or “route” means one of the routes to be followed by the drivers of
buses as specified in the First Schedule;
“subroute” means when different routes follow the same road then branch off to a
different destination.

3\. Prohibition regarding bus routes
(1) Subject to subregulations (2) and (3) of this Regulation and regulation 14(2) no
person may operate a motor omnibus for the transportation of persons for reward on a
route except under and in accordance with the conditions of a route sticker issued to the
person by the Licensing Authority.
(2) A motor omnibus may operate on any route on hire when prominently displaying
an “On Hire” sign in addition to the sticker on the front of the bus under the following
situations—
(i) the motor omnibus is on hire to a particular person or group of persons,
(ii) the motor omnibus is transporting staff to and from one particular place of
employment,
(iii) the motor omnibus is travelling from its home base to the point of
commencement of its route between the hours of 5:00 a.m. and 8 a.m.
(3) The motor omnibus may traverse any route on a Bank Holiday not for hire by
prominently displaying an “Off Duty” or “On Hire” sign on the front of the bus.
4\. Application for route sticker
(1) An application for a route sticker must be in the form prescribed in the Second
Schedule.
(2) An application under this Regulation must be accompanied by evidence of the
payment of the appropriate fee as prescribed in the Third Schedule.
(3) The application fee paid pursuant to regulation 4(2) is non-refundable.
(4) In respect of motor omnibus already in operation an application for a route sticker
may be made by a Bus Association on behalf of any or all of its members, provided each
member is named in and signs the application.
(5) Any person whether or not a member of a bus association may apply for a route
sticker individually.
5\. Requirements to be satisfied by applicant and driver
(1) An applicant for a route sticker must—
(a)

be a citizen of Grenada or a company incorporated under the laws of
Grenada;

(b)

prove to the satisfaction of the Licensing Authority that the applicant owns
or is in the process of purchasing a motor omnibus.

(2) Every driver of a motor omnibus must—
(a)

have attended and completed a defensive driving course conducted or
approved by the Licensing Authority;

(b)

be licensed to drive a public service vehicle;

(c)

be of good character and have attained the age of twenty-five years and have
at least five years’ driving experience.

(3) (a) The requirement in regulation 5(2)(a) does not apply until twelve months after
the commencement of these Regulations.
(b) The requirement of age and experience in regulation 5(2)(c) does not apply to
persons lawfully driving motor omnibuses at the commencement of these Regulations.
6\. Determination of application

(1) The Licensing Authority must determine an application within forty-five days
after receiving it duly completed except that for six months after the commencement of
these Regulations the period is three months.
(2) If the Licensing Authority refuses an application, the reason for the refusal must
be made known to the applicant in writing within fourteen days after the period of fortyfive days (or three months) mentioned in regulation 6(1).
7\. Consultation by Authority
(1) The Licensing Authority may, when considering an application for a route sticker,
consult the relevant Bus Association or other like body which has an interest in the route
applied for.
(2) Any opinion expressed by a Bus Association or other body regarding the
application must be considered by the Authority, but is not binding on it.
8\. Issue of route sticker
(1) If the Licensing Authority approves an application to operate a route it must, on
payment of the appropriate fee as prescribed in the Third Schedule, issue a route sticker
subject to the conditions specified in regulation 10 and keep a corresponding Route
Certificate on file as part of the Authority’s records.
(2) A sticker issued under regulation 8(1) will be 50.8 mm by 20.32 mm in dimension
and of the colour specified in the fourth column of the First Schedule.
(3) A route certificate for the purposes of this Regulation must be in the form
prescribed in Form 2 of the Second Schedule and must set out the following—
(a)

the name of the route sticker holder;

(b)

the registration number, engine number and chassis number of the motor
omnibus to which the route sticker relates; except where the applicant is in
the process of purchasing a motor omnibus and this information is
unavailable in which case the information must be inserted prior to the
commencement of operation;

(c)

the route in respect of which the sticker is granted.

9\. Duration of route sticker
(1) A route sticker issued under regulation 8 remains valid unless it is relinquished by
the permit holder or revoked or the yearly renewal fee is not paid by the required
deadline.
(2) A route sticker issued by the Licensing Authority is renewable on payment of the
appropriate fee as prescribed in the Third Schedule.
10\. Conditions of route sticker
(1) Every route sticker to operate a motor omnibus on a route is subject to the
following conditions—
(a)

it is non-transferable but the motor omnibus to which the route sticker relates
can be changed or replaced;

(b)

the motor omnibus to which the sticker relates must be maintained in good
physical and mechanical condition to the satisfaction of the Licensing
Authority;

(c)

the motor omnibus must operate on the route for the duration of the route
sticker;

(d)

a photo I.D. of the driver(s) meeting the requirement of the Road Traffic
Act, Chapter 289A, and these Regulations and issued by the Licensing
Authority must be prominently displayed in the bus at all times except that
where this is not possible because of an emergency, the licensing authority
must be notified and a photo I.D. must be obtained and displayed after one
working day;

(e)

the motor omnibus must not be used for tourism tours unless permitted to do
so by the Authority and the Board of Tourism; but a tourist can be
transported as a passenger on a route for the prescribed fare between any
authorised stopping points on that route;

(f)

every driver of the motor omnibus must while driving or in charge of the
omnibus comply with regulations as to dress and conduct himself or herself
in a seemly manner;

(g)

the holder of the route sticker must make every effort to ensure that the
motor omnibus to which it relates operates after peak hours or on bank
holidays if so scheduled to do by the Licensing Authority or the relevant Bus
Association pursuant to regulation 16;

(2) (a) any modification or addition to the motor omnibus or its equipment must
conform to the Road Traffic Act, any regulations made or saved under or by the Act, and
any other relevant enactment;
(b) the motor omnibus must be kept insured and licensed at all times;
(c) except in compliance with section 68A of the Road Traffic Act, Chapter 289A,
an amplified stereo or hi-fi system must not be operated on the motor omnibus;
(d) only a driver who conforms to regulation 5(2) of these Regulations and whose
driving of the motor omnibus will not invalidate any policy of insurance in respect of the
motor omnibus issued under the Motor Vehicles (Third Party Risks) Act may drive the
motor omnibus;
(e) the holder of the route sticker must not permit the motor omnibus to which it
relates to be driven by a person whom he or she knows has been convicted of—
(i) dangerous or reckless driving,
(ii) driving while under the influence of alcohol or a prohibited drug,
(iii) overloading a motor omnibus on more than one occasion.
11\. Revocation of route sticker
The Licensing Authority may at any time revoke a route sticker if the driver of the
motor omnibus to which the route sticker relates has been disqualified from driving and is
knowingly permitted to continue to drive the omnibus by the owner.
12\. Commencement of operations
A person to whom a route sticker is issued must commence to operate the route within
3 months after such issue, but the Licensing Authority may extend the period for a further
period not exceeding 3 months if it is satisfied that the holder of the route sticker has
made reasonable efforts to commence the operation of the route.
13\. Display of route sticker
(1) Subject to these Regulations the sticker issued by the Licensing Authority
indicating the route must be displayed at all times on the left top corner of the windscreen
of the motor omnibus while it is operating on the route.

(2) It is an offence for a motor omnibus to display a sticker in respect of a route
unless there is in force a route certificate for that omnibus in respect of that route.
(3) A route certificate may not be revoked unless the route sticker to which it relates
has been revoked.
14\. Prohibition regarding routes
(1) Subject to these Regulations, no driver of a motor omnibus may pick up
passengers on a route other than the route for which the sticker relating to that omnibus
has been issued.
(2) The driver of a motor omnibus may pick up passengers on another route being a
route to or from a special public event such as a sports meet or a celebration or festival as
defined by the Public Festivals Regulation Act, Chapter 262A, if notice of the intended
route has been given to the Licensing Authority at least 48 hours beforehand and the
authority approves after confirming that there are insufficient buses to service the route or
routes.
15\. Travelling procedures
(1) The first bus into the Terminus Stand or designated depot must be the first to
leave for a particular route or subroute unless it is unable to do so for some reason such as
breakdown of the first bus in line or any other valid reason.
(2) On every trip a bus must complete its route on the return or outward journey
unless the certificate allows otherwise.
16\. List of buses
Bus Associations may submit a list of buses on a rotation basis scheduled for plying
the various routes after peak hours and on bank holidays for approval by the Licensing
Authority in accordance with a schedule prepared by the Authority. If a list is not
submitted, the Licensing Authority may itself produce one.
17\. Offences
(1) A person who contravenes regulation 3 and any of the provisions of regulations
10(1), 13, 14 and 15 commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a
maximum fine of two thousand five hundred dollars for each offence or in default
imprisonment for three months.
(2) A person who contravenes any of the provisions of regulation 10(2) commits an
offence and is liable on summary conviction to a maximum fine of two thousand five
hundred dollars for each offence or imprisonment for three months or to both such fine
and imprisonment or such greater penalty imposed under the Road Traffic Act,

## Chapter 289A, for the offence.

18\. Amendment of regulation 49
Regulation 49 of the Motor Vehicles and Road Traffic Regulations (motor omnibus to
carry conductor) is repealed and replaced by the following—
49\. “Motor omnibus may carry conductor
A motor omnibus when in use on any road may carry a conductor in
addition to a driver and if a conductor is carried the regulations relating to a
conductor apply.”.

The Road Traffic (Bus Routes) Regulations, 2002 (SRO 2 of 2002) are repealed.

ROAD TRAFFIC (SEAT BELT) ORDER

[SRO 10 of 2005.]

[15th April, 2005.]
1\. Citation
This Order may be cited as the Road Traffic (Seat Belt) Order.
2\. Definitions
In this Order—
“anchorage point” means that part of a motor vehicle which is designed to hold a
seat belt securely in position on that motor vehicle;
“motor vehicle” means a private motor car, a public service vehicle and a goods
vehicle;
“seat belt assembly” means an assembly of straps with quick release coupling
devices, adjusting devices and an attachment and deployment system which deploys
automatically, requires no action by the occupant, is anchored to a motor vehicle and
is intended to be worn by an occupant of that motor vehicle.
3\. Type of seat belt
Every seat belt shall be of a type the designation of which shall be any of the
following—
(a)

a lap belt which is a seat belt assembly that is anchored at two anchorage
points and passes across the front of the wearer’s pelvic region from one side
of the hip to the other side;

(b)

a diagonal belt which is a seat belt assembly, that is anchored at two
anchorage points and passes diagonally across the front of the wearer’s chest
from the hip to the opposite shoulder;

(c)

a three point belt which is a seat belt assembly, that is anchored at three
anchorage points and passes across the front of the wearer’s pelvic region
and across the front of the chest from the hip to the opposite shoulder.

4\. Standard of seat belt
(1) Every seat belt assembly shall have legibly and permanently affixed to it a label
showing a mark of certification which shall include one or more of the following—
(a)

the Grenada Bureau of Standards (GBS) Standard Mark;

(b)

the Japanese Industrial Standard Mark (JIS);

(c)

an indication of conformity to the United States of America Federal Motor
Vehicle Safety Standard;

(d)

an approved European Economic Community Mark (EEC);

(e)

an approved Economic Commission for Europe Mark (ECE);

(f)

the British Standard Institute Mark (BSI);

(g)

the Australian Standard Mark; or

(h)

any other mark approved by the Grenada Bureau of Standards.

(2) The label referred to in subregulation (1) shall also have legibly and permanently
marked on it either the name of the manufacturer of the seat belt or his or her trade mark
and his or her country of origin.
