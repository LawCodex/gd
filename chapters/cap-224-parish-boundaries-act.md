## CHAPTER 224

PARISH BOUNDARIES ACT
An Act to define and declare the boundaries of the several parishes.
[19th August, 1908.]

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Parish Boundaries Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
For the purposes of this Act the term “Central Boundary Line” means an imaginary
line commencing at an iron beacon erected on the Qua Qua Peak of the Grand Etang
Main Ridge and bearing from such iron beacon in a north-easterly direction to a similar
iron beacon erected on the St. Catherine Peak and extending thence to a stone pillar
erected on the highest peak in the St. Mark’s Mountains north of Mount St. Catherine.

### 3.

Boundaries of various parishes
The respective parishes of Grenada shall be deemed to comprise all lands contained
within and surrounded by the boundary line hereinafter defined or described in respect of
each of the said parishes, that is to say—
(1) The parish of St. George shall comprise all lands included within a boundary line
extending from the mouth of the river Chemin along its bed or channel to its source at the
foot of the Mount Maitland Ridge; and thence direct to the top of the said ridge and along
the ridge in a north-easterly direction to a stone pillar on Mount Sinai; and thence along
the Grand Etang Main Ridge to the iron beacon erected on the Qua Qua Peak at the
southern end of the Central Boundary Line; and thence in a westerly direction along the
ridge of the Woodford Hills through a stone pillar erected on the same ridge, to the source
of the river Douce at the foot of the said hills; and thence along the bed of the river Douce
to its mouth where it empties itself into the sea; and thence along the sea-board to the
place of commencement of the line aforesaid.
(2) The parish of St. David shall comprise all lands included within a boundary line
extending from the mouth of the river Chemin along its bed or channel to its source at the
foot of the Mount Maitland Ridge; and thence direct to the top of the said ridge and along
the ridge in a north-easterly direction to a stone pillar on Mount Sinai; and thence along
the ridge extending from Mount Sinai in a north-easterly direction to the Mount Lebanon

Peak; and thence direct to the source of the river Crochu; and thence along the bed or
channel of the river Crochu to its mouth where it empties itself into the sea; and thence
along the sea-board to the place of commencement of the line aforesaid.
(3) The parish of St. Andrew shall comprise all lands included within a boundary line
extending from the mouth of the river Crochu along its bed or channel to its source; and
thence direct to the Mount Lebanon Peak; and thence along the ridge extending therefrom
in a south-westerly direction to the stone pillar on Mount Sinai; and thence in a northerly
direction along the Grand Etang Main Ridge to the iron beacon on the Qua Qua Peak at
the southern end of the Central Boundary Line; and thence along the whole length of the
Central Boundary Line to the stone pillar erected on the highest peak of the St. Mark’s
Mountains at the northern end thereof; and thence from the said stone pillar direct to the
source of the river Antoine; and thence along the bed or channel of the said river Antoine
to its mouth where it empties itself into the sea; and thence along the sea-board to the
place of commencement of the line aforesaid.
(4) The parish of St. Patrick shall comprise all lands included within a boundary line
extending from the mouth of the river Antoine along its bed or channel to its source; and
thence direct to the stone pillar erected on the highest peak of the St. Mark’s Mountains at
the northern end of the Central Boundary Line; and thence from the said pillar in a
northerly direction to the source of the river Duquesne; and thence along the bed or
channel of the river Duquesne to a point 350 yards from its mouth where a stone pillar is
erected on its northern bank; and thence northwards to that part of the sea-board known as
the northern end of the Duquesne beach, at or near which part of the sea-board another
stone pillar is erected; and thence along the sea-board to the place of commencement of
the line aforesaid.
(5) The parish of St. Mark shall comprise all lands included within a boundary line
extending from the stone pillar erected at or near that part of the sea-board known as the
northern end of the Duquesne beach in a southerly direction to the stone pillar erected on
the northern bank of the river Duquesne at a point 350 yards from its mouth; and thence
along the bed or channel of the river Duquesne to its source; thence direct to the stone
pillar on the highest peak of the St. Mark’s Mountains at the northern end of the Central
Boundary Line; and thence in a southerly direction along the Central Boundary Line to a
stone pillar thereon erected to the south of the iron beacon on the St. Catherine Peak; and
thence from the said stone pillar in a westerly direction to a stone pillar erected on the
ridge of the Mount Cenis Mountains; and thence direct from the last-mentioned pillar to
the source of the Sarah Hall river; and thence along the bed of the Sarah Hall river to its
mouth where it empties itself into the sea; and thence along the sea-board to the place of
commencement of the line aforesaid.
(6) The parish of St. John shall comprise all lands included within a boundary line
extending from the mouth of the Sarah Hall river along its bed or channel to its source at
the foot of the Mount Cenis Hills; and thence direct to the stone pillar erected on the ridge
of the Mount Cenis Mountains; and thence along the ridge to the stone pillar erected on the
Central Boundary Line to the south of the beacon on the St. Catherine Peak; and thence
from the last-mentioned stone pillar in a southerly direction along the Central Boundary
Line to the iron beacon erected on the Qua Qua Peak at the southern end of the Central
Boundary Line; and thence in a westerly direction, along the ridge of the Woodford Hills
and through or over a stone pillar erected on the same ridge, to the source of the river
Douce at the foot of the said hills; and thence along the bed of the river Douce to its
mouth where it empties itself into the sea; and thence along the sea-board to the place of
commencement of the line aforesaid.
