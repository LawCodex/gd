## CHAPTER 297

SAVINGS BANK ACT
An Act relating to the Government Savings Bank.
[Amended by Act No. 30 of 1969.]

[1st January, 1936.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Savings Bank Act.
2\. Interpretation
In this Act—
“depositor” includes a person or body of persons whether corporate or not, for
whose benefit a deposit is made and a person entitled to a deposit as personal
representative of a deceased depositor;
“Permanent Secretary” means the Permanent Secretary (Finance);

“prescribed” means prescribed by rules under this Act;
“revenue” of the Savings Bank does not include monies received on deposit.
3\. Appointment of Savings Bank
The Savings Bank at the Treasury in the town of St. George, and the District Savings
Banks subsisting at the time when this Act comes into operation, shall be deemed to be
constituted and appointed under this Act.
4\. Management and control
Subject to the provisions of section 11 the Savings Bank shall be under the
management and control of the Permanent Secretary who may, subject to the provisions
of this Act and rules made thereunder, take such steps as may be desirable for the
encouragement of thrift, for the proper management of the Savings Bank, and otherwise
for the promotion of the objects and purposes of this Act.
5\. Establishment and closure of branch savings banks
The Permanent Secretary may, with the approval of the Minister, open branch savings
banks at any District Revenue Office and may, with the like approval, close any branch
savings bank.
6\. Employment of officers
The Permanent Secretary, with the approval of the Minister, may engage such officers
as may be necessary for the execution of this Act, and for that purpose may employ for
part of their time officers in the service of the Government.
7\. Deposits and repayments
Deposits of money to be paid into the Savings Bank shall be received and repaid under
such conditions as may be prescribed.
8\. Security of Government
The repayment of all monies deposited in the Savings Bank together with interest
thereon is guaranteed by the Government, and accordingly if at any time or times the
assets of the Savings Bank shall be insufficient to pay the lawful claims of every
depositor the Minister shall cause such deficiency to be met out of the Consolidated Fund,
and the Permanent Secretary shall certify such deficiency to the House of Representatives
without delay.
9\. Interest
(1) Interest shall be payable on deposits at the rate of four and one-half per cent per
annum, or at such other rate as may be fixed from time to time by the Minister with the
approval of the House of Representatives:
Provided that not less than three months’ notice of any change of rate shall be given
in the Gazette.
(2) Interest for every month shall be allowed on the minimum balance standing to the
credit of a depositor on any day of the month, but no interest shall be calculated on any
fraction of a dollar or on any amount standing to the credit of a depositor in excess of the
limit authorised by the rules.

(3) Interest on deposits shall, subject to the provisions of subsection (2), be calculated
to the 31st December in every year and shall then be added to and become part of any
principal money remaining on deposit.
10\. Salaries and expenses
(1) All expenses incurred in the execution of this Act shall be met from the monies in
the Savings Bank.
(2) For the purposes of this Act, “expenses” shall mean the cost of any work or
service done by or in connection with the Savings Bank, including such sum on account
of administrative and other overhead expenses as may, with the approval of the Minister,
be reasonably assigned to that work or service.
11\. Disposal of monies
(1) Subject to the provisions of this Act, monies in the Savings Bank shall not be
applied in any way to the purposes of the Government but, except so far as any sums may
be prescribed to be kept in hand for the general purposes of the Savings Bank, shall be
deposited in the Treasury in the town of St. George and shall, as far as practicable, be
invested on behalf of the Savings Bank under the direction of the Permanent Secretary in
such securities or be employed at interest in such manner as shall be approved from time
to time by the Minister:
Provided that not more than one-third of such monies shall at any time be or remain
invested in securities of the Government.
(2) Any sums of money that may from time to time be required for the repayment of
any deposit or deposits under the authority of this Act, or for the payment of interest
thereon or expenses incurred in the execution of this Act, may be raised by the sale of the
whole or a part of such securities:
Provided that any sums of money which may be required for the purposes aforesaid
may, with the approval of the Minister, be advanced to the Savings Bank by the
Permanent Secretary out of the Consolidated Fund until they can be raised by the sale of
such securities and such advances shall bear interest at the rate from time to time payable
to depositors.
12\. Accounts
Annual accounts of the revenue and expenditure of the Savings Bank and of deposits
received and repaid and interest credited to depositors during the year ending on the 31st
December together with a statement of the assets and liabilities of the Savings Bank shall,
after being audited and certified by the Director of Audit, be laid by the Permanent
Secretary before the House of Representatives not later than the 31st May ensuing in
every year and shall as soon as practicable thereafter be published in the Gazette.
13\. Surpluses and deficits
(1) If in any year the revenue of the Savings Bank shall be insufficient to defray the
interest due to depositors and all expenses under this Act, such deficiency shall be met
out of the Consolidated Fund.
(2) If in any year the revenue of the Savings Bank shall be more than sufficient to
defray the interest due to depositors and all expenses under this Act, then the Minister
may direct the transfer of the surplus or any portion thereof to the Consolidated Fund:
Provided that no such transfer shall be made unless the assets of the Savings Bank
will thereafter exceed the liabilities by not less than fifteen per cent of the liabilities to
depositors.

(3) If on the 31st December in any year the assets of the Savings Bank exceed the
liabilities by more than fifteen per cent of the liabilities to depositors then the Minister
may direct that the surplus over fifteen per cent or any portion thereof shall be transferred
to the Consolidated Fund.
14\. Power to make rules
(1) The Minister may make rules for the management and regulation of the Savings
Bank.
(2) In particular and without prejudice to the generality of the foregoing power such
rules may—
(a)

prescribe limits of deposits;

(b)

prescribe the modes of making deposits;

(c)

prescribe the modes of withdrawing deposits and interest;

(d)

prescribe the times at which deposit books shall be returned to the Savings
Bank by depositors;

(e)

regulate deposits by minors, guardians, trustees, married women, Friendly
Societies and other charitable bodies;

(f)

prescribe conditions for the withdrawal of monies by minors, guardians,
trustees, married women, Friendly Societies and other charitable bodies;

(g)

prescribe the modes of dealing with the deposits of deceased or insane
persons;

(h)

prescribe penalties not exceeding a fine of five thousand dollars for the
breach of any such rules;

(i)

provide for the forfeiture of deposits made in wilful contravention of this
Act;

(j)

authorise the Permanent Secretary to enter into an arrangement with the
Department for National Savings of the United Kingdom of Great Britain
and Northern Ireland or with the national savings bank of any British
Dominion, Colony, Protected State, or Protectorate, or any territory in
respect of which a mandate on behalf of the United Nations is being
exercised by Her Majesty, or any foreign country, for the transfer of any
sums standing to the credit of depositors from that Department or any such
savings bank to the Government Savings Bank, and vice versa, and prescribe
conditions for such transfers.

15\. Names of depositors, etc., not to be disclosed
(1) No person appointed to carry this Act into effect shall disclose the name of any
depositor or the amount which may have been deposited or withdrawn by any depositor
except in due course of law, or to such person or persons as may be appointed to assist in
carrying this Act into operation.
(2) A person contravening the provisions of this section shall on summary conviction
be liable to a fine of five thousand dollars.
16\. Settlement of disputes
If any dispute shall arise between the Permanent Secretary (or the officer managing
and controlling any branch savings bank) and any individual depositor in the Savings
Bank, or any executor, administrator, next-of-kin of a depositor, or any creditor or
assignee of a depositor who may become bankrupt or insolvent, or any person claiming to
be such executor, administrator, next-of-kin, creditor or assignee, or to be entitled to any

money deposited in such Savings Bank then, and in every such case, the matter in dispute
shall be referred to an arbitrator to be appointed by the Minister, and whatever award,
order or determination may be made by such arbitrator shall be binding and conclusive on
all parties, and shall be final to all intents and purposes, without any appeal.
17\. Non-liability of Government
When any payment is made or act done by the Permanent Secretary or any person
acting under his or her authority in accordance with this Act, and the rules for the time
being made thereunder, the Government, the Permanent Secretary and such person shall
not be liable in respect of any claim on the part of any person in connection with such
payment or act, but any person may nevertheless recover any sum lawfully due to him or
her from the person to whom the Permanent Secretary has paid the same.
18\. Saving
(1) All rules made under the Savings Bank Act, 1935 (No. 40 of 1935) shall be
deemed to have been made under the provisions of this Act and shall continue in force
until other provisions shall have been made by virtue of this Act.
(2) Rules made under section 14 of this Act shall have the same effect as if they were
contained in this Act.

SAVINGS BANK RULES
[Section 14.]

[4th January, 1937.]
1\. Citation
These Rules may be cited as the Savings Bank Rules.
2\. Business hours
The bank shall be open for the transaction of business during the ordinary office hours
of the Treasury and the several District Revenue Offices.

3\. Permanent Secretary (Finance)
“Permanent Secretary (Finance)” includes the Revenue Officer in charge of any
branch of the Savings Bank.
4\. Fee for passbook
A depositor, at the time of making a first deposit, shall pay the sum of ten cents for the
passbook.
5\. Full name required
On opening an account the depositor shall state his or her Christian name and
surname, his or her occupation and residence, and whether he or she is married or single.
He or she shall also, if able to write, sign his or her name in a book or on a card to be kept
by the Permanent Secretary (Finance) with a view to his or her identification when
repayment of deposits is applied for. He or she shall likewise make a declaration in such
form as may be approved by the Permanent Secretary (Finance) as to any other account
which he or she may have opened at the Savings Bank.
6\. Vouchers for deposits or withdrawals
(1) Each deposit or withdrawal shall be represented by a separate voucher in the form
annexed to this Part, or a form approved by the Minister, to be signed by the depositor or
by his or her agent in the case of deposits.
(2) At the time of deposit or withdrawal the depositor shall present his or her
passbook, and the necessary entry will then be made therein by the Permanent Secretary
(Finance). No deposit will be received or repaid unless the passbook is produced.
(3) Where the account is closed interest shall, notwithstanding anything in rule 12, be
added up to the end of the month preceding the month in which the account is closed.
7\. Examination of passbook by depositor
A depositor should carefully examine his or her passbook before leaving the office,
and ascertain that the entry is correct, and he or she should keep his or her book in his or
her own possession. A passbook is not a proper security for money lent. No claim by any
person holding a passbook in respect of a loan can be recognised by the Bank.
8\. Deposits by depositor’s agent
A depositor being unable to attend in person may deposit by his or her agent, who will
fill up and sign on his or her behalf the necessary voucher. The passbook must, however,
be produced by the agent in order that the deposit may be entered. In no circumstances
whatever will a separate receipt be given.
9\. As to withdrawals
In order to protect the depositor and to guard against fraud, repayments will as a rule
be made only to the depositor in person. Payments will, however, if the Permanent
Secretary (Finance) sees fit, be made to the depositor’s agent; but in all cases, whether the
depositor appears in person or not, the passbook shall be produced and a receipted
voucher signed by the depositor, and where the depositor is unknown to the paying
officer the signature of the depositor on the withdrawal voucher shall be witnessed by a
justice of the peace, a minister of religion, a public officer (other than the officer making
the payment), a non-commissioned officer of police, or other respectable person whose
signature is known to the Permanent Secretary (Finance).

10\. Depositor resident abroad
If a depositor is resident abroad, his or her signature to a withdrawal voucher shall be
verified by some constituted authority of the place in which he or she is residing, or
power of attorney must be produced, before payment will be made. When residing in a
foreign country, the signature of the British or Grenadian Consul will be required. The
signature of the person presenting the receipted voucher shall also be taken as witness to
the payment.
11\. Notice of withdrawal
The Permanent Secretary (Finance) may require ten days’ notice of a withdrawal
before paying it, but may, in his or her discretion, pay it either immediately or before the
expiration of the ten days:
Provided that the Permanent Secretary (Finance) may, in any case in which he or
she has reason to believe that the depositor has used the Savings Bank for a purpose for
which it was not intended, require sixty days’ notice of a withdrawal before paying it.
12\. Examination and comparison of Passbooks
(1) Every depositor shall once in each year, in the month of February, forward his or
her book to the Permanent Secretary (Finance) for examination and comparison with the
books of the office, and for the entry of any interest due to the 31st December previous.
(2) If a depositor fails to so forward his or her book and omits to do so within one
month after a written request therefor has been served at his or her residence the
Permanent Secretary (Finance) shall, if he or she thinks fit, debit the depositor’s account
with a sum not exceeding twenty-five cents, to meet the costs of the collection of the
book.
13\. Forms of vouchers
The forms of vouchers shall be issued for use by depositors from books with
counterfoils bearing consecutive printed numbers.
14\. Counterfoils of Vouchers
The counterfoils shall be in the forms annexed to this Part and shall be filled in by the
Permanent Secretary (Finance) and signed (in addition to the voucher) by the depositor if
he or she can write, or, in the case of deposits, it may be signed by his or her agent as
provided by rule 8.
15\. When depositor cannot write
If the depositor cannot write, the Permanent Secretary (Finance) shall fill in and sign
on the counterfoil the particulars required in such cases.
16\. Issue of new books in cases of loss
Should any depositor lose his or her book or desire a new book he or she shall make
application to the Permanent Secretary (Finance), stating fully the circumstances of his or
her case; and the Permanent Secretary (Finance) shall if there be good occasion, furnish a
new book to such depositor upon payment by him or her of twenty-five cents.
17\. Control and direction
The general control and direction of the Bank is vested in the Permanent Secretary
(Finance) who is responsible for their due and efficient working.

18\. Purpose of Savings Bank
The purpose of the Savings Bank is to provide a ready means for the deposit of
savings and so to encourage thrift. The Bank is not intended to be used for the purpose of
keeping a current account, and the Permanent Secretary (Finance) may close an account
should he or she have reason to believe that the depositor has used the Savings Bank for a
purpose for which it was not intended.
19\. Limits of deposit
The Permanent Secretary (Finance) shall not receive any deposit which makes the
amount standing to the credit of any depositor exceed five thousand dollars.
20\. No interest on deposits over limit
Interest shall be allowed in full on the principal amount standing to the credit of a
deposit account so long as such amount does not exceed five thousand dollars, but
whenever the amount standing to the credit of any account exceeds the limit of five
thousand dollars interest shall not be allowed on any sum in excess of such limit.
21\. Deposits by trustees
Deposits may be made by any person as trustee for another, and it shall not be an
objection that the trustee is himself a depositor.
22\. Payment to infants
If a deposit is received from a minor his or her receipt shall, notwithstanding his or her
infancy, be a good discharge for it or for any part of it or for any interest.
23\. Payment to married women
If a deposit is received from a woman who is married, or who afterwards marries, her
receipt shall, notwithstanding her coverture, be a good discharge for it or for any part of it
or for any interest, without her husband’s concurrence.
24\. Payments to trustees
The receipt of the trustee or treasurer in whose name any sums are invested on account
of any charitable institution, gift, or bequest, or the receipt of the survivors or survivor of
such trustees or treasurers, or of the executors or administrators of the sole or surviving
trustee or treasurer, shall be a good discharge to the Government for the sums so invested
and for the interest thereon. And the receipt of any trustee or of the survivors or survivor
of any trustee, or of the executors or administrators of the trustee or surviving trustee,
shall be a good discharge to the Government for all deposits and interest standing in the
name of such trustee or trustees.
25\. Payment for support of depositor becoming insane or incapacitated
If a depositor becomes insane, or otherwise, whether from illness or any other cause,
incapacitated from acting and his or her insanity or incapacity is proved, to the
satisfaction of the Permanent Secretary (Finance), by the certificate of a duly qualified
medical practitioner in the case of insanity, or by such certificate or otherwise in the case
of any other incapacity, and if the Permanent Secretary (Finance) is satisfied of the
necessity of the case, he or she may pay out of the money standing in the Bank to the
credit of the depositor such sums, for his or her care, maintenance, medical attendance,
and otherwise as he or she thinks reasonable; and the receipt of the person to whom the
payments are made shall be a good discharge to the Government for them.

26\. Payment of not more than two hundred and fifty dollars to next of kin of
depositor, without will or letters of administration
If a depositor dies entitled at the time of his or her death to money in a Savings Bank
not exceeding two hundred and fifty dollars inclusive of interest, and if the Permanent
Secretary (Finance) is satisfied that the depositor died intestate, and that no letters of
administration of his or her personal estate are likely to be taken out, the Permanent
Secretary (Finance) may, if he or she thinks fit, pay the money to the person who appears
to him or her to be entitled to the personal estate of the deceased depositor according to
law, or, if there be more such persons than one, to such persons in the shares to which
they are entitled by law. The payment shall be a good discharge to the Government from
all demands made by any person claiming as representative of or otherwise through or in
right of the deceased depositor; but nevertheless every such claimant shall have remedy
for recovery of any money paid as aforesaid against the person or persons who received
it.
27\. Payment under probate or letters of administration
Payment of any deposit according to any probate of a will or letters of administration
actually granted by the High Court and appearing to be in force shall discharge the
Government as against the executors and administrators of the deceased depositor and all
other persons, notwithstanding that the probate or letters of administration may be
revoked or be other wise invalid; but nevertheless any person claiming right to the money
shall have remedy against the person or persons who received it.
28\. Payment to natural relative of deceased
If a depositor dies intestate and without next-of-kin the Minister may, if he or she
thinks fit, subject to any rights of the wife or husband of the deceased direct the payment
of the money in any Savings Bank to which the depositor was entitled, or any part
thereof, to the person or amongst the persons who, if the depositor or some natural
relative of the depositor had been lawfully begotten, or if some natural child or remoter
issue of the depositor had been lawful issue, would be entitled to the personal estate of the
depositor according to the law regulating the distribution of the estates of intestates, or to
or among some one or more of such persons, in such shares as the Minister thinks fit; and
the Permanent Secretary (Finance) shall pay it accordingly.
