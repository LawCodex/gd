## CHAPTER 140

HOUSES OF REFUGE ACT
An Act to provide for the means of management of any establishment declared to be
a House of Refuge.
[2nd November, 1896.]

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Houses of Refuge Act.

### 2.

Power of Minister to make rules for Houses of Refuge
The Minister may make rules for the management of any establishment declared to be
a House of Refuge by the Minister, and for the admission, care and maintenance of poor
persons therein.

RICHMOND HOME RULES
[Section 2.]

[9th September, 1939.]

---

### 1.

Citation
These Rules may be cited as the Richmond Home Rules.
Official visitors

### 2.

The official visitors of the Richmond Home shall be the members of the Cabinet and
such other persons as the Minister may, from time to time, appoint.

### 3.

The official visitors may enter the Richmond Home at any time and inspect every part
thereof and the books and documents kept thereat. They may interrogate any officer or
inmate respecting the management of the institution, and may address the Minister on any
point connected therewith, and offer suggestions or remarks. Their visits shall be
recorded by them in the official visitors’ book.
Officers

### 4.

The officers of the Richmond Home shall be a Medical Superintendent and a
Managing Steward, to be appointed by the Minister, and such assistants, nurses, cooks,
and other servants as are from time to time sanctioned by the Minister and appointed by
the Medical Superintendent with the Minister’s approval. All officers other than the
Medical Superintendent and Managing Steward shall be paid monthly wages at such rates
as are fixed, from time to time, by the Minister.
Medical Superintendent

The Medical Superintendent shall visit the Richmond Home at least once a day and
oftener if necessary in cases of illness of inmates, and shall be responsible for the general
management and good order of the institution, for the enforcement of the rules, and for
the medical and surgical treatment of the inmates. His or her visits shall be recorded in
the journal of occurrences.
He or she shall examine every inmate upon admission, and shall thereupon cause to be
entered in the register of inmates such description of the case and such directions as to
diet and treatment as may be necessary.

### 7.

He or she shall regulate and determine in accordance with the prescribed scale the diet
of each sick inmate, recording his or her directions and any medical extras he or she may
consider necessary, in the medical journal.

### 8.

He or she shall frequently inspect the diet of the inmates and satisfy himself or herself
that it is of proper quantity, quality and description.

### 9.

He or she shall, subject to the general direction of the Chief Medical Officer, appoint
such servants of the Richmond Home, other than the Managing Steward, and at such
wages as are from time to time sanctioned by the Minister.

### 10.

He or she shall inquire into all complaints made by inmates and others against any
officers, and into all matters connected with the government of the Richmond Home and
shall, when necessary, take evidence thereon in writing.

### 11.

He or she shall when necessary, investigate the conduct of any inmate of the
Richmond Home, and, take such action as may be provided for under these Rules.

### 12.

If any inmate is guilty of grave misconduct, for the due punishment of which the
power conferred by the preceding rule is inadequate, he or she shall report the same to the
Chief Medical Officer.

### 13.

He or she shall furnish to the Chief Medical Officer on or before the 31st of January in
each year a report giving a full, but not discursive, account of the working of the
Richmond Home in the past year, with all necessary comparative statements and tables of
statistics to illustrate the same.
Managing Steward

The Managing Steward shall reside in such quarters adjacent to the Richmond Home
as may be provided for him or her by the Minister. Only such relatives or other persons as
are sanctioned by the Minister may reside with him or her in his or her quarters.
He or she shall be responsible under the Medical Superintendent for the management,
care, and supervision of the Richmond Home and its inmates. He or she shall not absent
himself or herself therefrom without leave of the Medical Superintendent, and all such
absences shall be recorded in his or her journal.

### 16.

He or she shall visit and inspect every part of the Richmond Home and see each
inmate at least once in every twenty-four hours, and in default of such inspections shall
state in his or her journal how far he or she has omitted them and the cause thereof.

### 17.

All subordinate officers of the Richmond Home shall be under his or her immediate
control and direction, and he or she shall report to the Medical Superintendent any neglect
of duty, insubordination, or irregularity on the part of any officer.

### 18.

He or she shall treat the inmates with kindness and consideration, and shall take care
that they are so treated by the officers.

### 19.

He or she shall report the sudden illness of any inmate without delay to the Medical
Superintendent, and shall take care that all directions for medical treatment of sick
inmates are carried out. A death shall be at once reported to the Medical Superintendent,
and the arrangements for the decent burial of dead inmates shall be carefully attended to
by him or her.

### 20.

All complaints made shall be reported by him or her to the Medical Superintendent,
and briefly recorded in his or her journal.

### 21.

He or she shall regularly superintend the issue of food, and shall take care that it is
wholesome and good, and in accordance with the prescribed scales of dietary. Only such
alcoholic stimulants or other medical extras as are directed by the medical officer in
writing are to be allowed to any inmate.

### 22.

He or she shall be responsible for all stores, clothing, etc., and for all deficiencies in
the same. He or she shall carefully study the requirements of the Richmond Home for the
purpose of promoting economy and preventing waste.
At the close of each quarter of the year he or she shall prepare and forward to the
Medical Superintendent a list of articles which he or she considers unfit for further use,

and shall dispose of such articles as directed after their examination by a Board of
Survey. In the first week of January in each year he or she shall take stock of all property
of the Richmond Home and prepare and post on the walls inventories thereof.
At the end of every month he or she shall forward to the Medical Superintendent, for
transmission to the Minister, a return showing the inmates admitted and discharged
during the preceding month.

### 25.

He or she shall keep the following books and such others as may, from time to time,
be prescribed by the Minister—
(a)

a register of inmates;

(b)

a journal of occurrences (including the entry of all punishments inflicted by
proper authority on inmates);

(c)

a provision store account;

(d)

a diet account;

(e)

a clothing and other stores account;

(f)

a general visitors’ book;

(g)

an official visitors’ book;

(h)

an expenditure book.

### 26.

He or she shall be responsible for the proper compounding, preparing, and dispensing of
all medicines at all hours of the day or night, according to the written prescriptions of the
Medical Superintendent, and for the due administration of medicines and medical extras
to patients.
Nurses and other servants

### 27.

The nurses and such other servants as are required by the Minister to do so shall reside
on the premises. No servant shall be absent from duty without leave of the Managing
Steward. If unable to attend duty, they shall at once report to him or her the fact and its
cause.

### 28.

They shall obey all directions in respect of their duties which they may, from time to
time, receive from the Medical Superintendent and the Managing Steward.

### 29.

The nurses shall, under the supervision and control of the Managing Steward attend to
the inmates of both sexes, administer the medicines prescribed, distribute the meals, and
attend to the clothing and bedding, taking care that these are properly changed, washed
and mended and that the wards are kept clean and tidy.

They shall treat the inmates with kindness and consideration, reporting any
insubordination or breach of order and decorum to the Managing Steward. They shall not
allow alcoholic stimulants to be given to or taken by any inmate, except under the written
order of the Medical Superintendent.
All servants shall be individually responsible to the Managing Steward for all articles
of equipment issued to them for use in their respective duties.

### 32.

Any servant may, for misconduct, or disobedience of these Rules or of instructions
given to him or her, be punished by the Medical Superintendent by fine, suspension or
dismissal with the approval of the Chief Medical Officer and subject to an appeal to the
Minister. All fines will be deducted from wages at the time of payment.

### 33.

Such officers as are so required by order of the Minister shall be allowed rations, as
part of their emoluments. Dietary shall thereupon be issued to them on the approved diet
scale.
Inmates

### 34.

No person shall be admitted as an inmate of the Richmond Home without the written
order of the Poor Relief Officer or the Medical Superintendent.

### 35.

No person shall be admitted to the Richmond Home who is suffering from any
contagious or infectious disease likely to prove dangerous to other inmates.

### 36.

No insane person shall be detained in the Richmond Home for a longer time than is
necessary for arranging his or her transfer to the Mental Hospital except a person who, in
the opinion of the Superintendent of the Mental Hospital, is fit for discharge from the
institution but whose relatives are unwilling to undertake responsibility for his or her
good conduct during the period of probation. Such a person may, at the discretion of the
Medical Superintendent of the Richmond Home, be transferred to the latter institution for
the period of probation prior to formal discharge.

### 37.

Every inmate shall, upon admission, be supplied with the authorised clothing for
inmates, and his or her own clothes shall (if worth it) be washed and kept by the
Managing Steward until his or her discharge. If unfit to be so kept, they may be destroyed
upon the authority of the Medical Superintendent.
All inmates shall be required to wash themselves, or be washed, when so directed by
the Medical Superintendent. The hair of male inmates shall be cut when necessary. The

hair of a female inmate may only be cut for medical reasons, and upon the written
authority in that case of the Medical Superintendent.
Inmates shall, when able to do so, keep their clothing and bedding clean and in order,
and shall render every assistance in their power to the servants in the routine work of the
Richmond Home when so required.

### 40.

They shall perform such work as may be certified by the Medical Superintendent to be
suited to their age, strength, or capacity.

### 41.

The inmates of the Richmond Home shall not be allowed to go temporarily beyond the
boundaries of that institution without the written authority of the nurse in charge which
shall specify the place to which the inmate may proceed, and the duration of leave so
granted.

### 42.

No inmates shall enter the kitchen, dispensary, office, storeroom, officers’ quarters, or
any other ward than that assigned to him or her, unless required to do so by an officer.
Male and female inmates may not go into each other’s wards without the nurse’s
permission.

### 43.

Inmates are to be decent and orderly in their conduct. Quarrelling, cursing, and
swearing, indecent language or conduct, disobedience of orders, or disrespect, or other
misconduct will render an inmate liable to punishment. For minor offences the Medical
Superintendent has authority to place an inmate on half rations or on punishment diet of
bread and water for a period not exceeding three days. One pound of bread per diem is to
be allowed for adults in such cases and ten ounces for juveniles.

### 44.

No dietary punishment shall be inflicted without the certificate of the Medical
Superintendent that such inmate is in a fit state of health to undergo such punishment.

### 45.

An inmate who has cause of complaint against any officer or another inmate is to state
it to the Managing Steward, who is required to investigate it and report when necessary to
the Medical Superintendent. An inmate may also appeal to the Medical Superintendent or
may apply to see an official visitor.

### 46.

Inmates are allowed to receive visitors on Sundays between 10 a.m. and 12 noon,
and/or between 3 p.m. and 5 p.m.; on other days from 3 p.m. to 5 p.m. The ministers of
their respective religious denominations may, however, visit them at all reasonable hours.
