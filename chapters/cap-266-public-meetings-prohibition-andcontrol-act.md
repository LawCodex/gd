## CHAPTER 266

PUBLIC MEETINGS PROHIBITION AND CONTROL ACT
An Act to render illegal certain practices indulged in at public meetings or other
gatherings; to empower the Minister to prohibit and control public meetings if
the interests of good order or public safety so require, and to provide for
connected matters.
[Amended by Act No. 10 of 1959.]

[24th October, 1959.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Public Meetings Prohibition and Control Act.
2\. Public meetings and other gatherings prohibited at which any immoral
behaviour or practices are indulged in
A public meeting or any other gathering of two or more individuals, whether indoors
or in the open air, at which any obscene or immoral behaviour or practices are indulged
in, or at which behaviour or practices are indulged in which tend to exercise a pernicious
or demoralising effect upon persons attending such meeting, is prohibited.

3\. Owner or occupier of any land or building and other persons having knowledge
of such meetings shall inform Chief of Police
(1) If it shall come to the knowledge of the owner or occupier of any land or building,
or of a person having control over or charge of any land or building, or of any magistrate,
Justice of the Peace or public officer or any police officer, that any meeting prohibited by
section 2 has been held, or is being held, or is to be held, he or she shall forthwith inform
the Chief of Police.
(2) Any person contemplated by the preceding subsection who fails so to inform the
Chief of Police, or who knowingly permits the holding of such a meeting on any land or
in any house over which respectively he or she has control, or of which he or she is in
charge, shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of three
thousand dollars and, in default of payment, to imprisonment for five months.
4\. No person to hold, attend or take part in, or to commit or cause to be committed,
any act of indecency or immorality at or near a prohibited meeting
Any person who—
(a)

holds, takes part in or attends; or

(b)

commits or causes to be committed or induces or persuades to be committed
any act of indecency or immorality at or near,

a meeting prohibited by section 2, shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary
conviction, to a fine of three thousand dollars and, in default of payment, to imprisonment
for five months.
5\. Police or Justice of the Peace may enter without warrant house or place where
prohibited meeting is being held and take names and addresses
(1) It shall be lawful for any party of police (of whom one at least shall be of or above
the rank of police corporal), or for any person acting upon the authority of a Justice of the
Peace, without a warrant to enter at any time of the day or night any house or place in
which there is good ground to believe or suspect that a meeting prohibited by section 2 is
being held and to take the names and addresses of all persons present at any such meeting
or house or place.
(2) It shall also be lawful for any magistrate, Justice of the Peace or police officer to
demand the names and addresses of persons taking part in a meeting in the open air which
he or she has good reason to believe is a meeting prohibited by section 2.
(3) Any person refusing to give his or her name and address when lawfully required
to do so under the authority of this section shall be liable to be arrested and to be detained
at the nearest police station until his or her identity can be established.
6\. Power of Minister to prohibit meetings and processions, etc.
(1) Notwithstanding anything contained in any other written law, where at any time
the Minister is satisfied that it is in the interests of public order or public safety so to do,
he or she may by Order, subject to any exemption contained therein, prohibit in any area
of Grenada or in any parish, district, village or town—
(a)

all meetings, gatherings and assemblies of persons, and all processions and
marches; and

(b)

all persons from organising, holding, speaking at or attending any meeting,
gathering or assembly of persons, and any procession or march,

in a public place, except under the authority of a permit issued pursuant to section 7.
(2) Every Order made under this section shall remain in force for a period of not more
than one month (without prejudice to the power of the Minister to issue a further such
Order at or before the end of the period).
7\. Permits for meetings, etc., otherwise prohibited
(1) If a person desires to organise or hold in a public place any meeting, gathering, or
assembly of persons, or any procession or march, which would be prohibited by an Order
made under section 6, he or she shall, at least twenty-four hours before the occurrence of
the desired event, make application for a permit to the Chief of Police, or to some other
police officer duly authorised to act on behalf of the Chief of Police, and the person to
whom application is made may grant or refuse the application.
(2) If the application is granted, the Chief of Police shall issue to the applicant a permit
authorising the desired event, but every such permit shall be issued subject to such terms
and conditions (to be observed by all persons organising, speaking at or attending the event)
as the Chief of Police may think necessary to give effect to the objects of the Order.
(3) If the application is refused or the applicant is aggrieved by the terms or
conditions imposed by the Chief of Police, the applicant may, within seven days of the
refusal of his or her application or, as the case may be, issue of the permit, make written
representation to the Minister concerning the matter who thereupon may, in his or her
discretion, grant the permit or vary the terms or conditions thereof, or he or she may
decline to act upon the representation.
8\. Offences against sections 6 and 7
(1) If any person—
(a)

organises, holds, speaks at, takes part in, or attends a meeting, gathering or
assembly of persons, or any procession or march, in contravention of an
Order made under section 6;

(b)

contravenes any requirement of the terms or conditions of a permit issued
pursuant to section 7;

(c)

attempts, incites, aids, or abets any such contravention mentioned in
paragraph (a) or (b),

he or she is guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to imprisonment for
three months and to a fine of three thousand dollars.
(2) Any police officer, and any person duly so authorised by the Minister, may arrest
without warrant any person found committing, or who has committed, an offence under
subsection (1).
