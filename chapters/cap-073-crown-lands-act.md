## CHAPTER 73

CROWN LANDS ACT
An Act relating to Crown Lands.
[2nd November, 1896.]

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Crown Lands Act.

### 2.

All public lands to be vested in the Governor-General
There shall henceforth be vested in the Governor-General for the public uses of
Grenada—
(a)

all those lands referred to, whether by description or otherwise, in section 2
of Chapter 78 of the 1958 Edition of the Laws of Grenada;

(b)

all other lands hitherto vested in the Governor or the Governor-General for
the public uses of Grenada.

### 3.

Meaning of “Crown Lands”
The lands vested in the Governor-General by section 2, and all other lands which may,
from time to time, be vested in the Governor-General for the public uses of Grenada, are
hereinafter called Crown Lands.

### 4.

Grants, sales and leases of Crown Lands
Subject to section 6(1) of the National Parks and Protected Areas Act the GovernorGeneral may, from time to time, grant, sell, exchange or lease any of the Crown Lands, or
any right or easement in or over them or any of them, for such price or consideration or

rent, and for such estate or term of years, and on such conditions and with such
reservations, if any, as he or she may think reasonable.

### 5.

Proceeds to be paid into Treasury
All monies received in respect of any such grant, sale, exchange or lease shall be paid
into the Treasury.

### 6.

Rules for management, etc., of Crown Lands
The Governor-General may make rules for the management of the Crown Lands, for
the sale or letting thereof, for the prevention of squatting and encroachment thereon and
of spoil and injury to the woods thereon.

### 7.

Crown may resume possession for military purposes
It shall be lawful for Her Majesty to resume the possession of the lands, buildings and
premises, or any part thereof, specified in the Schedule for military purposes whenever
Her Majesty is advised that it is required for the public safety and defence of Grenada;
but it shall not be obligatory on the part of the Government to maintain the said buildings
or to incur any expense in respect thereof in case of possession being so resumed.

### 8.

Saving as to existing leases, if any, of Church Lands
Nothing in this Act shall be construed as affecting the rights, if any, of existing leases
of Church Lands.

### 9.

Right of Crown to mines and alluvial deposits of precious metals and minerals
All mines of gold or silver, and all other precious metals and minerals, and all precious
metals existing in the form of alluvial deposits, in, on or under any land in Grenada shall
be and the same are hereby reserved to the sole use and benefit of the Crown, with full
right at all times to enter and inspect the same, and for those purposes or any of them to
enter upon the lands, subject to such compensation for any injury done to or upon the
surface, or to any building standing thereon, as may be determined by two arbitrators, of
whom one shall be appointed on behalf of the Crown, and the other by and on behalf of
the private party, if any, interested in the lands. The arbitrators shall, before they enter on
the matter referred to them, appoint in writing an umpire to decide on any matter on
which they may differ, whose decision shall be final.

CROWN LANDS RULES
[Section 6. Amended by SRO 19 of 1963, SRO 19 of 1964, SRO 3 of 1965, SRO 39 of 1965.]

[2nd November, 1896.]

---

### 1.

Citation
These Rules may be cited as the Crown Lands Rules.

## PART I

Crown Land Rangers

### 2.

Appointment of Rangers

Each District Officer shall have power to appoint and dismiss such number of Rangers
as he or she may deem fit and shall have power to fix their remuneration, provided always
that no District Officer shall expend an amount beyond that allotted to him or her for
payment of Rangers.

### 3.

Notification of appointment
Each District Officer shall notify each appointment and dismissal to the Minister in
order that the latter may take the steps necessary to ensure the appointment or dismissal
of the Ranger to or as the case may be from the office of Rural Constable.

### 4.

Arrest without warrant
No Ranger shall arrest any person without warrant unless such person is found
committing an offence and his or her name and address is unknown to such Ranger.

### 5.

Boundaries
The boundaries of Crown Mountain Lands shall be kept clear.

### 6.

Destruction of provisions
No provisions found growing in such lands without authority shall be destroyed
without the sanction of a District Officer.

## PART II

Fort George

### 7.

Preserving order
It shall be the duty of the Commissioner of Police to preserve order on the “Fort
Rocks” and on every other portion of those Crown Lands and premises adjacent to the
city of St. George’s known as “Fort George”.

### 8.

Disorderly behaviour
No person shall behave on the said premises in a violent, indecent, noisy, vicious or
disorderly way, or beg for or gather alms, or otherwise conduct himself or herself therein
in a manner likely to annoy or disturb any of the respectable frequenters of the said
premises.

### 9.

Removal of persons
The Commissioner of Police is hereby empowered to prevent any person from
behaving or conducting himself or herself in violation of these Rules and, if necessary, to
remove any such person or any drunken person from out of the said Crown Lands.

### 10.

Prosecution for contravention
Any person who contravenes these Rules or who resists the police in the execution of
their duties hereunder, shall be prosecuted by the Commissioner of Police or by any
constable authorised by him or her in that behalf, and on conviction shall be liable to pay
a penalty not exceeding two hundred and fifty dollars.

## PART III

Damage to Crown Lands

### 11.

Damage to property
Any person who injures any timber or property on any Crown Lands shall be liable on
summary conviction to a penalty not exceeding two hundred and fifty dollars.

### 12.

Appointments
The Public Service Commission shall from time to time appoint Crown Land
Superintendents and Rangers whose duty it shall be to protect and foster the Crown
Forests and to report to the Commissioner of Police the violation of any of these Rules.

### 13.

Offences
Any person committing an offence against these Rules may be prosecuted by the
Commissioner of Police or by any police constable authorised by him or her in that
behalf.

## PART IV

Sale of Crown Lands

### 14.

Application of rules
These Rules shall apply to all Crown Lands, including those alienated by virtue of any
housing settlement.

### 15.

Surveys before allotment
Crown Lands to which the above rules apply shall, save as hereinafter provided, be
surveyed before any allotment of them is made, and the administration and disposal of
such lands and of all details connected therewith shall, subject to the above rules and to
such orders as may be given by the Cabinet, be entrusted to an officer hereunder styled
the “Land Officer”.

### 16.

Provisional valuation
The Cabinet may where circumstances so require, authorise an allotment being made
at a provisional valuation prior to the completion of the survey, which provisional
valuation shall, until such allotment shall be valued in accordance with these Rules, be
deemed to be the value thereof for the purpose of such rules; but thereafter the value so
fixed as hereinafter provided shall be deemed to have been and shall be the value thereof
for all purposes.

### 17.

Reservations
In making the survey the following reserves shall be made—
(a)

all ponds and wells, the beds and banks of rivers and of any important stream
or tributary, and a space of half a chain around the source and on either bank
thereof;

(b)

swamps, exposed ridges, and such forest as may be deemed necessary for the
purpose of forest conservation;

(c)

such land, including sites for churches, chapels, schools, or other public
purposes, as the Cabinet may approve as necessary or desirable for the
general good;

(d)

such land as may be necessary for roads or paths to each allotment or group
of allotments;

(e)

such land as may be requisite for villages;

(f)

any continuous tract of altogether precipitous or uncultivable land.

### 18.

Allotments
The remainder of the land shall be divided into allotments of not less than two, and not
more than five acres each, and no allotment shall exceed five acres except with the
approval of the Cabinet. In surveying the land for allotments the surveyor shall as far as
possible include in one allotment any cultivation of an existing tenant of the land.

### 19.

Allotments to be numbered
Each allotment shall be numbered and shall have its number clearly displayed on some
part of it.

### 20.

Marking of allotments
Every corner of each allotment shall be marked by hard wood posts or stone pillars
and the general outline by dragon’s blood or immortelle plants, which plants shall be
carefully tended by the allottee.

### 21.

Register
The Land Officer shall keep a register in the form approved by the Cabinet in which
shall be recorded in parallel columns—
(a)

the number, area and value of each allotment;

(b)

the name of the allottee;

(c)

the date of the allotment;

(d)

the amount paid previous to allotment;

(e)

the sums paid on account of instalments and the dates of such payments;

(f)

any other information which the Cabinet may prescribe.

### 22.

Payment of deposits
(1) Upon a survey being made on behalf of prospective purchasers whose applications
have been approved by the Cabinet, and before such survey is actually undertaken, a
deposit of the assessed cost of such survey shall be made by such purchasers, which
deposit shall not be refundable.
(2) When the survey is complete the Land Officer (assisted by such assessors, if any,
as may be appointed by the Cabinet) shall proceed to value each allotment, and shall so
apportion the value of each that the total valuation shall cover the total amount of the
expenses incurred by the Government in the purchase of the whole area of land including
any legal expenses and costs of survey.

### 23.

List of allotments
The Land Officer shall as soon as possible submit to the Cabinet a list of the
allotments with the value of each. On approval of the valuations by the Cabinet the value
of each allotment shall be recorded in the register.

### 24.

Applications for allotments

The Land Officer shall then by notice in the Gazette, and by means of posters, or in
any other way he or she may deem desirable, invite applications in writing (which shall
be made on a printed form to be obtained from the Land Officer) for allotments, and on
the expiration of fourteen days from the date of the notice shall proceed, subject to the
Cabinet’s approval, to allot the same in accordance with the following rules:
Provided, however, that he or she shall not make an allotment of land to any person
who is already an owner of land without the sanction of the Cabinet.

### 25.

Priority
In selecting applicants for allotments priority shall be given firstly to those who are
prepared to pay down the whole value of the lots, and secondly to those who are prepared
to pay not less than twenty-five per cent of that value, taken in order of date of
application:
Provided that an existing tenant shall have the first choice of purchasing the
allotment within which his or her cultivation falls, unless the Cabinet, on the report of the
Land Officer, shall decide that such tenant is otherwise ineligible.

### 26.

Purchase money
The remainder of the purchase money of an allotment on which twenty-five per cent
or more has been paid shall be divided into nine equal parts, and the allottee shall, on the
expiration of the third year from the date on which his or her conditional permit to occupy
(which will be the date of the first payment on account) was issued to him or her by the
Land Officer, and thereafter on the same day in each year, for nine consecutive years, pay
annually, as an instalment, one of those parts; and shall also so long after the date of the
conditional permit as any part of the purchase money remains due, pay interest thereon at
the rate of five per cent per annum on the same day in each successive year.

### 27.

Other allotments
When all the applications from persons who are able to pay cash or not less than
twenty-five per cent of the purchase money have been dealt with, the Cabinet may in his
or her discretion allot to the other applicants, according to the dates of their applications,
the remaining lots of land, if such applicants be recommended by the Land Officer, and
considered eligible by the Cabinet.

### 28.

Conditional permit
Such persons shall receive from the Land Officer a conditional permit to occupy the
allotment in respect of which they have been selected on condition of paying for such
allotment one-twelfth part of the purchase money; and at the expiration of one year from
the date of such permit and at the same date in each succeeding year, a further one-twelfth
part of the purchase money shall be payable together with interest at five per cent per
annum on the amount of such purchase money then outstanding.

### 29.

Outstanding instalments
An allottee may at any time pay off any instalment or instalments outstanding. An
allottee may at any time pay on account of any instalment any sum not less than ten
dollars:
Provided that there shall be no deduction of interest by reason of any part payment
of an instalment.

### 30.

Village lots

(1) Land reserved for a village under rule 17 shall be called a “Village Lot” and shall
be laid out in house spots not exceeding 60 feet square each, with suitable space reserved
for streets and cross streets to afford access to such house spots.
(2) For the purpose of valuation a Village Lot shall be treated as an allotment, and its
value fixed as in rule 22 with the addition of all expenses attending its division into house
spots.
(3) The value of each house spot shall be apportioned so that the total valuation shall
cover the total amount of the value of the Village Lot.
(4) Every allottee of an allotment shall be entitled to purchase and hold a house spot
on the adjacent Village Lot, so long as any remain unsold, on the same conditions as an
allotment may be purchased and held.

### 31.

House spots
House spots may be sold or leased to persons who are not allottees and special cases
dealt with on such terms and conditions as may be approved in each case by the Cabinet.

### 32.

Conditions of tenure
The conditions of tenure of allotments shall be as follows—
(a)

every purchaser shall for a period of twelve years reckoning from the date of
allotment reside ordinarily in the island of Grenada, commencing to do so
six months after obtaining a conditional permit under these Rules, unless the
Land Officer, with the approval of the Cabinet, shall otherwise permit;

(b)

no land may be alienated, let, or incumbered, for a period of twelve years
from the date of allotment without the consent of the Cabinet;
the word “alienated” does not refer to or include an involuntary alienation as
in the case of descent or bankruptcy nor an alienation partly involuntary as in
the case of a devise by will;

(c)

no exclusive right shall vest in any person to any spring, stream, pond, well,
or other natural source of water situate in or flowing through any land;

(d)

the Cabinet may at any time resume possession of any part or parts of an
allotment not exceeding one-tenth of the said allotment, for roads, on paying
therefor at the same rate per acre as that at which the allotment was
originally sold, and also for the value of such crops as may be on such
selected line of roads;

(e)

no cocoa shall be planted on any land for a period of twelve years from the
date of allotment without the consent in writing of the Cabinet;

(f)

all instalments and interest shall be punctually paid on or before the day on
which they are due.

### 33.

Failure to comply with conditions
On failure by an allottee of an allotment or house spot to comply with any of the
conditions of tenure hereinbefore detailed, the Land Officer shall immediately report all
the facts of the case to the Cabinet, who shall inquire as fully as possible into all the
circumstances of the default, whereupon the Cabinet may order that the right to such
allotment or house spot and all crops, and all instalments paid, and all other rights of the
allottee shall be forfeited, without any appeal to any Court or may make such other order
as the circumstances of the case may require.

### 34.

Service of order

Notice of an order so made shall be served upon the allottee or left at his or her last
known place of abode, and no act of re-entry shall be necessary, and such allotment or
house spot shall be at the disposal of the Cabinet, either to re-sell then, or at any future
time.

### 35.

Payment in full
On payment in full by an allottee of the purchase money of an allotment or house spot
he or she shall be entitled to a grant of the same. Such grant shall be in the form approved
by the Cabinet, and shall be recorded in the Land Registry Office free of all cost to the
allottee, except stamp duty.

## PART V

Government Pastures, Carriacou

### 36.

Grazing of animals
All animals, with the exception of pigs, shall be admitted to graze on the Brunswick,
Belair, Limlair, Mt. Pleasant, Harvey Vale and Recreation Ground Pastures, at the risk of
the owners:
Provided that only ewes, and she-goats shall be admitted to the Recreation Ground
Pasture.
This Rule shall not apply to animals owned by the Government of Grenada.

### 37.

Responsibility
No responsibility for any loss of, or any injury to, any animal so depastured shall
attach to the Government or to any officer or employee of the Government.

### 38.

No undertaking by Government
The Government does not undertake that the above pastures shall furnish sufficient
fodder for animals, or to fix any limit to the number of animals which shall be admitted in
the pastures.

### 39.

Hours of pasturage
No animal shall be admitted to, or removed from, the pastures before 6 a.m. and after
6 p.m.

### 40.

Emaciated animals
Upon any animal being discovered in a state of emaciation, the Ranger shall notify the
owner of its condition and request him or her to feed it or remove it from the pasture.
Should the owner fail to comply with this request he or she will render himself or herself
liable to prosecution.

### 41.

Pasturage fees
The pasturage fees in respect of the various pastures shall be payable monthly at the
Revenue Office, Hillsborough and shall be as follows—

### 42.

Charges
If an animal be admitted before the fifteenth day of any month the charge for the
whole month shall be paid in respect of such animal; but if admitted on or after the
fifteenth day of the month then half only of such charge shall be paid.

### 43.

Trespass
If any such monthly pasturage fees due in respect of any animal shall not be paid
within the first week of each month, such animal may, with the approval of the District
Officer or District Overseer, be dealt with as if committing a trespass.

### 44.

Non-payment of fees
No animal admitted to graze on the pastures shall be removed therefrom before all
pasturage fees have been paid, unless the owner shall, with the express consent of the
District Overseer, leave other animals of his or her own on the pasture as security for the
unpaid charges; and if such charges remain unpaid on the fifteenth day of the following
month, any animal belonging to the defaulter may be sold at public auction and the
proceeds of the sale paid into the Treasury. Any surplus monies arising from such sale
after payment of all charges and expenses shall be paid to the defaulting owner.

### 45.

Non-payment of fees as trespass
Where the fees and charges for any animal are not paid in any month and the owner of
the animal is unknown, the fact shall be reported to the District Officer, and the District
Officer shall give such instructions as he or she may deem necessary for its upkeep until
the fifteenth day of the following month, when, if the fees and charges are not paid, the
animal shall be dealt with as trespassing under the provisions of the Stock Trespass Act,

## Chapter 312.

### 46.

Pigs
No pigs shall be admitted to any of the pastures, and if any pig is found thereon it shall
be dealt with by the Ranger under the law relating to stock trespass.

### 47.

Removal of dead, etc., animals
The owner of any animal which may die or be injured shall remove such animal within
three hours after being notified of such fact by the Ranger.

### 48.

Disposal of dead or injured animals
Should the owner of any animal which may die or be injured on the pastures
aforementioned not remove such animal within three hours after notification by the
Ranger, the District Overseer shall direct the same to be disposed of in such manner as he
or she shall see fit, and the cost of burying or removing or otherwise disposing thereof
shall be paid by the owner to the District Overseer. Until such cost shall be paid, no other
animal belonging to such owner shall be received, and such cost shall be deemed to be a
charge upon any other animals depastured by such owner and may be recovered by sale at
public auction of any of such other animals; and any surplus of the proceeds of the sale

which may remain after paying the amount of such costs and of the expenses of the
auction sale shall be paid to the owner of the animal or animals so sold.

### 49.

Treatment of animals
No animal within the pasture shall be tethered or staked or have any food thrown to it
within 20 feet of any fence or shall be fastened to any fence.

### 50.

Responsibility of owner
Owners not conforming to this or any other of these Rules will render their animals
liable to be removed from and not afterwards to be re-admitted to the pastures.

### 51.

Pasture book
(1) A pasture book shall be kept by the District Overseer in which shall be entered—
(a)

the name of the owner of each animal admitted to the pastures; and

(b)

the description of animal.

(2) The District Overseer shall at least once in every month check this book with the
animals on the pastures.

### 52.

Refusal to admit animal
(1) It shall be competent for the District Overseer in his or her discretion to refuse to
admit any animal to be pastured on any of the estates or to cause any animal to be
removed from the pasture of any of the estates.
(2) The District Overseer shall have general control of the pastures; and, in his or her
absence, the Ranger of each estate shall act on his or her behalf, and report any changes to
him or her as soon after as possible.

### 53.

Penalty
Any person who contravenes these Rules, or who resists the Ranger or any public
officer in the execution of his or her duties hereunder shall be liable to pay a fine not
exceeding two hundred and fifty dollars.

## PART VI

Government Pastures, Grenada

### 54.

Grazing on Crown Lands
Animals may be admitted to graze on Crown Land pastures in the island of Grenada
subject to the conditions mentioned in these Rules.

### 55.

Non-liability of Government
No responsibility for any loss of, or any injury to, any animal so depastured shall
attach to the Government or to any officer or employee of the Government.

### 56.

No undertaking by Government
The Government does not undertake that the above pastures shall furnish sufficient
fodder for animals, or to fix any limit to the number of animals which shall be admitted
into the pastures.

### 57.

Hours of pasturage
No animal shall be admitted to the pastures before 6 a.m., or removed therefrom after
6 p.m., without the consent of the Government agent.

### 58.

Emaciated animals
The Government agent may require the owner of any sick or emaciated animal to
remove it from the pasture or to supply it with food. Any owner failing to comply with
any such requirement shall be guilty of a breach of these Rules.

### 59.

Removal of dead, etc., animals
The owner of any animal which may die or be injured shall remove the same within
three hours after being notified by the Government agent of such death or injury.

### 60.

Pasturage fees
The pasturage fees shall be payable monthly at the Treasury or Revenue Office. Such
fees shall be at the following rates per month—

### 62.

Charges
If an animal be admitted before the fifteenth day of any month the charge for the
whole month shall be paid in respect of such animal; but if admitted on or after the
fifteenth day of the month then half only of such charge shall be paid.

### 63.

Trespass
If any such monthly pasturage charges due in respect of any animal shall not be paid
within the first week of each month, such animal may be dealt with as if trespassing, and
compensation for such trespass shall be recovered as provided for under the law relating
to stock trespass.

### 64.

Non-payment of fees
No animal admitted to graze on the pastures shall be removed therefrom before all
pasturage fees have been paid, unless the owner shall, with the express consent of the
Government agent, leave other animals of his or her own on the pasture as security for the

unpaid charges; and if such charges remain unpaid on the fifteenth day of the following
month, any animal belonging to the defaulter may be sold at public auction and the
proceeds of the sale paid into the Treasury or Revenue Office. Any surplus monies arising
from such sale after payment of all charges and expenses shall be paid to the defaulting
owner.

### 65.

Non-payment of fees as trespass
Where the fees and charges for any animal are not paid in any month and the owner of
the animal is unknown, the fact shall be reported to the Minister, and the Minister shall
give such instructions as he or she may deem necessary for its keep until the fifteenth day
of the following month, when, if the fees and charges are not paid, the animal may be
dealt with as trespassing under the law relating to stock trespass.

### 66.

Pigs
No pigs shall be admitted to the pasture and if any pig is found thereon it shall either
be ejected or be dealt with by the Government agent under the law relating to stock
trespass.

### 67.

Disposal of dead or injured animals
Should the owner of any animal which may die or be injured on the pastures
aforementioned not remove such animal within three hours after notification by the
Government agent, he or she shall direct the same to be disposed of in such manner as he
or she shall see fit, and the cost of burying or removing or otherwise disposing thereof
shall be paid by the owner. Until such cost shall be paid no other animal belonging to
such owner shall be received and such cost shall be deemed to be a charge upon any other
animals depastured by such owner and may be recovered by sale at public auction of any
of such other animals; and any surplus of the proceeds of the sale which may remain after
paying the amount of such costs and of the expenses of the auction sale shall be paid to
the owner of the animal or animals so sold.

### 68.

Tethering or staking
All animals admitted to the pastures shall be tethered or staked at such points or within
such areas as the Government agent shall assign and no animals shall be fastened to any
building, fence or post which may be erected there.

### 69.

Penalty
Owners not conforming to any of these Rules will render their animals liable to be
removed from and not afterwards to be re-admitted to the pastures.

### 70.

Pasture book
A pasture book shall be kept by the Government agent in which shall be entered—
(a)

the name of the owner of each animal admitted to the pastures; and

(b)

the description of the animal.

### 71.

Refusal by overseer
It shall be competent for the Government agent in his or her discretion to refuse to
admit any animal to the pastures or to cause any animal to be removed therefrom.

### 72.

Exclusion from pasture

In the event of any pasture being required on any particular day or days for any public
purpose, any or every animal may, on the instructions of the Cabinet, be excluded from
the grounds altogether on such day or days.

### 73.

Penalty
Any person who contravenes these Rules or who resists any Government agent in the
execution of his or her duties hereunder shall be liable to pay a penalty not exceeding two
hundred and fifty dollars.

## PART VII

Crown Lands Held by Tenants

### 74.

Interpretation
In these Rules, the word “tenant” includes a life tenant.

### 75.

Register of tenants
(1) There shall be kept in the office of the Chief Technical Officer (Extension) a
register of all Crown Lands held by tenants.
(2) Such register shall contain the area and situation of the land, the name and address
of the tenant, the date on which the tenancy commenced, the annual rent, the date fixed
for the termination of the tenancy, a short statement of any special covenant or condition,
an entry of any transfers, assignments, subleases or other changes of ownership, and any
other particulars which may appear to be necessary or which the Cabinet may direct.

### 76.

Returns
The Registrar of the High Court shall, within the first ten days of the months of
January, April, July and October furnish the Chief Technical Officer (Extension) with a
return of all instruments affecting such Crown Lands registered in the Deeds and Land
Registry during the preceding three months, which return shall contain—
(a)

the date of the instrument;

(b)

the name and description of every party;

(c)

the situation of the land affected;

(d)

a short and general statement of the manner in which such Crown Land is
affected.

### 77.

Escheated lands
The Cabinet shall, from time to time, furnish the Chief Technical Officer (Extension)
with information as to any lands escheated to the Crown and any tenancies or life
tenancies granted in respect of such lands.

### 78.

Information to Cabinet
During the month of August in every year the Chief Technical Officer (Extension)
shall furnish for the information of the Cabinet a report on all matters relating to such
Crown Lands in respect of which action on the part of the government may appear to be
necessary.

### 79.

List of tenants

During the month of May in every year a list of such tenants shall be sent by the Chief
Technical Officer (Extension) to the Land Officer and the latter shall report, before the
month of August, whether such tenants are alive and in occupation of the land held by
them and, if any such tenant is dead or not in occupation, the name of the person in
occupation of the land.

## PART VIII

Management of the Spout Grounds

### 80.

Boundaries of Spout Grounds
For the purposes of these Rules the Spout Grounds comprise that portion of land
bounded by the St. George’s Lower Main Road, the road leading to the Lagoon or Spout
Bay, and the coast line facing the Carenage.

### 81.

Control of grounds
The Spout Grounds shall be under the immediate control and management of the
Agricultural Board.

### 82.

Hours of opening
The Spout Grounds shall be opened to visitors daily from 6 a.m. to sunset, also during
evening hours of moonlight between sunset and 10 p.m.

### 83.

Visitors
Visitors may be required to keep to the walks.

### 84.

Conduct of visitors
Visitors shall not touch any plants, fruits or flowers in these grounds; and shall not
stand or walk on any of the garden beds.

### 85.

Exclusion of animals
With the exception of dogs under the control of visitors, no animals or vehicles of any
description shall be allowed within the Spout Grounds except for the purposes of the
Ministry of Agriculture.

### 86.

Prohibition on public meetings
No public meetings or demonstrations shall be allowed to take place in the Spout
Grounds except with the permission of the Cabinet.

### 87.

Dress of visitors
Visitors to the Spout Grounds shall be decently dressed.

### 88.

Disorderly conduct
Any person who shall conduct himself or herself in a disorderly manner in the Spout
Grounds, or shall act in contravention of any of these Rules may be summarily ejected
from the grounds by the Foreman in Charge or any of his or her assistants, and shall also
be liable on summary conviction to a penalty not exceeding two hundred and fifty dollars.

PREVENTION OF SQUATTING ON CROWN LANDS RULES
[SRO 5 of 2007.]

[30th March, 2007.]
1\. Citation
These Rules may be cited as the Prevention of Squatting on Crown Lands Rules.
2\. Definition
In these Rules—
“building or structure” includes any hut, shack, tent or similar structure or any
other form of temporary or permanent dwelling structure;
“Crown Lands” means land vested in the Crown or vested in the Governor-General
for the public uses of Grenada but shall not include land acquired pursuant to the
provisions of the Land Settlement Act;
“evict” means to deprive a person of occupation of a building or structure, or the
land in which such building or structure is erected against his or her will, and
“eviction” has a corresponding meaning;
“Minister” means the Minister with the responsibility for the management of
Crown Lands.
3\. Squatting on Crown Lands
(1) A person shall not take possession of, encroach upon, cultivate or put up any
building or structure on any part of any Crown Land without the express authorisation in
writing of the Minister.
(2) A person who fails to comply with subsection (1) or who is a person in occupation
of Crown Land without any right or authority, shall be deemed to be a squatter.
4\. Eviction of squatters
(1) Where the Minister is satisfied that there is a squatter on Crown Lands, he or she
or she may cause a notice to be issued to the squatter calling upon him or her to vacate the
land within a time to be specified in the notice.
(2) A notice issued pursuant to subsection (1) shall be served on the squatter in person
or, where the squatter cannot be traced or served with the notice within forty-eight hours
of its issue, posted up in a conspicuous place on the land or any building or structure
existing on the land.
(3) Where a squatter fails to comply with a notice served on him or her or posted up
in accordance with subsection (2) he or she commits an offence and shall be liable on
summary conviction to a fine not exceeding one thousand dollars or to a term of
imprisonment not exceeding three months.
(4) Subject to subsection (2) and notwithstanding subsection (3), the Minister may
with the assistance of a police officer, cause a squatter to be removed immediately from
any land occupied by him or her and cause any building or other structure existing in the
land to be demolished.
(5) Where a squatter who has been removed from any Crown Land re-occupies the
Crown Land or occupies any other Crown Land without the express authorisation in
writing of the Minister, he or she commits an offence and shall be liable on summary

conviction to a fine not exceeding five thousand dollars or to a term of imprisonment not
exceeding one year.
(6) Subsection (5) shall be without prejudice to the right of the Minister to remove the
squatter from the land or to cause any building or other structure put up by him or her to
be demolished.
(7) Where any building or other structure is demolished pursuant to the provisions of
these Rules, such part of the materials as are still in good or usable condition shall be kept
by the Minister and returned to the squatter upon his or her application,
(8) In any proceedings under these Rules, a certificate from the Chief Surveyor that
land which is the subject matter of the proceedings is Crown Land shall be prima facie
evidence that the land is Crown Land.
(9) No action for damages or structure shall be taken against the Minister or any
person acting under his or her authority in respect of anything done under this section
except where the person evicted or the person whose building or structure was
demolished was in possession of an authority in writing from the Minister to occupy the
Crown Lands or to put up the building or other structure on the land or was lawfully in
occupation of the Crown Lands.
