## CHAPTER 211A

NOISE CONTROL ACT
An Act to provide for the regulation and control of noise.
[Act No. 7 of 2006.]

[1st September, 2006.]

1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Noise Control Act.
2\. Interpretation
(1) In this Act—
“Carnival Period” means the period designated as such under the Carnival (August
Celebration) Act, 1994, or the Carnival Regulation Act, Chapter 41;
“Loud speaker” includes any amplifier, microphone, gramophone or other similar
instrument used for the amplification and projection of sound;
“Minister” means Minister responsible for health and the environment;
“Noise” means sound and may include vibration;
“Noise Abatement Notice”, means a Notice which directs the person making noise,
to stop or cease doing so;
“Noise Pollution” means harmful or annoying sounds or vibrations or sounds that
amount to a nuisance;
“Nuisance” means an unreasonable interference with the rights of another;
“Person Responsible”, in relation to the emission of noise, means the person to
whose act, default or sufferance the noise is attributable;
“Public Festivals” means the public festivals designated as national or grand
festivals under the Public Festivals Regulations Act, 1994;
“Public Meeting” includes any meeting in a public place and any meeting to which
the public or any section of the public is permitted to attend, whether upon payment or
otherwise and whether by special or general invitation or otherwise and includes any
event of public entertainment;
“Public place” includes any public space, building or conveyance to which the
public is entitled to have access, whether on payment or otherwise;
“street” includes any road, highway, park, marketplace, bridge, footway or open
space to which the public is granted access, and includes any privately owned road or
open space to which the public is granted access either generally or conditionally;
“Work of Engineering Construction” includes among other things but is not limited
to, the construction, structural alteration, maintenance repair or demolition of any
dock, harbour, town, building, structure, bridge, waterworks, road, reservoir, pipeline,
sewer, sewerage works, gas/holder, telephone lines, plant or factory.
(2) Where one or more persons are responsible for noise, this Act shall apply to each
one of those persons, whether or not the noise for which any of them is individually
responsible would itself amount to a nuisance or would result in a level or noise justifying
action under this Act.
3\. Excessive noise always unreasonable and always actionable
Every person in Grenada shall observe the basic principle that the making or
continuance of excessive noise shall be deemed to be unreasonable and shall be
actionable under this Act or otherwise, and the granting of permission to any person
under this Act to operate a loudspeaker or a musical instrument shall not be deemed to
constitute an authorisation to such person to create or maintain a significantly higher
volume of sound than is reasonably necessary to satisfy the primary purpose for which
such loudspeaker or musical instrument is being operated. What amounts to excessive
noise will be measured by the standard of reasonableness.

4\. Test of reasonableness
(1) A court in deciding the question of reasonableness under this Act, shall use the
objective test of ordinary standards and ordinary sensitivities, and shall attach to purely
personal sensitivities and idiosyncrasies only such weight as the court might deem
necessary to prevent injustice.
(2) A court in deciding any matter which falls to be decided, whether originally or on
appeal, shall ensure a balance between the personal rights and interests of individuals, as
against the rights and interests of the community as a whole, and shall take into account
all relevant factors including but not limited to—
(a)

the desirability for cultural and social developments;

(b)

the desirability of the pursuit of just economic rewards for labour;

(c)

the traditions of particular localities and of the society as a whole;

(d)

the sanctity of religious worship, teaching, practice and observance;

(e)

the rights of privacy of family life and the right of every individual to the
peaceful enjoyment of his or her home and immediate surrounding;

(f)

the rights of the disabled, the aged, the young and the sick;

(g)

the necessity for orderly progress involving changing values and lifestyles,
but preserving the fundamental principle of respect for the rights of others.

5\. Exemptions from the application of this Act
(1) This Act does not apply to noise caused—
(a)

by the Police, fire-brigade, ambulance or other emergency vehicles;

(b)

for the purpose of communicating with the persons on a vessel for the
purpose of directing the movement of that or any other vessel;

(c)

by a loudspeaker if the loudspeaker forms part of a public telephone system;

(d)

by a loudspeaker if the loudspeaker—
(i) is in or fixed to a vehicle and is operated solely for the entertainment
of or the communicating with the driver or a passenger of the vehicle,
or where a loudspeaker is or forms part of the horn or similar warning
instrument of the vehicle solely for giving warning of other traffic,
and
(ii) is so operated as not to cause a nuisance to persons in the vicinity;

(e)

during a Carnival period;

(f)

in cases of emergency;

(g)

for such purposes as the Minister may, by Order specify;

(h)

at any other time permitted by the Commissioner of Police.

6\. Restriction on use of loudspeaker
(1) Subject to the Act, a loudspeaker shall not be operated in a street or public place—
(a)

between the hours of 11.00 p.m. and 7.00 a.m. the following morning for any
purpose;

at any time except between 10.00 a.m. and 6.00 p.m. on the same day for the
purpose of advertising any entertainment, meeting, procession, trade or
business.

(2) Notwithstanding subsection (1), the Commissioner of Police may grant permission
for the use of a loudspeaker in a street during such time and for such purposes and subject
to such conditions as the Commissioner may impose. Applications for permission are to
be made in accordance with section 18 of this Act.
7\. Abatement of nuisance
(1) Where the Commissioner of Police is satisfied that noise amounting to a nuisance
exists, or is likely to occur or recur, or continue in any area of the State of Grenada, the
Commissioner may serve a notice—
(a)

requiring the abatement or reduction of the nuisance, or prohibiting or
restricting its occurrence or recurrence or continuation; and

(b)

requiring the executing of such work, and the taking of such other steps, as
may be necessary for the purpose of the notice or as may be specified in the
notice,

and the Notice shall specify the time or times within which the requirements of the notice
are to be complied with.
(2) The Notice shall be served by post or personally on any of the persons deemed by
the Commissioner to be responsible for the noise or, if the noise has not yet occurred, on
any of the persons who would be responsible if it shall occur.
(3) Any person served with the Notice may appeal against the Notice to a Magistrate
within fourteen days of service of the Notice.
(4) Any person on whom a notice is served under this section who, without
reasonable excuse, contravenes any requirement of the Notice is guilty of an offence.
(5) In proceedings for an offence under subsection (4) in respect of noise caused in
the course of a trade or business, it shall be a defence to prove that the best practicable
means have been used for preventing, or counteracting the effect of the noise.
(6) In proceedings for an offence under subsection (4), contravening requirements
imposed by virtue of a notice served under subsection (1) it shall be a defence to prove—
(a)

that the alleged offence was covered by consent given under section 9; or

(b)

where the alleged offence was committed at a time when the premises were
subjected to a Notice under section 10, that the level of noise coming from
the premises at that time was not such as to constitute disregard of the Notice
under section 10.

(7) Where the Commissioner is of the opinion that proceedings for an offence under
subsection (4) would not afford an adequate remedy in the case of any noise which is a
nuisance affecting any person, the Commissioner may take proceedings in the
Magistrates Court for the purpose of security, the abatement, reduction, prohibition or
restriction of the nuisance; but in any proceedings taken in pursuance of this subsection it
shall be a defence to prove that the noise was authorised by Notice under section 10 or by
consent under section 9.
8\. Summary proceedings
(1) A Magistrate may act under this section on a complaint by any person on the
ground that, that person is aggrieved by noise amounting to a nuisance.
(2) If the Magistrate is satisfied that the alleged nuisance exists or that although
abated it is likely to recur, the Magistrate may make an order for either or both of the
following purposes—

requiring the defendant to abate and or cease the nuisance, within a time
specified in the order, and to execute any works necessary for that purpose;

prohibiting a recurrence of the nuisance, and requiring the defendant, within
a time specified in the order, to execute any work necessary to prevent a
recurrence.

(3) Proceedings under this section shall be brought against the person directly
responsible for the nuisance or, if that person cannot be found, against the owner or
occupier of the premises from which the noise is emitted or would be emitted.
(4) Any person who without reasonable excuse contravenes any requirement of an
order under subsection (2) is guilty of an offence.
(5) In proceedings for an offence under this section in respect of noise caused in the
course of a trade or business, it shall be a defence to prove that the best practicable means
have been used for preventing, or for counteracting the effect of, the noise.
9\. Prior consent for work on construction sites
(1) A person who intends to carry out works to which section 10 applies, shall make
an application to the Minister for consent under this section.
(2) An application under this section shall contain particulars of—
(a)

the works, and the method by which they are to be carried out; and

(b)

the steps proposed to be taken to reduce noise resulting from the works.

(3) If the Minister considers that the application contains sufficient information for
the purpose and that, if the works are carried out in accordance with the application, no
actionable nuisance would arise or be occasioned in respect of those works, the Minister
may give consent to the application.
(4) In acting under this section the Minister shall have regard to the considerations set
out in section 10(4) and shall have power to—
(a)

attach such conditions to a consent as may appear reasonable in all the
circumstances;

(b)

limit or qualify a consent to allow for any changed circumstances; and

(c)

limit the duration of a consent.

(5) Any person who knowingly carries out the works or permits the works to be
carried out, in contravention of any condition attached to a consent under this section, is
guilty of an offence.
(6) The Minister shall inform the applicant of a decision on the application within
twenty-eight days from the receipt of the application.
(7) Where the Minister gives consent to the application a notice of the consent shall
be published, setting out the works to which it relate, in the Gazette.
(8) Where—
(a)

the Minister does not give a consent within the said period of twenty eight
days; or

(b)

the Minister gives consent within the said period of twenty eight days, but
attaches any condition to the consent, or limits or qualifies the consent in any
way,

the applicant may appeal to a judge in chambers within fourteen days from the end of that
period or from the date of notification of the Minister’s decision, but until the disposal of
the appeal the applicant shall in law be deemed not to have received the Minister’s consent.
(9) In any proceedings for an offence under section 9(5) it shall be a defence to prove
that the alleged contravention, amounted to the carrying out of the works in accordance
with the consent given under this section.

(10) A consent given under this section shall contain a statement to the effect that the
consent does not of itself constitute a defence against any proceedings instituted under
section 8.
(11) Where a consent has been given under this section and the works are carried out
by a person other than the applicant for the consent, it shall be the duty of the applicant to
take all reasonable steps to bring the consent to the notice of that other person; and if such
person fails to comply with this subsection that person will be guilty of an offence. It
shall not be a defence for any person to claim that they were unaware of the terms of a
consent.
10\. Control of noise on construction sites
(1) This section applies to works of the following description that is to say—
(a)

an erection, construction, alteration, repair or maintenance of buildings,
structures or roads;

(b)

breaking up, opening or boring under any road or adjacent land in
connection with the construction, inspection, maintenance or removal of
works;

(c)

demolition or dredging work; and

(d)

any work of engineering construction.

(2) Where it appears to the Minister that works to which this section applies are
being, or are going to be, carried out, the Minister may serve a notice imposing
requirements as to the way in which the works are to be carried out and may publish a
notice of the requirements in such a way as appears to be appropriate.
(3) The Notice may in particular—
(a)

specify the plant or machinery which is or is not to be used;

(b)

specify the hours during which the works may be carried out;

(c)

specify the level of noise which may be emitted or which may be so emitted
during specified hours; and

(d)

provide for any change of circumstances.

(4) In acting under this section the Minister shall have regard—
(a)

to the need for ensuring that the best practicable means are employed to
minimise noise;

(b)

before specifying any particular method or plant or machinery, to the
desirability in the interest of any recipients of the notice in question of
specifying other methods or plant or machinery which would be
substantially as effective in minimising noise and more acceptable to them;

(c)

to the need to protect any person in the locality in which the works in
question are situated from the effects of noise.

(5) A notice under this section shall be served on the person who appears to the
Minister to be carrying out, or going to carry out, the works, and on such other person
appearing to the Minister to be responsible for, or to have control over, the carrying out of
the works.
(6) A notice under this section shall specify the time within which the notice is to be
complied with, and may require the execution of such works, and the taking of such other
steps as may be necessary for the purpose of the notice, or as may be specified in the
notice.

(7) A person who has been served a notice under this section may appeal against the
notice to a judge in chambers within fourteen days from the date of the service of the
notice, but must comply therewith from the date of its receipt until disposal of the appeal.
(8) Any person on whom a notice is served under this section and who without
reasonable excuse contravenes a requirement of the notice, is guilty of an offence.
11\. Designation of noise abatement zone
(1) The Minister may, by order in the Gazette, designate any area in the State of
Grenada a noise abatement zone.
(2) An order under this section shall specify the area or classes of premises to which it
applies, the reason for the order and the conditions that apply.
12\. Reduction of noise levels by noise abatement notice
(1) If it appears to the Minister after consultation with the relevant parties that—
(a)

the level of noise emanating from any premises within a noise abatement
zone is not acceptable having regard to the purposes for which the order was
made; and

(b)

a reduction in that level of noise is practicable at reasonable cost and would
afford a public benefit,

the Minister may serve a Noise Abatement Notice on the person responsible.
(2) The notice under subsection (1) shall require that person—
(a)

to reduce the level of noise emanating from the premises to such level as
may be specified in the notice;

(b)

to prevent any subsequent increase in the level of noise emanating from
those premises without the consent of the Minister; and

(c)

to take such steps as may be specified in the notice to achieve those purposes.

(3) A notice under this section shall specify a time, from the date of service of the
notice, within which the noise level is to be reduced to the specified level, and where the
notice specifies any steps necessary to achieve that purpose, a time within which those
steps shall be taken.
(4) A Noise Abatement Notice may specify particular times, or particular days, during
which the noise level is to be reduced, and may require the noise level to be reduced to
different levels for different times or days.
(5) Any person who is served with a Noise Abatement Notice may, within fourteen
days of the date of service, appeal to a judge in chambers against the notice.
(6) Any person who, without reasonable excuse, contravenes a Noise Abatement
Notice is guilty of an offence.
(7) In proceedings for an offence under subsection (6) in respect of noise caused in
the course of a trade or business, it is a defence to prove that the best practicable means
had been used for preventing, or for counteracting the effect of, the noise.
13\. Noise from plant or machinery
(1) Without prejudice to the generality of the powers conferred by section 10, the
Minister may make regulations—

for requiring the use of in connection with any plant or machinery, devices
or arrangements which reduce the noise caused by the plant or machinery;

for limiting the level of noise which may be caused by any plant or
machinery when used for works to which section 10 applies, or which may
be caused outside a factory within the meaning of the Factories Act,

## Chapter 100, by the use of plant or machinery in the factory,

and regulations under this section may apply standards, specifications, descriptions or
tests laid down in documents not forming part of the regulations.
(2) The Minister may, before making regulations under this section, consult persons
who represent producers and users of plant and machinery, with a view to ensuring that
the regulations do not contain requirements, which would be impracticable or involve
unreasonable expense.
(3) Any person who contravenes, or causes or permits another person to contravene,
regulations under this Act is guilty of an offence; but in any proceedings for a
contravention of regulations made in pursuance of subsection (1)(a) it shall be a defence
to prove that means were used for the purpose of reducing the noise in question which
were not less effective for that purpose than the means required by the regulations.
14\. Appeal to judge in chambers
(1) Where any provision of this Act provides for an appeal to a judge in chambers the
procedure shall be by way of an application for an order, and the Eastern Caribbean
Supreme Court Civil Procedure Rules, 2000, shall apply to the proceedings.
(2) The judge may at any stage of the proceedings, either on his or her own motion or
an application by any party to an appeal, adjourn the hearing of the appeal or any part
thereof into open court as he or she thinks fit. No appeal shall lie from the exercise of the
judge’s discretion to adjourn or not to adjourn the proceedings into open court.
15\. Codes of practice for minimising noise
For the purpose of giving guidance on appropriate methods (including the use of
specified types of plant or machinery) of reducing noise the Minister may, by order—
(a)

prepare and approve and issue suitable codes of practice; and

(b)

approve such codes of practice issued otherwise than by the Minister that in
the opinion of the Minister are suitable for the purpose.

16\. Noise disturbing church meetings, etc.
(1) Subject to section 5, no person shall operate or continue to operate a loudspeaker
or musical instrument in such a manner that, by reason of the level of noise emitted, it
unreasonably disturbs—
(a)

persons participating or attempting to participate in a religious meeting or
religious service, or any function of a religious nature, whether or not the
premises being used or sought to be used for the religious function on the
occasion in question are normally or usually used for religious functions;

(b)

persons living or residing in the neighbourhood.

(2) Any person who contravenes subsection (1) is guilty of an offence.
17\. Noise near to hospital, school, etc.
(1) No person shall operate a loudspeaker or musical instrument—

within the compound of, or near to, a hospital, health centre, clinic or any
premises used either ordinarily or occasionally as a day care centre for
young children;

within the compound of, or near to, a school during school hours or the
hours during which evening classes are held,

in such manner that the level of noise given off from that loudspeaker or musical
instrument unreasonably disturbs any person in the hospital, health centre, clinic or day
care centre, or interferes with the holding of any class at the school, as the case may be.
(2) Any person who contravenes subsection (1) is guilty of an offence.
18\. Permission required in certain cases
(1) No loudspeaker or musical instrument shall be operated or used for the purpose of
providing music in any public place in circumstances where such music is reasonably
capable of disturbing any person occupying or residing in private premises unless written
application for permission for the operation, shall have been made to the police by
lodging the same at the police station nearest to the proposed venue, not later than ten
clear days before the date on which it is proposed to operate the loudspeaker or musical
instrument, and permission for the same shall have been granted in writing by the
Commissioner of Police; provided that the Commissioner of Police shall respond to any
such application within ninety-six hours of receiving the same.
(2) The Commissioner of Police may attach such conditions and restrictions to any
permission granted, in response to an application made to the police pursuant to
subsection (1) as may appear to be reasonable in all the circumstances.
(3) Any applicant who is aggrieved by any decision of the Commissioner of Police
refusing permission, or imposing any condition which to the applicant appears
unreasonable, may appeal in writing within three days of the decision to the Magistrates
Court.
(4) Any person who operates a loudspeaker or musical instrument in circumstances
that require permission from the Commissioner of Police further to subsection (1) without
first having obtained that permission commits an offence.
(5) Any person who operates a loudspeaker or musical instrument in breach of the
conditions or restrictions attached to the permission further to subsection (1) commits an
offence.
19\. Hours of holding of meeting
(1) The operation of a loudspeaker for the holding of any public or political meeting
shall end not later than 11.00 p.m. except during a period of elections when the operation
of a loudspeaker for the holding of a political meeting shall end not later than 12.00
midnight.
(2) Any person who contravenes subsection (1) is guilty of an offence.
20\. Loudspeakers operated in dance halls, etc.
(1) Subject to the other provisions of this Act, where a loudspeaker or musical
instrument is regularly operated or used for the purpose of providing music for dancing or
for other forms of entertainment at any location (whether private or public) located within
two hundred yards of any dwelling house, guest house or hotel, the level of noise shall at
all times be reasonable and the operation of the loudspeaker or musical instrument shall
be turned down to a level determined by the Commissioner of Police by 2.00 a.m. except
during the carnival period.
(2) Any person who contravenes subsection (1) is guilty of an offence.
21\. Excessive noise from vehicles

Any person who, without written permission from the Commissioner of Police, drives
or permits to be driven a motor vehicle on a road when that vehicle makes excessive
noise from its engine, exhaust, radio, tape, tape deck, air horn or any other part or
equipment attached, whether such equipment is built into the vehicle or not, and whether
for the reproduction of music, speech, other sound or otherwise, commits an offence.
22\. Control of noise
(1) Where any person is unreasonably disturbed by noise emitted from a loudspeaker
or musical instrument, such person may lodge an oral or written complaint about the
matter to the police, who immediately upon receiving such complaint, shall, investigate
the complaint and if satisfied of the reasonableness of the complaint, orally, followed as
soon as is reasonably practicable by a notice in writing, notify the person who is
suspected of being responsible for the noise, that a complaint has been made and shall
request that person to reduce, and maintain, the level of noise omitted from the
loudspeaker, musical instrument, or event to and at a reasonable level having regard to
the circumstances.
(2) If the person responsible for the noise fails to comply with a request from the
police under subsection (1), or where such person has complied for the time being with
the request under subsection (1) but at any time thereafter resumes or causes or permits or
suffers to be resumed the operation of the loudspeaker, musical instrument or event in a
manner which again unreasonably disturbs any person in the neighbourhood (whether
such person is the original complainant or not) and another complaint is made to the
police, the police shall immediately visit the scene and shall undertake such investigations
and inquiries as may appear appropriate. Upon being satisfied that there had been wilful
non-compliance with the request by the police made under subsection (1), the police shall
inform the person responsible that a complaint will be made against him or her for an
offence under the relevant section of this Act and shall institute the said complaint:
Provided that, even in the absence of a formal complaint, a police officer shall have
the duty to act on his or her own initiative pursuant to the foregoing whenever he or she
becomes aware of excessive or unreasonable noise which is likely to disturb, or has
actually disturbed, any person.
(3) Where the police have informed the person responsible that a complaint will be
made against him or her for an offence as provided for in subsection (2), a police officer
not below the rank of sergeant may with a warrant issued for such purpose, enter any
premises and confiscate the loudspeaker and the musical instrument, or any of them,
which the said police officer reasonably suspects to have been operated to produce the
sounds which gave rise to the complaint, regardless of the ownership of such loudspeaker
or musical instrument. Wherever possible a receipt for the items confiscated should be
given to the owner or person responsible for the items.
(4) The loudspeaker or musical instrument removed under subsection (3) shall be kept
in the custody of the police until the hearing of the complaint (reasonable care being
taken to keep the same safe and secure), and the police shall produce the same to the
court as exhibits in the case.
(5) Where no proceedings are commenced within fourteen days from the date of the
confiscation of any loudspeaker or musical instrument under this section that loudspeaker or
musical instrument shall be forthwith returned to the person from whom it was confiscated.
(6) The Magistrate hearing the complaint may, in addition to imposing any of the
penalties provided for in this Act make any of the orders set out in subsection (7) as he or
she may think fit in respect of the loudspeaker or musical instrument (hereinafter referred
to as “the equipment”) confiscated under subsection (3):
Provided that if in the opinion of the magistrate the equipment ought to be forfeited to
the Crown and the magistrate makes an order to that effect, any person entitled to
possession of the equipment ordered to be forfeited, whether or not that person was a

party to the proceedings before the magistrate, may, within seven days of such order,
lodge an appeal to a judge in chambers against such order.
(7) The orders referred to in subsection (6) are as follows—

that the equipment or any of them be returned forthwith by the police to
specified premises or to specified persons;

(b)

that the equipment or any of them be detained in the custody of the police for
a specified period subsequent to the date of the order not exceeding three
months in respect of each offence, and that at the end of the specified period
the equipment thus detained be returned immediately thereafter by the police
to specified premises or to specified persons;

(c)

that the equipment or any of them be forfeited to the Crown.

(8) No liability shall arise on the part of the police for any action taken, or in respect
of any loudspeaker or musical instrument removed under subsection (3) or ordered to be
detained under subsection (6).
23\. Application for return of equipment
(1) Where any equipment has been forfeited under section 22, a magistrate may, on
application by a claimant of the equipment other than the person in whose case the
forfeiture order was made, make an order for delivery of the equipment to the applicant if
it appears to the court that he or she is the owner of the equipment.
(2) No application may be made under subsection (1) by any claimant of the
equipment after expiry of the period of twenty eight days beginning with the date of the
forfeiture order which was made in respect of the equipment.
(3) A person who brings an application under subsection (1) must satisfy the court—
(a)

that he or she had not consented to the offender having possession of the
equipment; or

(b)

that he or she did not know, and had no reason to suspect, that the equipment
was likely to be used in the commission of an offence under this Act.

(4) Where the Commissioner of Police is of the opinion that the person in whose case
the forfeiture order was made is not the owner of the equipment, he or she shall take
reasonable steps to bring to the attention of the person who may be entitled to do so, his
or her right to make an application under subsection (1).
(5) An order under subsection (1) does not affect the right of any person to take,
within the period of twenty eight days beginning with the date of the order, proceedings for
the recovery of the equipment from the person in possession of it in pursuance of the order.
(6) If no application is made under this section within twenty eight days of the
forfeiture order, the Commissioner of Police may dispose of the equipment.
24\. Civil proceedings
(1) Nothing contained in this Act shall be held to affect the right of any person to
institute or pursue civil proceedings in the High Court or in a magistrate’s court
(including but not limited to action for damages for nuisance) against any person or
authority in respect of any complaint or grievance involving noise or vibration, and such
civil proceedings may be prosecuted in respect of acts and omissions which constituted
the subject matter of complaints under this Act:
Provided that in making any award or order in such civil proceedings as last
mentioned, the relevant court may take into account the results of any prior proceedings
under this Act.

(2) On the conviction of any person in respect of any complaint under this Act a
magistrate, in addition to imposing the penalties prescribed under this Act, may award
damages and costs (subject to the maximum amounts prescribed by the written laws
governing the monetary jurisdiction of magistrate’s courts generally) payable by the
person or persons convicted of an offence under this Act, and payable to the person or
persons found by the magistrate to have been adversely affected (whether materially,
physically, psychologically or otherwise) by the act or omission on which the conviction
was based.
25\. Inconsistencies
Subject to section 26 and except as is otherwise provided in this Act, where there
occurs any inconsistency between the provisions of this Act and the provisions of any other
written law, the provisions of this Act to the extent of such inconsistency, shall prevail.
26\. Appeal to Court of Appeal
(1) Subject to the provisions hereof providing for appeals in certain cases to a judge in
chambers, there shall be preserved to all parties (both prosecution and defence) the right
to appeal to the Court of Appeal from decisions of a magistrate or the High Court in
accordance with all enactments regulating appeals from Magistrates courts and from the
High Court to the Court of Appeal.
27\. Non-derogation from other legislation
The provisions of this Act shall be supplementary to and not in derogation of the
provisions of all written laws now in force in respect of the regulation of town and
country planning, and where a power or authority is exercisable under this Act and a
similar power or authority is exercisable under any other written law, whether or not in
relation to the same person who is likely to be affected by the exercise of the power or
authority, that power or authority may be exercised pursuant to either or both, whether
simultaneously or successively, as the person exercising the same may deem fit:
Provided that a court may, at any stage in any proceedings before it, take cognisance of
the exercise of that power or authority (and the known consequences thereof) and may
adjudicate upon the matter then before it, in the light of those known consequences.
28\. Penalties
(1) Any person who is guilty of an offence under this Act is liable on summary
conviction—
(a)

in the case of a first offence, to a fine of two hundred and fifty dollars;

(b)

in the case of a second offence, to a fine not exceeding five hundred dollars
or to imprisonment for six months, or to both;

(c)

in the case of a third offence, to a fine not exceeding one thousand dollars or
to imprisonment for nine months, or to both; and

(d)

in the case of a fourth offence, to imprisonment for twelve months.

(2) A magistrate, in dealing with any complaint under this Act, may order the
production before him or her of any loudspeaker or musical instrument in respect of
which it has been alleged or proved that an offence has been committed under this Act,
and upon such production the magistrate may, upon the conviction of any person, in
addition to the penalties provided in subsection (1), make an order under section 22(7) in
relation to such loudspeaker or musical instrument notwithstanding the circumstances that
such loudspeaker or musical instrument had not been detained by the police pursuant to
section 22(3).

(3) The powers conferred on a magistrate by subsection (2) may be exercised by him
or her either on his or her own motion or on the application of a party to the proceedings.
(4) The provisions of section 22(6) relating to appeals to a judge in chambers shall
apply to any decision by a magistrate in respect of forfeiture pursuant to the powers
conferred by subsection (2).
29\. Form
(1) The Form set out in the Schedule hereto may be used in connection with
applications made pursuant to section 18.
(2) The Form referred to in subsection (1) may be amended by the Minister.
30\. Regulations
The Minister may make regulations generally for carrying out the purposes of this Act
for providing for such matters as are contemplated by or necessary for giving full effect to
this Act and for its due administration.
31\. Commencement
This Act comes into force on the 1st day of September, 2006.
