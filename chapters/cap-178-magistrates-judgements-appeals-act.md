## CHAPTER 178

MAGISTRATES JUDGEMENTS (APPEALS) ACT
An Act relating to appeals to the Court of Appeal from the judgements of
Magistrates Courts.
[Act No. 22 of 1991 amended by SRO 15 of 1991, SRO 22 of 1991, SRO 26 of 1991, Act No. 52
of 1991, Act No. 5 of 1995.]

[1st August, 1991.]

## PART I

Preliminary

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Magistrates Judgements (Appeals) Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires—
“Court” means the Court of Appeal of the Supreme Court established by the West
Indies Associated States Supreme Court Order, 1967;

“judgement” means any final adjudication of a Magistrate in a cause or matter
before him or her and includes any nonsuit, dismissal, judgement, conviction, order or
other determination of the cause or matter;
“Magistrate’s Court” means a court constituted and established under and by virtue
of the Magistrates Act; Chapter 177, and includes for purposes of this Act, the Family
Court constituted and established under and by virtue of the Family Court Act,

## Chapter 100B, as from the commencement of the whole of the Family Court Act,

## Chapter 100B;

“Registrar” means the Registrar of the Court.

## PART II

### 3.

Right of appeal
Unless the contrary is in any case expressly provided by any Act any party may appeal
from the judgement of a Magistrate to the Court in the manner and subject to the
conditions hereinafter mentioned:
Provided that there shall be no appeal—
(a)

in civil cases where the sum claimed is less than twenty-four dollars, save by
leave of the Court granted on an ex parte application;

(b)

in criminal cases from any judgement dismissing the complaint, save by
leave of the Director of Public Prosecutions.

### 4.

Notice of appeal
(1) A party who desires to appeal shall—
(a)

at the time of the pronouncing of the judgement, give verbal notice to the
Magistrate and the opposite party of his or her intention to appeal; or

(b)

within fourteen days after the pronouncing of the judgement, serve notice in
writing in the Form A in the First Schedule hereto of his or her appeal upon
the Magistrate and upon the opposite party.

(2) Where the appellant is in prison, the Commissioner of Prisons shall, on being
requested by the appellant to do so, render to the appellant all reasonable assistance in the
preparation of the notice of appeal and shall cause it to be lodged and a copy thereof to be
served as required above without payment of any fees or expenses.

### 5.

Security to be given
(1) The appellant shall also, within fourteen days after the judgement appealed against
is pronounced, enter into a recognisance, in the Form B in the First Schedule hereto,
before the Magistrate, with or without sureties in the discretion of the Magistrate,
conditioned for the due prosecution of the appeal and for abiding the order of the Court,
or if the Magistrate thinks it expedient the appellant may, instead of entering into a
recognisance, give such other security by deposit of money with the Magistrate or
otherwise as the Magistrate deems sufficient.
(2) Where the Crown is the appellant, it shall be sufficient for the officer acting on
behalf of the Crown to give notice in writing to the Magistrate and to the other party
without entering into a recognisance; and if the Crown fails to prosecute the appeal to a

final determination, the other party shall be entitled to recover his or her costs of the
appeal from the Crown on the production of a taxed bill of costs signed by the Registrar.

### 6.

Suspension of execution
Upon notice of appeal being given under section 4 and upon such recognisance being
taken on money being received as required by section 5 the execution of the judgement
under appeal shall be suspended until the appeal is determined by the Court or is
abandoned, and if the appellant is in custody, he or she may be released on the order of
the Magistrate:
Provided that nothing herein shall prevent the Magistrate from granting a stay of
execution at the time of his or her judgement on such terms as he or she thinks proper.

### 7.

Where recognisance not entered into
(1) A defendant who, on being committed to prison on a Magistrate’s conviction or
order, shall have given notice of appeal and is unable to find the necessary surety or
sureties or give such other security in lieu thereof, may prosecute his or her appeal
without entering into a recognisance; provided he or she remains in custody pending the
hearing of the appeal, and in such case, the Magistrate shall, by warrant under his or her
hand, direct the appellant to be detained in custody accordingly, and shall in such warrant
intimate to the Commissioner of Prisons that notice has been given of appeal.
(2) The appellant shall in such a case be detained in custody and may be taken
without any fresh order or warrant in custody by a constable to the Court to attend the
hearing of his or her appeal.
(3) The Magistrate shall, when the appellant is detained in custody as aforesaid,
transmit to the Registrar within three days of the judgement, a copy of the proceedings in
which the judgement has been appealed against.
(4) The Court shall hear and determine with all possible dispatch any appeal in which
the appellant is detained in custody as aforesaid.
(5) Notwithstanding anything in this section contained, an appellant may, at any time
before his or her appeal is heard, enter into a recognisance or give such other security as
provided for in section 5 and thereupon he or she may be released in accordance with
section 6 unless he or she is in custody in respect of any other charge or matter.

### 8.

Serving notice of reasons for appeal
(1) The appellant shall also, within twenty-eight days after the judgement appealed
against is pronounced, serve upon the Magistrate and upon the opposite party, notice in
writing of the reasons for his or her appeal in the Form C in the First Schedule hereto:
Provided that the Court may extend the time for such service for any sufficient reason.
(2) The provisions of section 4(2) shall apply to a notice of reasons for appeal.

### 9.

Service of notice of reasons for appeal
A notice of reasons for appeal may be served either at the time of giving or serving
notice of appeal or at some other time within the time hereinbefore specified, and may be
either embodied in the notice of appeal when such notice is in writing or in a separate
document.

### 10.

Signing of notice
(1) Every notice of appeal in writing and every notice of grounds of appeal shall be
signed by the appellant or by his or her counsel or solicitor.
(2) If the appellant signs as a marksman, his or her signature shall be verified by at
least one person, not a minor, who shall add to his or her signature the address of his or
her usual place of abode.

### 11.

Manner of setting forth reasons for appeal
An appellant shall set forth in his or her notice of reasons for appeal the particular
matter on which he or she relies or of which he or she complains.

### 12.

Right of party to have copy of proceedings
(1) Every party to a cause or matter in a Magistrate’s Court in which the judgement is
subject to appeal shall be entitled, after such judgement has been pronounced, on making
application therefore to the Magistrate, and on paying the proper fee therefore, to receive
from the Magistrate a copy of the proceedings as described in section 15, including a copy,
if any, of the memorandum of the reasons of the Magistrate for the judgement.
(2) Any such application may be made by the party himself or herself or by his or her
counsel or solicitor.
(3) Such copy of the proceedings shall be furnished by the Magistrate as soon as
practicable, and not later than seven days after the application therefore.

### 13.

Leave to appeal
(1) If any person entitled to appeal is unavoidably prevented from so doing in the
manner or within the time hereinbefore specified, he or she may apply to the Court for
special leave to appeal.
(2) Where the leave referred to in subsection (1) is granted, the provisions of
sections 4 and 5 shall apply as though the date for granting leave where the date of the
pronouncement of the judgement by the Magistrate.

### 14.

Procedure where appellant makes default in prosecuting appeal
(1) If the appellant in criminal cases makes default in duly prosecuting his or her
appeal, or at any stage notifies the Registrar that he or she does not intend to proceed
therewith, the Registrar shall forthwith transmit to the Magistrate a certificate to that
effect in the Form D in the First Schedule hereto and the Magistrate shall thereupon either
treat the recognisance as forfeited and deal with the same in accordance with the
provisions of the Criminal Procedure Code, Chapter 72B, relating to forfeiture of
recognisance, or make such order as may be just with respect to the amount lodged by the
appellant, as the case may be.
(2) If the appellant, making such default, has been released from custody, and if he or
she remains or becomes liable to be kept in custody, the Magistrate shall forthwith issue a
warrant for his or her apprehension, in order that he or she may be rendered to prison
accordingly.

### 15.

Transmission by Magistrate of proceedings in case under appeal

(1) On the appellant giving or serving him or her with notice of appeal and serving
him or her with notice of grounds or reasons for appeal, and also complying with the
requirements of section 5, the Magistrate shall fourteen days after such compliance has
been made, transmit to the Registrar a copy of the proceedings in the case, duly certified
under his or her hand consisting of a copy of the complaint or information and plea or of
the plaint and defence, as the case may be, of the notes of evidence taken in the case, and
of the adjudication, together with copies of the notice of appeal if such notice is in
writing, and of the notice of grounds or reasons for appeal served on the Magistrate, of
the recognisance, if any, and of all other documents connected with the case.
(2) It shall not be necessary for the Magistrate to transmit any conviction or order
formally drawn up. In all cases of convictions and orders, it shall be sufficient for the
Magistrate to transmit in place of such formal instrument, a copy of the notes or
memorandum of the conviction or order required to be made under the provisions of the
Criminal Procedure Code, Chapter 72B.
(3) The Magistrate shall also transmit with the copy of the proceedings a concise
memorandum of the reasons for the judgement. Such memorandum shall be included in,
and form part of, the record of the appeal.

## PART III

Hearing and Judgement

### 16.

Sittings of Court
The Court shall be held on the second Tuesday in every month for the hearing of
appeals under this Act or on such other day as may, by rules made under this Act, be
appointed.

### 17.

Special sittings of Court
Notwithstanding anything contained in the last preceding section, it shall be lawful for
the Chief Justice to fix special sittings of the Court from time to time as circumstances
require for the purpose of hearing appeals under the provisions of section 7.

### 18.

Power of adjournment
The Court, or in the absence of a Judge of the Court, the Registrar may adjourn any
sitting of the Court or the hearing of any appeal for such time and to such hour of the day
as may be deemed expedient.

### 19.

Procedure where appellant fails to appear
(1) If, on the day of hearing or at any adjournment thereof, the appellant does not
appear, the case shall be struck out and the judgement affirmed, unless the Court thinks fit
for sufficient cause to order otherwise.
(2) If in the cases referred to in subsection (1) the respondent appears, the judgement
shall be with costs of the appeal against the appellant unless the Court expressly orders
otherwise; but if the respondent does not appear, the costs of the appeal shall be in the
discretion of the Court.

### 20.

Procedure where appellant appears

If on the hearing and at every adjournment of the case the appellant appears, the Court
shall proceed to the hearing or further hearing and the determination of the case:
Provided that where any respondent is in default of appearance at the time fixed for the
hearing of an appeal, the Court shall not proceed to hear the appeal as against him or her
unless and until it is satisfied by affidavit or otherwise that the provisions of sections 4, 5
and 8 have been observed.

### 21.

Limitation of hearing by grounds of appeal
On the hearing, it shall not be competent for the appellant to go into, or to give
evidence of, any grounds of appeal other than those set forth in his or her notice of
grounds or reasons for appeal unless the Court otherwise orders and on the terms it deems
just.

### 22.

Objections to defect in the form of grounds appeal
On the hearing, on objection to any defect in the form of stating any ground or reason
of appeal shall be allowed and no objection to the reception of evidence offered in
support of any ground of appeal shall prevail unless the Court is of opinion that the
ground of appeal is so imperfectly or incorrectly set forth as to be insufficient to enable
the respondent to inquire into the subject of the statement and to prepare for the hearing,
but the Court may, if it thinks fit, cause the ground of appeal to be forthwith amended
upon the terms and conditions, if any, the Court thinks just.

### 23.

Objection to defect in the form of information or conviction
If, on the hearing, any objection is made on account of any defect in a complaint or
information, or on account of any omission or mistake in the drawing up of a conviction
or order, and if it is shown, to the satisfaction of the Court, that sufficient grounds were in
proof before the Magistrate who made the conviction or order to have authorised the
drawing up thereof free from that omission or mistake, the Court shall amend the
complaint or information, or the conviction or order, and proceed thereafter as if the
defect, omission, or mistake had not existed. Nothing in this section shall affect the
provisions of section 21.

### 24.

Defects in proceedings under appeal
On an appeal, no objection shall be taken or allowed to any proceedings in a
Magistrate’s Court for any defect or error which might have been amended by that Court,
or to any complaint, summons, warrant, or other process to or of that Court for any
alleged defect therein in substance or in form, or for any variance between any complaint
or summons and the evidence adduced in support thereof in that Court:
Provided that if any error, defect or variance mentioned in this section appears to the
Court that the hearing of an appeal to be such that the appellant has been thereby
deceived or misled, the Court may either refer the cause back to the Magistrate with
directions to re-hear and determine it, or reverse the decision under appeal, or may make
any other order for disposal of the cause which justice requires.

### 25.

Error or defect in notice of appeal or recognisance
No objection shall be taken or allowed on an appeal to any notice of appeal which is in
writing, or to any recognisance entered into under this Act for the due prosecution of the
appeal, for any alleged error or defect therein; but if the error or defect appears to the

Court to be such that the respondent on the appeal has been thereby deceived or misled,
the Court may amend it and, if it is expedient to do so, may also adjourn the further
hearing of the appeal, the amendment and the adjournment, if any, being made on any
terms the Court deems just.

### 26.

Power of the Court with regard to fresh evidence
The Court may, in any case where it considers it necessary that first evidence should
be adduced, either—
(a)

order that evidence be adduced before the Court on some day to be fixed in
that behalf, whereupon any witness required under the provisions of this
section shall be summoned or produced in the same manner, and
remunerated at the same rate and be subject to the same pains and penalties
and
liabilities for non-attendance, as are provided in respect of witnesses
summoned to attend at the hearing or trial of a civil case before the Court; or

(b)

refer the case to the Magistrate to take that evidence, and in that case either
direct the Magistrate to adjudicate afresh after taking the evidence subject to
such directions in law, if any, as the Court thinks fit to give, or may direct
him or her, after taking the evidence, to report specific findings of fact for
the information of the Court; and on any such reference the case shall, so far
as may be practicable and necessary, be so dealt with as if it were being
heard in the first instance. On the conclusion of the hearing the Court shall
either at the same or subsequent sitting of the court, pronounce judgement on
the appeal.

### 27.

Powers of Court in giving judgement
(1) In giving its decision the Court may—
(a)

affirm, modify, amend, or reverse the decision, either in whole or in part
and, if the Court thinks that a different sentence should have been passed,
quash the sentence passed by the Magistrate and pass such other sentence
warranted in law (whether more or less severe) in substitution therefor as the
Court thinks should have been passed;

(b)

refer the case back to the Magistrate with directions to re-hear the same, or
otherwise to deal with the same as the Court may think just, and thereafter
either to return the case to the Court for further hearing and determination, or
to determine the same, as the Court may think fit, or refer the case back as
mentioned in section 26; and in every such case the provisions of
section 26(b) shall apply; or

(c)

make such other order for disposing of the case as justice may require.

(2) The Court may, so far as may be necessary for doing complete justice between the
parties, review any order made by the Magistrate.
(3) Upon the hearing of every appeal in a criminal cause or matter the Court, shall
record its judgement in writing, and every such judgement shall contain the point or
points for determination, the decision therein, and the reasons for the decision, and shall
be dated and signed by the Court at the time of pronouncing it.

### 28.

Transmission and publication of judgement

(1) Within eight days after the pronouncement of the decision of the Court, the
Registrar shall transmit a certified copy thereof to the Magistrate of the Court from which
the appeal came.
(2) Any person may, on paying the fee for office copy documents, obtain from the
Registrar a copy of the decision.
(3) Where a judgement is varied or reversed on appeal, the Registrar shall without
delay cause the decision of the Court to be published in the Gazette.

### 29.

Enforcing judgement
(1) After the pronouncement of the decision of the Court, and subject to the
provisions hereafter in this section contained, the Magistrate of the Court from which the
appeal came shall have the same jurisdiction and power to enforce, and shall enforce any
judgement which has been affirmed, modified or amended by the Court, or any decision
pronounced by the Court in the same manner in all respects as if that decision or
judgement had been pronounced by himself or herself:
Provided that in any case where an order for the imprisonment of a person is affirmed
on appeal, whether with or without modification or amendment, the Court may, if it
considers it expedient to do so, forthwith commit the person to prison in pursuance and in
execution of the order.
(2) The imprisonment of the person (if it has not previously commenced) shall be
reckoned to begin from the day on which he or she is arrested to be taken to the prison
wherein he or she has been ordered to be imprisoned; and, if the person has been
discharged from imprisonment on giving the security hereinbefore mentioned, he or she
shall be imprisoned for such further period as, with the time during which he or she has
been already imprisoned, is equal to the period for which he or she was ordered to be
imprisoned as aforesaid.

## PART IV

Costs

### 30.

Award of costs
(1) Where an appeal is abandoned before the hearing, the Registrar may, on
application made in that behalf, order any costs occasioned by the appeal to be paid to
anyone by whom they have been reasonably incurred.
(2) In any other case the Court may make any order as to costs, both in the
Magistrate’s Court and in the Court, as it thinks just; if no order is made, costs shall
follow the event.
(3) The costs occasioned by any application to the Court shall be in the discretion of
the Court.

### 31.

Taxation of costs
(1) The Court may, at any time of making an order as to costs, fix the amount thereof,
but if no amount is fixed, costs shall be taxed by the Registrar.
(2) The Registrar, when transmitting a certified copy of the decision as required by
section 28 (1), shall also send to the Magistrate a certificate in Form E in the First
Schedule to this Act of the amount of costs fixed or taxed.

### 32.

Payment of costs
On receipt of the certificate in the last preceding section mentioned, the Magistrate
shall add to the amount therein specified the costs of any copy of proceedings obtained
from him or her by the successful party for the purposes of the appeal and, at the request
of that party, proceed to pay or enforce payment of the total costs as follows—
(a)

if money was deposited as security for the payment of costs, he or she shall
pay the costs of the successful respondent out of the money if it is sufficient;

(b)

if money was not deposited, or if the money deposited is insufficient to pay
all the costs, he or she shall enforce the payment of the costs, or of any
balance due, by enforcing any recognisance entered into by the appellant, or
otherwise in accordance with the provisions of any Act for the enforcement
of costs in Magistrates’ Courts, according as the decision appealed against
was given in the exercise of summary jurisdiction, whether criminal or civil.

### 33.

Table of fees and costs
(1) The table of fees and costs set forth in the Second Schedule hereto shall govern the
fixing or taxation of costs payable for and in respect of the several matters therein
mentioned.
(2) The Chief Justice may delete from, vary or add to the said table of fees and costs
by order published in the Gazette.

## PART V

Miscellaneous Provisions

### 34.

Service of document
Any notice or other document required to be served or transmitted under this Act may
be served or transmitted by registered post or may be served by delivering or leaving it at
the last known place of abode of the party to be served.

### 35.

Power to make rules
The Chief Justice may by order published in the Gazette make rules not inconsistent
with the provisions of this Act for the following matters—
(a)

regulating the practice and procedure of the Court;

(b)

altering the times for the sittings of the Court and fixing special sittings of
the Court for any special purposes;

(c)

stipulating the conditions under which appeals may be heard in cases in
which a defendant is unable to enter into a recognisance or give other
security for the prosecution of an appeal.

### 36.

Commencement
This Act shall come into force on such date as may be fixed by the Governor-General
by proclamation published in the Gazette.
