## CHAPTER 249

POWER-CRAFT ACT
An Act to regulate the operation and use of power-craft, and for connected matters.
[Act No. 22 of 1966 amended by Act No. 15 of 1987.]

[8th October, 1966.]

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Power-Craft Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act—
“operate” means drive a power-craft while it is being propelled through water by
means, wholly or partly, of machinery;
“power-craft” means any water-borne craft capable of a speed of or exceeding five
knots and of being used as a means of conveyance when propelled directly or
indirectly by machinery of any kind, whether or not the machinery is the principal
method of propulsion, but does not include—
(a)

Her Majesty’s Ships and harbour craft;

(b)

ships and craft in the service of a foreign power;

(c)

sea-going vessels engaged on voyages to or from places outside Grenada;

(d)

ships employed in the coasting trade of Grenada;

(e)

ships’ lifeboats;

(f)

flying-boats, sea-planes, and other types of aircraft capable of landing on the
water;

(g)

yachts with auxiliary engines;

(h)

boats engaged in fishing for commercial purposes;

(i)

any other craft or class of craft exempted by the Minister by regulations
made under section 11.

### 3.

Restrictions on the operation of power-craft
Without prejudice to any other written law relating to the navigation of vessels, a
person shall not operate a power-craft within the territorial sea of Grenada unless—
(a)

he has attained the age of sixteen years; and

(b)

the power-craft is validly licensed in accordance with the regulations,

and any person who contravenes this section shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on
summary conviction, to a fine of five hundred dollars and to imprisonment for one month.

### 4.

Restrictions on the use of power-craft, etc.
(1) A person who operates a power-craft within the territorial sea of Grenada in any
manner dangerous to navigation or to the life or limb of any other person, having regard
to all the circumstances including the nature and condition of the waters and the use that
at the time is or might reasonably be expected to be made of those waters, shall be guilty
of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of three thousand dollars, and
in default of payment to imprisonment for six months.
(2) A person who operates a power-craft within two hundred metres from the shore
shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of three
thousand dollars, and in default of payment to imprisonment for six months.
(3) A person who operates a power-craft within the territorial sea of Grenada so as to
create a nuisance to any other person or boat (whether anchored or not) shall be guilty of
an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand dollars, and in
default of payment to imprisonment for one month.
(4) A person who operates a power-craft within the territorial sea of Grenada while
towing another person on a water-ski, surf-board, water-sleigh or other object of a similar
nature when there is not on board the power-craft at least one individual of or above the
age of sixteen years keeping watch on the person being towed shall be guilty of an
offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand dollars, and in
default of payment to imprisonment for two months.
(5) A person who operates a power-craft within the territorial sea of Grenada while
his or her ability to operate the power-craft is impaired by alcohol or a drug shall be
guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of five hundred dollars,
and in default of payment to imprisonment for one month.

### 5.

Procedure in case of accident
(1) Where a power-craft is involved in an accident or collision, the person having
care, charge or control thereof shall give, on demand, his or her name and address and the
number of the licence of the power-craft to any person injured and the owner of any
property damaged thereby; and if in consequence of an accident or collision any other

person is injured or appears to require assistance, he or she shall offer assistance to that
person.
(2) An accident involving a power-craft shall be reported, by the person operating the
same at the time of the accident, to a police officer or to the Port Manager as soon as it is
practicable for him or her to do so and, in any event, within twelve hours of the
occurrence.
(3) A person who contravenes any requirement of subsection (1) or (2) shall be guilty
of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of five hundred dollars and to
imprisonment for one month.

### 6.

Additional punishment for contravention of section 4
When a person is convicted of an offence for any contravention of section 4 the court,
in addition to any other punishment that may be imposed for that offence, may order—
(a)

that he or she be prohibited from operating a power-craft for a specified
period not exceeding one year; and

(b)

that the licence relating to the power-craft be cancelled for such period not
exceeding one year as to the court seems proper.

### 7.

Operating power-craft while prohibited, or after licence cancelled
A person who, in contravention of an order of a court under section 6, operates a
power-craft within the territorial sea of Grenada while prohibited from doing so, or while
the licence with respect to the power-craft remains cancelled, shall be guilty of an offence
and liable; on summary conviction, to a fine of three thousand dollars, and in default of
payment to imprisonment for six months.

### 8.

Powers of arrest without warrant
Without prejudice to any other power of arrest without warrant, the Port Manager or
any police officer may, without warrant, arrest a person operating or aboard a power-craft
whom he or she has reason to suspect of having committed an offence against section 4 or
who contrary to section 5(1) has failed to give his or her name and address or the number
of the licence of a power-craft.

### 9.

Power to board power-craft and to require information
(1) Where the Port Manager or a police officer has reason to suspect that an offence
concerning a power-craft has been committed against any provision of this Act or the
regulations made hereunder, it shall be lawful for him or her—
(a)

to board the power-craft and address such pertinent enquiries to the
occupants thereof as he or she may deem necessary;

(b)

to require the owner of the power-craft to give all information in his or her
possession as to the name, address and description of the operator, and of the
occupants of the power-craft, at the time of the suspected offence, together
with details of the present whereabouts of any or all of the foregoing.

(2) A person who wilfully neglects or refuses to give information required of him or
her under the preceding subsection shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary
conviction, to a fine of five hundred dollars, and in default of payment to imprisonment
for one month.

### 10.

Proof of speed of power-craft

In proceedings under this Act the burden of proving that a power-craft was not capable
of attaining a speed of five knots shall lie on the person alleging the same.

### 11.

Power of Minister to make regulations
The Minister may, by regulations made under this section—
(a)

regulate the navigation, operation and conduct of power-craft;

(b)

provide for the issue of licences under this Act of power-craft, instead of
licensing the same under any other written law, and for the fees to be paid
for such licences;

(c)

provide for the exemption of any craft or class of craft from the provisions of
this Act; and

(d)

make provision generally for the better carrying out of the purposes of this
Act,

and may provide penalties not exceeding a fine of five hundred dollars and in default of
payment imprisonment for one month in respect of any contravention of the regulations.

POWER CRAFT (LICENSING) REGULATIONS
[SRO 26 of 2000.]

[1st September, 2000.]
1\. Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Power Craft (Licensing) Regulations.
2\. Definitions
In these Regulations—
“Act” means the power-craft Act, Chapter 249;
“Director” means the Director of Maritime Affairs under the Shipping Act,

## Chapter 303.

3\. Application of these Regulations
These Regulations apply to—
(a)

all power-craft within the meaning of the Power-Craft Act, except yachts
licensed or registered under the Yachting Act, Chapter 345, and power-craft
licensed by the Grenada Ports Authority;

(b)

fishing boats licensed or registered with the Department of Fisheries but
which are engaged in other commercial activities.

4\. Application for licence
An application for a licence to operate a power-craft, for the purpose of section 3 of
Act, shall be made to the Director in Form 1 of the First Schedule.
5\. Power to inspect
(1) The Director may inspect or cause to be inspected, for purpose of sea-worthiness
and safety, any power-craft in respect of which an application for a licence has been
submitted to him.
(2) For the purposes of paragraph (1), the Director may carry out or cause to be
carried out such tests as may be appropriate in terms of sea-worthiness and safety of any
power-craft.
(3) Without prejudice to anything contained in paragraph (1), the Director may carry
out periodic inspections of any power-craft licensed under these Regulations.
6\. Issue of licence
(1) Where the Director is satisfied as to the sea-worthiness and safety of the powercraft and the suitability of the applicant to operate the said craft, he or she shall issue a
licence in Form 2 of the First Schedule and which licence must be laminated.
(2) For the purposes of determining suitability under paragraph (1), the Director may
carry out such tests as he or she considers to be reasonable and appropriate.
(3) A licence issued by the Director is valid for twelve months from the date of issue
and must be renewed on the anniversary of such issue.
(4) In addition to the licence, the Director shall issue an identification number bearing
the prefix “PC” which identification number shall be painted on or affixed to the powercraft in such manner as the Director approves.

7\. Conditions of licence
The Director may attach such conditions to a licence as he or she considers
appropriate in each case.
8\. Revocation, suspension and variation of licence
(1) The Director may revoke a licence or suspend a licence for a breach of the PowerCraft Act, these Regulations or any condition subject to which the licence was issued for
such period as he or she considers appropriate in each case.
(2) Where a licence is revoked or suspended the licence shall surrender the licence to
the Director.
(3) The Director may vary a licence so as to add new conditions to such licence.
9\. Appeals
A person who is aggrieved by a decision of the Director under regulation 8 may within
thirty days of such decision, appeal to the Minister in such form as the Minister approves.
10\. Fees
The fees payable for licences under these Regulations are prescribed in the Second
Schedule.
