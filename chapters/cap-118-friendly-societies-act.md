## CHAPTER 118

FRIENDLY SOCIETIES ACT
An Act to make provision with respect to the constitution, registration,
incorporation, and management of friendly and other societies.
[Amended by Act No. 18 of 1964, Act No. 1 of 2008.]

[1st July, 1958.]
Preliminary

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Friendly Societies Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act—
“amendment of rule” includes a new rule and a resolution rescinding a rule;
“Authority” means the Grenada Authority for the Regulation of Financial
Institutions established under the Grenada Authority for the Regulation of Financial
Institutions Act;
“branch” means any number of the members of a society under the control of a
central body, having a separate fund administered by themselves or by a committee or
by officers appointed by themselves and bound to contribute to a fund under the
control of a central body;
“meeting” includes (where the rules of the society so allow) a meeting of delegates
appointed by members;
“officer” includes any trustee, treasurer, secretary, or member of committee of
management of the society or branch and any person appointed by the society or
branch to sue and be sued on its behalf;
“person claiming through a member” includes the personal representative,
executors, administrators, heirs, and assigns of a member and also his or her nominees
where nomination is allowed;
“property” extends to all property, whether real or personal (including books and
papers);
“registered society” means a society registered or deemed to be registered under
this Act;
“rules” means rules for the time being in force;
“society” applies to a registered branch unless such application is inconsistent with
the position and objects of a branch.
Constitution, Registration and Incorporation

### 3.

Societies which may be registered

The following societies may be registered under this Act—
(a)

friendly societies established to provide, by voluntary subscriptions of the
members thereof with or without the aid of donations—
(i) for the relief or maintenance of the members, their wives, husbands,
children, fathers, mothers, brothers, sisters, nephews, nieces, or wards
being orphans, during sickness or other infirmity, whether bodily or
mental, in old age (which shall mean any age after fifty), or in
widowhood, or for the relief or maintenance of the orphan children of
members during minority,
(ii) for insuring monies to be paid on the birth of a member’s child, or on
the death of a member, or for the funeral expenses of the wife,
husband, or child of a member, or of the widow of a deceased
member,
(iii) for the relief or maintenance of the members when on travel in search
of employment, or when in distressed circumstances, or in the case of
shipwreck or loss or damage of or to boats or nets,
(iv) for the endowment of members or nominees of members at any age,
and
(v) for the insurance against fire, to any amount not exceeding seventyfive dollars in each case, of the tools or implements of the trade or
calling of the members:
Provided that no such society which contracts with any person for the
assurance of an annuity exceeding two hundred and fifty dollars per
annum, or of a gross sum exceeding one thousand dollars, shall be
registered under this Act;

(b)

loan societies for establishing a fund for making loans to members and taking
payment of the same, whether by instalments or otherwise, with interest;

(c)

co-operative societies for the carrying on of any labour, trade, or handicraft,
whether wholesale or retail, including the farming or cultivation of land and
the manufacture of the produce of land;

(d)

benevolent societies for any benevolent or charitable purposes;

(e)

working men’s clubs for purposes of social intercourse, mutual helpfulness,
mental and moral improvement, and rational recreation; and

(f)

authorised societies for any purpose which the Minister may authorise as a
purpose to which the powers and facilities of this Act ought to be extended.

### 4.

Limitation of application
The Minister may limit the application of this Act in respect of specially authorised
societies to such of the provisions herein contained as may be specified in the authority
for registering any such society.

### 5.

Prohibition of registration of banking society
No society for carrying on the business of banking shall be registered under this Act:
Provided that the taking of deposits of not more than two dollars and fifty cents in any
one payment or more than one hundred dollars for any one depositor, payable on not less
than two days’ notice, shall not be deemed to be included in the business of banking
within the meaning of this Act if the society has not a withdrawable share capital.

### 6.

Limitation of member’s interest in society

(1) No member, other than a registered society, shall have or claim an interest
exceeding one thousand dollars in the funds of any registered society other than a cooperative society.
(2) No member, other than a registered society, shall have or claim an interest
exceeding two thousand dollars in the funds of any registered co-operative society.

### 7.

Appointment and remuneration of Authority
The Authority shall be entitled to demand and receive fees as set out in the Third
Schedule.

### 8.

Office of Authority
The Authority shall keep his or her office in St. George’s.

### 9.

Clerical assistance for Authority
The Authority shall, if the Minister deems it necessary, be furnished from time to time
with clerical assistance out of any monies which may be provided by the House of
Representatives for that purpose.

### 10.

General duties and powers of Authority
(1) The Authority shall keep a register, of all Societies registered under this Act, in
such form as may be approved by the Minister, and shall discharge all the duties required
of him or her by this Act and regulations made thereunder.
(2) The Minister may direct the Authority to do all or any of the following things—
(a)

prepare and cause to be circulated, for the use of societies, model forms of
accounts, balance sheets, and valuations;

(b)

collect from the returns under this Act, and from other sources, and publish
and circulate, either generally or in a particular district, or otherwise make
known, such information on the subject of the statistics of sickness and
mortality, and the application thereof to the business of friendly societies,
and from time to time publish, either generally or in particular districts, such
particulars of their returns and valuations, and such other information useful
to the members of or to persons interested in friendly or other societies
registered, or which might be registered under this Act, as the Minister may
from time to time think fit; and

(c)

cause to be constructed and published tables for payment of sums of monies
on death, in sickness, or in old age, or on any other contingency forming the
subject of an assurance authorised under this Act which may appear to be
calculable.

(3) The adoption of such model forms and tables by a society if required by the
Authority shall be compulsory:
Provided that a society aggrieved by the requirement of the Authority to adopt any
such forms and tables may appeal therefrom to the Minister whose decision shall be final.
A society which after having been required by the Authority or, in the case of appeal, by
the Minister as the case may be, to adopt such forms and tables, neglects or refuses to do
so shall be guilty of an offence.
(4) The Authority, or a person appointed by him or her in writing for the purpose,
may, on giving at least seven days’ notice thereof to the secretary of a society, attend any
meeting of such society for the purpose of offering advice to the meeting or to the
committee of management of the society.

### 11.

Annual report of Authority
The Minister may in writing direct the Authority to transmit to him or her for the
purpose of being laid before the House of Representatives a report of his or her
proceedings and of the principal matters transacted by him or her, and of the valuations
returned to or caused to be made by him or her, and generally of the working of this Act,
during the preceding year.

### 12.

Applications for registration
(1) Where any number of persons, being not less than seven, have established or
intend to establish a society which may be registered under this Act, and which they
desire should be so registered, they shall make application in writing to the Authority for
that purpose.
(2) The application shall be signed by at least seven members, including the secretary.
(3) Two copies of the rules and two lists of the names of the treasurer, secretary, and
of every trustee or officer authorised to sue or be sued on behalf of the society shall be
transmitted to the Authority together with the application, and shall be signed by the
persons who are required to sign the application and by the treasurer, secretary, and by
the trustees and other officers, if any. The list so signed shall, on the society being
registered, be evidence that the persons named have been duly appointed.

### 13.

Societies to register
(1) No society, entitled to be registered under this Act, and consisting of more than
seven persons, shall carry on business unless it shall be registered under this Act, or has
been formed in pursuance of Letters Patent:
Provided that it shall be lawful for such a society to carry on business without being so
registered if the Authority, on the application of the society, shall have granted to it a
certificate exempting the society from registration. Such a certificate may be granted,
with the approval of the Minister, in any case in which the Authority is of opinion that the
society is not one to which the provisions of this Act ought to apply; and such certificate
shall be subject to revocation by the Authority, with the approval in each case of the
Minister, but shall remain in force until so revoked, and until notice of such revocation
shall have been advertised in the Gazette and in a local newspaper, and also transmitted
by a registered letter to the society at its registered office.
(2) For the purposes of this section, the mere collection by a society of entrance fees
and contributions for a period not exceeding six months after the first collection thereof
shall not be deemed to be carrying on business:
Provided that every such first collection shall within seven days of the receipt thereof
be reported by the society to the Authority who shall be empowered, at any time
thereafter, either by himself, herself or by some person duly authorised by him or her in
writing to do so, to inspect the books and affairs of the society and to advise generally
thereon.
(3) No society shall be registered under a name identical with that under which any
other existing society is registered, or so nearly resembling that name as to be likely, or
under any name likely, in the opinion of the Authority, to deceive the members of the
public as to its nature or its identity, and no society shall change its name without the
sanction of the Authority, as hereinafter provided.
(4) A society, other than a benevolent society or a working men’s club, shall not be
disentitled to registration by reason of any rule for or practice of dividing any part of the
funds thereof, if the rules thereof contain distinct provision for meeting all claims upon
the society existing at the time of division before any such division takes place.

(5) No society shall contract for the assurance of a certain annuity or a certain
superannuation, whether deferred or immediate, unless the tables of contributions payable
for such kind of assurance are in accordance with those constructed and published under
section 10, or unless such tables of contributions have been certified under the hand of a
competent actuary approved of by the Authority and such certificate shall have been
transmitted to the Authority to be deposited by him or her with the rules and with any
other documents in his or her custody relating to the society.
(6) Every society or officer or member of a society or other person who contravenes
any of the provisions of this section shall be guilty of an offence.

### 14.

Construction of rules
The Authority shall consult with the secretary or other officer if he or she is of the
opinion that the rules of a society registrable under this Act are not calculated to carry
into effect the intentions and objects of the persons who have formed or desire to form the
society.

### 15.

Acknowledgment of registration
The Authority, on being satisfied that a society has complied with the provisions as to
registration in force under this Act shall enter the society accordingly in the register of
societies, and shall give to the society an acknowledgment of registration.

### 16.

Appeal from refusal to register
(1) If the Authority refuses to register the society, the persons who signed the
application for registration or any of them, may, within fourteen days after his or her
refusal has been notified to any of them, appeal from such refusal to the Minister.
(2) If the refusal to register is overruled on appeal, an acknowledgment of registration
shall thereupon be given to the society by the Authority.

### 17.

Effect of acknowledgment
The acknowledgment of registration shall be conclusive evidence that the society
therein mentioned is duly registered, unless it is proved that its registration has been
suspended or cancelled under the provisions hereinafter contained.

### 18.

Publication of acknowledgment
Immediately on giving an acknowledgment of registration to a society, the Authority
shall cause it to be published in the Gazette and in a local newspaper.

### 19.

Disposal of copies of rules transmitted with application
On registering a society, the Authority shall return to the society one of the copies of
the rules, and one of the lists of the names of the officers transmitted with the application
for registration, verified by his or her signature, and shall keep the other copy and the
other list in his or her office.

### 20.

Change of name of society
A registered society may, by special resolution with the approval in writing of the
Authority, change its name; but no such change shall affect any right or liability of the
society or any member, and any pending legal proceedings may be continued by or
against the society notwithstanding its change of name.

### 21.

Society to be a body corporate
(1) On being registered, a society shall ipso facto become and be a body corporate by
the name described in the acknowledgment of registration, with perpetual succession and
a common seal, and by that name shall be capable of suing and of being sued in all courts
and before all magistrates and, subject to the rules of the society and to the provisions of
this Act, of purchasing, receiving, possessing, holding, mortgaging and otherwise dealing
with property.
(2) The name of a registered society shall be engraved on its common seal.
(3) Every society shall have two or more trustees.
(4) The trustees shall be appointed at a general meeting of the society by a resolution
of a majority of the members present and entitled to vote thereat.
(5) The society shall send to the Authority a copy of every resolution appointing
trustees, signed by the trustees so appointed and by the secretary and president of the
society.
(6) The same person shall not be secretary or treasurer of a society and a trustee of
that society.

### 22.

Branch societies
With respect to societies having branches the following provisions shall have effect—
(a)

with the application for registration there shall be transmitted to the
Authority—
(i) a list of every branch, and of the place wherein the same is
established, and a list of the names of the trustees and other officers of
each branch,
(ii) if the rules of all branches (herein called branch rules) are or are
intended to be identical, a statement to that effect, and two copies of
the rules, and
(iii) if the branch rules are not or are not intended to be identical, a
statement to that effect, and two copies of all branch rules;

(b)

a society having a fund under the control of a central body to which every
branch is bound to contribute may be registered as a single society;

(c)

notice of the establishment of every new branch by a registered society, and
of the place where it is established and a statement whether or not the rules
of the branch are identical with those of the other branches of the society,
and if not so, two copies of the rules of the branch, shall be transmitted,
under the hand of the secretary, to the Authority.

A list of the names of the trustees and other officers of every new branch shall be
transmitted to the Authority under the hand of the secretary—
(d)

until a branch (in the case hereinbefore mentioned) has been registered, the
society shall not be entitled to any of the privileges of this Act in respect of
the branch; and until a copy of any amendment of the rules of a branch has
been registered, the amendment shall not take effect as respects that branch;

(e)

the provisions of this Act as to appeals and the results thereof, as to
amendments of rules, as to the acknowledgment of registration, and as to the
evidence of registration and of rules, shall apply to branches and branch
rules;

(f)

where a society has no fund under the control of a central body to which
every branch is bound to contribute, every such branch shall be deemed to

be, and shall be registered as, a separate society, whether its rules are
identical with those of other branches or not;
(g)

a registered branch shall not be registered as a society except on production
to the Authority of a certificate, under the hand of the chief secretary or other
principal officer of the society, that the branch has wholly seceded or has
been expelled from the society:

Provided that the like appeal shall lie from the refusal of the chief secretary or other
principal officer of the society, or from his or her omission, after one month from the
receipt of a request in writing made on behalf of a branch to grant such a certificate, as
from the refusal of the Authority to register a society:
Provided further that a body which, having been a branch of a society, has wholly
seceded or been expelled from that society shall not thereafter use the name of that
society or any name implying that it is a branch thereof, or the number by which it was
designated as such branch.

### 23.

Conversion of societies into branches
With respect to the conversion of registered societies into branches the following
provisions shall have effect—
(a)

a society may, by a resolution passed by not less than three-fourths of the
members or delegates present and entitled to vote at a general meeting, of
which meeting notice specifying the intention to propose the resolution has
been duly given according to the rules of the society, determine to become a
branch under this Act of any other registered society, and also, if thought fit,
of any registered branch thereof; and if the rules of the society do not
comply with all the provisions of this Act, and of regulations made
thereunder in respect of the registration of branches, the meeting at which
the resolution is passed may amend the rules so as to bring them into
compliance with this Act and with the regulations;

(b)

a copy of the rules of the first-mentioned society marked to show the
amendments, if any, made at the meeting, and two copies of the resolution
and of the amendment of rules, if any, each signed by the chairperson of the
meeting and by the secretary of the society so determining to become a
branch of any other society, and countersigned by the secretary of the other
society, shall be transmitted to the Authority, and if the Authority finds that
the rules, with or without the amendments, comply with the provisions of
this Act and of the regulations, he or she shall cancel the registration of the
first-mentioned society, and register it as a branch of the other society, and
also, if so specified in the resolution before mentioned, of any branch of the
other society, without further request or notice, and shall register the
amendment of rules without further application or evidence; and until the
registration the resolution shall not take effect;

(c)

no advertisement of any cancelling of registration under this section shall be
requisite;

(d)

the rules of a society which becomes a branch under this section shall, so far
as they are not contrary to any express provision of this Act or of the
regulations, and subject to any amendment thereof as hereinbefore provided,
continue in force as the rules of the branch until amended.
Rules

### 24.

Rules to contain certain provisions

(1) The rules of every society for registration shall, according to the class in which the
society is to be registered, contain provisions in respect of the several matters mentioned
in the First Schedule.
(2) A copy of the rules of a registered society shall be delivered by the society to a
member within twenty-one days of the demand therefor on payment of a sum not
exceeding fifty cents.
(3) Every society which fails to comply with the provisions of subsection (2) shall be
guilty of an offence and shall, on summary conviction, be liable to a penalty of one
hundred dollars.

### 25.

Alteration of rules and registration thereof
(1) After the rules of a society have been registered it shall be lawful for the society,
by resolution at a meeting specially called for that purpose, to alter, amend or rescind
them or any of them, or to make new rules:
Provided that no alteration, amendment, rescission or new rule shall take effect until it
has been registered.
(2) Two copies of the proposed alterations or amendments or of the new rules, as the
case may be, signed by three members of the society and by the secretary or other officer,
shall be transmitted to the Authority by the secretary within thirty days of the passing of
the resolution adopting them, and to one of the copies there shall be attached a declaration
by the secretary or one of the officers of the society that, in making them, the rules of the
society respecting the making, altering, amending and rescinding of rules have been duly
complied with.
(3) If the Authority is satisfied that the alterations, amendments or new rules are not
contrary to the provisions of this Act, and have been made in accordance with the rules,
he or she shall give to the society an acknowledgment of registration of them; and shall
return one of the copies to the society, verified by his or her signature, and shall keep the
other, with the declaration annexed, with the rules of the society, in his or her custody.
(4) The provisions hereinbefore contained with respect to appeals from a refusal to
register a society shall apply to a refusal to register amendments of rules.

### 26.

Rules binding on society and members
(1) The rules when registered shall bind the society and the members thereof, and all
persons claiming through them, to the same extent as if each member had subscribed his
or her name and affixed his or her seal thereto and as if the rules contained a covenant on
the part of himself or herself, his or her heirs, executors and administrators, to conform to
the rules, subject to the provisions of this Act.
(2) All sums of monies, other than subscriptions, payable by a member to a society or
branch, shall be deemed to be a debt due from the member to the society or branch, and
shall be recoverable by action in a court:
Provided that where such action is instituted in the Magistrate’s Court, such court shall
be the court of the district in which the office of the society or branch is situate.
(3) It shall be lawful for the Minister to authorise any society registered as a
“specially authorised society” to recover as debts the subscriptions of members thereof,
save as aforesaid no subscription shall be recoverable at law.
Amalgamation

### 27.

Amalgamation or conversion of registered societies

(1) Any two or more registered societies may, by special resolution of both or all the
societies, become amalgamated as one society, with or without any dissolution or division
of the funds of the societies or either or any of them; and any registered society may, by
special resolution, transfer its engagements to any other registered society which may
undertake to fulfil them.
(2) Any two or more registered societies may by special resolution of both or all of
the societies amalgamate a portion of their funds for co-operative purposes, including the
provision of an educational scholarship.
(3) A registered society may, by special resolution, determine to convert itself into a
company under the Companies Act, Chapter No. 47 of 1934 Revision, or to amalgamate
with or transfer its engagements to any such company.
(4) No amalgamation or transfer of engagements shall prejudice any right of a
creditor of a society which is party thereto.
(5) If a special resolution for converting a registered society into a company contains the
particulars by the Companies Act, Chapter required to be contained in the memorandum of
association of a company, and a copy thereof has been registered by the Authority, a copy of
the resolution certified by the Authority shall have the same effect as a memorandum of
association duly signed and attested under the said Act.
(6) If a registered society is registered as, or amalgamates with, or transfers all its
engagements to a company, its registration under this Act shall thereupon be void, and
shall be cancelled by the Authority; but the registration of the society as a company shall
not affect any right or claim for the time being subsisting against the society, or any
penalty for the time being incurred by the society; and for the purpose of enforcing such
right, claim or penalty, the society may be sued and proceeded against in the same manner
as if it had not become registered as a company; and every such right or claim, or the
liability to such penalty, shall have priority as against the property of the company, over
all other rights or claims against or liabilities of the company:
Provided that in respect of friendly societies—
(a)

no special resolution by a society for amalgamation or transfer of
engagements under this section shall be valid unless five-sixths in value (to
be calculated as for dissolution) of the members assent thereto, either at the
meetings at which the resolution is passed and confirmed, or one of them, or
in writing if such members were not present thereat, nor without the consent
in writing of every person for the time being received or entitled to any
relief, annuity or other benefit from the funds of the society, unless the claim
of such person is first duly satisfied, or adequate provision is made for
satisfying it;

(b)

the provisions hereinafter contained in case of dissolution as to the
punishment of officers, and the remedy of members or persons dissatisfied
with the provision made for satisfying their claims, shall apply to the case of
amalgamation and transfer of engagements; and

(c)

upon the application of the committee of management of a society desiring
to amalgamate or transfer its engagements, notice of the application being
published in the Gazette, the Authority, after hearing the committee of
management and any other persons whom he or she considers entitled to be
heard upon the application, may, with the consent of the Minister, order that
any of the consents and conditions prescribed in this Act, or in any
regulations made thereunder, be dispensed with, and may confirm the
amalgamation or transfer.

(7) This section shall not apply to branches.
General Duties and Obligations

### 28.

General duties of registered societies
(1) Every society shall—
(a)

keep an office to which all communications and notices may be addressed,
and notice of the situation of the office, and of every change therein, shall be
sent to the Authority, and shall be published in the Gazette. The office is
hereinafter referred to as the registered office;

(b)

paint or affix, and keep painted or affixed, its name on the outside of every
office or place in which the business of the society is carried on, or on a
signboard erected in the vicinity thereof, in a conspicuous position, in letters
easily legible, and have its name mentioned in legible characters in all notices,
advertisements and other official publications of the society, and in all bills of
exchange, promissory notes, endorsements, cheques and orders for monies or
goods purporting to be signed by or on behalf of the society, and in all bills of
parcels, invoices, receipts and letters of credit of the society;

(c)

cause its proceedings, transactions and accounts to be regularly entered in
the prescribed records and books, separate accounts being kept of all monies
received or paid on account of every particular fund or benefit assured by the
society for which a separate table of contributions payable is adopted distinct
from all monies received and paid on account of any other benefit or fund,
and keep a separate account of the expenses of management of the society
and of all contributions on account thereof;

(d)

within six weeks of a change in the members of the committee of
management and other officers, if any, forward to the Authority notice of
such change under the hand of the secretary;

(e)

once at least in every year, submit its accounts for audit to one of the public
auditors appointed as hereinafter mentioned, and the auditors shall have
access to all the books and accounts of the society, and shall examine the
general statement of the receipts and expenditure, funds and effects, of the
society, and verify it with the accounts and vouchers relating thereto, and
shall either sign it as found by him, her or them to be correct, duly vouched
and in accordance with law, or specially report to the society in what respect
he or she or they finds or find it incorrect, unvouched or not in accordance
with law;

(f)

once in every year, before the first day of June, send to the Authority a
general statement (to be called the annual return) of the receipts and
expenditure, funds and effects, of the society as audited, and such statement
shall show separately the expenditure in respect of the several objects of the
society, and shall be made out to the thirty-first day of December
immediately preceding inclusively, and shall state the name, address and
calling or profession of each auditor, and the manner in which and the
authority under which he or she was appointed, and together therewith shall
send a copy of the auditor’s report;

(g)

within six months after the thirty-first day of December, 1954, and so again
within six months after the expiration of every five years thereafter
succeeding, send to the Authority a return (to be called the quinquennial
return) of the sickness and mortality experienced by the society during the
five years ending with the thirty-first day of December last past; and an
abstract of all such returns shall be transmitted by the Authority to the
Minister, to be laid before the House of Representatives;

(h)

once at least in every five years, either cause its assets and liabilities to be
valued by one of the public valuers appointed as hereinafter mentioned or by
a valuer to be appointed by the society, and send to the Authority a report,
signed by the valuer (which shall also state his or her address and calling or

profession) on the condition of the society, and an abstract made by him or
her of the results of his or her valuation together with a return containing
such information with respect to the benefits assured and the contributions
receivable by the society, and of all funds and effects, debts and credits, as
the Authority may from time to time require, or send to the Authority a
return of the benefits assured and contributions receivable from all the
members of the society, and of all its funds and effects, debts and credits,
accompanied by such evidence in support thereof as the Authority may
prescribe, in which case the Authority shall cause the assets and liabilities of
the society to be valued and reported on by some actuary, and shall send to
the society a copy of his or her report, and an abstract of the result of his or
her valuation;
(i)

allow a member or person having an interest in the funds of the society to
inspect the books at all reasonable hours at the registered office of the
society or at any place where the same are kept;

(j)

keep a copy of the last annual balance sheet for the time being, and of the
last quinquennial valuation for the time being together with the report of the
auditor or auditors, if any, always hung up or deposited in a conspicuous
place at the registered office of the society.

This subsection shall apply to a registered branch of a society as if it were a registered
society, except that every notice, copy of a resolution and annual return required by this
subsection to be sent to the Authority shall be sent through an officer appointed in that
behalf by the society of which the branch forms part.
(2) No society shall pay a sum of money upon the death of a member or other person
whose death is or ought to be entered in any register of deaths, except upon the
production of a certificate of the death under the hand of the person having the care of the
register of deaths in which the death is or ought to be entered, and such certificate shall be
retained by the society and submitted for audit with the relative accounts:
Provided that this subsection shall not apply to deaths at sea.
(3) It shall be an offence under this Act if a registered society or an officer or member
thereof—
(a)

fails to give any notice, send or transmit any return or document, or do or
allow to be done any act or thing which the society, officer, or person is by
this Act, or by a regulation made thereunder, required to give, send, transmit,
do or allow to be done;

(b)

wilfully refuses or neglects to do an act or to furnish any information
required for the purpose of this Act by the Authority or other person
authorised under this Act, or does any act or thing forbidden by this Act, or
by a regulation made thereunder; or

(c)

makes any return or wilfully furnishes information in any respect false or
insufficient.

(4) Every annual or other return, abstract of valuation, and other document required
for the purposes of this Act shall be made in such form and shall contain such particulars
as the Authority may prescribe.
(5) All documents by this section required to be sent to the Authority shall be
deposited with the rules of the societies to which they relate, and shall be registered, or
recorded by the Authority, with such observations thereon, if any, as the Authority may
think proper:
Provided that the Authority may, with the approval of the Minister, dispense with the
quinquennial return and with the valuation herein required in respect of societies to whose
purposes or to the nature of whose operations he or she may deem them inapplicable; and

may also dispense with the quinquennial return in cases where, at the request of a society,
he or she may, by inspection of its books, by himself or herself or by any person whom he
or she may appoint for that purpose, obtain such information as he or she may deem
sufficient of the sickness and mortality experienced by the society; and the provisions
herein contained with respect to the quinquennial return and valuation shall not apply to
benevolent societies, or to co-operative societies unless they are carrying on insurance
business, or working men’s clubs, nor to specially authorised societies, unless it is so
directed in the authority for registering them.

### 29.

Membership of minors
(1) The rules of a registered society or branch may provide for the admission of a
person under twenty-one years of age, but above one year of age as a member. Any such
member may, if he or she is over sixteen years of age, by himself or herself, and if he or
she is under that age, by his or her parent or guardian, execute all instruments, and give
all acquittances necessary to be executed or given under the rules, but shall not be a
member of the committee of management, or a trustee, president, or treasurer of the
society or branch.
(2) Societies and branches consisting wholly of members of any age under twentyone years, but exceeding three years, may be allowed to register under this Act, subject to
such regulations as may be made by the Minister in that behalf.

### 30.

Fee for certificate of birth or death
For the purposes of this Act, a certificate of the birth or death of any member of or
person insured or to be insured with a registered friendly society shall be given under his
or her hand by the person having the care of the register of births and deaths in which the
birth or death is entered, for a sum of twenty-five cents for the first certificate and of ten
cents for every subsequent certificate.

### 31.

Financial officers to execute bond and render account
(1) (a) Every officer having receipt or charge of monies shall, before taking upon
himself or herself the execution of his or her office, become bound with one sufficient
surety at least in a bond in Form 6 of the Second Schedule or give the security of a
guarantee society to the society in the sum of fifty dollars, or in such larger sum as the
rules of the society may require conditioned for his or her rendering a just and true
account of all monies received and paid by him or her on account of the society at such
times as its rules may appoint or as the committee of management thereof may require
him or her to do and for the payment by him or her of all sums due from him or her to the
society.
(b) Both the principal and the surety shall make a statutory declaration testifying to
the sufficiency of the surety furnished in the bond hereinafter referred to.
(c) The bond shall, within seven days after execution thereof, be forwarded,
together with the declaration, by the society to the Authority who shall thereupon issue
his or her certificate as to the lodgement thereof; and a copy of the bond certified under
the hand of the Authority shall be receivable in evidence without further proof of the
contents or execution thereof.
(d) The committee of management of a society or the Authority in their or his or
her absolute discretion and without assigning any reason for doing so may require the
principal at any time to verify the sufficiency of the bond so given by him or her or to
furnish a fresh bond with one sufficient surety at the least.
(2) No person who enters into any such obligation as surety, and no guarantee society
which gives any such security shall be entitled to the benefit of any exception or shall be

permitted in any action or proceeding thereon to rely on any exception or matter of
defence, except that the principal has performed his or her obligation.
(3) Every officer shall, at such times as by the rules of the society he or she is
required to render account, or upon demand made or notice in writing given or left at his
or her last or usual place of residence, give in his or her account as may be required by
the society or by the committee of management thereof, to be examined and allowed or
disallowed by them, and shall, on the like demand or notice, pay over all monies and
deliver all property for the time being in his or her custody or possession to such person
as the society, or the committee of management thereof, may appoint; and in case of any
refusal or neglect to deliver such account, or to pay over such monies, or to deliver such
property in manner aforesaid, the society may sue upon the obligation or security before
mentioned or may apply to a magistrate who may order the delivery of such accounts or
property or the payment over of such sums of monies, with costs, and in default of such
delivery or payment the person making such default may be sentenced to imprisonment
for any term not exceeding three months.
Property

### 32.

Property of society to vest in society on registration
(1) The registration of a society shall vest in the society all property for the time being
vested in any person in trust for the society; and all legal proceedings pending by or
against the trustees of a society may be prosecuted by or against the society in its
registered name without abatement.
(2) Where a person being or having been a trustee of a registered society or branch,
and whether appointed before or after the registration thereof, in whose name any stock,
land, or other securities belonging to that society or branch is or are standing, either
jointly with another or others or solely—
(a)

is absent from Grenada;

(b)

becomes bankrupt, or files a petition, or executes a deed for liquidation of
his or her affairs by assignment or arrangement or for composition with his
or her creditors;

(c)

becomes of unsound mind or is dead;

(d)

has been removed from his or her office of trustee; or

(e)

if it is unknown whether he or she is living or dead,

the Authority may, on application in writing from the secretary and three members of the
society or branch, and on proof satisfactory to him or her, direct the transfer of the stock,
land, or other securities, into the names of any other persons as trustees for the society or
branch.
The transfer shall be made by the surviving or continuing trustees, or if there are no
such trustees, or if the trustees refuse or are unable to make the transfer, and the Authority
directs, then, by such person as may be ordered by the High Court.

### 33.

General provisions as to property and funds
(1) The trustees of a registered society or branch may, with the consent of a majority
of the members present and entitled to vote at a general meeting, invest the funds of the
society or branch, or any part thereof, to any amount, in any of the following ways—
(a)

in the Government Savings Bank;

(b)

in Government Funds;

(c)

in the purchase of land, and in the erection or alteration of offices or other
buildings thereon;

(d)

upon any other security expressly directed by the rules of the society, not
being personal security, except as hereinafter authorised with respect to
loans;

(e)

in an investment in which trustees are for the time being by law authorised to
invest funds; and

(f)

in any other manner prescribed by regulations made under this Act.

(2) A society, or branch of a society, may (if its rules so provide) hold, purchase, or
take on lease land, and may sell, exchange, mortgage, lease or build upon the land (with
power to alter and pull down buildings and again rebuild); and no purchaser, assignee,
mortgagee, or tenant shall be bound to inquire as to the authority for any sale, exchange,
mortgage, or lease by the committee of management; and the receipt of the committee of
management, or of a majority thereof, shall be a discharge for all monies arising from or
in connection with such sale, exchange, mortgage or lease.
(3) Any other body corporate may, if its regulations so allow, hold shares by its
corporate name in a society.
(4) The rules of a society with branches and of any branch thereof may provide for
the investment of funds of the society or of such branch by the trustees of that branch, or
by the trustees of the society, and the consent required for any such investment shall be
the consent of the committee of management, or of a majority of the society or branch by
whom the funds are invested.
(5) When all monies intended to be secured by any mortgage given to a society under
its rules have been fully paid or discharged the society may endorse upon or annex to the
mortgage a reconveyance of the mortgaged property to the then owner of the equity of
redemption or to such person and to such uses as he or she may direct, or a receipt under
the seal of the society countersigned by the president and secretary in the prescribed form
and such receipt shall discharge the mortgage and vest the estate of and in the property
therein comprised in the person for the time being entitled to the equity of redemption
without any reconveyance; and if the mortgage has been duly recorded the Authority of
the Supreme Court shall on production of the receipt make an entry across or in the
margin of the page on which the mortgage is recorded to the effect that the mortgage is
satisfied and that the receipt has been produced and is in due form and such entry shall
have the effect of cleaning the record of the mortgage.
Loans and Payments

### 34.

Loans to members
(1) Not more than one-half of the amount of an assurance on the life of a member of
at least one full year’s standing may be advanced to him or her, on the written security of
himself or herself and two satisfactory sureties for repayment; and the amount advanced,
with all interest thereon, may be deducted from the sum assured, without prejudice in the
meantime to the operation of the security.
(2) A society, not being a building society, may, out of a separate loan fund to be
formed by contributions or deposits of its members, make loans to its members on their
personal security, with or without sureties, as may be provided by the rules, subject to the
following restrictions—
(a)

no loan shall at any time be made out of monies contributed for the general
purposes of the society;

(b)

no member shall be capable of holding an interest in the loan fund exceeding
one thousand dollars;

(c)

no society shall make a loan to a member on personal security beyond the
amount fixed by the rules, or shall make a loan which, together with any
monies for the time being owing by a member to the society, exceeds two
hundred and fifty dollars; and

(d)

no society shall hold at any one time on deposit from its members any
monies beyond the amount fixed by the rules, which shall not exceed twothirds of the total sums for the time being owing to the society by the
members who have borrowed from the loan fund:

Provided that nothing in this subsection shall be construed to require a loan society to
form a separate loan fund.

### 35.

Accumulation at interest of surplus contributions
The rules of a society may provide for accumulating at interest, for the use of a
member of the same, any surplus of his or her contribution to the funds of the society
which may remain after providing for any assurance in respect of which it is paid, and for
the withdrawal of the accumulations from time to time.

### 36.

Declaration of member’s interest
A society may require a member, or person claiming through a member, to make and
sign a declaration that the total amount to which such member or person is entitled from
one or more such societies does not exceed the sums set out in section 6 and a person who
knowingly makes a false or fraudulent declaration in the matter shall be guilty of an
offence.

### 37.

Payments on death of children under ten years of age
(1) No society shall insure or pay on the death of a child under five years of age a sum
of money which, added to an amount payable on the death of the child by any other
society, exceeds fifty dollars, or on the death of a child of five years of age and over but
under ten years of age a sum of money which, added to any amount payable on the death
of the child by any other society, exceeds seventy-five dollars.
(2) No society shall pay a sum on the death of a child under ten years of age except to
the parent of the child, or to the personal representative of the parent, and upon the
production by the parent or his or her personal representative of a certificate of death
issued by the person having the lawful care of the register of deaths in which the death is
or should be entered, containing the particulars hereinafter mentioned, and such
certificate shall be retained by the society and submitted for audit with the relative
accounts.
(3) Whenever a certificate of the death of a child is applied for, for the purpose of
obtaining a sum of money from a society, the name of the society and the sum sought to
be obtained therefrom shall be stated to the person aforesaid, who shall write on or at the
foot of the certificate the words “to be produced to the .................... Society said to be
liable for the payment of the sum of ....................” and all certificates of the same death
shall be numbered in consecutive order, and each certificate subsequent to the first shall
also be endorsed with the sum of the claim, and the society from which claimed, alleged
in every preceding certificate, and the sum charged by the person aforesaid for the first of
such certificates shall be twenty-five cents, and for every subsequent certificate ten cents.
(4) No person as aforesaid shall give any one or more certificates of death for the
payment in the whole of a sum of money exceeding fifty dollars on the death of a child
under five years of age or for the payment in the whole of a sum of money exceeding
seventy-five dollars on the death of a child of five years of age and over but under ten
years of age; and no such certificate shall be granted unless the cause of death has been
previously entered in the register of deaths on the certificate of a coroner or of a

registered medical practitioner who attended the deceased child during its last illness, or
except upon the production of a certificate of the probable cause of death under the hand
of a registered medical practitioner, or of other satisfactory evidence of the same.
(5) A society to which is produced a certificate of the death of a child which does not
purport to be the first shall, before paying any monies thereon, inquire whether any, and,
if any, what sums of money have been paid on the death of the child by any other society.
(6) Nothing in this section shall apply to assurances on the lives of children of any
age, where the person insuring has an interest in the life of the person insured.

### 38.

Power of nomination for sums not exceeding five hundred dollars
(1) A member of a registered society (other than a benevolent society or working
men’s club), not being under the age of sixteen years, may, by writing under his or her
hand delivered at or sent to the registered office of the society, nominate a person, not
being an officer or employee of the society, unless such officer or employee is the wife,
husband, father, mother, child, brother, sister, nephew, or niece of the nominator, to
whom any monies, payable by the society on the death of the member (which shall
include deposits made by the member under section 34, and monies accumulated for the
use of the member under section 35, with the interest on such deposits or monies), not
exceeding five hundred dollars, shall be paid at his or her decease, and may from time to
time revoke or vary such nomination by a writing under his or her hand similarly
delivered or sent; and, on receiving satisfactory proof of the death of a nominator, the
society shall pay to the nominee the amount due to the deceased member, not exceeding
five hundred dollars.
(2) A nomination may be partly printed, and if made in a book kept at the registered
office of the society, shall be taken to be delivered at the office.
(3) Every registered society shall keep a book wherein the names of all persons so
nominated shall be regularly entered, and all the shares comprised in any such nomination
shall be transferable to the nominee although the rules of the society declare its shares to
be generally not transferable; and, on receiving satisfactory proof of the death of a
nominator, the society shall either transfer the shares in the manner directed in such
nomination or pay to every person entitled thereunder the full value of his or her interest
at his or her option unless the shares, if transferred to any such nominee, would raise his
or her interest in the society to an amount exceeding the amount hereinbefore specified in
respect of the society, in which case the society shall pay him or her the full value of the
shares, not exceeding five hundred dollars.

### 39.

Registered society may contribute to funds of other registered society
Nothing in this Act shall prevent any registered society or branch from contributing to
the funds or taking part, by delegates or otherwise, in the government of any other
registered society or branch, as may be provided in the rules of the first-named society or
branch, without becoming a branch under this Act of the other society or branch.

### 40.

Registered society may subscribe to hospitals, etc.
A registered society may subscribe out of its own funds to a hospital, infirmary, or
charitable or provident institution any annual or other sum which may be necessary to
secure to members of the society and their families the benefits of the hospital, infirmary,
or institution, according to its rules.
Disputes and Legal Proceedings

### 41.

Mode of deciding dispute between member and registered society

(1) Every dispute between a member, or a person claiming through a member or
under the rules of a registered society and the society or an officer thereof, or between a
registered society or registered branch and the registered society or branch of which the
other party to the dispute is a registered branch or an officer thereof, or between any two
or more registered branches of a registered society, or branch, or any officers thereof,
shall be decided in manner directed by rules of the society, approved by the Authority,
and the decision so made shall be binding and conclusive on all parties without appeal,
and shall not be removable into any court or restrainable by injunction; and application
for the enforcement thereof may be made to a Magistrate’s Court:
Provided that—
(a)

a party to a dispute in a society may apply to the Authority to hear and
determine the same and the Authority may, if he or she thinks fit, and after
proper notice given to the parties to such dispute, hear and determine such
dispute, and shall have power to order the expenses of determining it to be
paid either out of the funds of the society or by such parties to the dispute as
he or she may think fit, and such determination and order shall have the
same effect and be enforceable in like manner as a decision made in the
manner directed by the rules of the society;

(b)

the Authority may administer oaths, and may require the attendance of all
parties concerned and of witnesses and the production of all books and
documents relating to the matter in question; and a person refusing to attend,
or to produce a document, or to give evidence before the Authority, shall be
guilty of an offence;

(c)

where the rules of a society direct that disputes shall be referred to a
magistrate, the dispute shall be determined by a Magistrate’s Court either
with or without the right of appeal as may be provided by the rules;

(d)

where the rules contain no direction as to disputes, and the Authority refuses
to hear and determine a dispute, or where no decision is made on a dispute
within forty days after application to the society for a reference under its
rules, the member or person aggrieved may apply to a Magistrate’s Court,
which may hear and determine the matter in dispute;

(e)

the Authority or the magistrate, as the case may be, may, at the request of
either party, state a case for the opinion of the High Court on any question of
law.

(2) Subject to the provisions of the proviso to subsection (1)(c) a decision given under
this section shall be binding and conclusive on all parties without appeal.

### 42.

How societies may sue and be sued
(1) The trustees of a registered society or branch, or any other officers authorised by
the rules thereof may, in the name and on behalf of the society or branch, bring or defend,
or cause to be brought or defended, any action or other legal proceeding in any court
whatsoever concerning any property, right or claim of the society or branch.
(2) Legal proceedings shall not abate or be discontinued by the death, resignation or
removal from office of a trustee or officer, or by any act of a trustee or officer after the
commencement of the proceedings.
(3) The summons, writ or other proceedings to be issued to or against the trustees or
other officers authorised by the rules of the society to defend such action or other
proceedings on behalf of a registered society or branch shall be sufficiently served by
personally serving such trustees or other officers or by leaving a true copy thereof with an
adult person at the registered office of the society or branch, or at a place of business of
the society or branch within the jurisdiction of the court in which the proceedings are

brought, or if that office or place of business is closed, by pasting or securely nailing the
copy on the outer door of that office or place of business.
(4) In all cases where the summons, writ, or other document is served by leaving a
true copy of it at the registered office of the society or branch, a copy of it shall be sent in
a registered letter addressed to the secretary at the registered office of the society or
branch, and posted at least six days before any further step is taken on the proceedings.
(5) Where the proceedings are taken against a society for the recovery of a fine under
this Act the summons or other process shall be sufficiently served by leaving a true copy
thereof at the registered office of the society, or at a place of business of the society or, if
that office or place of business is closed, by pasting or securely nailing the copy on the
outer door of that office or place of business.
(6) Where the trustees are authorised by the rules of a society to defend an action or
other legal proceedings and one of such trustees is the person against whom the
proceedings are to be taken, the proceedings may be brought by the other trustee or
trustees.
Inspection of Affairs

### 43.

Special power of Authority to direct inspection of affairs
(1) Upon the application of not less than one-fifth of the whole number of members of
a registered society, or of not less than one hundred members in the case of a registered
society having more than one thousand members, the Authority may, with the consent of
the Minister—
(a)

appoint one or more inspectors to examine into the affairs of the society, and
to report thereon, and the inspector may require the production of all or any
of the books and documents of the society, and may examine on oath its
officers, members, agents and employees in relation to its business and may
administer oaths accordingly; and

(b)

call a special meeting of the society in such manner and at such time and
place as the Authority may think fit, and may direct what matters shall be
discussed and determined on at the meeting which shall have all the powers
of a meeting called according to the rules of the society, and shall in all cases
have power to appoint its own chairperson, any rule of the society to the
contrary notwithstanding:
Provided that—
(a)

the application herein mentioned shall be supported by such evidence,
for the purpose of showing that the applicants have good reason for
requiring the inspection to be made or meeting to be called, and that
they are not actuated by malicious motives in their application, and
such notice thereof shall be given to the society, as the Authority may
require and direct;

(b)

the Authority may, if he or she thinks fit, require the applicants to give
security for the costs of the proposed inspection or meeting, before
appointing an inspector or calling such meeting;

(c)

all expenses of and incidental or preliminary to any such inspection or
meeting shall be defrayed by the members applying for it, or out of the
funds of the society, or by the members or officers, or former
members or officers of the society, in such proportions as the
Authority may direct; and

(d)

the foregoing provisions of this section shall not apply to a society
having branches except with the consent of the central body of the
society.
[Editorial Note: Numbering as per Gazette.]

(2) The Authority may, in his or her absolute discretion, and without giving notice or
without assigning reason for doing so, either by himself or herself or by a person
appointed by him or her in writing for the purpose—
(a)

inspect and if necessary audit the books, records, securities and documents
of the society;

(b)

demand the production of, and check, the cash held on behalf of the society,

at all reasonable times at the registered office of the society. A person appointed by the
Authority as aforesaid shall report his or her findings to the Authority. The cost of such
inspection, audit, or check shall be a charge upon the Consolidated Fund.
(3) A person who hinders, obstructs or molests the Authority or any other person
appointed by him or her whilst acting under this section shall be guilty of an offence and
liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of five hundred dollars and to imprisonment for
one month.
Special Resolutions

### 44.

Passing and registration of special resolutions
(1) A special resolution is one which is passed by a majority of not less than threefourths of such members of a society for the time being entitled under the rules of the
society to vote, as may be present in person or by proxy (where the rules allow proxies) at
a general meeting of which notice specifying the intention to propose the resolution has
been duly given according to the rules, and which resolution is confirmed by a majority
of such members for the time being entitled under the rules to vote as may be present in
person or by proxy (where the rules allow proxies) at a subsequent general meeting of
which notice has been duly given, held not less than fourteen days nor more than one
month from the day of the meeting at which the resolution was first passed.
(2) At any meeting mentioned in this section a declaration by the chairperson that the
resolution has been carried shall be deemed conclusive evidence of the fact.
(3) A copy of every special resolution, signed by the chairperson of the meeting and
countersigned by the secretary of the society, shall be sent to the Authority and be
registered by him or her; and until the copy is so registered the resolution shall not take
effect.
Suspension and Dissolution

### 45.

Cancellation or suspension
(1) The Authority may, or, in the case hereinafter mentioned in paragraph (c) shall, by
writing under his or her hand, cancel the registration of a society—
(a)

if he or she thinks fit, at the request of a society, to be evidenced in such
manner as he or she may direct;

(b)

with the approval of the Minister, on proof to the satisfaction of the
Authority that a certificate of registration has been obtained by fraud or
mistake, or that a society exists or is used for an illegal or improper purpose,
or has wilfully, and after notice from the Authority, violated any of the
provisions of this Act, or has ceased to exist; or

(c)

by the direction of the Minister, on proof, to the satisfaction of the Minister,
that a society exists or is used for an illegal or improper purpose.

(2) The Authority, in any case in which he or she might, with the approval or by the
direction of the Minister, cancel the registration of a society, may or shall, with or by the
like approval or direction, by writing under his or her hand suspend it, for any term not
exceeding three months, and may or shall, with or by the like approval or direction, renew
the suspension from time to time for up to the same period.
(3) Not less than two months previous notice in writing, specifying briefly the ground
of any proposed cancellation or suspension of registration, shall be given by the Authority
to a society before its registration shall be cancelled (except at its request) or suspended;
and notice of every cancellation or suspension shall be published at the expense of the
Government in the Gazette and in a local newspaper as soon as practicable after it takes
place.
(4) A society may appeal from the cancellation of its registration, or from any
suspension of it which is renewed after six months, in manner hereinbefore provided for
appeals from the refusal of the Authority to register a society.
(5) A society whose registration has been cancelled or suspended shall from the time
of the cancellation or suspension (but if suspended only while the suspension lasts, and
subject also to the right of appeal hereby given), absolutely cease to enjoy as such the
privileges of a registered society, but without prejudice to any liability actually incurred
by the society, which may be enforced against it as if the suspension or cancellation had
not taken place.

### 46.

Dissolution
(1) A society may terminate or be dissolved in any of the following ways—
(a)

upon the happening of an event declared by the rules to be the termination of
the society;

(b)

as respects all societies, other than societies having shares and friendly
societies, by the consent of not less than three-fourths of the members,
testified by their signatures to the instrument of dissolution;

(c)

as respects societies having shares, by the consent of members holding not
less than two-thirds of the number of shares in the society, testified by their
signatures to the instrument of dissolution;

(d)

as respects friendly societies, by the consent of not less than five-sixths in
value of the members (including honorary members, if any) testified by their
signatures to the instrument of dissolution, and also by the consent in writing
of every person, for the time being receiving or entitled to any relief, annuity
or other benefit from the funds of the society, unless the claim of such
person is first duly satisfied, or adequate provision made for satisfying the
claim, and in the case of a branch, with the consent of the central body of the
society or in accordance with the general rules of the society; and

(e)

by the award of the Authority in the cases hereinafter specified.

(2) The instrument of dissolution shall set forth—
(a)

the liabilities and assets of the society in detail;

(b)

the number of members and the nature of their interests in the society
respectively;

(c)

the claims of the creditors, if any, and the provisions to be made for their
payment; and

(d)

the intended appropriation or division of the funds and property of the
society, unless it is stated in the instrument of dissolution to be left to the
award of the Authority.

(3) Alteration in the instrument of dissolution may be made with the like consents as
hereinbefore provided, testified in the same manner.
(4) A declaration in writing shall be made by three members and the secretary of the
society that the provisions of this Act have been complied with, and shall be sent to the
Authority with the instrument of dissolution; and a person who knowingly makes a false
or fraudulent declaration in the matter shall be guilty of an offence.
(5) The instrument of dissolution, and all alterations therein, shall be registered in the
manner hereinbefore provided for the registration of rules, and shall be binding upon all
the members of the society.
(6) The Authority shall cause a notice of the dissolution to be advertised, at the
expense of the society, in the manner hereinafter provided for advertising an award of the
Authority for dissolution; and unless within three months from the date of the Gazette in
which the advertisement appears a member or other person interested in or having any
other claim on the funds of the society commences proceedings to set aside the
dissolution of the society, and the dissolution is set aside accordingly, the society shall be
legally dissolved from the date of the advertisement, and the requisite consents to the
instrument of dissolution shall be considered to have been duly obtained, without proof of
the signature thereto.
(7) As respects friendly societies—
(a)

the value of members shall be ascertained by giving one vote to every
member, and an additional vote for every five years that he or she has been a
member, but to no one member more than five votes in the whole;

(b)

no instrument of dissolution shall direct or contain any provision for an
appropriation of division of the funds of the society, or any part thereof,
otherwise than for the purpose of carrying into effect the objects of the
society as declared in its rules, unless the claim of every member or person
claiming any relief, annuity, or other benefit from its funds is first duly
satisfied or adequate provision is made for satisfying the claim;

(c)

any officer or person who aids or abets in the dissolution of a society,
otherwise than as in this Act provided, shall be guilty of an offence and
liable, on summary conviction, to a penalty of two hundred and fifty dollars;
and

(d)

if a member of a dissolved society, or a person claiming any relief, annuity,
or other benefit from its funds, is dissatisfied with the provision made for
satisfying his or her claim, he or she may apply to the Magistrate’s Court of
the district within which the chief or any other place of business of the
society is situate for relief or other order, and the court shall have the same
powers in the matter as in regard to the settlement of disputes under this Act.

(8) With respect to dissolutions and the distribution of funds upon the award of the
Authority—
(a)

upon the application of not less than one-fifth of the whole number of
members in the case of a registered society, or of not less than one hundred
members in the case of a society having more than one thousand members,
made in writing under their hands, setting forth that the funds of the society
are insufficient to meet the existing claims thereon, or that the rates of
contribution fixed in the rules of the society are insufficient to cover the
benefits assured by them, and the grounds upon which the insufficiency is
alleged, and requesting an investigation into the affairs of the society with a
view to its dissolution, the Authority may, by himself or herself or by an

actuary, or public auditor, or other person whom he or she may appoint in
writing under his or her hand, investigate the affairs of the society, giving
nevertheless not less than one month’s previous notice in writing to the
society whose affairs are to be investigated at its registered office;
(b)

if, upon the investigation, it appears that the funds of the society are
insufficient to meet the existing claims thereon, or that the rates of
contribution fixed in the rules of the society are insufficient to cover the
benefits assured, the Authority may, if he or she considers it expedient to do
so, award that the society shall be dissolved, and its affairs wound up, and
shall direct in what manner its assets shall be appropriated or divided:
Provided that he or she may suspend his or her award for such period as he
or she may deem necessary to enable the society to make such alteration and
adjustment of contributions and benefits as will in his or her judgement
prevent the necessity of the award of the dissolution being made;

(c)

the Authority, when proceeding under this section, shall have all the same
powers and authorities enforceable by the same penalties as in the case of a
dispute referred to him or her under this Act;

(d)

every award under this section, whether for dissolution or distribution of
funds, shall be final and conclusive on the society in respect of which it is
made and on all its members and other persons having claims on the funds of
the society, without appeal, and shall be enforced in the same manner as a
decision on a dispute under this Act; and the expenses of every investigation
and award, and of publishing every notice of dissolution, shall be paid out of
the funds of the society before any other appropriation thereof is made;

(e)

notice of an award of dissolution shall, within twenty-one days after it has
been made, be advertised by the Authority in the Gazette and in a local
newspaper and unless, within three months from the date of the Gazette in
which the advertisement appears, a member or other person interested in or
having a claim on the funds of the society, commences proceedings to set
aside the dissolution of the society consequent upon the award, and the
dissolution is set aside accordingly, the society shall be legally dissolved
from the date of the advertisement, and the requisite consents to the
application of the Authority shall be considered to have been duly obtained,
without proof of the signatures thereto.

(9) This section shall not apply to a society having branches except with the consent
of the central body of the society.
(10) Notice shall be sent to the Authority of any proceedings to set aside the
dissolution of a society or branch, not less than seven days before it is commenced, by the
person taking the proceeding, and of any order setting a dissolution aside, by the society
or branch, within seven days after the order has been made.

### 47.

Liability of members in winding-up
When a society is dissolved and its affairs are wound up under the last preceding
section, the liability of present or past members of the society, to contribute for payment
of the debts and liabilities of the society, the expenses of winding-up, and the adjustment
and the rights of contributories amongst themselves shall be qualified as follows—
(a)

no individual, society, or company who or which has ceased to be a member
for one year or upwards prior to the commencement of the winding-up shall
be liable to contribute;

(b)

no individual, society, or company not a member shall be liable to contribute
in respect of any debt or liability contracted after he or she or it ceased to be
a member;

(c)

no individual, society, or company not a member shall be liable to
contribute, unless it appears that the contributions of the existing members
are insufficient to satisfy the just demands on the society;

(d)

no contribution shall be required from any individual, society, or company
exceeding the amount, if any, unpaid on the shares in respect of which he or
she or it is liable as a past or present member; and

(e)

an individual, society, or company shall be taken to have ceased to be a
member, in respect of any withdrawable share withdrawn, from the date of
the notice or application for withdrawal.
Offences, Penalties and Proceedings

### 48.

Offences
It shall be an offence under this Act if—
(a)

a society or branch or an officer or member thereof fails to give any notice,
deliver up, return or send any contribution card, return or document, do or
allow to be done anything which the society, branch, officer, or person is by
this Act required to give, deliver up, return, send, do or allow to be done;

(b)

a society or branch or an officer or member thereof wilfully neglects or
refuses to do an act or to furnish any information required for the purposes
of this Act by the Authority or by any other person authorised under this Act,
or does anything forbidden under its rules or by this Act;

(c)

a society or branch or an officer or member thereof makes a return or
wilfully furnishes information in any respect false or insufficient;

(d)

an officer or member of a body which, having been a branch of a society, has
wholly seceded or been expelled from that society, thereafter uses the name
of that society or any name implying that the body is a branch of that society
or the number by which that body was designated as such branch;

(e)

where application is made the Authority to hear and determine a dispute or
where a dispute is referred under this Act to the Authority, a person refuses
to attend or to produce any books, documents, or things or to give evidence
before the Authority;

(f)

a society or branch pays monies on the death of a child under ten years of
age otherwise than is provided by this Act;

(g)

a parent or personal representative of a parent claiming monies on the death
of a child produces a certificate of the death other than is in this Act
provided to the society or branch from which the monies is claimed, or
produces a false certificate, or one fraudulently obtained, or in any way
attempts to defeat the provisions of this Act with respect to payments upon
the death of children; or

(h)

an officer of a society or branch wilfully refuses to observe or comply with
any of the provisions of the rules of such society or branch relating to his or
her duties or functions under such rules or with any of the provisions of the
regulations made under this Act relating to his or her duties or functions
under such regulations:

Provided that no complaint shall be laid against any officer under this paragraph
except by, or with the consent in writing of, the Authority.

### 49.

Illicit payments

No officer of a society or branch shall, either in addition to or in substitution for the
remuneration prescribed under this Act or authorised by the rules of the society, or
branch, receive from any other person any gift, bonus, commission, or benefit, for or in
connection with any benefit paid or any loan made by the society or branch, and any
person paying or accepting any such gift, bonus, commission, or benefit, shall be guilty of
an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of three thousand dollars, and, in
default of payment, to be imprisoned for six months, and the person accepting any such
gift, bonus, commission, or benefit, shall as and when directed by the court by whom he
or she is convicted, pay over to the society or branch the amount or value of such gift,
bonus, commission, or benefit, and in default of such payment shall be liable, on
summary conviction, to imprisonment for six months.

### 50.

Bankrupt, etc., not to be officer of society
(1) (a) No person who is an undischarged bankrupt or has compounded with his or
her creditors or has made an arrangement or composition with his or her creditors under
the Bankruptcy Act, Chapter 27, shall be appointed, or if appointed, shall act as the
president, vice-president, secretary, assistant secretary, treasurer, or trustee of a society.
(b) A person acting in contravention of the provisions of this subsection shall be
guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand dollars.
(2) A person shall be disqualified for being elected and for being an officer of a
society or branch if within the previous ten years he or she has been convicted of an
offence involving dishonesty. If a person, who, under the provisions of this subsection, is
disqualified for being elected and for being an officer of a society or branch, or has
become disqualified to hold such office, acts as an officer of a society or branch he or she
shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to imprisonment for six
months and to a fine of three thousand dollars.

### 51.

Limitation of office holders
(1) No person shall hold both the offices of president and treasurer or secretary and
treasurer of the same society or branch thereof.
(2) No person shall hold the office of treasurer in more than one society.
(3) No person holding any office therein shall be or act as, medical officer, dentist,
optician or druggist of a society.
(4) A person contravening any of the provisions of this section shall be guilty of an
offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand five hundred dollars.

### 52.

Offences by societies to be also offences by officers
Where a society or branch is guilty of an offence under this Act, every member of the
committee, unless that member is proved to have been ignorant of or to have attempted to
prevent the commission of the offence, shall be liable to the same penalty as if he or she
had committed the offence.

### 53.

Continuing offences
Every default under this Act constituting an offence, if continued, shall constitute a
new offence in every week during which the default continues.

### 54.

Punishment of fraud, false declarations and misappropriation
(1) If a person, with intent to mislead or defraud, gives to any other person a copy of
any rules, laws, regulations, or other documents, other than the rules of a society or
branch, on the pretence that they are the existing rules of that society or branch, or that

there are no other rules of the society or branch, or gives to any other person a copy of
any rules on the pretence that those rules are the rules of a registered society or branch
when the society or branch is not registered, the person so offending shall be guilty of an
offence.
(2) If a person obtains possession by false representation or imposition of any
property of a society or branch, or withholds or misapplies any such property in his or her
possession, or wilfully applies any part thereof to purposes other than those expressed or
directed in the rules of the society or branch and authorised by this Act, he or she shall,
on such complaint as is in this section mentioned, be guilty of an offence and liable, on
summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand five hundred dollars and costs, and to be
ordered to deliver up all such property, or to repay all sums of money applied improperly,
and in default of such delivery or repayment, or of the payment of such fine and costs as
aforesaid, to be imprisoned for three months:
Provided that where on such a complaint against a person for withholding or
misapplying property, or applying it for unauthorised purposes, it is not proved that that
person acted with any fraudulent intent, he or she shall not be liable to conviction but may
be ordered to deliver up all such property or may be ordered to repay any sum of money
applied improperly, with costs, and in default of such delivery or repayment or of the
payment of such costs as aforesaid to be imprisoned for three months.
(3) Complaint under this section may be made—
(a)

in the case of a society, by the society or a member authorised by the society,
or the trustees or committee of the society;

(b)

in the case of a branch, by—
(i) the branch or a member authorised by the branch or the trustees or
committee thereof,
(ii) the central body of the society of which the branch forms part, or
(iii) any member of the society or branch authorised by the central body;
or

(c)

in any case, by the Authority or by a person authorised in writing by the
Authority.

(4) Nothing in this Act shall prevent any such person from being proceeded against
by way of indictment, if not previously convicted of the same offence under the
provisions of this Act.
(5) In any proceedings instituted under this section against an officer, member or other
person in respect of any sum surcharged or balance certified by an auditor appointed under
this Act upon audit of the books and accounts of a society or branch to be due by the
officer, member or other person, the onus of proving the same to have been the result of
any accidental or formal slip, error or omission shall lie upon such officer, member or
other person, as the case may be.

### 55.

Penalties for falsification, etc.
(1) If a person wilfully makes, or causes to be made, any entry, erasure in, or
omission from, a balance sheet of a society or branch, or a return or document required to
be sent, produced or delivered, for the purposes of this Act with intent to falsify the same,
or to evade any of the provisions of this Act, he or she shall be guilty of an offence and
liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of three thousand dollars.
(2) An officer or other person who makes any material omission or omits to make any
material entry which by this Act or the regulations thereunder or by the rules of a society
or branch he or she is required to make in any cash book, general ledger, blotter,
statement showing receipts and expenditure, balance sheet, contribution card, members’
register, account record, return or other document affecting or relating to the property or

affairs of a society or branch or to the benefits, rights or privileges of a member thereof
shall, unless he or she proves that he or she had no intent to defraud, be guilty of an
offence.
(3) If in any statement showing receipts and expenditure, balance sheet, return,
record, form, account or other document required for the purposes of this Act an officer
wilfully makes a statement or entry false in any material particular or knowing the same
to be untrue in any material particular, he or she shall, unless he or she proves that he or
she had no intent to defraud, be guilty of an offence.

### 56.

Mutilation, etc., of books, cards, etc.
If a person conceals, destroys, mutilates, alters or falsifies any book, paper, security,
contribution card, receipt, birth certificate, death certificate or other document affecting,
belonging or relating to a society or branch or makes or is privy to the making of any
false or fraudulent entry in any register, book, contribution card, paper or other document
affecting, belonging or relating to the property or affairs of a society or branch with intent
to defraud or deceive the society or branch or any person he or she shall be liable, on
summary conviction, to imprisonment for six months and to a penalty of three thousand
dollars.

### 57.

Penalty for ordinary offences
A society or branch, and an officer or member of a society or branch, or other person
guilty of an offence under this Act for which a penalty is not expressly provided shall be
guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand five
hundred dollars.

### 58.

Special offences in the case of friendly societies
If an officer or person aids or abets in the amalgamation or transfer of engagements or
in the dissolution of a friendly society otherwise than as in this Act provided he or she
shall be liable to the penalty imposed by this Act for offences thereunder and to be
imprisoned for three months.

### 59.

Recovery of penalties
(1) Unless otherwise expressly provided, all offences under this Act may be
prosecuted, and all penalties imposed by this Act or by regulations thereunder, or by the
rules of a society or branch, may be recovered in the manner provided by the Criminal
Procedure Code, Chapter 72B, and in the Magistrates Act, Chapter 177.
(2) Save as otherwise herein provided any such penalty shall be recoverable at the suit
of the Authority or of any person authorised in writing by the Authority or of any person
aggrieved.

### 60.

Limitation
In every case where no time is specially limited for making a complaint for an offence
under this Act such complaint shall be made within four years from the time when the
matter of such complaint arose, and not after.
Miscellaneous

### 61.

Making of regulations

(1) The Minister may make regulations to carry out the purposes of this Act. In
particular and without prejudice to the generality of the foregoing power such regulations
may—
(a)

prescribe the duties and functions of the Authority;

(b)

prescribe provisions respecting matters to be registered and procedure under
this Act;

(c)

prescribe the form and particulars of the register of members of a society or
branch and register of nominees and dependants of members;

(d)

prescribe provisions respecting levies and the form and particulars of a
record to be kept of the levies made by a society or branch;

(e)

prescribe the accounts and books to be kept by a society or branch and
provide for the audit of such accounts and other matters affecting the same,
and the fees for such audit, and prescribe the duties, functions, powers and
authorities to be assigned to auditors;

(f)

prescribe the duties of the committee of management respecting the
operation of a loan fund with provisions for the due recovery of loans;

(g)

prescribe provisions relating to management and administration, and for a
half-yearly inspection and administration report by every committee of
management;

(h)

provide for the payment of and other matters affecting bonus monies, and
prescribe the mode of recording such payments and the form and particulars
relating thereto; and provide for the payment of extra remuneration or
allowances to be paid to officers and members in respect of duties
appertaining to or connected with the payment of bonus monies;

(i)

provide for the safe keeping and recording of securities held by the trustee of
societies and branches; and prescribe provisions relating to certificates to be
given by trustees at certain times;

(j)

prescribe for the keeping of a rent-roll by a society or branch owning or
holding property, and the form and particulars thereof;

(k)

prescribe a scale of fees to be paid for matters to be transacted or for the
inspection of documents under this Act, and the fees to be paid in respect to
any act, matter or thing done or required to be done under the provisions of
this Act;

(l)

prescribe procedure with respect to voting at meetings of societies and
provisions relating to meetings generally;

(m)

provide for matters incidental to the payment and collection of contributions
and dues by the members of societies and branches and for the form, issue,
sale, custody, production, and delivery up of contribution cards and re-issue
of any which have been lost, destroyed, or defaced, and for the penalties to
be imposed for contravention or non-observance of any regulations relating
thereto;

(n)

prescribe the amount of security to be given by officers having receipt or
charge of monies and their sureties and the forms and attestation of bonds to
be given by such officers;

(o)

prescribe provisions for the keeping of an annual account of the anniversary
expenses of societies;

(p)

prescribe provisions relating to nominations by members;

(q)

prescribe for the keeping of a record of benefits paid to members and the
form and particulars thereof;

(r)

prescribe provisions for the payment of the funeral expenses of members;

(s)

prescribe provisions for reduction of benefits in certain circumstances;

(t)

prescribe provisions touching the rights, privileges and status or pensioners;

(u)

prescribe provisions for the examination of members of societies and
branches;

(v)

prescribe allowances in respect of vacation and sick leave to officers and
members of the committee of management and grants to be made to officers
with long service upon retirement;

(w)

prescribe provisions relating to inspections and inquiries under this Act;

(x)

prescribe fines not exceeding five hundred dollars for breaches of the said
regulations;

(y)

provide for any matters which are authorised by this Act to be prescribed;

(z)

provide for the extension of the services and facilities of hospitals
administering services related to health to the members of friendly societies
and arrangements under which the same shall be extended; and

(aa) prescribe the terms and conditions under which the members of friendly
societies shall be entitled to medical and surgical care and treatment in
hospitals, including specialist and consultant services and arrangements
under which friendly societies may subscribe and pay for the provision of
such services.
(2) Regulations made under this Act shall have no force or effect until they have been
approved by the House of Representatives and shall have been published in the Gazette.

### 62.

Validity of forms, etc.
Until amended, varied, rescinded or revoked in the manner respectively provided
under this Act, the forms contained in the Second Schedule and the fees set out in the
Third and Fourth Schedules shall be in force and effect.

### 63.

Appointment of public auditors and valuers
The Minister shall appoint public auditors and valuers for the purposes of this Act, and
shall determine the rates of remuneration to be paid by societies for their services.

### 64.

Evidence
Every instrument or document, or copy of or extract from an instrument or document,
bearing the seal or stamp, if any, of the office of the Authority, shall be received in
evidence without further proof; and every document purporting to be signed by the
Authority or an inspector or public auditor or valuer under this Act, shall, in the absence
of evidence to the contrary, be received in evidence in all courts and for all other
purposes, without proof of the signature or of the official position of the person signing it,
and shall be prima facie evidence of the truth of all matters contained in it.

### 65.

Protection of rights of volunteers, etc.
No person, by reason of his or her enrolment or service in any corps or militia, or
volunteers, shall lose or forfeit any interest he or she possesses, in any friendly society,
whether registered or unregistered, at the time of his or her being so enrolled or serving or
be fined for absence or non-attendance at any meeting of the society, if the absence or
non-attendance is occasioned by the discharge of his or her military duty as certified by
his or her commanding officer, any rules of the society to the contrary notwithstanding.

### 66.

Exemption from stamp duty
Stamp duty shall not be chargeable upon any of the following documents—
(a)

draft or order or receipt given by or to a friendly society or branch in respect
of monies payable by virtue of its rules or of this Act;

(b)

letter or power of attorney granted by a person as trustee for the transfer of
any monies of a friendly society or branch invested in his or her name in the
public funds;

(c)

bond given to or on account of a friendly society or branch, or by the
treasurer or other officer thereof;

(d)

policy of insurance or appointment or revocation of appointment of an agent,
or other document required or authorised by this Act or by the rules of a
friendly society or branch;

(e)

affidavit or statutory declaration for the purpose of this Act.

conferred upon the Authority by section 41.
10\. The imposition of a levy on each member of the society to be applied towards the
cost of its anniversary celebration, the amount of such levy to be fixed by the committee
of management and agreed to by the general body and not to exceed one dollar.
11\. In case of dividing societies, a provision for meeting all claims upon the society
existing at the time of division before any such division takes place.
12\. The keeping of separate accounts of all monies received or paid on account of every

13\. A valuation once in every five years, when required by the Authority, of the assets

14\. The voluntary dissolution of a society by consent of not less than five-sixths in value

15\. The right of one-fifth of the total number of members, or of one hundred members in

16\. Provisions may be made—

members under the age of fifty years.

18\. The determination as to whether the shares or any of them shall be transferable; and
provision for the form of transfer and registration of the shares, and for the consent of the
committee thereto; the determination as to whether the shares or any of them shall be

withdrawable, and provision for the mode of withdrawal and for payment of the balance
due thereon on withdrawing from the society.

FRIENDLY SOCIETIES REGULATIONS
[Section 61.]

[1st December, 1956.]

---

### 1.

Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Friendly Societies Regulations.

### 2.

Definitions
In these Regulations—
“Act” means the Friendly Societies Act, and any Act amending the same;
“auditor” includes every person appointed under the provisions of the Act to audit,
examine, verify, surcharge, allow or disallow the accounts of a society or branch;
“secretary” includes any officer appointed under the rules of a society or branch to
assist the secretary in the discharge of his or her duties.

### 3.

Accounts
The committee of management of every society and branch shall cause proper books
of account to be kept, including cash books for both the secretary and the treasurer, a
register of members, a record of benefits paid to members and a general ledger so
arranged as to exhibit on one side all the sums at the debit of the account of each
particular fund or benefit assured by the society and on the other all the sums at the credit
of each account.

### 4.

Inspection statements
The committee of management of every society or branch shall file with the Authority
half-yearly an inspection and administration report to be signed by every member thereof
as to his or her having personally inspected the books and accounts, funds and effects of
the society or branch and as to the same having been found to be correct and in order or
otherwise as the case may be. Such report shall be in the form numbered 14 in the
Schedule hereto or as near as may be, as the circumstances of the case shall require.

### 5.

Payment vouchers to be witnessed
Proper vouchers shall be obtained for every payment. Every receipt given by an
illiterate, infirm or other person incapable of writing to a society or branch in respect of
monies payable under or by virtue of its rules or of the Act for a benefit arising out of the
death of a member or any other person shall be witnessed by a Justice of the Peace,
Barrister-at-Law, Solicitor, Medical Practitioner, Minister of Religion, official attestor,
head teacher of a public elementary school or a member of the Police Force not below the
rank of Corporal.

### 6.

Financial provisions
The following provisions shall be observed in every society and branch—
(a)

all cash balances must be paid into the bank on the last day of the period to
be reviewed;

(b)

the books shall be balanced and the statements for audit prepared in
duplicate prior to the handing over of the accounts to the auditor; and the
Secretary shall sign the receipts and expenditure account and Balance Sheet
before they are submitted to the auditor;

(c)

for the purposes of audit all vouchers of payment shall be arranged in the
order of the dates of entries made in the treasurer’s cash book of the year to
be reviewed;

(d)

all bankers ‘Pass Books’, made up to the end of the period under review,
must be at hand at the time of audit;

(e)

the funds of the society or branch shall be invested in strict accordance with
the provisions of the Act. The investment of monies on notes of hand and
other personal securities is not legal;

(f)

the funds of a society or branch shall not be employed or used for promoting
excursions, or in almsgiving, donations to public causes or funds except in
accordance with the provisions of section 33 of the Act, or to officers or
members, or for rallies, dances, balls, concerts, or other social occasions or
enterprises. The said funds shall only be used in furthering the objects of a
society or branch and not otherwise;

(g)

the financial year of every society and branch shall end on the last day of
December.

### 7.

Guidance in keeping accounts
If, at any time, it appears to the Authority that the accounts of a society or branch are
not kept in such a manner as to show correctly the state and condition of the society or
branch as required under the Act the Authority may nominate an auditor or accountant to
give such instructions as will enable the officers of the society or branch to keep the
accounts correctly thereafter and the cost thereof shall be borne by the society or branch.

### 8.

Expenses of management
If the Authority, upon inquiry or other examination, or from the returns, or from the
special report of an auditor, or upon other evidence, finds that the receipts and funds of a
society or branch are insufficient to justify the continuance of the current expenses of
management and administration or of any other expenses, or that the rates of salaries or
other advances paid to its officers and members are excessive, unfairly discriminatory or
otherwise unreasonable, he or she may call upon the society or branch to reduce the
whole or any item thereof and may fix a limit therefor which shall in no case be exceeded
until further direction from him or her.

### 9.

Bonus statement
In the case of a society or branch whose rules provide for the payment of bonus
annually to its members the committee of management shall depute yearly such officers
and members, as it may think fit, to draw up and prepare the bonus list of the society or
branch and pay the amounts respectively payable to members therein appearing. For the
due and proper fulfilment of such duties there shall be allowed to the officers and
members deputed as aforesaid allowances on the monies so paid out at the rates
following—
two per cent on the amount distributed up to five thousand dollars;
one per cent on the next five thousand dollars or any part thereof;
one-half per cent on any additional amount.

The allowances herein provided shall be based on the aggregate of the net amount paid
out and shall be divisible among such officers and members in such proportions as the
committee of management shall decide.

### 10.

Contents of bonus statement
Where by the rules of a society or branch provision is made for the payment of bonus
to members the annual bonus list shall contain a statement as to the bonus declared by the
society or branch for the year under reference, the registered number of and name of
every member entitled to bonus under the rules of the society or branch, the deductions to
be made, if any, against every such member respectively, the balance or amount
individually receivable by every such member and his or her receipt therefor, the
aggregate amount paid out as bonus for the said year, and a certificate signed at the foot
or end thereof by the officers and members deputed by the committee to execute the said
payments that the same have been duly and truly effected. The mark or signature of an
illiterate person in receipt of bonus shall be witnessed by an independent person. Every
person receiving bonus shall produce his or her current contribution card for stamping.

### 11.

Statement to be laid on table
The treasurer of every society and branch shall lay the last bonus list of the society or
branch duly certified by the officers and members required, on his or her table for the
inspection of members for the space of two months or during at least four consecutive
meetings of the society or branch, whichever is the less, following payment thereof.

### 12.

Manner of payment of bonus
No officer or servant of a society or branch shall draw or receive any bonus for or on
behalf of a member of the society or branch except at the express desire in writing of the
member or on production of the member’s card.

### 13.

Minutes of meetings
Every society shall cause full and proper minutes of all proceedings of its general and
special meetings and of all committee meetings to be entered in a book or books kept for
the purpose.

### 14.

Hours of meeting
Notwithstanding anything in the rules of a society or branch to the contrary no
meeting of any kind whatsoever shall take place or continue, and no subscriptions, levies
or fines or other monies shall be paid into the funds of a society or branch to any officer
thereof, between the hours of eleven o’clock in the night and six o’clock of the following
morning.

### 15.

Notice of meetings
It shall not be necessary to give or serve notice of any general or regular meeting, the
time or times for the holding of which are specifically stated in the rules of a society or
branch, upon any member respectively thereof, anything to the contrary in the rules or
constitution of the society or branch notwithstanding.

### 16.

Special meetings
Notice of a special meeting of a society or branch shall be deemed to have been given
or served upon every member of the society or branch if a notification thereof be stuck up
in a conspicuous place in the registered office of the society or branch, as the case may
be, for at least fourteen clear days previous to such special meeting, or in the alternative,

if notice thereof be published once a week in a daily newspaper circulating in Grenada
during at least two consecutive weeks immediately prior to such special meeting,
anything to the contrary in the rules or constitution of the society or branch
notwithstanding.

### 17.

Loan fund statement
The committee of management shall present yearly to the general body in general
meeting a statement of accounts with respect to the operation of the society’s loan fund, if
any, showing the loans outstanding, deposits on hand, a report as to the instalments or
repayments due and unpaid, the action proposed to be taken in each case and a general
review of the operation of the said fund.

### 18.

Committee of management to consider loan fund statement
Notwithstanding the functions or powers of any other committee under the rules of a
society the committee of management shall satisfy itself that the provisions of the Act,
and of any regulations thereunder, and all rules relating to the operation of a loan fund,
have been complied with, and shall consider whether there has been any alteration in the
status of any borrower or his or her sureties, if any, which would justify special action
being taken for the termination of any loan.

### 19.

Loan books – Form of
If the loan be repayable by instalments the borrower shall be supplied with a card or
book (for which he or she shall pay a sum not exceeding sixty cents) in which shall be
recorded the amount and the terms of the loan and in which the instalments repaid shall
be entered and initialled by the officer to whom the monies are paid.

### 20.

Committee of management to sue for outstanding loans
Except in the cases where the committee of management renews a promissory note at
maturity so soon as the debt or monies due in respect of any loan made from a loan fund
shall become due and payable by a member to a society, the committee of management
shall, except for good cause, forthwith take all reasonable and necessary steps to sue for
recovery and receive such debt or monies and all interest due thereon together with all
legal, usual and customary costs, charges and expenses.

### 21.

Deposit of securities
Except the Authority shall otherwise order, the trustees of every society or branch
shall deposit any securities, Crown Grants and other muniments of title held by them
relating to the society or branch or to the property of the society or branch in the
aggregate exceeding the value of one thousand dollars with any banker or banking
company or any other company whose business includes the undertaking of the safe
custody or control of documents, and any sum payable in respect of such deposit shall be
paid out of the funds of the society or branch.

### 22.

Voting slips
Every financial member of a society or branch shall at any meeting held for the
election of an officer or officers on production of his or her current contribution card, be
furnished with a voting slip, if the chairperson thinks it necessary, at the registered office
or other place of meeting where the election is to take place.

### 23.

Method of voting

At all meetings of a society or branch voting shall be by ballot or show of hands as
required by the rules of the society or branch:
Provided that, where the rules are silent, the majority of financial members present
shall determine which of such methods shall be followed. Only financial members present
and entitled to vote under the rules of the society or branch may vote. No member shall
vote for more than one candidate for one office. A member shall in no circumstances
whatsoever vote by proxy.

### 24.

Vote to be for one officer at a time
Not more than one officer may be voted for at one time. Officers shall not be elected
en bloc save in the case of the election to office of the entire committee of management
by acclamation, that is to say, without any dissentient vote or protest.

### 25.

Who may be elected
Except in the case of the office of trustee, no person shall be a candidate for any office
unless he or she shall be a financial member of the society or branch.

### 26.

Nominators to be financial
No candidate shall be submitted for election except his or her proposer and seconder
are both financial members of the society or branch.

### 27.

Nominator only to propose one officer for one office
No member may propose or second more than one person for one office.

### 28.

Presiding at election
No member may preside at his or her own election or re-election to office.

### 29.

Counting of votes
(1) Two or more tellers, as the case may be, shall be appointed by the chairperson for
the purpose of counting the votes.
(2) If the chairperson thinks it necessary, votes may be counted in groups, that is to
say, those supporting candidate A may be collected in one group; those supporting
candidate B may be collected in another group, and so on, with the formation of as many
groups as there are candidates.

### 30.

Minors disqualified for office
A member under the age of 21 years shall not hold an elective office in a society or
branch.

### 31.

Probationers disqualified for office
Probationary members, that is to say, members who have not yet qualified for benefits,
are not eligible for office.

### 32.

Tenure of office
In the event of the failure of an election in respect of any office, those previously
appointed shall continue in office pending the holding of a valid election as respects such
office. Every officer shall remain in office until the installation of his or her successor.

### 33.

Eligibility for re-election
Where the rules of a society or branch are silent retiring members shall be eligible for
re-election.

### 34.

Contribution cards – Form of
The committee of management of every society and branch shall issue to every
member a contribution card in the prescribed form, and it shall be the duty of the
secretary to enter thereon from time to time all payments made by the member in respect
of entrance fee, monthly or weekly contributions, as the case may be, anniversary
subscription, levies, dues, fines, arrears and other payments made under the rules of the
society or branch. Every entry shall be initialled by the secretary. Every card shall contain
the necessary columns to provide for all the above entries from the 1st day of January to
the 31st day of December in any one year. No card shall be issued to a member unless
and until it is signed by the secretary and the president.

### 35.

Contribution cards – Return of
Every card whenever issued shall be returned to the secretary as soon as possible after
the 31st December of the year of its issue but in no event later than the 31st January of the
year following its issue, and no card for the following year shall be issued to a member
until he or she shall have returned his or her card for the previous year or satisfactorily
accounted for its loss. It shall be the duty of the secretary to furnish the committee of
management with a list of those members who have failed to return their cards by the 31st
January as required hereunder and the committee of management of a society shall
inquire into the reason or reasons for the non-return of every card not on the said list, and
may impose a fine not exceeding one dollar and twenty-five cents on any member of the
society who has failed so to return his or her card.

### 36.

Contribution cards to be audited
All cards returned by members shall be kept by the secretary and submitted for audit
along with the said list showing the action taken in each case by the committee of
management as respects those members who have failed to return their cards as hereunder
provided.

### 37.

Secretary to produce certified list of contributions
It shall be the duty of the secretary of every society or branch to supply the auditor for
the purposes of the annual audit with a certified list of the amount of contributions, dues,
levies, and fines in arrear as respects every individual member in respect of every
particular fund for the year under review; and it shall be the duty of the auditor to check
and verify the items therein appearing with the register of members and contribution
cards of the society or branch.

### 38.

Discipline
Every society and branch shall under its rules have full power and authority to enforce
discipline among its members and obedience to its said rules and to expel or otherwise
discipline its members:
Provided that notwithstanding anything to the contrary contained in the rules of a
society or branch a member shall be entitled to be fully informed of any charges against
him or her, with the opportunity to be heard in his or her own defense, before he or she
can lawfully be suspended or deprived of membership.

### 39.

Submission of accounts

The accounts of every society and branch shall close on the 31st December in every
year, and shall be submitted together with vouchers, receipts, and all other supporting
documents not later than the 31st May in each year to audit at the cost and charge of the
society or branch to a person nominated by the Minister.

### 40.

Annual audit
(1) The annual audit under the Act shall extend to the whole of the accounts of the
society or branch.
(2) The auditor shall make himself or herself acquainted so far as necessary for the
purposes of the audit with the provisions of the Act, any amendments thereto and the
regulations made thereunder, and with the rules of the particular society or branch, the
accounts of which he or she is auditing.
(3) The auditor shall satisfy himself or herself—
(a)

as to the accuracy and sufficiency of the books and accounts of the society or
branch and that all transactions of the society or branch are correctly and
adequately recorded therein;

(b)

that all payments made are duly vouched and have been authorised in the
manner prescribed by the Act, or by these Regulations;

(c)

that so far as reasonably practicable all sums received and all payments
made by the society or branch for the period have been brought to account;

(d)

that where separate contributions are paid to particular funds these have been
stated separately and that any payments from the funds have been correctly
charged thereto; and

(e)

that the accounts submitted to him or her for audit are drawn up in the form
and disclose such particulars as are prescribed or required under the Act or
by these Regulations.

(4) Should there be errors in the annual return, or in the books, accounts, vouchers or
other documents submitted to the auditor, the books, accounts, vouchers or other
documents shall be returned (at the cost of the society) for correction or adjustment,
unless the auditor be requested by the society to correct the inaccuracies, in which case he
or she shall be entitled to claim an additional fee to be arranged between him or her and
the society, the amount however, to be paid personally by the officer or officers
respectively responsible for the particular error or errors, as the case may be. In the event
of any dispute arising between the society and the auditor relating to any matter or thing
under this section either party shall be at liberty to refer the same to the Authority whose
decision shall be final.

### 41.

Powers of auditor
For the purpose of carrying into effect the provisions of the Act and of these
Regulations the auditor shall at every audit make such investigations as may be
necessary, interrogate officers or persons concerned and examine accounts.

### 42.

Secretary to deliver list of documents
Where any accounts of a society or branch are to be audited by an auditor appointed
under the provisions of the Act the secretary of the society or branch shall make out and
deliver to the auditor a list in writing of all vouchers, receipts, invoices and other
supporting documents and the books of the society or branch in which the accounts of the
society or branch are kept.

### 43.

Auditor to examine accounts

The auditor of every society or branch shall at every audit personally examine the
securities, Crown Grants and other muniments of title belonging to the society or branch
where these are in the possession of the society or branch, or he or she shall obtain a
certificate from the Bank of what securities, Crown Grants or other muniments of title the
Bank holds for the society or branch in safe keeping at the close of business on the day to
which the accounts are made up and audited.

### 44.

Auditor to verify Balance Sheet
Every auditor shall verify every Balance Sheet and shall either certify that it is correct
and in accordance with law, or specially report to the society or branch and the Authority
in what respect he or she finds it defective, incorrect, or not in accordance with law.

### 45.

Auditor to report to Authority
The person appointed to audit the accounts of a society or branch shall send to the
Authority within three months of such accounts being submitted to him or her for audit a
statement of the funds or affairs including receipts and expenditure and a copy of the
Balance Sheet of the society or branch made out to the preceding 31st December together
with full, honest and true answers to the questionnaire addressed to him or her by the
Authority relating to the books and accounts of the society or branch and in the event of
there being any misapplication or wrongful withholding of the funds, monies or property
of the society or branch by any person or persons a special report thereon.

### 46.

Auditor to examine every entry in books
(1) Upon every audit of the accounts of a society or branch it shall be the auditor’s
duty to examine every entry in the books of the society or branch; and under no
circumstances whatsoever may the accuracy of the said accounts be verified by test
checks.
(2) It shall be the duty of the auditor to demand a voucher for every item of
expenditure and in the case of sick, death and funeral benefits to compare the books of
account with the contribution cards of those who received such benefits, and to satisfy
himself or herself that those who received such benefits were in good compliance or
standing and entitled to be paid.
(3) The auditor shall also require to be produced a certificate of death, in accordance
with the provisions of section 37 of the Act, in respect of every death and funeral benefit
paid by the society or branch.

### 47.

Auditor to surcharge where necessary
Any sum surcharged by the auditor or any balance certified by him or her to be due
shall be paid into the office of the Authority by the person or persons who are surcharged
or from whom the balance is certified to be due within thirty days after delivery of the
special report to the society or branch and to the Authority, and if not paid within the time
aforesaid, the person or persons in default may be proceeded against in accordance with
the provisions of the Act. In the event of default being made by any such person the
surety or sureties of such person shall be informed in writing of such default by the
Authority and unless the amount due shall be paid by such surety or sureties into the
office of the Authority within a period of thirty days after such demand such surety or
sureties may be proceeded against in accordance with the provisions of the Act. In the
event of any person or persons dying without having fully paid the sum surcharged or
balance certified to be due, the same may be recovered by the Authority as a civil debt
from the legal personal representatives of the said deceased person or persons. On any
proceedings under this regulation a certificate purporting to be signed by the auditor shall
be conclusive evidence of the facts certified.

### 48.

Auditor to disallow unauthorised expenditure
If it appears to any auditor to whom the accounts of a society or branch shall have
been submitted under the Act, that any item of account is contrary to law, or that any
monies or income which ought to have been brought into account has not been so brought
into account, the auditor shall disallow the item of account and shall in his or her special
report surcharge the amount of any unlawful payment or expenditure, or of any loss or
deficiency, upon the committee of management of the society or branch, or upon any
officer or member thereof, or upon any other person by whose negligence or wrongful act
that payment or expenditure has been made or authorised or that loss or deficiency has
been incurred:
Provided that any item of account disallowed or surcharged by the auditor may be
reviewed by the Authority and the disallowance or surcharge varied or remitted by him or
her if he or she is of opinion that in the circumstances of the case it is fair and equitable to
do so.

### 49.

Auditor to consider sufficiency of funds
Where in the opinion of one auditor the annual return or Balance Sheet of a society or
branch shows that the society or branch is not in a position to provide for the payment of
its benefits at the rates in force without increase in its existing rates of contribution or that
owing to depletion in membership, excessive mortality, sickness, epidemic, or other cause
its funds are insufficient or are likely to be insufficient to provide in the future for the
payment thereof, he or she shall specially report the same to the Authority and in such
report shall set out all material facts relating to its financial condition as in his or her
opinion warrant alteration in its existing rates of contribution. The Authority after
consideration of the said report may call upon the society or branch to make within such
time as he or she may fix, but not exceeding six months, such increase in its rates of
contribution as will enable the society or branch to provide for the payment of its benefits
at the rates in force, or alternatively such reduction in the benefits payable by the society
or branch, as will permit and justify its continuance upon a sound and solvent basis.

### 50.

Appointment of Actuary
The Authority may appoint an actuary, auditor, accountant, or other qualified person
to review for any period the operation of any society or branch proposing or intending to
give a benefit payable on the death of a member exceeding two hundred and fifty dollars,
and make a report to him or her on the financial condition of the said society or branch
and the adequacy or otherwise of the subscriptions received by such society or branch or
of its funds to support the benefits or bonus monies payable by the society or branch
having regard to the obligations or liabilities of the said society or branch under its rules
or any proposed rules, the cost of every such report to be borne and paid by the society or
branch.

### 51.

Auditor to complete questionnaire
Every auditor shall, if he or she shall think fit, set out, in answer to the questionnaire
submitted to him or her under the Act in relation to the audit of the accounts of a society
or branch, such requirements and recommendations as he or she may deem desirable for
the better operation of the society or branch or the better arrangement of its accounts, and
in the discretion of the Authority effect shall be given thereto by the committee of
management within such time as may be directed by him or her.

### 52.

Delivery of documents to Authority
Auditors are not entitled to travelling expenses save with the express leave of the
Authority. Books and documents must be delivered or posted to the auditor. Each society
and branch must take delivery of the said books and documents from the auditor at its

own expense. Delivery by an auditor to a society or branch of the annual return, Balance
Sheet and special report or any other document relating to an audit may be transmitted
through the post free, the envelope or cover being franked “O.H.M.S.” and superscribed
in clear legible handwriting with the signature and designation of the sender and the date
sent, for the purpose.

### 53.

Offences by auditors
Every auditor who fails to comply with any of the provisions of these Regulations
imposing a duty on him or her shall be guilty of an offence against these Regulations and
liable on summary conviction to a penalty of five hundred dollars.

### 54.

Levy – Amount of to be estimated
It shall be the duty of the committee of management in determining a levy authorised
by the rules of a society or branch to exercise a reasonable and honest discretion in
estimating the amount that such levy may be expected to realise. The committee shall not
make a larger or heavier levy than that which shall reasonably suffice to satisfy the
purpose for which such levy may lawfully be made.

### 55.

Levy book to be kept
A book shall be kept for the purpose of recording all levies imposed upon members by
a society or branch during each year, and of the estimated results of such levies, and of
the reason for each such levy, and of the purposes for which the monies arising thereby
have been applied. Such record shall contain the particulars in the Form 13 set forth in the
Schedule hereto.

### 56.

Levy – Maximum amount of
Notwithstanding anything to the contrary contained in the rules of a society or branch,
the society or branch shall not, save with the written consent of the Authority, impose a
levy or levies which shall in the aggregate in relation to a Building Fund exceed the sum
of five dollars, or in relation to a Banner Fund the sum of one dollar respectively, payable
by any member of the society or branch.

### 57.

Additional levies
The committee of management may make such additional levies from time to time
upon all members of the society or branch as shall be necessary, in the opinion of the
committee of management to prevent any deficit arising in the management fund thereof,
and such additional levies shall be binding on the members of the society or branch
notwithstanding anything to the contrary contained in the provisions of the rules or
constitution of the society or branch.

### 58.

Rent-roll
Where a portion of the income of a society or branch is derived from the receipt of
rents a proper rent-roll shall be kept by the society or branch, and it shall be lawful for
every society and branch owning or holding property bringing in rent to pay a
commission at the usual or customary rate to an agent or other person on the amount of
rent collected by him or her. Where a society or branch has paid any sums by way of
commission in respect of the collection of rent the amount so paid or allowed shall be
stated on the rent-roll of the society or branch.

### 59.

Trustees certificates

(1) Where the rules provide that elections be held or that financial officers be
appointed semi-annually or at any other time during a financial year, the committee of
management shall cause a proper check of the books of account to be made at the time of
such election or appointment and a certificate shall thereupon be issued under the hands
of the trustees to the treasurer certifying the amount of cash in his or her hands on the
date the said check was made.
(2) A similar check shall be made at the end of a financial year and a similar
certificate issued by the trustees, and the balance found to be in the hands of the treasurer
shall thereupon be forthwith deposited by the treasurer in a savings bank and the receipt
or deposit slip produced to the trustees. The trustees shall provide the treasurer with
monies required by him or her for the purposes of the business of the society if necessary.

### 60.

Anniversary expenses
A detailed statement of receipts and expenditure in connection with the annual
Anniversary celebrations of every society shall be prepared yearly by the officer or
officers responsible therefor and presented to the committee of management with the
necessary supporting vouchers, invoices and documents. The said statement shall be
submitted for audit by the society together with its accounts.

### 61.

Bonds – Amount of
(1) In all cases where under the rules of a society or branch a bond is required to be
given by a treasurer, the bond shall, except where the Authority otherwise orders, be
given in an amount not less than double the amount of monies to be placed in the
possession or control of the treasurer and with at least one surety in a like sum.
(2) In no case under the rules of a society or branch a bond is required to be given by
any other officer having receipt or charge of monies be less than one-half of the amount
required from the treasurer, with at least one surety in a like sum.
(3) In all cases under the rules of a society Authority branch the bond required to be
given by a trustee shall, except where the Authority otherwise orders, be in a sum not less
than two hundred and fifty dollars with at least one surety in a like sum.

### 62.

Bonds – Attestation of
Bonds shall be attested by a Justice of the Peace or commissioner of oaths who shall take
care (as far as possible) that the sureties to bonds are responsible persons. Sureties to bonds
must testify. Every bond shall upon execution be filed in the office of the Authority.

### 63.

Bonds – Continuation of
A bond may be continuing to the extent that an officer re-elected to the said office in a
society or branch for a consecutive term or terms need not furnish a fresh bond, and the
surety thereto may therein undertake to become bound, without further attestation, in
respect of any such consecutive term or terms of office to be held by the principal in the
society or branch. The committee of management of every society or branch shall at least
once in every year verify the existence of the sureties to bonds furnished by officers.

### 64.

Reduction of benefits
In the event of any epidemic, excessive sickness, emergency or other unforeseen
contingency impairing the funds of a society or branch the committee of management of
the society or branch may by a resolution of the majority of its members reduce the
benefits payable or afforded under its rules or impose a special levy or levies upon the
members of the society or branch and such reduction of benefits or special levy or levies

shall be binding on the members of the society or branch notwithstanding anything to the
contrary contained in the provisions of its rules or constitution.

### 65.

Reduction of benefit: Resolution to be filed with Authority
Where the committee of management of a society or branch shall determine by
resolution to reduce the amount payable in respect of any benefit under its rules on the
grounds of epidemic, excessive sickness, emergency or for other good cause, a copy of
every such resolution signed by the secretary and countersigned by the president or chief
executive officer of the society or branch shall be sent to the Authority and until that copy
is so sent and an acknowledgement received therefor the resolution shall not take effect.
The committee of management shall also cause a notification of every such reduction of
benefit to be hung up in a conspicuous place in the registered office of the society or
branch for a period of not less than two months or to be mentioned during at least four
consecutive general meetings of the society or branch, whichever is the less.

### 66.

Nominations
Every nomination made by a member, and every revocation and variation of a
nomination, shall be recorded by the society or branch, and for that recording the rules of the
society or branch may require the member to pay a sum not exceeding twenty-five cents.

### 67.

Examination of members
The committee of management of every society and branch shall have the right, and
every member of every such society and branch shall afford to the committee of
management of the society or branch, an opportunity to examine, through its medical,
dental or optical officer, as the case may be, the person of the said member when and as
often as it may reasonably require while a claim for benefits is pending or within a
reasonable time thereafter notwithstanding any provisions contained in the rules or
constitution of the society or branch to the contrary.

### 68.

Inspections and inquiries
(1) The Authority or any person authorised in writing under section 43 of the Act
shall, in addition to any other powers, authorities and privileges assigned to him or her
respectively thereunder, have power to do all or any of the following things: namely—
(a)

to make such examination and inquiry as may be necessary to ascertain
whether the provisions of the Act relating to a society or branch are
complied with;

(b)

to enter the registered office or place of meeting of the society or branch and
inspect any cards, vouchers, documents, accounts, returns, books, ledgers,
funds, securities, monies and effects of such society or branch thereof at all
reasonable times of the day or night, but so as not to impede or obstruct the
working of the said society or branch;

(c)

to examine into the administration of benefits by the society or branch and
make inquiries respecting the state and condition of any member of the
society or branch and for such purposes to enter any premises where he or
she is, or may be, stated to be at all reasonable hours during the daytime; and
to examine into the payment of the benefits paid, alleged to have been paid,
or required to be paid, to him or her under the rules of the society or branch,
from time to time in force, and all or any matters and things connected with
or relating to the health, care and treatment of members of the society or
branch in receipt of benefits or claiming the same and the imposition or
attempted imposition by members in respect of any benefits upon the funds
of the society or branch; and

(d)

to exercise such other powers as may be necessary for carrying the Act into
effect.

(2) Every person authorised as aforesaid shall be furnished by the Authority with a
certificate of his or her appointment, and on applying for admission to any premises or
place for the purposes of the Act or these Regulations shall, if so required, produce the
said certificate.

### 69.

Pensioners – Creation of
No society or branch shall save by resolution of a majority of the members present and
entitled to vote in general meeting declare any person to be a pensioner on its funds. At
least fourteen (14) days’ notice of such meeting and intended resolution shall be given to
such person. Every pensioner shall be provided without charge with a card or document
signed by the secretary and countersigned by the president containing the name and
registered number of the society or branch, the name, age and address of the person
declared to be a pensioner, the reason for such declaration and the date from which such
person is declared to be a pensioner as aforesaid.

### 70.

Pensioners – List of
Every society and branch shall keep and maintain in a book (to be known as the “List
of Pensioners”) a list of the pensioners upon its funds, and the secretary shall immediately
duly record therein the particulars herein required of every person declared to be a
pensioner of the society or branch. Every such list shall contain the name, age, address,
and reason for the declaration of pensionable status in respect of every person recorded
therein and shall be open to inspection at all reasonable times by members of the society
or branch without fee.

### 71.

Pensioner, revision of status of member
Nothing herein shall prevent a member who has become a pensioner of a society or
branch from being by majority vote of the members present and entitled to vote at a
general meeting of the society or branch taken off the list of pensioners and from regaining
his or her previous status as an ordinary member subject to such terms and conditions as
the society or branch may impose. A note to such effect shall however be made by the
secretary in the list of pensioners against the name of every pensioner who has regained
his or her status as an ordinary member and the date thereof.

### 72.

Pensioner’s right of appeal
Notwithstanding anything to the contrary contained in the rules or constitution of a
society or branch every pensioner respectively thereof, who has against his or her will
been restored to the status of an ordinary member with the duties and obligations
attendant thereon as required under the rules of the society or branch, shall be entitled to
apply to the Authority who may if he or she considers the said restoration to be unfair or
inequitable in the circumstances of the case order him or her to be retained as a pensioner
of the society or branch or make such other order as the justice of the case may require.

### 73.

Vacation and sick leave
Notwithstanding anything contained in the Act or in the rules or constitution of a
society or branch to the contrary every member of the committee of management of a
society or branch shall in every calendar year be entitled to leave at the full salary or
allowance payable in respect of his or her particular office therein for the following
periods, that is to say—
(i) fourteen days’ vacation leave, and

(ii) a period not exceeding fourteen days in respect of sickness on the production
of a medical certificate stating that the member is incapacitated by reason of
illness from carrying out his or her duties as such member, without prejudice
to the rights or privileges to which he or she may otherwise be entitled as a
financial member in good standing:
Provided that not more than two members of the committee of management shall be
granted vacation leave at one and the same time.

### 74.

Acting appointment of officers
It shall not be requisite for any person acting for or on behalf of any member of the
committee of management absent on vacation leave or on sick leave for the respective
periods in the foregoing regulation provided to enter into a bond in respect of the
fulfilment of his or her acting duties. The committee of management shall make all acting
appointments.

### 75.

Forms returns, etc.
(1) Forms, and other documents prescribed under the provisions of the Act or of these
Regulations shall be strictly adhered to, and the Authority may in default of compliance
therewith return any such form, or other document to the society, branch, officer or other
person sending or tendering the same for such further entries or amendments as he or she
may deem necessary:
Provided that where the circumstances shall so require forms to the like effect with the
necessary modifications subject to the approval of the Authority shall be used:
Provided always that all such forms are in substantial compliance with the
requirements of the Act and the regulations made thereunder.
(2) The Forms in the Schedule hereto shall be used until other forms are substituted
therefor.
