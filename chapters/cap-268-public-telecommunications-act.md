## CHAPTER 268

PUBLIC TELECOMMUNICATIONS ACT
An Act to provide for an internal and an international telecommunication system in
place of the services hitherto operated by the Grenada Telephone Company
Limited and by Cable and Wireless (West Indies) Limited, and for connected
matters.
[Act No. 1 of 1989 amended by Act No. 20 of 1997, Act No. 31 of 2000.]

[12th January, 1989.]

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Public Telecommunications Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
(1) In this Act—
“Company” means Grenada Telecommunications Limited having its registered office
in the town of Saint George and any successor or lawful assignee of the Company;
“highway” means street or road owned by or under the control of the Government
or any public body and public right-of-way, including any bridge forming part thereof,
and any land abutting and forming part of a highway;
“line” includes wire, post, cable, insulator, pipe, conduit and covering in
connection with or for the purpose of any telecommunication facility, and such plant,
machinery and equipment as may be necessary at any time for installing, maintaining
or removing the same;
“message” means any communication transmitted or intended to be transmitted by
means of the system;
“Minister” means the Minister to whom responsibility for the subject of
communications is assigned;
“owner”, as regards Crown lands, means the Minister to whom responsibility for
Crown lands is assigned, and as regards other land includes the person having actual
occupation thereof;

“post” includes pole, standard, bracket, stay, support, and any other contrivance for
carrying, suspending or supporting a line;
“rate” includes charge, rental, and any other payment whatsoever for service
provided by the Company;
“service” includes the accommodation afforded subscribers and users of the
telecommunication system provided by the Company and the use and accommodation
afforded the public by the facilities employed by or in connection with the Company,
excluding in each case any commodity, by-product or article produced or
manufactured by the Company or any associate thereof that is offered for sale to the
public;
“subscriber” means any person who shall enter into an agreement or contract with
the Company for the provision of any service or appliance;
“system” includes buildings, installations, exchanges, lines, plant, machinery,
appliances, apparatus for electronic or light waves and any other device or thing,
whether similar to the foregoing or not, required or used for the purpose of
telecommunication;
“telecommunication” means the transmission, emission or reception of signs,
signals, writing, images and sound or intelligence of any nature by wire, radio, optical
or other electromagnetic means;
“tree” includes any or any part of any growing wood, bamboo, undergrowth or
other produce of the soil whether cultivated or not;
“village” means any community consisting of not less than ten inhabited houses;
“works” includes work which the Company has authority to perform under this or
any other Act and everything of whatever description required to supply or maintain
the system or the service and to carry into effect the objects of the Company in
pursuance of this Act.
(2) Expressions referring to the construction or maintenance of a line along any street,
highway, land or building, include the placing or maintaining of a line over, along, under,
or across such street, highway, land or building and any post or other structure in, upon or
under such street, highway, land or building.

## PART I

Rights and Obligations of the Company

### 3.

Repealed.

### 4.

Company may execute works
(1) Without prejudice to any other power vested in it, the Company may, from time to
time, and with the approval of the Minister and subject to such conditions as he or she
may reasonably specify—
(a)

place and maintain a line over, under or along a highway or land and, for
such purpose, open or break-up the highway or land, and alter the position
thereunder of any pipe, wire, cable or conduit for the supply of water or
electricity; and

(b)

temporarily close a highway or a section thereof for the purpose of laying or
maintaining a line.

(2) In subsection (1), “land” means public land including land owned or occupied by
the Government or a public body.

### 5.

Authority to cut trees
(1) In the course of constructing or maintaining any works the company may, by its
officers or agents duly authorised in writing, cut and remove from any highway or land
any tree tending to interfere with, endanger, or otherwise prejudicially affect the works,
but no person shall for this purpose, except with the consent of the owner, enter land until
after the expiration of seven days, or in case of emergency three days, from the day on
which notice of such entry shall have been served on the owner of the land.
(2) Where any tree so removed was not growing on or projecting over a highway, the
Company shall make adequate compensation for the loss, if any, occasioned by such
cutting or removal.

### 6.

Company to comply with international treaties, etc.
Nothing in this Act shall exempt the Company from complying with the provisions of
any enactment, whether passed before or after the commencement of this Act, giving
effect to or arising out of any international treaty, convention or agreement concerning
telecommunication.

### 7.

Repealed.

### 8.

Company to pay fee annually to Government
(1) The Company shall pay to the Government an annual fee amounting to three per
cent of the Company’s net annual revenue, payment being made in arrears and each
instalment being made within sixty days of the submission of the Company’s annual
accounts pursuant to section 13(5).
(2) For the purposes of this section, “net revenue” means the net proceeds of all
billings to customers (including subscribers) within Grenada less all out payments to
foreign administrations and other foreign national bodies for traffic originating in
Grenada which terminates in or transits other territories, plus all payments received from
such administrations and bodies for traffic originating in other territories which
terminates in or transits Grenada.

### 8A.

Fees for licence granted by Minister
(1) Where, on or after the commencement of this Act, the Minister grants a licence to
a person under section 3(1)(c) to operate a television service, that person shall pay the
following fees—
(a)

an annual fee of ten thousand dollars commencing upon the grant of the
licence and thereafter on the anniversary of such grant;

(b)

a surcharge of two and one-half per cent of the gross revenue of the licensee
which shall be paid within one month of the end of the financial year of the
licensee.

(2) Notwithstanding anything contained in any enactment, rule of law or legal
instrument, where a licence was granted prior to the commencement of this Act, the
licensee shall pay the fees specified in subsection (1) with effect from the anniversary of
such grant starting in the year 1998.
(3) The Minister may, by Order, vary the fees specified in subsection (1); but any
such order shall be subject to negative resolution of Parliament.

## PART II

The Telecommunication System

### 9.

Company to maintain service in an efficient manner
The Company shall construct, maintain and operate its works in a safe and efficient
manner so as to secure prompt and satisfactory service, and if there shall be any
interruption of the service the Company shall, without unnecessary delay, employ all such
means within its power to remove the cause of the interruption and to restore the service.
The Company shall utilise suitable equipment and apparatus incorporating proven
technological advances wherever these can be shown to be appropriate and cost effective
and shall seek to develop, expand and improve the system and service it provides in a
manner consistent with these objectives and with the advance of telecommunication
technology.

### 10.

Company to maintain communication with towns, etc.
The Company shall not, except with the consent of the Minister, cease to maintain
telephonic communication with any town, village, or rural area, in respect of which the
means of such communication exist or shall at any time have been established through the
works of the Company.

### 11.

Public telephones
The company shall establish in every town and as far as practicable in every village in
which it shall construct or maintain a telephone line (except a line maintained at the
request of the Minister under this Act) at least one public pay telephone which shall be
open at all times for the reception, transmission and delivery of the messages of all
persons alike, without favour or preference, on payment of the authorised rate.

## PART III

Rates

### 12.

Temporary provision
Until altered by the Company upon such notice as is required by its contracts with
subscribers the rates in force immediately before the commencement of this Act shall be
the rates payable in respect of the Company’s services.

### 13.

Procedure for increasing rates
(1) The Company shall be at liberty at any time and from time to time to serve upon
the Minister a notice in writing referring to this section and requesting specified increases
in respect of specified current rates.
(2) Within one month next following the receipt of such notice the Minister may, in
writing served upon the Company, elect—
(a)

to accede to the Company’s request, in which case, upon the expiry of the
period of three months next following receipt by the Minister of the
Company’s notice, the specified rates shall be increased as specified in the
Company’s request; or

(b)

to decline the request, in which case his or her reasons for so declining shall
have been stated by the Minister:

Provided that if on any occasion the Minister does not exercise his or her right to elect
he or she shall be deemed on that occasion to have acceded to the Company’s request.
(3) If the Minister elects to decline the Company’s request, he or she and the
Company shall promptly endeavour to reach agreement concerning the matter; if
agreement has not been reached within four weeks after the Minister so declined, the

matter shall, at the request of the Company, be referred to arbitration by two arbitrators,
one to be appointed by each party.
(4) For the purposes of an arbitration under subsection (3) the Arbitration Act,

## Chapter 19, shall apply subject as follows. The arbitrator shall be required, within a

period not exceeding four months, to determine the amount and nature of the increases, if
any, in the rates which should be made in order to provide the Company with sufficient
revenue to give to the Company a fair return on the fair value of its assets after meeting
all expenditure on income account and after making reasonable provision for bad or
doubtful debts and for depreciation or replacement or diminution in value of assets, and
for all taxes or other imposts, except that all revenue, expenditure, and assets not relating
to or not employed in the Company’s telecommunication business shall be ignored.
(5) A copy of the audited annual accounts of the Company and a copy of the auditor’s
report thereon shall be submitted to the Minister within one month after the annual
general meeting of the Company.

## PART IV

Installation, etc., of Lines for Government

### 14.

Company to maintain telephonic communication for the public service
(1) The Minister may in writing direct the Company to provide, erect and maintain
such lines and appliances as may be necessary for securing special telephonic
communication or separate telephonic intercommunication for the public service or for
any specified Ministry or Department.
(2) Every such direction shall specify the time within which the Company must
comply therewith, being a period not less than three months from the date of service of
the direction on the Company unless, in the opinion of the Minister, it is a matter of
urgent necessity for the purpose of the Government that such communication be
established sooner. The Government shall remunerate the Company for providing,
erecting and maintaining such lines and appliances; the extent and manner of payment of
such remuneration shall, in case of difference between the Minister and the Company, be
referred to arbitration by two arbitrators, one to be appointed by each party, and the
Arbitration Act, Chapter 19, shall apply to any such arbitration.
(3) If the Company shall fail to comply with any such direction the Minister may
cause such lines and appliances to be connected with any works of the Company; and for
the purposes of this subsection the Minister shall have and may exercise all the powers
vested in the Company under this or any other Act subject nevertheless to the restrictions
and other provisions contained in every such Act.

### 15.

Transmission of messages for purposes of the public service
The Minister, or any public officer authorised by him or her in writing, shall have the
power to demand immediate transmission of a message urgently required to be delivered
for the purposes of the public service and, upon such request, the Company shall cause the
message to be transmitted as a matter of priority and shall, if necessary, suspend the
transmission of all other messages until such message shall have been transmitted.

## PART V

Miscellaneous Provisions

### 16.

Repealed.

### 17.

Service of documents on owners and occupiers
Any document by this Act required or authorised to be served on an owner or occupier
of premises shall be deemed to be properly addressed if addressed by the description
“owner” or “occupier” of the premises (naming the premises) without other name or
description; such document may be served by delivering it or a true copy of it to some
person on the premises, or, if there is no person on the premises to whom the same can
with reasonable diligence be delivered, by affixing the document to some conspicuous
part of the premises.

### 18.

Powers of entry
On reasonable notice to a subscriber, the Company’s employees and agents authorised
in writing by the Company may on production of their authority enter the premises of the
subscriber at any reasonable hour to make collection from any coin box or to install,
inspect, repair or replace any part of the equipment, instruments and lines or, upon
termination or cancellation of the service, to recover the equipment, instruments and
lines.

### 19.

Repealed.

### 20.

Powers of the Company in relation to subscribers
Where a subscriber—
(a)

abandons the service supplied to him or her;

(b)

fails, neglects or refuses on demand to pay the amount of any rate due from
him or her;

(c)

uses the service supplied to him or her in such a way as to impair or interfere
with the service supplied to other subscribers;

(d)

in any message uses or knowingly permits to be used profane, obscene or
abusive language or, after reasonable notice, fails, neglects or refuses to
prevent the use of such language in any message by others under his or her
control;

(e)

sends or knowingly permits to be sent any message calculated or likely to
cause annoyance to another subscriber, or the family or domestic staff or
business employees of such other subscriber;

(f)

damages or misuses any equipment, apparatus, circuits or devices furnished
by the Company; or

(g)

fails, neglects or refuses to observe any obligation or condition binding on
him or her and contained in a contract with the Company,

the Company may direct that the service supplied to such subscriber be suspended and may,
if the reason for the suspension is not corrected within a reasonable period, cause such
service to be disconnected and may make such arrangements as it may consider necessary
for the removal of equipment, instruments and lines from the premises of the subscriber.

### 21.

Telephone, etc., call numbers
The Company may re-assign or redistribute any telephone or other call number or
series of telephone or other call numbers to a subscriber—
(a)

at the request of the subscriber;

(b)

where in its opinion the retention of a particular number would embarrass or
cause unusual inconvenience to the subscriber;

(c)

if satisfied that it is expedient to do so; or

(d)

if it is requested so to do by the Minister.

### 22.

Limitation of liability to cost of message
If any message accepted for transmission by the Company or transmitted by means of
the system fails to reach its intended destination or is delayed by reason of some act or
default of the Company or any of its officers or the failure of any of its equipment or
apparatus or otherwise, the liability (if any) of the Company to any person suffering loss
or damage thereby shall in no circumstances exceed the sum payable or paid for the
transmission of such message.

### 23.

Force majeure
The company shall not be in default of any of its obligations under this Act by reason
of a failure resulting directly or indirectly from force majeure which for the purposes of
this section includes but is not limited to any law, order, regulation or direction of any
government, or any strike, lock-out or other labour difficulty, or any civil insurrection,
riot, national emergency, war (whether declared or not), fire, flood, act of God or any
other catastrophe, or any cause not reasonably within the control of the Company.

### 24.

Exemption from tax, customs duty, etc.
Notwithstanding anything contained in any enactment passed before or after the
commencement of this Act, a foreign exchange tax shall not be levied on the Company
and the Company shall not be liable to customs duty, tax, or other similar impost levied
or charged on any article imported for the purpose of the works.

## PART VI

Offences and Penalties

### 25.

Obstructing conveyance of message
A person who wilfully prevents or obstructs the conveyance or delivery of any
message shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one
thousand five hundred dollars.

### 26.

Obstructing person in execution of his or her duty, etc.
A person who wilfully obstructs or attempts to obstruct any other person acting in the
execution of this duty under this Act or under any order or direction made or given
thereunder shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of
three hundred dollars.
