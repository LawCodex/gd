## CHAPTER 198

MONEY LENDING ACT
An Act relating to money lending.
[Amended by Act No. 11 of 1984.]

[8th April, 1915.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Money Lending Act.
2\. Prohibition of rates of interest above sixteen per cent per annum
No person shall directly charge or receive for the loan of money, or under any
agreement or security in respect of money lent, a rate of interest or discount exceeding
sixteen per cent per annum.
3\. Power of courts to reopen transactions and relieve debtors
In any suit, action or other proceeding concerning a loan of money or any agreement
or security in respect of money lent, wherein it is alleged that the amount of interest paid
or claimed exceeds the rate of sixteen per cent per annum, including the charges for
discount, commission, expenses, inquiries, fines, bonus, renewals, or any other charges,
but not including taxable costs and charges, the court may reopen the transaction and take
an account between the parties, and may, notwithstanding any statement or settlement of
account, or any contract purporting to close previous dealings and create a new
obligation, reopen any account already taken between the parties, and relieve the person
liable from payment of any sum in excess of the said rate of interest; and if any such
excess has been paid, or allowed in account, by the debtor, the court may order the

creditor to repay it, and may set aside, either wholly or in part, or revise or alter, any
security given in respect of the transaction.
4\. Application of Act to existing contracts
Where money has been lent or an agreement or security has been made or taken in
respect of money lent before the 8th of April, 1915, interest becoming due on or after the
said day in respect of any such loan, agreement, or security shall not be at a rate
exceeding twelve and a half per cent per annum.
5\. Application of Act to all money lending
The provisions of this Act shall apply to any transaction which, whatever its form may
be, is substantially one of money lending.
6\. Rights of bona fide holder or assignee
Nothing in this Act shall affect the rights of any bona fide assignee or holder for value
without notice of a negotiable instrument, but the party discharging any such instrument
may recover from the lender of the money any amount paid on such instrument for
interest or discount in excess of the amount allowed by this Act.
7\. Offence to charge or receive excessive interest
A person who charges or receives for the loan of money, or under any agreement or
security in respect of money lent, a rate of interest exceeding that authorised by this Act
shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of five thousand
dollars and to imprisonment for six months.
8\. Rates of interest on small short-term loans to be regulated
Notwithstanding anything to the contrary in this Act, where the money lent shall not
be secured by mortgage, judgement, bill of sale or other form of security required by law
to be registered and there shall be no solicitors’ fees or other taxable costs and charges
payable by the borrower, the interest or discount charged or received may be, in the case
of a loan not exceeding twenty-five dollars made for a period not exceeding three months,
at a rate not exceeding sixty per cent per annum during such period, and in the case of a
loan not exceeding two hundred and fifty dollars made for a period not exceeding three
months, at a rate not exceeding thirty per cent per annum during such period.
9\. Substitution of rate
In cases where the provisions of the preceding section shall apply, the word “sixty” or
“thirty”, as the case may require, shall be substituted for the word “sixteen” occurring in
this Act.
