## CHAPTER 312

STOCK TRESPASS ACT
An Act for the recovery of damages done by reason of trespasses by horses, mules,
asses, cattle, sheep, goats, and pigs upon land.
[Amended by Act No. 43 of 1973, Act No. 13 of 1974, Act No. 41 of 1990, Act No. 29 of 2000.]

[20th February, 1849.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Stock Trespass Act.
2\. Interpretation
The word “animal” in this Act shall apply to any horse, mare, gelding, bull, cow, ox,
heifer, calf, sheep, goat, and to any of the swine kind; the word “cattle” shall apply to any

bull, cow, ox, heifer or calf; the word “horse” shall extend to any mare or gelding, as well
as to any horse; and the word “pig” shall include any of the swine kind.
3\. Cattle trespassing may be impounded
It shall be lawful for any owner, renter or occupier of any land, his or her employees,
assistants and agents, to seize and take, or cause or procure to be seized and taken, any
horse, mule, ass, cattle, sheep, goat or pig trespassing upon his or her land, and to
impound it as a distress for the damage done to his or her land, and to detain it until either
its owner shall pay for the damage done at the rate or in the manner hereinafter
mentioned, or the animal is sold or disposed of in the manner hereinafter mentioned.
4\. Compensation for trespass
It shall be lawful for the owner, renter or occupier of land, by himself or herself, or by
his or her employees, assistants or agents, to demand and receive from the owner or
claimant of any such animal trespassing upon his or her land, and the owner or claimant
of the same animal shall pay the following rate, for the damage done (subject, however,
to the proviso hereinafter next mentioned), for each horse, ass or cattle, the sum of one
hundred dollars, and the further sum of twenty-five dollars for each day after the first day
the animal is detained impounded; for each sheep, goat and pig the sum of fifty dollars
and the further sum of ten dollars for each day after the first day any of such last
mentioned animals is detained impounded:
Provided that if the owner, renter or occupier of the land upon which any such trespass
is committed deems the rate hereinbefore mentioned in that behalf insufficient to satisfy the
actual damage done to the land, or to anything planted or growing thereon, or in case any
such animal so trespassing, is not impounded, and the owner, renter or occupier of the land,
and the owner or claimant of the animal cannot agree as to the amount to be paid for the
actual damage done by the trespass, it shall be lawful for the magistrate of the district, upon
the application of the distrainor, or the owner, renter or occupier of the land trespassed
upon, to summon the party complained of before him or her, and on hearing such parties, or
in default of attendance of the party complained of and due proof of the service of such
summons on him or her, it shall be lawful for such magistrate to assess the actual damage
done and to make such award as he or she thinks fit.
5\. Persons impounding to give notice to owners, if known
If the owner of such animal so trespassing and impounded is known to the distrainor, the
distrainor shall, as soon as possible after making the distress, give or cause to be given
notice to the owner; otherwise the distrainor shall not be entitled to receive from the
owner of the animal the further sum of money hereinbefore allowed for each day after the
first day the animal shall be impounded.
6\. If owner unknown, distress to be advertised
If the owner of such animal so trespassing and impounded is unknown to the distrainor,
the distrainor shall, as soon as possible after making the distress, advertise the distress in
a local newspaper and in an appropriate radio broadcast, which shall be sufficient legal
notice to the owner or claimant of the animal; and in case no claim to the animal shall be
made within seven days from the time of such advertisement being madepublic; or
(whether or not the owner of such animal be known) if a claim be made to any such
animal so trespassing and impounded, or if the animal so trespassing shall not be
impounded, and such owner or claimant shall refuse or neglect on demand to make
satisfaction to the owner, renter or occupier of the land so trespassed upon, according to
the rate, or according to any such valuation, as hereinbefore in that behalf mentioned, as
the case may be, it shall be lawful for the magistrate of the district, on application made to
him or her, and proof upon oath before him or her of the fact of the trespass, to issue his

or her warrant, directed to any police officer, requiring and commanding him or her to
levy upon and to sell such animal, or other goods and chattels of the owner or claimant of
such animal, at public auction, at such time and place as shall be specified in the said
warrant, and out of the proceeds of the sale to pay and satisfy to the owner, renter or
occupier of the land so trespassed upon the amount, to be specified in the said warrant, of
the damages, costs and charges of such trespass, notice by advertisement and broadcast,
and other incidental expenses, together with the sum of twenty dollars to the use and
benefit of the police officer to whom the warrant was directed, rendering the overplus, if
any, to the owner or claimant of the animal, if known, and if unknown, then to the
Accountant-General for the credit of the Consolidated Fund.
7\. Duty to attend to the needs of impounded animals
The distrainor and impounder of any such animal shall furnish the animal with
sufficient and suitable food and water and in every respect care for and maintain its life
and health, and in default thereof he or she shall be liable in damages at the suit of the
owner of such animal.
8\. If animal die for want or from neglect, distrainor to forfeit double value
If any animal so impounded shall die for want of sufficient and suitable food and
water, or from any other cause of neglect, or want of care and good treatment, by or on
the part of the distrainor, he or she shall forfeit and pay to the owner or claimant of the
animal double the value of the animal.
9\. Persons rescuing, or attempting to rescue, animals, to be guilty of an offence
If any person rescues or attempts to rescue an animal from being impounded or from
going or being driven to be impounded or pulls down, damages or destroys any pound or
place where an animal is impounded, he or she shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on
summary conviction, to a fine of two thousand dollars and to imprisonment for one year.
10\. Impounded cattle suspected of glanders, etc., on proof thereof, to be killed
If the distrainor of any such animal so trespassing upon his or her land, his or her
employee, assistant or agent, shall have reason to believe or to suspect that such animal
has the glanders or any other contagious disease, it shall be lawful for him or her to apply
to the magistrate of the district, who, on such application, is hereby required as soon as
possible to call upon a veterinary surgeon or other person competent to form a just
opinion in the premises, and if either of them shall be of opinion that the animal has the
glanders, or any other contagious disease, and shall make oath thereof before the
magistrate, then it shall be lawful for the magistrate, by warrant, to authorise the distrainor
of the animal, his or her employee, assistant or agent, to kill the animal and to cause its
body to be buried, or towed out to sea, and for his or her so doing, this Act, and the said
warrant, shall be a sufficient warrant and indemnity.
11\. Trespassing animals may be killed
(1) The owner, renter or occupier of any land, or his or her servant, or any other
person acting with the owner’s authority may, after having first made every effort to
secure and impound the same, kill any animal trespassing on such land.
(2) Where an animal is killed pursuant to subsection (1) the person who killed the
animal shall immediately give notice of such killing to the owner of the animal, if known.
(3) Where any animal is killed or disabled while trespassing, the owner thereof may
have and remove the same within four hours.

(4) If the carcass of the animal is not removed within four hours, the person who
killed the animal shall distribute such carcass to any public institution.
(5) A person who kills an animal pursuant to subsection (1) and takes away or
removes the animal so killed or any part thereof for his or her own use commits an
offence and shall be liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand dollars or
imprisonment for three months.
12\. Horse, donkey, cattle, sheep, goat or pig at large in town may be impounded
and owner liable to penalty
Any horse, donkey, cattle, sheep, goat or pig found at large in any public road or place
within any town may be taken up and impounded by any person whomsoever, and on the
owner thereof claiming the same, he or she shall forfeit a sum of one hundred dollars for
each horse, donkey, cattle, sheep, goat or pig so found at large, one-half of such forfeiture
to be paid to the Accountant-General for the credit of the Consolidated Fund and the other
half to the person taking up and impounding the horse, donkey, cattle, sheep, goat or pig,
and if no claim be made for the horse, donkey, cattle, sheep, goat or pig within three days
after it has been impounded, it may be sold by the person impounding it, and the proceeds
may be applied to his or her own use.
13\. Animals found in public places may be impounded
(1) Any animal found to be on the loose or unattended—
(a)

on or alongside any public road;

(b)

in any public park or pleasure ground;

(c)

on any beach or other public property or any place to which the public has
access or which the public uses generally,

may be seized and impounded by any police officer or remunerated public officer and
kept at a pound or in such other place as the Minister may appoint for that purpose.
(2) Whosoever permits an animal to be on the loose or unattended in the circumstances
mentioned in subsection (1)(a), (b) or (c) shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on
summary conviction, to a fine of two thousand dollars and to pay the cost of any damage
done by that animal.
(3) Subject to subsection (5), an animal impounded pursuant to subsection (1) may be
detained until the owner or other person claiming its release forfeits to the pound keeper
an amount not exceeding one thousand dollars assessed by the Minister together with the
reasonable expense of impounding and keeping the animal.
(4) Where an animal is impounded pursuant to subsection (1) the pound keeper or any
person appointed by the Minister for the purpose shall, out of monies provided by the
Ministry, advertise in a local newspaper and in an appropriate radio broadcast the following
details, namely—
(a)

a brief description of the animal;

(b)

the place where, and the date upon which, the animal was seized; and

(c)

the place where the animal is being impounded.

(5) If no claim is made for the return of an animal impounded pursuant to
subsection (1) within three days after advertisement of the details required by subsection
(4) or within such longer period thereafter as may be specified in the advertisement with
respect to that animal, the animal may either be sold at public auction or, by the direction
of the Minister, slaughtered for distribution of the carcass to any public institution.
(6) Where an animal is sold at public auction pursuant to subsection (5), the proceeds
of sale of the animal, if any proceeds remain after the deduction therefrom of any penalty

imposed and all expenses incurred under this section by public funds, shall be transmitted
to the Treasury and there held for payment, if so demanded within thirty days of the sale,
to a lawful claimant and thereafter, in default of such demand by a lawful claimant, for
the credit of the Consolidated Fund.
(7) For the purposes of subsection (1)(a) an animal shall be deemed to be alongside a
public road if the animal is found within a distance of four feet therefrom.
14\. Power of Minister to create pounds
The Minister may authorise the construction of pounds for the reception of animals to
be impounded under this Act, and shall appoint in respect of each pound a keeper having
responsibility for the administration thereof and the care of animals kept therein.
15\. Power of Minister to make regulations
The Minister of Agriculture may make regulations for carrying into effect the
foregoing provisions of this Act, but all such regulations shall be subject to affirmative
resolution of both Houses of Parliament.
16\. Offence to keep certain animals within any town
Notwithstanding the provisions of any other written law, if the owner of any cattle,
horse, mule or pig shall cause or permit the same to be kept within the boundaries of any
town, he or she shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine
of three thousand dollars and to imprisonment for three months.
