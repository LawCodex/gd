## CHAPTER 344

WRECKS ACT
An Act to make provision for inquiry into the causes of wrecks on the coast of
Grenada for the appointment of Receivers of wreck and defining their
duties, and for the apportionment of salvage.
[Amended by S.I. 223 of 1964, S.I. 1661 of 1964.]

[14th August, 1889.]

## PART I

Preliminary
1\.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Wrecks Act.

2\.

Act not to apply to ships of Her Majesty or of foreign country

Nothing in this Act shall be deemed to apply to any ship belonging to or in the
service of Her Majesty, or belonging to any foreign country.

Interpretation
In this Act—

“master” means the person for the time being in command or in charge of a
ship;
“Minister” means the Minister of Finance;
“Receiver” means Receiver of wreck;
“ship” means a vessel navigating the sea and propelled by sails, steam or other
power and not by oars only;
“wreck” includes all jetsam, flotsam, ligan or derelict found in the sea or on the
seashore or in any tidal water.

## PART II

Wrecks, Casualties and Enquiries
Inquiry by Magistrate
4\.

Definition of the expression “shipping casualties”

For the purpose of inquiries and formal investigations hereafter made under this
Act, a shipping casualty shall be deemed to occur—
(a)

when on or near the coasts of Grenada a ship is lost, abandoned or
materially damaged;

(b)

when on or near the coasts of Grenada a ship has been stranded or
damaged, and any witness is found in Grenada;

(c)

when on or near the coasts of Grenada a ship causes loss or material
damage to any other ship;

(d)

when any loss of life ensues by reason of any casualty happening to or on
board a ship on or near the coasts of Grenada;

(e)

when in any place such loss, abandonment, material damage, or casualty
as above-mentioned occurs, and any witness is found in Grenada;

(f)

when in any place a British ship is stranded or damaged, and any witness
is found in Grenada;

(g)

when a British ship is lost or is supposed to have been lost, and any
evidence is obtained in Grenada as to the circumstances under which she
proceeded to sea or was last heard of.

5\. Duties and powers of magistrates and other authorised persons in respect
to shipping casualties
(1) Subject to the provisions contained in this Act in any of the following cases,
that is to say—
(a)

where a shipwreck or casualty occurs to a British ship on or near the
coasts of Grenada or to a British ship in the course of a voyage to a port
within Grenada;

(b)

where a shipwreck or casualty occurs in any part of the world to a British
ship registered in Grenada;

(c)

where some of the crew of a British ship which has been wrecked or to
which a casualty has occurred, and who are competent witnesses to the
facts, are found in Grenada;

(d)

where any incompetency or misconduct has occurred on board a British
ship on or near the coasts of Grenada or on board a British ship in the
course of a voyage to a port within Grenada;

(e)

where the incompetency or misconduct has occurred on board a British
ship registered in Grenada;

(f)

when the master, mate, or engineer of a British ship who is charged with
incompetency or misconduct on board that British ship is found in Grenada,

it shall be lawful for the Magistrate of the district in or near which such loss,
abandonment, stranding, damage, or casualty occurred, or competent witnesses have
arrived, or evidence can be obtained, or for any person appointed for the purpose by
the Minister, to make inquiry respecting the loss, abandonment, stranding, damage,
casualty, supposed loss, incompetency or misconduct, and he or she shall for that
purpose have the following powers, that is to say he or she may—
(i) go on board the ship and inspect it or anything on board,
(ii) enter and inspect any premises, the entry or inspection of which appears
to him or her to be requisite for the purpose of the report which he or she
is directed to make,
(iii) by summons require the attendance of all such persons as he or she
thinks fit to call before him or her and examine, and may require answers
to any inquiries he or she thinks fit to make,
(iv) require and enforce the production of all books, papers or documents
which he or she considers important,
(v) administer oaths.
(2) A person who refuses or neglects to attend as a witness when summoned, or
who refuses or neglects to make any answer, to give any return or to produce any
document in his or her possession or to make or subscribe any oath required of him or
her under the authority of this Act, shall be guilty of an offence and liable, to a fine of
five hundred dollars, and upon the conclusion of the case the Magistrate, or other
person appointed as aforesaid, shall send in his or her report thereof to the Minister.
6\.

Penalty on obstructing magistrate making inquiry

A person who assaults, obstructs or resists a magistrate or other person appointed
as aforesaid in the execution of his or her duty under this Act, whether on board any
ship or elsewhere, shall be guilty of an offence and liable, to a fine of one thousand
dollars and may be seized and detained by the Magistrate or other person appointed
as aforesaid, or by any person whom he or she may call to his or her assistance, until
the offender can be conveniently taken before a magistrate to be dealt with according
to law.
Courts of Inquiry
7\.

Constitution of court

Should it appear to the Minister, on reading the report, that a more formal
investigation is requisite or expedient he or she shall notify his or her opinion to the
Magistrate, who shall thereupon associate with himself or herself as an assessor one
other person having nautical or engineering skill and knowledge, to be nominated by
the Minister, and who shall together with him or her form a court for the purpose of
hearing the case.
8\.

Nomination of Assessors

Where any investigation involves or appears likely to involve any question as to the
suspending or cancelling of the certificate of a master, mate or engineer, the court
shall be held with the assistance of not less than two persons having experience in the
Merchant service to act as Assessors with the Magistrate and to be nominated by the
Minister.

Remuneration of Assessors

The Minister is hereby authorised to pay to the Assessors such sums for their
remuneration as may be just, and the Minister may issue his or her warrant on the
Permanent Secretary (Finance) for payment of the same.

Investigations: how conducted

(1) Every investigation held before the court shall be conducted in such manner
that if a charge is made against a person he or she shall have an opportunity of
making a defence.
(2) All investigations shall be held in the police station or some other suitable
public building.
11\. Cases in which certificates of competency or service may be suspended
or cancelled
(1) The court may suspend or cancel the certificate whether of competency or
service of a master, mate or engineer, if, upon investigation, the court finds—
(a)

that the loss, abandonment or serious damage to any ship or that loss of
life has been caused by his or her wrongful act or default, provided that at
least one of the assessors concurs in the finding of the court;

(b)

that such master, mate or engineer is incompetent or has been guilty of
any gross act of misconduct, drunkenness or tyranny; or

(c)

that in case of collision such master, mate or engineer having been in
charge of one of the vessels has without reasonable cause failed—
(i) to render to the other vessel, her crew and passengers, if any, such
assistance as was practicable and necessary to save them from any
danger caused by the collision,
(ii) to stay by the other vessel until he or she has ascertained that she
had no need of further assistance, or
(iii) to give to the master or person in charge of the other vessel the
name of his or her own vessel and of the port to which she belongs,
and also the names of the ports from which she was coming and to
which she is bound,
but no certificate shall be cancelled or suspended unless a copy of
the report or statement of the case upon which the investigation is
ordered has been served upon the owner of the certificate before
the commencement of the investigation.

(2) Every master, mate or engineer whose certificate is suspended or cancelled in
pursuance of this Act shall, upon demand of the court or of the Minister, deliver up his
or her certificate; and if any master, mate or engineer fails so to deliver his or her
certificate he or she shall be liable to pay a fine of three thousand dollars.
12\. Court to state in open court its decision with respect to cancelling
certificate
(1) The court shall upon the conclusion of the case, or as soon thereafter as
possible, state in open court the decision to which they have come with respect to
cancelling or suspending certificates, and shall in all cases send a full report upon the
case, with the evidence, to the Minister, and shall, if they determine to cancel or
suspend any certificate, forward the certificate, if delivered up, to the Minister with
their report, and a copy of the report and evidence, together with the certificate, if
any, shall forthwith in all cases be forwarded to the Marine Accident Investigation
Department of the Department of Transport in England.
(2) Each Assessor shall either sign the report or report to the Minister his or her
reasons for dissenting from it.

13\. Inquiries not to be held if the matter has already been investigated
elsewhere or by a Naval Court
An inquiry shall not be held under this Act into any matter which has once been the
subject of an investigation or inquiry and has been reported on by any competent
court or tribunal in any part of Her Majesty’s dominions or in respect of which the
certificate of a master, mate or engineer has been suspended or cancelled by a Naval
Court.
14\.

Powers of court

(1) For the compulsory examination and attendance of parties and witnesses, for
the remuneration of witnesses, and for the regulation of all proceedings, the court
shall have the same powers as are possessed by a magistrate in any case where he or
she has summary jurisdiction, or as near thereto as circumstances permit, including
the power to order and enforce payment of costs to be awarded or apportioned and paid
in such manner as may be deemed just.
(2) The court shall have the like powers of visiting and inspection as are
hereinbefore conferred upon the Magistrate. A person who obstructs, resists, or
assaults any of the members of the court in so doing shall be guilty of an offence and
liable, to a fine of one thousand dollars.
15\.

If inquiry has been commenced in U.K. no inquiry to be held in Grenada

Where an investigation or inquiry has been commenced in the United Kingdom with
reference to any matter, an inquiry with reference to the same matter shall not be
held under this Act.
16\.

Re-hearing of inquiry and appeal

The Department of Transport in England may, if the Secretary of State so
determines, conduct the re-hearing of any inquiry made or held under this Act in like
manner as that Department may conduct an investigation in England, but if an
application for re-hearing either is not made or is refused an appeal shall lie, from any
order or finding of the court or tribunal holding the inquiry, to the High Court—
Provided that an appeal shall not lie—
(a)

from any order or finding on an inquiry into a casualty affecting a ship
registered in a British possession; or

(b)

from a decision affecting the certificate of a master, mate or engineer, if
that certificate has not been granted either in the United Kingdom or in a
British possession, under the authority of the laws for the time being in
force relating to Merchant Shipping.

## PART III

Receivers, Offences and Settlement of Disputes

17\.

Receiver of wreck

(1) The Permanent Secretary (Finance) shall be ex officio Chief Receiver of wreck
throughout Grenada, and shall have the general superintendence of all matters
relating to wreck; and he or she may, with the Minister’s approval, appoint any
customs officer or other person to be Receiver of wreck in and for any district, or for
any special occasion to perform the duties of Receiver.
(2) Notice of every appointment of Receiver shall be given in the Gazette, and the
insertion of the notice in the Gazette shall be conclusive proof of the appointment.

(3) Where the Receiver is mentioned in this Act he or she shall be taken to be the
person appointed by the Permanent Secretary (Finance) unless the context otherwise
requires.

Duty of Receiver when ship is stranded or in distress

(1) Whenever any British or foreign ship is wrecked, stranded or in distress at any
place on or near the coast of Grenada or any tidal water within the limits of Grenada
the Receiver shall, upon being made acquainted with the accident, as soon as possible
proceed to the place, and upon his or her arrival there take the command of all
persons present, and assign such duties to each person and issue such directions as he
or she may think fit with a view to the preservation of the ship, of the lives of the
persons belonging thereto, and of the cargo and apparel thereof.
(2) If a person wilfully disobeys any such direction he or she shall be guilty of an
offence and liable, to a fine of one thousand dollars; but it shall not be lawful for the
Receiver to interfere between the master of the ship and his or her crew in matters
relating to the management thereof unless he or she is requested to do so by the
master.
19\.

General powers of Receiver

(1) The Receiver may, with a view to the preservation of the ship, persons, cargo
and apparel, do the following things or any of them—
(a)

summon such number of men as he or she thinks necessary to assist him
or her;

(b)

require the master of any ship or boat near at hand to give such aid with
his or her men, ship or boat as may be in his or her power; and

(c)

demand the use of any vehicle or horses that may be near at hand.

(2) Every person who refuses or neglects, without reasonable cause, to comply
with any summons, requisition or demand so made shall be guilty of an offence and
liable, to a fine of three thousand dollars.
20\.

Custody of articles washed on shore, etc.

(1) All cargo and other articles belonging to the ship which may be washed on
shore or otherwise be lost or taken from the ship shall be delivered to the Receiver.
(2) Every person, whether he or she is the owner or not, who secretes or keeps
possession of any such cargo or article, or refuses or neglects to deliver the same to
the Receiver or to any person authorised by him or her to demand the same, shall be
guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of three thousand dollars; and it shall be lawful
for the Receiver or other person as aforesaid to take the cargo or article by force from
the person so refusing or neglecting to deliver it.
21\.

Power of Receiver to suppress plunder and disorder by force

Whenever any such accident happens to any ship and any person plunders, creates
disorder, or obstructs the preservation of the ship, lives or cargo, it shall be lawful for
the Receiver to cause the person to be apprehended and to use force for the
suppression of any such plundering, disorder or obstruction, with power to command
all citizens to assist him or her in the use of such force; and if any person is killed,
maimed or hurt by reason of his or her resisting the Receiver in the lawful execution of
the duties hereby committed to him, her or any person acting under his or her orders,
such Receiver or other person shall be free and fully indemnified as well against the
Crown as against all persons so killed, maimed or hurt, and all other persons
whomsoever.

Exercise of Receiver’s powers in his or her absence

During the absence of the Receiver from the place where any such accident occurs
to any ship, or in a place where no Receiver has been appointed, then until he or she
is appointed the following officers in succession (that is to say), the Chief of Police, any
commissioned officer in full pay in Her Majesty’s army, navy, or air force, any Justice
of the Peace or any Inspector of Police or Subordinate Officer of the Police Force may
do all things and matters hereby authorised to be done by the Receiver; and any
officer so acting shall be considered as the agent of the Receiver and shall deliver any
goods or articles belonging to the ship into the custody of the Receiver.
23\.

Power of Receiver to examine with respect to ships in distress

(1) A Receiver or, in his or her absence, any Justice of the Peace shall, as soon as
conveniently may be, examine upon oath (which oath they are hereby empowered to
administer) any person belonging to any British or foreign ship which may be or may
have been in distress on the coast of Grenada, or any other person who may be able
to give any account thereof or of the cargo or stores thereof, as to the following
matters, that is to say—
(a)

the name and description of the ship;

(b)

the names of the master and the owners;

(c)

the names of the owners of the cargo;

(d)

the ports or places from and to which the ship was bound;

(e)

the occasion of the distress of the ship;

(f)

the services rendered;

(g)

such other matters or circumstances relating to the ship or cargo as the
Receiver or Justice thinks necessary.

(2) The Receiver or Justice shall take the examination down in writing and shall
make two copies of it of which he or she shall send one to the Minister and the other
to the Permanent Secretary (Finance). For the purposes of the examination every
Receiver or Justice shall have all the powers given to a magistrate and to a court of
inquiry by Part II of this Act.
24\.

Power in case of ship being in distress to pass over adjoining land

(1) Whenever any such accident occurs to any ship, all persons may for the
purpose of rendering assistance to the ship or preserving the lives of the persons on
board it or its cargo or its apparel, unless there is some public road equally convenient,
pass and re-pass, either with or without vehicles or horses, over any adjoining lands
without being subject to interruption by the owner or occupier, so that they do as little
damage as possible; and may also on the like condition deposit on the lands any cargo
or other article recovered from the ship. All damage sustained by any owner or
occupier in consequence of any such passing or re-passing or deposit shall be a charge
on the ship, cargo or article in respect of or by which the damage was occasioned, and
shall in default of payment be recoverable in the same manner as salvage is by this
Act made recoverable; and the amount payable in respect thereof, shall, if disputed,
be determined in the same manner as the amount of salvage is by this Act in a case of
dispute to be determined.
(2) If the owner or occupier of any land over which any person is hereby
authorised to pass or re-pass for any of the purposes hereinbefore mentioned impedes
or hinders any such person from passing or re-passing or impedes or hinders the
deposit of any cargo or other article recovered from any such ship, he or she shall be
guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of three thousand dollars.
Finding of Wreck

Rules to be observed by person finding wreck

(1) The following rules shall be observed by any person finding or taking
possession of wreck within Grenada, that is to say—
(a)

if the person so finding or taking possession is the owner, he or she shall
as soon as possible give notice to the Receiver, stating that he or she has
so found or taken possession, and he or she shall describe in the notice
the marks by which the wreck is distinguished; and

(b)

if any person not being the owner finds or takes possession of any wreck
he or she shall as soon as possible deliver it to the Receiver.

(2) Every person making default in obeying the provisions of this section shall be
guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of three thousand dollars, and where he or she
is not the owner he or she shall forfeit all claim to salvage.
26\.

Notice of wreck to be given by Receiver

Every Receiver shall, within seven days after taking possession of any wreck, cause
to be posted up in some public place near to which the wreck was found or seized a
description of it, and of any marks by which it is distinguished, and shall also, if the
value of the wreck in his or her opinion exceeds one hundred dollars, but not
otherwise, transmit a similar description to the agent of the Committee of Lloyd’s to be
posted up in some conspicuous place for the inspection of all persons desirous of
examining it.
27\.

Receiver’s power to seize concealed wreck

If any Receiver suspects or receives information that a wreck is secreted or in
possession of some person who is not the owner thereof or is otherwise improperly
dealt with, he or she may enter into any house or other place wherever situate, and
also into any ship or boat, and search for and seize and detain the wreck or any part
thereof there found, and if any such seizure is made in consequence of information
given by any person to the Receiver the informer shall be entitled by way of salvage to
such sum as the Permanent Secretary (Finance) may allow.
28\.

Payments to Receiver and others

There shall be paid from the Treasury to all Receivers and to all persons who act in
their stead or otherwise under this Act such expenses properly incurred by them in the
performance of their duties as the Minister may allow; and there shall also be paid to
all Receivers in respect of the matters specified in the Schedule such fees, not
exceeding the amounts therein mentioned, as may, from time to time, be directed by
the Minister which fees shall be paid by the Receiver into the Treasury; and the
Receiver and other persons aforesaid shall have the same lien and other remedies for
the recovery of such expenses and fees as a salvor is entitled to in respect of salvage
due to him or her. It shall be lawful for the Minister to award to the Receiver such
proportion to the said fees when paid as he or she shall think fit.
Offences relating to Wreck
29\.

Wreck found in the possession or on the premises of any person

If any goods, merchandise or articles of any kind belonging to any ship in distress
or wrecked, stranded or cast on shore, are found in possession of any person or on his
or her premises with his or her knowledge that he or she came unlawfully by them,
then they shall by order of the Magistrate be forthwith delivered over to the Receiver
of the police district to be disposed of according to law and such person shall be guilty
of an offence and liable to a fine of one thousand dollars.

Penalty in case of shipwreck for plundering obstructing or secreting

Whoever—

(a)

wrongfully carries away or removes any part of a ship stranded or in danger
of being stranded or otherwise in distress, on or near the shore of any sea
or tidal water, or any part of the cargo or apparel thereof, or any wreck;

(b)

endeavours in any way to impede or hinder the saving of such ship, cargo,
apparel or wreck; or

(c)

secretes any wreck or obliterates or defaces any mark thereon,

shall, in addition to any other penalty or punishment to which he or she may be
subject under this or any other law, be guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of three
thousand dollars and to imprisonment for six months.
31\.

Penalty for unlawfully boarding ship in distress

Whosoever not being a Receiver or person authorised to take the command where
ships are stranded or in distress, or not acting under the orders of such Receiver or
person but without the master’s leave, endeavours to board any such ship, shall be
guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of three thousand dollars; and it shall be lawful
for the master of the ship to repel by force any such person so attempting to board it.
Unclaimed Wreck
32\.

Proceedings with respect to unclaimed wreck

(1) If no owner establishes a claim to wreck found in any part of Grenada before
the expiration of a year from the date at which it came into the possession of the
Receiver, the Receiver shall forthwith sell it and shall after paying all expenses
attending the sale and deducting all expenses incurred by him or her, and paying to
the salvors such amount of salvage as the Minister may in each case or by any general
rule determine, pay the proceeds of the sale to the Permanent Secretary (Finance) to
be disposed of in accordance with the law for the time being in force regulating the
disposal of droits of the Admiralty:
Provided that in cases where any wreck in the custody of a Receiver is under the
value of twenty-five dollars or is of so perishable a nature or so damaged that it
cannot in his or her opinion be advantageously kept, the Receiver may sell it before
the expiration of the period hereinbefore mentioned, and the proceeds of the sale after
the payment of the expenses thereof shall be paid to the Permanent Secretary
(Finance) to be held by him or her for the same purposes and subject to the same
claims for and to which the article sold would have been held and liable if it had
remained unsold.
(2) Before paying the proceeds of unclaimed wreck to the Permanent Secretary
(Finance) in accordance with this section and paying any surplus to any owner or other
person in accordance with section 47, the Receiver shall deduct the proper duty, if any,
payable in respect of the property sold and shall pay the same into the Treasury.
Dispute as to Wreck
33\.

Disputed title to wreck, how to be decided

(1) If any dispute arises between the Permanent Secretary (Finance) or any
Receiver and any person as to the validity of such person’s claim to wreck, or if divers
persons claim to be entitled to wreck found at the same place, the matter in dispute
may be decided by arbitrators in the same manner in which disputes as to salvage
coming within the jurisdiction of arbitrators are hereinafter directed to be determined,
with the substitution of a Justice of the Peace as one of the arbitrators to be
nominated by the Minister in the place of the Permanent Secretary (Finance).
(2) If any party to such dispute is dissatisfied with the decision of the arbitrators
he or she may within three months from the date of the decision, but not afterwards,
take such proceedings as he or she may be advised in any court having jurisdiction in
the matter for establishing his or her title.

34\. Discharge of Permanent Secretary (Finance) or Receiver from liability on
delivery of wreck
Upon the delivery of wreck or the proceeds of wreck by the Permanent Secretary
(Finance) or by a Receiver to any person in pursuance of the provisions of this Act the
Permanent Secretary (Finance) or Receiver shall be discharged from all liability in
respect thereof; but such delivery shall not be deemed to prejudice or affect any
question concerning the right or title to the wreck which may be raised by third
parties.
35\.

Who to be deemed agent of owner in case of wreck of foreign ship

Whenever any foreign ship which has been wrecked, or any article of or belonging
to such ship or forming part of the cargo thereof, is found on or near the coast of or is
brought into any port in Grenada, the consular officer of the country to which the ship
or the owners of the cargo may have belonged shall, in the absence of the owner and
of the master or other agent of the owner, be deemed to be the agent of the owner so
far as relates to the custody or disposal of the article.

## PART IV

Salvage, Import Duties, Wrecks, etc.
36\.

Provisions relating to salvage

Whenever any ship is stranded or otherwise in distress on the shore of any sea or
tidal water, or whenever any wreck is found within the limits of Grenada, and services
are rendered by any person—
(a)

in assisting the ship;

(b)

in saving the life of any person belonging to the ship; or

(c)

in saving the cargo or apparel of the ship, or any portion thereof,

or whenever any wreck is saved by any person other than a Receiver, there shall be
payable by the owner of the ship, cargo, apparel or wreck to the person by whom such
services are rendered or by whom the wreck is saved a reasonable amount of salvage,
together with all expenses properly incurred by him or her in the performance of such
services or the saving of the wreck, the amount of such salvage and expenses (which
expenses are hereinafter included under the term “salvage”) to be determined in case
of dispute in the manner hereinafter mentioned.
37\.

Dispute as to salvage

(1) Whenever any dispute arises between the owner of any such ship, cargo,
apparel or wreck and the salvor as to the amount of salvage, and the parties to the
dispute cannot agree as to the settlement thereof by arbitration or otherwise—
(a)

if the sum claimed does not exceed one thousand dollars the dispute shall
be referred to the arbitration of the Permanent Secretary (Finance) or any
Receiver and any magistrate, hereinafter called the arbitrators; and

(b)

if the sum claimed exceeds one thousand dollars the dispute may, with
the consent of the parties, be referred to the arbitration of the arbitrators,
but if they do not consent shall be decided by the High Court.

(2) Every dispute with regard to salvage may be heard and adjudicated upon the
application either of the salvor or of the owner of the property salved or of their
agents.

Appointment of valuer

(1) Whenever any salvage question arises, the Receiver may, upon application of
either of the parties, appoint a valuer to value the property in respect of which the
salvage claim is made, and shall when the valuation has been returned to him or her
give a copy of it to both parties, and any writing purporting to be a copy of the
valuation and to be signed by the valuer and attested by the Receiver shall in any
subsequent proceeding be prima facie evidence of the valuation without proof of the
signature or official character of the valuer or Receiver.
(2) There shall be paid to the valuer in respect of the valuation by the party
applying for it such fee as the Minister may direct.
39\.

Manner in which arbitrators may decide dispute

(1) Whenever in pursuance of this Act any dispute as to salvage is referred to the
arbitrators they may, if they think fit, call to their assistance any person conversant
with maritime affairs as Assessor, or they may, if a difference of opinion arises
between them, appoint some person conversant with maritime affairs as an Umpire to
decide the point in dispute.
(2) The arbitrators shall make an award as to the amount of salvage payable
within seven days after the dispute has been referred to them, and the Umpire shall
give his or her decision within three days after his or her appointment; with power
nevertheless for the arbitrators or Umpire by writing under their hands to extend the
periods aforesaid.
40\.

Costs of arbitration

There shall be paid to every Assessor and Umpire in respect of his or her services
such sum, not exceeding fifty dollars, as the Minister may direct; and all costs of the
arbitration, including any such payment as aforesaid, shall be paid by the parties to
the dispute in such manner and in such shares and proportions as the arbitrators or
Umpire shall direct by their award.
41\.

Power to arbitrators to call for documents and administer oaths

The arbitrators or the Umpire may call for the production of any documents in the
possession or power of either party which they may think necessary for determining
the question in dispute, and may examine the parties or their witnesses on oath, and
may administer the oaths necessary for that purpose.
42\.

Appeal to High Court

If any person is aggrieved by the award made by the arbitrators or Umpire he or
she may appeal to the Court having Admiralty Jurisdiction in Grenada; but no such
appeal shall be allowed unless the sum in dispute exceeds two hundred and fifty
dollars, nor unless within ten days after the date of the award the appellant gives
notice to the arbitrators or Umpire of his or her intention to appeal, nor unless the
appellant, within thirty days from the date of the award, takes such proceedings as
according to the practice of the Court are necessary for the institution of such an
appeal.
43\.

Arbitrators to send proceedings and certificate to Court

Whenever an appeal is made the arbitrators or Umpire shall transmit to the proper
officer of the Court a copy certified under their hands to be a true copy of the
proceedings had before them and of the award made by them, accompanied by a
certificate in writing of the gross value of the article respecting which salvage is
claimed, and the copy and certificate shall be admitted in the Court as evidence in the
cause.
44\. Payment of salvage, to whom to be made in case of dispute as to
apportionment

Whenever the aggregate amount of salvage payable in respect of salvage services
has been finally ascertained either by agreement or by the award of the arbitrators or
Umpire, but a dispute arises as to the apportionment thereof amongst several
claimants, then if the amount does not exceed one thousand dollars it shall be lawful
for the party liable to pay the amount to apply to the Permanent Secretary (Finance)
for liberty to pay it to him or her, and he or she shall, if he or she thinks fit, receive it
accordingly and grant a certificate under his or her hand stating the fact of payment
and the services in respect of which it is made, and such certificate shall be a full
discharge and indemnity to the persons to whom it is given, and their ship, boats,
cargo, apparel and effects, against the claims of all persons whomsoever in respect of
the services therein mentioned; but if the amount exceeds one thousand dollars it
shall be apportioned in the manner hereinafter mentioned.
45\.

Apportionment of salvage

Upon the receipt of the amount the Permanent Secretary (Finance) shall with all
convenient speed proceed to distribute it among the persons entitled to it upon such
evidence and in such shares and proportions as the Minister thinks fit, with power to
retain any monies that may appear to be payable to any absent parties; but any
distribution made in pursuance of this section shall be final and conclusive against the
rights of all persons claiming to be entitled to any portion of the monies distributed.
46\.

Manner of enforcing payment of salvage

(1) Whenever any salvage is due to any person under this Act, the Receiver shall
act as follows—
(a)

if it is due in respect of services rendered in assisting any ship or in saving
the life of any person belonging to a ship or the cargo or apparel thereof,
he or she shall detain the ship and the cargo or apparel thereof until
payment is made or process has been issued by some competent court for
the detention of the ship, cargo or apparel; and

(b)

if it is due in respect of the saving of any wreck, and the wreck is not sold
as unclaimed in pursuance of the provisions of this Act, he or she shall
detain the wreck until the payment is made or process has been issued in
manner aforesaid.

(2) But it shall be lawful for the Receiver, if at any time previously to the issue of
such process security is given to his or her satisfaction for the amount of salvage due,
to release from his or her custody any ship, cargo, apparel or wreck so detained by
him or her, and in any case where the claim for salvage exceeds one thousand dollars
it shall be lawful for the Court on summary application made to it to determine any
question that may arise concerning the amount of the security to be given or the
sufficiency of the sureties.
(3) Notwithstanding anything contained in this section it shall not be lawful for a
Receiver to release any cargo or wreck unless and until arrangements have been made
to the satisfaction of the Permanent Secretary (Finance) for the payment of the proper
duty.
47\.

Power of Receiver to sell property salved in case of non-payment

Whenever any ship, cargo, apparel or wreck is detained by a Receiver for nonpayment of any sums so due as aforesaid, and the parties liable to pay the sums are
aware of the detention, then—
(a) in cases where the amount is not disputed and payment is not made
within twenty days after it has been claimed;
(b) in cases where the amount is disputed but no appeal lies from the first
tribunal to which the dispute is referred, and payment is not made within
twenty days after the decision of the first tribunal; and
(c) in cases where the amount is disputed and an appeal lies from the
decision of the first tribunal to some other tribunal and payment is not

made within thirty days after the decision, and such proceedings as
according to the practice of such other tribunal are necessary for the
prosecution of an appeal are not instituted within such thirty days,
the Receiver may forthwith sell the ship, cargo, apparel or wreck or a sufficient part
thereof, and out of the proceeds of the sale, after payment of all expenses thereof,
defray all sums due in respect of all expenses, fees and salvage, paying the surplus, if
any, to the owners of the property sold or such other parties as may be entitled to
receive it.
48\.

Title to wreck subject to payment of expenses and salvage

Subject to the payment of such expenses, fees and salvage as aforesaid, the owner
of any wreck who establishes his or her claim thereto to the satisfaction of the
Permanent Secretary (Finance) within one year from the date at which the wreck has
come into the possession of the Receiver shall be entitled to have it delivered up to
him or her; but no person shall be deemed to have established a claim for delivery of
any wreck under this section unless he or she—

49\.

(a)

shall supply to the Permanent Secretary (Finance) such information as the
latter may deem necessary in order to ascertain the amount of duty
payable in respect of the wreck delivery of which is claimed; and

(b)

shall pay to the Permanent Secretary (Finance) the proper duty on such
wreck.

Powers of Court to apportion salvage

When the amount of salvage payable for salvage services rendered to Grenada has
been finally ascertained and exceeds one thousand dollars, then, if any dispute or
delay arises as to the apportionment thereof, a Judge of the Court may cause it to be
apportioned amongst the persons entitled thereto in such manner as may be just, and
may for that purpose, if he or she thinks fit, appoint any person to carry the
apportionment into effect, and may compel any person in whose hands or under whose
control the amount may be to distribute it or to bring it into Court to be there dealt
with as the Court may direct, and may for the purposes aforesaid issue such process
as it thinks fit.
Import Duty on Wrecks, Salvage, etc.
50\.

Wrecked goods liable to import duty

All wreck being foreign goods brought or coming into Grenada shall be subject to
the same duties as if the same were imported, and if any question arises as to the
origin of the goods they shall be deemed to be the produce of such country as the
Permanent Secretary (Finance) may, on investigation, determine.
51\. Wrecked goods may be sent to port of original destination without
paying duty
The Permanent Secretary (Finance) shall permit all goods, wares, and merchandise
saved from any ship stranded or wrecked on her homeward voyage to be forwarded to
the port of her original destination, and all goods, wares, and merchandise saved from
any ship stranded or wrecked on her outward voyage to be returned to the port at
which the same were shipped; but the Permanent Secretary (Finance) shall take
security for the due protection of the revenue in respect of those goods.
52\. Owners of wrecked goods to give information to the Treasury of the
value, etc., of the goods
(1) If any wreck liable to duty as aforesaid be taken possession of by the owner in
accordance with section 25(1) such owner shall as soon as possible file a statement at
the Treasury showing—

(a)

the date when he or she took possession;

(b)

the marks, numbers, denomination and description of the goods taken
into possession;

(c)

their quantity and description in accordance with the official import list;

(d)

the value (without any deduction for deterioration) of such goods as are
liable to specific duty and ad valorem duty respectively;

(e)

an estimate of the present value of such goods, allowing for deterioration;
and

(f)

the place at which such owner has such goods in possession. The owner
shall at the time of filing make a statutory declaration that the statement
is correct and that the sums and prices in figures therein contained are
the real and bona fide value at the place from whence the goods have
been imported.

(2) Every owner taking possession as aforesaid shall refrain from using, selling or
disposing of the goods until those goods have been inspected by or on behalf of the
Permanent Secretary (Finance) and until he or she has paid the proper duty thereon,
and an owner who does not comply with the provisions contained in this section shall
be guilty of an offence and, in addition to his or her liability to pay the duty, shall be
liable to a fine of three thousand dollars.
53\.

Calculation of the amount of duty to be paid in respect of wrecked goods

The amount of duty payable as aforesaid shall be calculated as follows—
(a)

if goods subject to ad valorem duty are sold by a Receiver the duty shall
be calculated according to the purchase price and shall be deducted
therefrom by the Receiver;

(b)

if goods subject to a specific duty are sold by a Receiver the latter shall
deduct the duty from the purchase price:
Provided that if any such goods by reason of their damaged condition
realise a sum which in the opinion of the Receiver is less than the amount
which would otherwise have been paid, it shall be lawful for the Receiver
in his or her discretion to reduce the specific duty to an extent which will
make the duty deducted be in the same proportion to the whole specific
duty as the price realised bears to the sum which the Receiver estimates
would have been realised if the goods had been undamaged;

(c)

with respect to goods of the kind referred to in the preceding section and
in the proviso to section 48, full ad valorem duty or specific duty shall be
paid unless the goods are in the opinion of the Permanent Secretary
(Finance) materially damaged.

In the latter case the Permanent Secretary (Finance) may fix the amount to be paid
as duty at a sum bearing the same proportion to the whole specific duty or ad valorem
duty that the value of the injured goods bears to what would be the gross value of the
goods if undamaged.
Miscellaneous Provisions
54\.

Recovery of penalties

All fines and penalties under this Act shall be recovered summarily before a
magistrate.

Regulations

It shall be lawful for the Minister to make regulations for duly carrying this Act into
effect.
