## CHAPTER 244

POLICE ACT
An Act to provide for the organisation, administration, discipline, powers and duties
of the Police Force, and for matters incidental thereto.

[Act No. 38 of 1966 amended by Act No. 36 of 1968, Act No. 16 of 1973, Act No. 6 of 1974, Act
No. 18 of 1974, Act No. 23 of 1985, SRO 2 of 1990, Act No. 38 of 1992, Act No. 28 of 2000, Act
No. 8 of 2006.]

[21st January, 1967.]

## PART I

Preliminary

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Police Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act—
“arms” includes firearms;
“Chief of Police” means the Chief of Police of the Force appointed under section 7;
“constable” means a police officer below the rank of Police Corporal;
“Force” means the police force established under the provisions of section 3 and
includes any part of the Force;
“Gazetted Officer” means a police officer of or above the rank of Assistant
Superintendent of Police;
“Inspector” means a member of the Force holding the rank of Inspector of Police;
“Medical Board” means a Board of Medical Officers appointed by the GovernorGeneral;
“offences against police force discipline” means the offences against police force
discipline prescribed under section 33;
“police officer” means any member of the Force;
“police station” means a post or place appointed to be a police station, and includes
any local area policed from such station;
“property” includes any movable property, money or valuable security;
“subordinate officer” means any Police Sergeant or Police Corporal.

## PART II

Force Constitution and Administration

### 3.

Establishment of Police Force
(1) There is hereby established a police force to be known as the Royal Grenada
Police Force, which shall consist of such number of police officers as may from time to
time be ordered by the Governor-General and enrolled in the Force.
(2) All members of the Royal Grenada Police Force employed at the date of coming
into operation of this Act shall be deemed to have been appointed under this Act and to
have taken the oath of office prescribed for a police officer appointed under this Act.

### 4.

Seniority of ranks

The Force shall consist of the undermentioned ranks in the following order of
seniority—
(a)

Gazetted Officers
Commissioner of Police
Deputy Commissioner of Police
Assistant Commissioner of Police
Superintendent of Police
Assistant Superintendent of Police

(b)

Inspector of Police

(c)

Subordinate Officers
Cadet Officer
Police Sergeant
Police Corporal

(d)

Police Constable:

Provided that the Governor-General may, by Notice published in the Gazette, from
time to time amend those ranks.

### 5.

Functions of the Force
The Force shall be employed in Grenada for the maintenance of law and order, the
preservation of the peace, the protection of life and property, the prevention and detection
of crime, the enforcement of all laws and regulations with which it is charged, and the
apprehension of offenders; and for the performance of such duties police officers may
carry arms in accordance with regulations made under this Act.

### 6.

Police serving with armed forces
The Governor-General may, in case of war or other emergency, employ the Force to
serve with Her Majesty’s Armed Forces or otherwise in defence of Grenada:
Provided that any part of the Force so employed shall continue to be under the
command of the Chief of Police or such other police officer as the Chief of Police may
appoint for that purpose.

### 7.

Appointment, etc., of police officers
(1) There shall be a Chief of Police who shall have command and superintendence of
the Force and who shall be appointed and be subject to removal from office in accordance
with section 89(1) of the Constitution.
(2) There shall be a Deputy Commissioner of Police who shall act as principal
assistant to the Chief of Police in the performance of his or her duties in respect of the
Force and shall have power during the absence or incapacity of the Chief of Police, or
when so authorised by the Governor-General, to do or to permit any act or thing which
may by law be done or permitted to be done by the Chief of Police.
(3) Subject to the provisions of section 91 of the Constitution, the power to appoint
persons to hold or act in offices in the Force below the rank of Chief of Police but above
the rank of police sergeant (including the power to confirm appointments) and the power
to remove such persons from office shall vest in the Public Service Commission.
(4) Subject to the provisions of section 91 of the Constitution, the power to appoint
persons to hold or act in offices in the Force of or below the rank of police sergeant

(including the power to confirm appointments) and the power to remove such persons
from office shall vest in the Chief of Police.
(5) The Chief of Police may, by directions given in such manner as he or she thinks
fit, and subject to such conditions as he or she thinks fit, delegate any of his or her powers
under subsection (4) to any other member of the Force.
(6) If provision is made by or under any written law—
(a)

altering the ranks into which the Force is divided; or

(b)

establishing a police force other than the Force or altering the ranks into
which any such other police force is divided,

the Public Service Commission may, by Order, specify some rank (other than the rank of
police sergeant) in the Force or, as the case may be, in that other police force as being
equivalent to the rank of Sergeant as it existed in the Force under this Act immediately
before the 3rd of March, 1967, and the references in subsections (3) and (4) to the rank of
police sergeant shall then be construed as if they were, in relation to the Force, or as the
case may be, in relation to that other police force, references to the rank for the time being
so specified.

### 8.

Resignation of Gazetted Officer or Inspector
The resignation of a Gazetted Officer or Inspector shall not become effective until
three months have expired from the date upon which written notice of the intended
resignation of such officer has been delivered to the authority by whom he or she was
appointed.

### 9.

Discharge of subordinate officer or constable
A subordinate officer or constable may be discharged by the Chief of Police—
(a)

if the Chief of Police considers that he or she is unlikely to become or has
ceased to be an efficient police officer;

(b)

if he or she is certified by a Medical Board to be incapacitated by reason of
some infirmity of mind or body for further service in the Force and that such
infirmity is likely to be permanent; or

(c)

on reduction of the establishment of the force:

Provided that a subordinate officer or constable discharged under paragraph (a)
or (c) of this section shall be entitled to three months notice:
Provided also that where a subordinate officer or constable discharged under the
provisions of this section was, at the time of his or her discharge, eligible for a pension,
gratuity or other allowance under the provisions of the Police Pensions Act, Chapter 245,
it shall be lawful for the Governor-General to grant to that officer such pension, gratuity
or other allowance.

### 10.

Pay and allowances
Every police officer shall receive such pay and allowances as may be assigned to him
or her from time to time by the Governor-General with the consent of the House of
Representatives.

### 11.

Good conduct badges and pay
Any police officer below the rank of Inspector shall for continuous good service be
entitled to good conduct badges and pay therefor at the following rates—

(a)

service for two years during which he or she has committed no offence
involving punishment by fine exceeding two dollars and fifty cents or
fourteen days confinement to barracks, or imprisonment for any period, or
has not received more than four reprimands or more than two severe
reprimands, one badge and ten cents per day;

(b)

service for a further two years, under the same conditions, two badges and
fifteen cents per day;

(c)

service for a further four years, under the same conditions, three badges and
twenty cents per day;

(d)

service for a further four years, under the same conditions, four badges and
twenty-five cents per day:

Provided that a member of the Force who has earned good conduct pay, on
subsequently becoming entitled to a higher rate of such pay, shall not be entitled
thereafter to both rates, but only to the higher rate.

### 12.

Oath of Allegiance, etc.
(1) Every police officer shall, on being appointed, take and sign the following Oath of
Allegiance in such manner as may be most binding on his or her conscience—
I ............................................................................ do hereby swear by Almighty God (or do hereby
solemnly and sincerely declare and affirm) that I will be faithful and bear true allegiance to Her
Majesty Queen Elizabeth the Second, Her Heirs and Successors, and that I will faithfully serve Her
Majesty the Queen, Her Heirs and Successors during my service in the Royal Grenada Police
Force, that I will subject myself to all Acts, Orders and regulations for the time being in operation
that relate to the Force and will discharge all the duties of a police officer according to law,
without fear or favour, affection or ill-will.
Sworn at ............................................................................... this .......................................................
(day)
of ................................................................................................ , .......................................................
(month)
(year)
Before me ............................................................................................................................................

Such Oath of Allegiance or declaration shall be taken by the Chief of Police before the
Governor-General and by every other police officer before the Chief of Police.
(2) A certificate of appointment in such form as may be prescribed by regulations
made by the Governor-General and signed by such officer as is authorised by the Chief of
Police, shall on appointment be issued to every police officer and shall be evidence of his
or her appointment under this Act.

### 13.

Police officer not to be engaged in any other employment
No police officer shall, without the consent of the Chief of Police, engage in any other
employment or office.

### 14.

Police officers to keep prescribed books and to render returns
Every police officer in charge of a police station shall keep a station diary in such
form as the Chief of Police may, from time to time, direct, and shall record therein all
complaints and charges preferred, the names of all persons arrested and the offences
charged against such persons, and shall also keep such further books and records, and
shall render such returns to the Chief of Police, as the Chief of Police may from time to
time direct.

### 15.

Gazetted Officer to be Justice of the Peace

Every Gazetted Officer shall be ex officio a Justice of the Peace and as such shall at all
times act ministerially for purposes of the preservation of the peace, the prevention of
crime, and the detection of crime and offenders, but he or she shall not in any way act
judicially except as expressly provided by this Act.

## PART III

Conditions of Service of Inspectors, Subordinate Officers and Constables

### 16.

Qualifications for appointment
(1) No person shall be appointed to the Force as an Inspector, subordinate officer or
constable unless—
(a)

he or she has attained the age of nineteen years but has not reached the age
of thirty-five years; except that in any particular case the Chief of Police
may, with the approval of the Governor-General, appoint a person who has
already attained the age of thirty-five years;

(b)

he or she is of the required height and chest measurements according to the
standard from time to time fixed by the Chief of Police;

(c)

he or she passes a medical examination as to his or her bodily fitness to be
held by a medical officer appointed for the purpose by the GovernorGeneral;

(d)

he or she produces to the Chief of Police satisfactory proof of his or her good
character; and

(e)

he or she satisfies the Chief of Police that he or she has attained a reasonable
standard of education.

(2) Any candidate who with a view to appointment in the Force knowingly makes a
false statement or produces a birth certificate or testimonial which is false in any material
particular shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one
thousand dollars and to imprisonment for six months.

### 17.

Terms of appointment
(1) Initial appointment to the Force shall be for a period of two years during which
period a subordinate officer or constable shall be deemed to be on probation. At the end
of the probationary period every Inspector, subordinate officer or constable of good
character and considered qualified for permanent appointment to the Force shall be so
appointed provided that if the Inspector, subordinate officer or constable does not intend
to be permanently appointed he or she shall give six months’ notice to the Chief of Police
of his or her intention not to be permanently appointed.
(2) If at any time during the probationary period a constable shall be found to be
wanting in character or intelligence or otherwise unfitted to be a police officer his or her
services may be terminated by the Chief of Police.
(3) An Inspector, subordinate officer or constable may withdraw himself or herself
from the Force at any time by giving three months notice in writing of his or her intention
to do so:
Provided that the Chief of Police may dispense with this condition in any particular
case if he or she sees fit.

### 18.

Prolongation of service in time of war or emergency

A police officer whose period of service expires during a state of war, or during a time
at which the Governor-General has, by notice in the Gazette, declared a state of
emergency to exist, may be retained in the Force and his or her services prolonged for
such further period, but not exceeding six months from the cessation of such state of war
or emergency, as the Chief of Police may direct.

### 19.

Illegally withdrawing from Force
(1) Every Inspector, subordinate officer or constable who absents himself or herself
from roll call and from duty for the space of forty-eight hours without lawful excuse, and
every police officer appointed by the Chief of Police who—
(a)

while serving on probation in the Force withdraws himself or herself from
the Force without the permission of the Chief of Police;

(b)

withdraws himself or herself from the Force without the permission of the
Chief of Police during the term for which he or she has been appointed to
serve in the Force; or

(c)

withdraws himself or herself at any time from the Force without the
permission of the Chief of Police or without giving a valid notice of his or
her intention to withdraw himself or herself from the Force,

shall be deemed to have illegally withdrawn himself or herself from the Force and he or
she shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of seven
hundred and fifty dollars and to imprisonment for six months.
(2) It shall be sufficient, in a charge or complaint for an offence under this section, to
state that the person proceeded against did illegally withdraw himself or herself from the
Force.

### 20.

Warrant to arrest person illegally withdrawing from Force
(1) A magistrate, on complaint being made to him or her on oath by a Gazetted
Officer that an Inspector, subordinate officer or constable has illegally withdrawn himself
or herself from the Force in breach of the last preceding section and that there is
reasonable cause to suspect that such police officer is concealed in his or her own
premises or on the premises of any other person, or is on board a vessel within the
territorial sea of Grenada shall grant to such Gazetted Officer a warrant to search with the
proper assistance the premises or vessel in which the police officer is believed to be
concealed, and if found to arrest him or her in order that he or she may be dealt with
according to law.
(2) At the hearing by a magistrate of such a complaint the onus of proving that any
withdrawal was with the permission of the Chief of Police, or that a valid notice was
given, shall be on the person proceeded against.

### 21.

Aiding in illegal withdrawal from the Force
Every person who—
(a)

knowingly aids or assists a police officer illegally to withdraw himself or
herself from the Force;

(b)

knowingly conceals a police officer who has illegally withdrawn himself or
herself from the Force; or

(c)

knowing that a police officer has illegally withdrawn himself or herself from
the Force aids him or her to quit Grenada,

shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of two hundred
and fifty dollars and to imprisonment for one month.

## PART IV

Powers and Duties

### 22.

Powers of the police
(1) Every police officer shall have throughout the island of Grenada and in the several
bays, creeks, and inlets thereof, and also on board any vessel in which any indictable
offence, or any offence punishable on summary conviction is or may be suspected to be
committed, all such authorities, privileges, protection and advantages, and be liable to all
such duties and responsibilities as any police officer duly appointed now has or is subject
or liable to or may hereafter have or be subject or liable to either by the common law of
England or by virtue of any law which now is or which may hereafter be in force in
Grenada.
(2) For the purpose of preserving order and decorum in public places and places of
public resort, at public meetings and in assemblies for public amusements, each and every
police officer when on duty shall have free admission to all such meetings and assemblies
while open to any member of the public.
(3) It shall be lawful for any police officer to detain for questioning, for a period not
exceeding forty-eight hours, any person whom he or she believes upon reasonable
suspicion to have committed or to be about to commit a criminal offence.

### 23.

Duties of the police
(1) It shall be the duty of the Force to take lawful measures for—
(a)

preserving the public peace;

(b)

preventing and detecting crimes and other infractions of the law;

(c)

apprehending and causing to be apprehended persons who shall have
committed, or shall be charged with or suspected of having committed or
having abetted the commission of, or being about to commit, any crime or
offence;

(d)

control of processions and assemblies in public places;

(e)

controlling the traffic upon public roads and removing obstructions
therefrom;

(f)

preserving order and decorum in public places and places of public resort, at
public meetings and in assemblies for public amusements;

(g)

assisting in carrying out the laws relating to revenue, excise, price control,
quarantine, emigration and immigration;

(h)

assisting in preserving order in the different ports and harbours and in
enforcing the port regulations;

(i)

executing summonses, subpoenas, warrants, commitments and other
criminal or quasi-criminal process issued by the several Magistrates, Justices
of the Peace and Coroners on complaints or proceedings before them;

(j)

exhibiting information and conducting prosecutions for crimes and offences;

(k)

protecting unclaimed and lost property and finding the owners thereof;

(l)

taking charge of and impounding any animal found straying in any public
place;

(m)

assisting in the protection of life and property in cases of fire, hurricane,
earthquake, flood and other disasters;

(n)

protecting public property from loss or injury;

(o)

attending the Supreme Court and the magistrates’ courts and keeping order
in and within the precincts and in the vicinity of the said courts;

(p)

escorting prisoners to and from prisons and other places of detention;

(q)

doing and performing generally the duties appertaining to the office of a
police officer.

(2) Every police officer shall be liable for service in defence of Grenada under the
authority of section 6, and during the continuance of such service the Governor-General
may make regulations in that behalf, or may suspend for the like period the provisions of
any section of this Act.

### 24.

Road barriers
(1) Notwithstanding the provisions of any other written law, a Gazetted Officer or
Inspector stationed in any area may, if he or she considers it necessary to do so for the
maintenance and preservation of law and order or for the prevention or detection of
crime, erect or place barriers in or across any road or in any public place in such manner
as he or she thinks fit.
(2) A police officer may take all reasonable steps to prevent a vehicle being driven
past a barrier and the driver of any vehicle who fails to comply with any reasonable
signals of a police officer, requiring such person to stop such vehicle before reaching such
barrier, shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one
thousand five hundred dollars and to imprisonment for six months.
(3) No police officer shall be liable for any loss or damage resulting to a vehicle or for
any injury to the driver or other occupant of such vehicle as a result of the driver of such
vehicle failing to obey a police officer acting under the provisions of subsection (2).

### 25.

Power to take measurements, photographs, fingerprints, palm and foot
impressions
(1) The police officer in charge of a police station may, for purposes of use and
record, cause measurements, photographs, fingerprints, palm impressions and, where
necessary, foot impressions to be taken of any person in lawful custody accused or
convicted of an offence punishable by imprisonment.
(2) Any person who refuses to permit the lawful taking of a measurement,
photograph, fingerprint, palm impression or foot impression under the provisions of
subsection (1) shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of
one thousand five hundred dollars and to imprisonment for six months and, after
conviction, reasonable force may be used to enable such measurements, photographs,
fingerprints, palm impressions and foot impressions to be taken.
(3) On the release, discharge or acquittal of a person who has not previously been
convicted of an offence punishable by imprisonment the negative and all copies of any
measurements, photographs, fingerprints, palm impressions or foot impressions of that
person taken under the provisions of this section shall be destroyed:
Provided that “release” in this subsection does not mean or include release on bail
pending trial.

### 26.

Arrest without warrant
A police officer and all persons whom he or she shall call to his or her assistance may
take persons into custody without a warrant in the following cases—
(a)

any person who within view of the police officer commits an offence
punishable on summary conviction;

(b)

any person whom any other person states that he or she wishes to charge
with having committed—
(i) an indictable offence not punishable on summary conviction, or
(ii) the offence of stealing or obtaining goods under false pretences or
receiving stolen goods;

(c)

any person whom any other person states that he or she suspects of having
committed an indictable offence or the offence of stealing or obtaining goods
by false pretences or receiving stolen goods, if the suspicion appears to the
police officer to be well founded and the informant is willing to accompany
the police officer and at the police station is willing to enter into a
recognisance conditioned to prosecute the charge;

(d)

any person whom any other person states that he or she wishes to charge
with having committed an aggravated assault, if the informant is willing to
accompany the police officer and at the police station is willing to enter into
a recognisance conditioned to prosecute the charge;

(e)

any person whom any other person states that he or she wishes to charge
with having committed an aggravated assault, if the police officer has good
reason to believe that such an assault has been committed, although not
within his or her view, and that by reason of its recent commission a warrant
could not have been obtained for the apprehension of the person charged;

(f)

any idle or disorderly person whom he or she finds in any way disturbing the
peace, whether in a public or a private place, or causing public annoyance, or
whom he or she has reasonable cause to suspect of having committed or
being about to commit any crime;

(g)

any person whom he or she finds between sunset and six in the morning
lying or loitering in any street, yard, or other place and not giving a
satisfactory account of himself or herself.

### 27.

Proceedings against persons whose names are unknown, etc.
If the name or residence of a person taken into custody for the commission of an
offence within view of a constable is unknown and cannot be ascertained, the police
officer shall detain him or her and proceed against him or her according to law. But if his
or her name and residence are known to or can be ascertained by the constable, if the
offence is not a crime the police officer shall either discharge him or her or shall take his
or her recognisance’s, with or without sureties, conditioned as hereinafter mentioned, and
shall then discharge him or her and proceed against him or her for the offence by
complaint before a magistrate.

### 28.

Police officers may seize articles suspected of being smuggled
(1) It shall be the duty of every police officer to stop and take into his or her
possession all goods which are or which he or she reasonably suspects to be liable to
forfeiture under any law relating to customs or excise, and all goods which are or which
he or she reasonably suspects to be smuggled, and to deliver them to an officer of the
Treasury or the Department of Customs and Excise; and in any of the aforesaid cases the
police officer shall seize all vessels, packages, carts, animals, or other things whatsoever
in which the goods are contained or by which they are carried (together with the person
from whom the goods have been taken) and he or she may detain them to be produced as
evidence at the trial; and in such case the police officer shall give notice to the Permanent
Secretary (Finance) or the Chief of Police of the goods and things being so detained, with
particulars of them; and immediately after the trial of the offender all the goods and
things shall be placed under the custody of the Permanent Secretary (Finance) or the
Chief of Police for such proceedings to be taken as may be expedient. A police officer

who under the aforesaid circumstances neglects to convey such goods or things or to give
such notice as aforesaid shall be liable to a fine of two hundred and fifty dollars.
(2) A police officer or person acting in his or her aid upon reasonable suspicion may
stop and search any vessel within three miles of the shore in any part of Grenada, and any
cart, carriage, or other conveyance, with the object of ascertaining whether any goods
liable or suspected to be liable to forfeiture as aforesaid, or smuggled or suspected to be
smuggled, are contained therein or were carried thereby; if no such goods are found, the
police officer or other person shall not be liable to any prosecution or action for having so
stopped and searched the vessel, cart, carriage or other conveyance. Any person in charge
of such vessel, cart, carriage or other conveyance, refusing to stop when required to do so
by a police officer or such other person, shall be liable to a fine of five hundred dollars.

### 29.

Persons arrested without warrant, how dealt with
A person taken into custody without a warrant, except when detained for the purpose
merely of ascertaining his or her name, shall be taken to the nearest police station and
delivered to the officer in charge thereof in order that he or she may be secured until he or
she can be brought up before a magistrate (upon the charge upon which he or she has
been taken into custody) to be dealt with according to law. The officer in charge of the
station, when the magistrate’s court is closed, may if he or she thinks it prudent take that
person’s recognisance, with or without sureties, conditioned as hereinafter mentioned,
unless he or she has been arrested on a charge of an indictable offence or of stealing,
fraudulently obtaining or dishonestly receiving anything the value of which exceeds ten
dollars:
Provided that if the person arrested is prepared to enter into recognisances as
aforesaid in respect of any of the offences hereinbefore excepted, he or she shall be taken
before the magistrate of the district or before a Justice of the Peace for that purpose.

### 30.

Police officer may require person making charge to enter into recognisance to
prosecute
Whenever a person is arrested without a warrant and taken to a police station charged
with any of the offences in the last preceding section excepted, the officer in charge of the
station, if the magistrate’s court is then closed, shall require the person making the charge
to enter into recognisances to prosecute, conditioned as hereinafter mentioned; if he or
she refuses, the officer may release the person charged upon his or her own recognisance,
with or without sureties.

### 31.

Recognisance
Every recognisance under this Act shall be without fee, and shall be conditioned for
the appearance of the person bound before the magistrate of the district in which the
station is at his or her next sitting, either to appear and answer the charge, or to appear
and prosecute the charge, as the case may be; and the time and place of appearance shall
be specified in the recognisance. The officer in charge shall enter in a book to be kept for
that purpose at every police station the name and residence of the party and his or her
surety or sureties entering into the recognisance, together with the condition thereof and
the sum thereby acknowledged, and shall, in case of default, return the recognisance to
the magistrate present at the time when and the place where the party is bound to appear.
If the person bound does not appear at the time and place specified in the recognisance
the conditioned sum shall be forfeited, and the sum forfeited may be recovered in the
same manner as forfeited recognisances entered into before a magistrate or Justice of the
Peace.
PART IVA

Police Dogs

### 31A.

Acquisition of dogs by the Force
The Commissioner of Police may from time to time acquire such number of dogs,
hereinafter referred to as “police dogs”, to be used in connection with the execution of the
duties of police officers under this Act or any other enactment.

### 31B.

Obstruction, etc., of police dog
No person shall obstruct, hinder, injure or kill a police dog which is being used in the
investigation of any crime or in the enforcement of public order and is under the control
or in the custody of a police officer.

### 31C.

Penalty for obstruction, etc., of police dog
A person who obstructs, hinders, injures or kills a police dog commits an offence and
shall be liable on summary conviction—
(a)

in the case of where the police dog is obstructed, hindered or injured, to a
fine of five thousand dollars or imprisonment for three months; and

(b)

in the case of where a police dog is killed, to a fine of ten thousand dollars or
imprisonment for six months.

## PART V

Offences and Discipline

### 32.

Punishment of serious offences
(1) Any police officer who—
(a)

begins, raises, abets, countenances or excites mutiny, or causes or joins in
any sedition amongst the members of the Force; or

(b)

coming to the knowledge of an actual or intended mutiny, rebellion, or
insurrection, does not without delay give information thereof to a Gazetted
Officer or to a Justice of the Peace,

shall be guilty of an offence punishable on indictment and liable to imprisonment for
three years.
(2) Any police officer who—
(a)

being present at any assemblage tending to a riot, does not use his or her
utmost endeavours to suppress the same;

(b)

assaults a Justice of the Peace or superior officer;

(c)

draws or lifts, or offers to draw or lift, a weapon or offers any violence
against a Justice of the Peace or superior officer;

(d)

wilfully permits the escape of a prisoner;

(e)

uses unnecessary violence to or ill-uses a prisoner; or

(f)

pawns, sells, makes away with or wilfully spoils or damages his or her arms,
accoutrements, clothing or any public property,

shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand
five hundred dollars and to imprisonment for six months.

### 33.

Offences punishable by the Chief of Police
A police officer of or below the rank of Police Sergeant who is guilty of any of the
following offences—
(a)

cowardice;

(b)

leaving his or her guard or post or beat without authority;

(c)

sleeping on his or her post or beat;

(d)

mutinous conduct, or being accessory thereto;

(e)

assault on a member of the Force;

(f)

wilful disobedience;

(g)

insubordinate language or conduct;

(h)

desertion;

(i)

permitting the escape of a prisoner;

(j)

committing wilful damage to Government property;

(k)

making false statements, entries or returns;

(l)

gambling or betting in barracks;

(m)

absence without leave;

(n)

drunkenness at any time;

(o)

malingering;

(p)

breaking arrest or out of barracks;

(q)

wilful or negligent destruction of, or damage to, any arms, clothing, or
accoutrements, or any other Government property;

(r)

pawning, losing, selling, or making away with his or her arms, clothing or
accoutrements or any other Government property;

(s)

breach of any regulation or standing order;

(t)

conduct to the prejudice of good order and discipline;

(u)

loitering on duty;

(v)

neglect of duty;

(w)

breach of confidence; or

(x)

corrupt practice,

may, on conviction thereof by the Chief of Police or, in his or her absence, by an officer
or other member of the Force deputed by him or her, be punished by such officer or other
member of the Force for each offence by any one or any combination of not more than
four of the following punishments, namely—
(i) reprimand,
(ii) severe reprimand,
(iii) forfeiture of good conduct pay and badges or of any benefit arising
from the whole or part of his or her past or future service,
(iv) a fine of one hundred and fifty dollars,
(v) confinement to barracks for any period not exceeding twenty-eight
days (and such confinement shall involve the performance of ordinary
duty and parades as well as fatigue duties),
(vi) detention in cells not exceeding fourteen days,

(vii) reduction in rank,
(viii) dismissal.

### 34.

Method of dealing with offences by certain police officers
The power to exercise disciplinary control over persons holding or acting in offices in
the Force below the rank of Chief of Police but above the rank of Police Sergeant shall
vest in the Public Service Commission.

### 35.

Hearing and determination of charge or complaint
(1) When a charge or complaint is made against a police officer of or below the rank
of Police Sergeant for an offence against police force discipline the Chief of Police or the
Gazetted Officer as in the following subsection prescribed may hear and determine the
charge or complaint and may impose any of the following sentences coupled in the case
of a Gazetted Officer other than the Chief of Police, if he or she thinks fit, with a
recommendation of dismissal from the Force, that is to say—
(a)

caution or reprimand;

(b)

suspension, deferment or stoppage of increment;

(c)

a fine of one hundred and fifty dollars;

(d)

reduction in rank;

(e)

forfeiture of good conduct pay or badges, or of any benefit arising from
service;

(f)

in the case of constables only, confinement to barracks for any period not
exceeding twenty-eight days (and such confinement shall involve the
performance of ordinary duty and parades as well as fatigue duties).

(2) (a) A Superintendent of Police deputed by the Chief of Police may hear and
determine a complaint or charge in respect of any offence against police force discipline
committed by a police officer of or below the rank of Police Sergeant;
(b) A Gazetted Officer deputed by the Chief of Police may hear and determine a
complaint or charge in respect of any offence against police force discipline committed
by a Police Corporal or a constable.
(3) The Chief of Police or other Gazetted Officer shall have the same powers in
respect of securing and compelling the attendance of witnesses and their examination
upon oath and otherwise as are conferred upon a magistrate under the Civil Procedure
Act, Chapter 55.
(4) A person who gives false evidence on oath at the hearing of any such charge or
complaint shall be deemed guilty of perjury.

### 36.

Appeals
(1) Where—
(a)

a police officer of or below the rank of Police Sergeant is dissatisfied with a
decision given by the Chief of Police, on the determination of a charge for an
offence against police force discipline, he or she may within seven days after
such decision has been communicated to him or her appeal to the Public
Service Board of Appeal;

(b)

a police officer of or below the rank of Police Sergeant is dissatisfied with
the decision of a Gazetted Officer other than the Chief of Police, he or she
may within seven days after the Gazetted Officer’s decision has been
communicated to him or her appeal to the Chief of Police and if he or she is

dissatisfied with the decision of the Chief of Police he or she may appeal to
the Public Service Board of Appeal within seven days after the decision of
the Chief of Police has been communicated to him or her:
Provided that there shall be no right of appeal if the only punishment awarded is a
caution or reprimand nor against a finding of guilt in a case where the police officer has
admitted the charge.
(2) An appeal under subsection (1) shall be sent or delivered to the Gazetted Officer
against whose decision the appeal is made and he or she shall forward it to the Chief of
Police or to the Public Service Board of Appeal, as the case may be.
(3) Where an appeal is made the notes of evidence in the case, together with any
statement which may have been made by the offender in his or her defence, shall be
forwarded to the Chief of Police or the Public Service Board of Appeal, as the case may
be.
(4) The Chief of Police or the Public Service Board of Appeal, after considering the
appeal and the notes of evidence and statements and any other documents forwarded
pursuant to subsection (3), shall either—
(a)

allow the appeal;

(b)

dismiss the appeal; or

(c)

vary the punishment by substituting some other punishment which the
Gazetted Officer might have awarded.

(5) Every appeal under this section shall be in writing and the decision of the Chief of
Police (if there is no further appeal), or the Public Service Board of Appeal shall be final.
(6) Every appeal under this section shall have the effect of suspending the execution
of the decision until the appeal has been disposed of.

### 37.

Variation of punishment
(1) The Chief of Police may without any appeal vary the punishment imposed by a
Gazetted Officer on a police officer of or below the rank of Police Sergeant by
substituting some other punishment (whether more or less severe) which such Gazetted
Officer might have awarded.
(2) Where the Chief of Police is himself or herself a witness or otherwise personally
involved in a charge or complaint against a police officer of or below the rank of Police
Sergeant he or she shall not have in respect of the punishment awarded thereon the power
of variation referred to in subsection (1).

### 38.

Dismissal and reduction in rank
(1) The Chief of Police may order the dismissal from the Force or reduction in rank of
a police officer of or below the rank of Police Sergeant who is convicted of a criminal
offence or an offence against police force discipline.
(2) Dismissal or reduction in rank under subsection (1) may be ordered in addition to
any punishment which may be imposed on conviction as aforesaid whether, in the case of
a dismissal, a recommendation to that effect has or has not been made and without calling
on the offender or defaulter to show cause why he or she should not be dismissed.

### 39.

Payment during suspension, etc.
(1) A police officer of or below the rank of Police Sergeant against whom a complaint
or information for an offence punishable on summary conviction or on indictment is laid
or against whom a charge is made for breach of any disciplinary regulation made under

this Act may, pending and until final determination of such complaint, information or
charge—
(a)

be suspended from duty and placed on half pay by the Chief or Police; or

(b)

if admitted to bail and not so suspended, be employed on full-time duty (in
which case he or she shall receive full pay) or be employed on part-time duty
(in which case he or she shall receive such rate of pay, not being less than
half pay, as the Chief of Police thinks fit).

(2) If a police officer of or below the rank of Police Sergeant is acquitted on a
complaint or information, or obtains a decision in his or her favour on a charge, he or she
shall be entitled to receive all pay which has been withheld from him or her; if he or she
is convicted on the complaint or information or does not obtain a decision in his or her
favour on the charge and is subsequently dismissed, he or she shall not be entitled to
receive any pay so withheld.
(3) In the application of subsection (1), a police officer of or below the rank of Police
Sergeant shall not be deprived of any part of the house and lodging allowance or the use
of any free quarters to which he or she may be entitled.

### 40.

Public property not to be used privately
Unless he or she is specially authorised to do so by the regulations, no police officer
shall be entitled to keep or use for his or her private benefit any article whatever which
has been supplied to him or her at the public expense, but he or she shall hold every such
article at the order and disposal of the Chief of Police.

### 41.

Delivery up of articles supplied on leaving the Force
(1) A police officer dismissed from the Force, or who resigns from or otherwise
leaves the Force, shall forthwith deliver to the person appointed by the Chief of Police for
that purpose every article whatsoever supplied to him or her at the public expense which
he or she has not been expressly authorised to keep or use for his or her private benefit.
(2) A police officer who contravenes subsection (1) shall be guilty of an offence and
liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of two hundred and fifty dollars and to
imprisonment for three months; and it shall be lawful for a Justice of the Peace to issue
his or her warrant to search for and seize all such articles which are not so delivered,
wherever the same may be found, and to arrest the person in whose possession the same
may be found.

## PART VI

Police Band, Liabilities and Reward Fund

### 42.

The Police Band
(1) There shall be a Police Band comprising a Bandmaster, who shall be appointed as
a police officer, and such number of bandsmen (also to be appointed as police officers,
cadets and apprentices) as may be approved by the Governor-General:
Provided that members of the Police Band under the age of eighteen years
immediately before the commencement of this Act shall be deemed to be police officers
and to remain as appointed police officers and bandsmen of the Police Band.
(2) Cadets not under the age of fourteen years and not over the age of eighteen years
may be engaged for training as bandsmen for the Police Band as the need may arise, on
the conditions hereafter set forth and subject to such regulations relating to their
employment, discipline and duties as may be approved by the Governor-General.

(3) An applicant for cadetship shall become bound to serve faithfully for the specified
term as soon as the Chief of Police is satisfied that the youth is willing to serve and if the
consent of one of the following persons has been obtained, namely—
(a)

his or her father, if he or she is in Grenada and can be found;

(b)

his or her mother, if she is in Grenada and the attendance of the father cannot
be had;

(c)

his or her guardian, if the attendance of neither his or her father nor mother
can be had;

(d)

the magistrate of the district if the attendance of none of the abovementioned can be had.

(4) The cadet shall be subject to the orders and control of the Chief of Police and the
Bandmaster; and for insubordination, disobedience, or neglect of duty, may be
reprimanded or dismissed by the Chief of Police.
(5) Subject to subsection (6), the term of cadetship shall be five years; but as soon as
a cadet is, in the opinion of the Bandmaster, qualified for duty as a bandsman, he or she
shall with the approval of the Chief of Police, and notwithstanding that the five years of
his or her cadetship shall not have expired, be enrolled as a constable in the Force and
thereafter shall receive the pay of a police constable and shall be subject in all respects to
the provisions of this Act as it applies to a police constable.
(6) A cadet who has not been enrolled as a constable in accordance with the last
preceding subsection may, on giving one month’s notice in writing to the Chief of Police,
terminate his or her service at the expiration of the notice or before such expiration with
the approval of the Chief of Police.
(7) The Band may include apprentices up to a maximum of six who shall be paid a
small monthly remuneration for out of pocket expenses and who shall attend such
functions of the Band as the Bandmaster may from time to time direct.

## PART VII

Liabilities

### 43.

Liabilities of police officers
(1) In civil proceedings instituted in respect of an act done by a police officer in
obedience to the warrant of a magistrate or Justice of the Peace, the party against whom
the action is brought shall not be responsible for any irregularity in the issuing of the
warrant or for any want of jurisdiction of the magistrate or Justice of the Peace issuing the
same but may give the warrant in evidence at the trial, and on proving that the signature
thereto is the handwriting of the person whose name shall appear subscribed thereto and
that such person was reputed to be and acted as a magistrate for the district or Justice of
the Peace and that the act complained of was done in obedience to the warrant there shall
be a verdict in the action for the defendant.
(2) It shall be the duty of a police officer, if required to do so in the execution of a
warrant to him or her directed, to produce the same to the party or parties taken into
custody thereunder, and to permit a copy thereof to be taken by him or her or them, or on
his or her or their behalf, either at the time of the arrest or at some later time while the
warrant remains in his or her custody.

### 44.

Satisfaction of civil judgements against police officers
No execution shall be issued without the consent of the Chief of Police upon a
judgement obtained in a civil court against a police officer, except in the case of a

judgement for house rent. Whether the execution shall be issued or not, a certificate
setting forth the full particulars of the judgement and the nature of the action in which it
was obtained shall be forwarded to the Chief of Police by the registrar or magistrate, as
the case may be, of the court in which the judgement shall have been obtained, and the
Chief of Police shall make such order thereon as to the mode and manner in which the
judgement is to be satisfied out of the salary of such police officer as the Chief of Police
shall deem just. The Chief of Police shall, pursuant to the order, cause the amount of the
judgement, and any costs, to be deducted from the pay of the police officer, and shall pay,
or, from time to time pay the amount or amounts so deducted as aforesaid, to the registrar
or magistrate, as the case may be, to be applied by the police officer in satisfaction of the
judgement according to law. This section, however, does not continue to apply in respect
of a former police officer after he or she has left the service of the Force.

### 45.

Police acquitted of offences not liable to further punishment
No police officer, after acquittal by a court of competent jurisdiction of a crime or
offence, shall be punished on the same charge under this Act.

### 46.

The Police Reward Fund
(1) All fines and penalties imposed on an Inspector, sub-ordinate officer, or constable
or any other person under the authority of this Act, and all penalties and proportions of
penalties awarded to a police officer on any summary conviction as the prosecutor of an
information, shall be received by the Chief of Police or by the senior officer of the Force
for the time being in Grenada and shall be placed on deposit in the Treasury to a separate
account to be called the “Police Reward Fund”:
Provided that where in any statute in force in Grenada it is enacted that a part or
share of a seizure or forfeiture, or proceeds thereof, shall or may be awarded to a person
as prosecutor, informer, or otherwise, or to a police officer, then such police officer shall
have for his or her own personal use and benefit any such part or share or proceeds
awarded to him or her thereunder.
(2) No payment shall be made from the Police Reward Fund except upon the
authority of the Chief of Police.
(3) The Chief of Police may, in his or her discretion, authorise payments from the
Police Reward Fund for any of the following purposes—
(a)

payments to subordinate officers or constables as rewards for meritorious
acts or service in the execution of duty, if such payments are not authorised
to be met from any other fund;

(b)

assistance to the wives or families of deceased police officers below the rank
of Assistant Superintendent or to any police officers discharged from the
Force as medically unfit for further service;

(c)

expenditure for the benefit and advancement of authorised recreation and
sport and other branches of police activity organised within the Force;

(d)

the purchase of ammunition for the encouragement of range practice among
police officers; and

(e)

expenditure for the benefit and advancement of education organised within
the Force.

(4) A police officer who has been dismissed from the Force shall not benefit from the
Police Reward Fund.
(5) An account shall be kept by the Chief of Police, in such form as the Director of
Audit may approve, of all sums expended and received in connection with the Police
Reward Fund and such account shall be duly audited by the Director of Audit.

## PART VII

Police Welfare Association and Canteens

### 47.

Constitution of Police Welfare Association
(1) For the purpose of enabling subordinate officers and constables to consider and
bring to the notice of the Chief of Police and the Governor-General all matters affecting
their general welfare and efficiency, other than questions of discipline, promotion,
transfer, leave or any other matter affecting individuals, there shall be established in
accordance with rules to be made by the Governor-General an organisation to be called
the Police Welfare Association (hereinafter referred to as “the Association”) which shall
act through Boards and a Central Committee as provided in the rules.
(1A) The Police Welfare Association shall be a body corporate with a common seal
and is subject to the provisions of sections 48 and 49 of the Interpretation and General
Provisions Act, Chapter 153.
(2) The Association shall be entirely independent of and not associated with any body
or person outside the Force.

### 48.

Police Welfare Association rules
The Governor-General may make rules for the constitution and governance of the
Association and any matter appertaining to the Association.

### 49.

Police officers not to join prohibited associations
(1) It shall not be lawful for a police officer to be or become a member of a prohibited
association or of a trade union as defined by the Trade Unions and Trade Disputes Act,

## Chapter 326, whether the trade union is registered or incorporated or not.

(2) If a police officer whilst in the service of the Force shall have become a member
of a prohibited association or of a trade union he or she shall be guilty of an offence and
liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of two hundred and fifty dollars.
(3) A police officer whilst in the service of the Force who is or who becomes a
member of an association which itself becomes a prohibited association, and who at the
expiration of fourteen days from the date of the association becoming a prohibited
association remains a member of that prohibited association, shall be guilty of an offence
and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of two hundred and fifty dollars.
(4) If any prohibited association or trade union permits a police officer to become a
member of or to receive any benefit, financial or otherwise, from such association or
trade union or if such an association or trade union permits receipt of money from a
police officer, the association or trade union and every officer of the association or trade
union who knowingly so permits shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary
conviction, to a fine of seven hundred and fifty dollars.
(5) The Chief of Police may, with the approval of the Governor-General, declare as a
prohibited association for the purposes of this section, any association or body which
appears to him or her likely in the pursuit of its objects to promote feelings of ill will or
hostility between different classes or races or to be subversive of good discipline on the
part of police officers.

### 50.

Canteens
(1) There may be established for the Force such number of police canteens as may be
approved by the Chief of Police at which the keepers thereof may sell intoxicating liquors
by retail to the members of the Force.

(2) The Chief of Police may make rules in regard to the hours during which such
canteens shall be kept open on each day and for the proper management and control
thereof.
(3) Notwithstanding the provisions of the Liquor Dealers Licences Act, Chapter 174,
the keepers of such canteens shall be exempted from the payment of licence fees for the
sale of intoxicating liquor and tobacco.

## PART VIII

Royal Grenada Special Reserve Police Force

### 51.

Establishment of Royal Grenada Special Reserve Police Force
There shall be established a supplemental body of police styled the Royal Grenada
Special Reserve Police Force (hereinafter referred to as “the Reserve”) which may be
called out for service by the Chief of Police—
(a)

whenever additional police are required for the preservation of good order;
and

(b)

for purposes of drill and training at such times and places as may be
prescribed by regulations made by the Governor-General from time to time.

### 52.

Chief of Police to command Reserve
(1) The Chief of Police shall have the general command and superintendence of the
Reserve.
(2) The Reserve shall consist of a Commandant, an Adjutant, and such other officers
and constables as the Chief of Police with the approval of the Governor-General may
direct.

### 53.

Appointment of officers
The Governor-General may appoint fit and proper persons to be officers of the
Reserve.

### 54.

Appointment of non-commissioned officers and constables
The Chief of Police may appoint fit and proper persons to be non-commissioned
officers and constables of the Reserve.

### 55.

Qualifications for appointment
Every male person who—
(a)

is not less than eighteen years of age;

(b)

is able-bodied; and

(c)

is of good character,

may, upon making application in writing to the Chief of Police in that behalf, be
appointed a constable of the Reserve.

### 56.

Precept of appointment and oath of office
(1) On the appointment of a person to be a non-commissioned officer or constable of
the Reserve, the Chief of Police shall cause to be delivered to him or her a precept
authorising him or her to act as such.

(2) The precept shall be in the following form—
To ................................................................................ of ..................................................................
I, ...................................................................................... Chief of Police, under and by virtue of the
power in me vested, hereby appoint you to be a ............................................. of the Royal Grenada
Special Reserve Police Force and I do issue to you this precept authorising you to act as such.
Dated: etc.
....................................................................
Chief of Police

(3) Every person appointed to be an officer, non-commissioned officer or constable of
the Reserve shall take and subscribe before an officer of the Force the following oath of
office—
I ........................................................................ of ..............................................................................
do swear that I will well and truly serve Our Sovereign Lady the Queen as a member of the Royal
Grenada Special Reserve Police Force. So help me God.

### 57.

Equipment
Every member of the Reserve shall be provided with a short manual describing the
powers and duties of the Reserve, a badge, a baton, and such articles of clothing and
equipment as may be approved by the Chief of Police. Such badge shall be prima facie
evidence that the officer or constable is a member of the Reserve and shall in all cases be
displayed by every such member when exercising the duties of that office. The cost of
these articles of clothing and equipment shall be defrayed from such funds as may be
provided by the House of Representatives.

### 58.

Revocation of appointment
The appointment of a member of the Reserve may be revoked at any time—
(a)

by the Governor-General in the case of officers;

(b)

by the Chief of Police in other cases.

### 59.

Resignation
A member of the Reserve—
(a)

who has been a member for a period of at least two years and who is not
actually on service or called out for service under the provisions of this Act;
or

(b)

who, at any time, for special reasons (which shall be to the expressed
satisfaction of the Governor-General) desires to resign his or her
appointment as a member of the Reserve,

may resign his or her appointment as such member by giving to the Governor-General in
the case of officers, and to the Chief of Police in other cases, one month’s notice in
writing:
Provided that the Chief of Police may in any fit case falling under paragraph (a),
dispense with such notice.

### 60.

Jurisdiction of officers
(1) The Chief of Police, or any officer of the Reserve deputed by him or her for the
purpose shall, subject as is hereinafter provided, have and possess power and jurisdiction
to hear and determine a charge against any non-commissioned officer or constable of the

Reserve for any of the offences mentioned in section 33, and to inflict on such noncommissioned officer or constable who is found guilty of any of those offences, one of
the following punishments, namely—
(a)

admonishment;

(b)

reprimand;

(c)

a fine of up to twenty-five dollars;

(d)

reduction in rank;

(e)

a fine exceeding twenty-five dollars but not more than one hundred and
twenty dollars:

Provided that the punishments mentioned in paragraphs (d) and (e) shall only be
inflicted after approval thereof by the Governor-General, who may confirm the same or
remit the whole or any part thereof. For this purpose the Chief of Police and every officer
so deputed by him or her shall have the same powers as hereinbefore mentioned.
(2) The relevant provisions of the Criminal Code Act, Chapter 72A, shall apply to
proceedings under subsection (1) as they apply to proceedings in the magistrates’ court.

### 61.

Powers, duties, responsibilities, pay, etc.
(1) Every member of the Reserve when called out for service by the Chief of Police
under the provisions of section 51 shall have, exercise and enjoy the same powers,
authorities, advantages and immunities, as a member of the Force, and shall be liable to
perform the same duties and undertake the same responsibilities.
(2) Such member when so called out shall be paid for his or her services at the
following rates—
(a)

if he or she is a private individual not in the Government Service, at the
same rate as a member of the Force of equivalent rank, except that any
regular allowance received by him or her in respect of services rendered to
the Reserve shall cease to be payable during the period of the call out;

(b)

if he or she is an officer and is in the Government Service and is receiving
his or her substantive salary, the difference between that salary and the pay
of a member of the Force of equivalent rank, except that any regular
allowance received by him or her in respect of services rendered to the
Reserve shall not be drawn concurrently with any emoluments received
under this section during the period of the call out, and that being already an
officer of the Force he or she shall receive no additional remuneration but
shall continue to receive any regular allowance payable to him or her in
respect of services rendered to the Reserve;

(c)

if he or she is a non-commissioned officer and is in the Government Service
and is receiving his or her substantive salary, two dollars and fifty cents a
day;

(d)

if he or she is a constable and is in the Government Service and is receiving
his or her substantive salary, one dollar and twenty-five cents a day.

### 62.

Member of Reserve to remain within his or her district when called out for
service
No member of the Reserve shall leave or absent himself or herself from the district in
which he or she is stationed during any period in which the Reserve is called out for
service, without the permission of the officer in charge of the district.

### 63.

Penalties for offences

(1) Any member of the Reserve who, when called out for service, neglects or refuses
to serve, or neglects or refuses to obey any lawful command, shall be guilty of an offence
and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of two hundred and fifty dollars.
(2) Any member of the Reserve who improperly lends, sells, pledges or otherwise
disposes of, or damages or spoils, any equipment issued to him or her at public expense
while a member shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine
of one hundred and twenty-five dollars.
(3) Every person who, during any period in which the Reserve is called out for
service, impersonates or in any way pretends to be a member of the Reserve shall be
guilty of a misdemeanour and liable, on conviction on indictment, to imprisonment for
two years, or on summary conviction to imprisonment for six months and to a fine of one
thousand five hundred dollars.
(4) Every person who, not being a member of the Reserve, is in possession of any
badge, baton, equipment or clothing provided for the use of a member of the Reserve
shall, in the absence of a reasonable excuse for such possession, be guilty of an offence
and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of two hundred and fifty dollars.

### 64.

Illness, bodily injury and death payments
(1) If a member of the Reserve through no fault of his or her own contracts any illness
or sustains any bodily injury while in the actual discharge of his or her duties as such,
although he or she has not been called out for service by the Chief of Police under the
provisions of section 51 or detailed for duty by a superior officer, he or she shall be
provided with medicines, medical comforts and medical attendance at the public expense
on the written authority of the Chief of Police.
(2) If a member of the Reserve sustains bodily injury in the circumstances specified in
the preceding subsection, and is permanently disabled (either totally or partially) as a
result thereof, the Governor-General may grant him or her a pension or gratuity, the
amount of which shall be subject to the approval of the House of Representatives.
(3) If the aforesaid bodily injury results in death, the Governor-General may award to
the dependents (including any illegitimate children to whose support the deceased was
contributing) a pension or gratuity, the amount of which shall be subject to the approval
of the House of Representatives.
(4) Members of the Reserve while serving as such shall not be regarded as workmen
for the purposes of the Workmen’s Compensation Act, Chapter 343.

### 65.

Saving for members of Special Reserve Police
Every person who, immediately before the commencement of this Act, was a member
of the Special Reserve Police, shall be deemed to have been appointed a member of the
Reserve under this Part and to have taken the oath of office prescribed for a member of
the Reserve.

## PART IX

Police Regulations, Standing Orders, Offences and Rural Constable

### 66.

Regulations
(1) The Governor-General may, subject to the provisions of this Act, make
regulations relating to all or any of the following matters, that is to say—
(a)

the description and issue of arms, ammunition and necessaries to be supplied
to the Force;

(b)

the form of certificate of appointment of police officers;

(c)

prescribing the acts or omissions of Inspectors, subordinate officers and
constables which shall be offences against police force discipline;

(d)

the conduct of disciplinary proceedings against Inspectors, subordinate
officers and constables;

(e)

the constitution, procedure and functions of the Police Association;

(f)

good conduct pay to subordinate officers and constables;

(g)

leave, resignation, release and dismissal of police officers including those of
the Reserve;

(h)

the treatment of persons detained or confined in any police building;

(i)

prescribing anything which by this Act is required or permitted to be
prescribed; and

(j)

generally for the good order and government of the Force.

(2) The Chief of Police may from time to time issue Standing Orders, not inconsistent
with this Act and the regulations made thereunder, for any of the following purposes, that
is to say—
(a)

the description and issue of accoutrements and badges of rank to be supplied
to the Force;

(b)

the duties to be performed by police officers;

(c)

the training of the Force;

(d)

the management and good government of police buildings, accommodation,
stores and furniture;

(e)

the posting of police officers and the duties to be performed by them;

(f)

the management of police canteens and recreation rooms;

(g)

the welfare of police officers;

(h)

such other matters as may be necessary for preventing abuse or neglect of
duty, for rendering the Force more efficient in the discharge of its duties, and
for carrying out the objects of this Act.

(3) Every Standing Order—
(a)

shall be subject to the approval of the Governor-General; and

(b)

shall be brought to the notice of every police officer, but need not be
published in the Gazette.

### 67.

Offences against the Police
(1) Whoever—
(a)

has in his or her possession any clothing, arms, ammunition, accoutrements
or other appointments furnished for the use of the Force, and does not
account satisfactorily for his or her possession thereof, or puts on and
assumes the dress, name, designation or description of a police officer;

(b)

knowingly harbours, entertains, or either directly or indirectly sells or gives
any intoxicating liquor to any police officer on duty, or permits him or her to
remain in his or her house without lawful excuse; or

(c)

refuses to aid or assist a police officer when lawfully called upon by such
officer to do so,

shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of two hundred
and fifty dollars and to imprisonment for one month.
(2) Whoever—
(a)

assists or conceals any deserter from the Force knowing him or her to be
such; or

(b)

puts on or assumes the dress, name, designation or description of a police
officer, in such a manner or under such circumstances as to be likely to bring
contempt upon the Force, or employs any other person to do so,

shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to imprisonment for six
months.
(3) A person who assaults, obstructs, resists, or counsels or incites another to assault,
obstruct or resist, a police officer in the execution of his or her duty, shall be guilty of an
offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand and five hundred
dollars and to imprisonment for one year.

### 68.

Appointment of Rural Constables
The Chief of Police may, from time to time, appoint fit and proper persons to be Rural
Constables.

### 69.

Powers, duties, etc., of Rural Constables
The name of every person appointed to be a Rural Constable shall be published in the
Gazette and after the date of such publication, upon his or her making before a magistrate
or the Chief of Police the declaration prescribed by section 12(1), he or she shall have all
such powers, authorities and immunities, and be subject to all such duties, responsibilities
and discipline, as are conferred or imposed upon a constable.

### 70.

Striking Rural Constable off roll
It shall be lawful for the Chief of Police to strike off the roll any Rural Constable
who—
(a)

shall have reached the age of sixty years;

(b)

is unable through ill health or infirmity to carry out his or her duties; or;

(c)

shall have left Grenada and remained away for a continuous period of six
months.

### 71.

Retirement of Rural Constable
A Rural Constable may retire from office by giving to the Chief of Police not less than
one month’s notice in writing of his or her intention to do so.

### 72.

Badge, etc., of Rural Constable
(1) Every Rural Constable shall be furnished by the Chief of Police, at the public
expense, with such distinctive badge, clothing, and appointments as the GovernorGeneral may from time to time direct, all such articles being for the use of the Rural
Constable when on duty and remaining the property of the Government.
(2) When a Rural Constable retires, or is removed or dismissed from his or her office,
he or she shall forthwith return all such articles to the Chief of Police or to a person
appointed by the Chief of Police for this purpose, and in default of his or her so doing he
or she shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, that the value
thereof be recovered from him or her by the Chief of Police, or any police officer deputed

by him or her, and to a fine of fifty dollars; and the court may also, for every such
offence, award imprisonment for two months.

### 73.

Liability of Rural Constable to be called out for duty
Every Rural Constable shall at all times be liable to be called out for duty by the Chief
of Police and shall conform to and observe the laws regulating and governing the Force in
so far as they apply to him or her.

### 74.

Remuneration of Rural Constable on duty
Every Rural Constable called out for duty shall receive, for every day or part thereof
that he or she may be so employed, such payment as may be prescribed by regulations
made by the Governor-General.

### 75.

Appearance of Rural Constable in January and July in each year before a
superior
Every Rural Constable shall, in the months of January and July in every year, appear
personally before the Chief of Police or the officer in charge of the police station nearest
to where he or she resides, and state his or her occupation and place of residence; no
Rural Constable shall change his or her place of residence so stated unless he or she has
given notice of his or her intention to do so to the Chief of Police or officer in charge of
the police station nearest to that place.

### 76.

Offences by Rural Constable and against Rural Constables
(1) Every Rural Constable who wilfully relinquishes his or her office without giving
at least one month’s notice in writing of his or her intention to do so, or refuses or
neglects to perform all lawful commands or directions which may be given to him or her
by the Chief of Police, or wilfully refuses or neglects to observe the regulations, rules and
orders of the Force, shall be guilty of an offence and liable to be dismissed, and shall (in
addition thereto) on summary conviction for any such offence be liable to a fine of two
hundred and fifty dollars, and in default of payment to imprisonment for up to one month.
(2) Whoever assaults, or resists, or incites any other person to assault or resist, any
Rural Constable in the execution of his or her duty shall be guilty of an offence and
liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of five hundred dollars and to imprisonment for
three months.

## PART X

Miscellaneous

### 77.

Medical, etc., benefit and funeral expenses
(1) All police officers shall be entitled to hospital treatment and medical, surgical,
ophthalmic and dental attention at the public expense subject to such conditions as may
be prescribed by regulations made under this section.
(2) If a police officer is incapacitated for duty by his or her own misconduct the
Governor-General may order that he or she shall forfeit the whole or any part of his or her
pay for the period of such incapacity.
(3) A sum of not more than eight hundred dollars shall be paid towards the funeral
expenses of a police officer or member of the Reserve, up to and including the rank of
Police Sergeant or equivalent, if such officer or member has died through accident, injury
or illness sustained in the performance of his or her duty.

(4) A sum of not more than one thousand dollars shall be paid towards the funeral
expenses of a police officer or member of the Reserve above the rank of Police Sergeant
or equivalent, if such officer or member has died through accident, injury or illness
sustained in the performance of his or her duty.

### 78.

Entitlement of police to prosecute
(1) Notwithstanding anything to the contrary in the Criminal Procedure Code,

## Chapter 72B, it shall be lawful for a police officer to lay any information or to make any

complaint before a magistrate in the name of the Chief of Police, and every such
information or complaint shall be signed by the police officer laying or making the same.
(2) Where a police officer has laid an information or made a complaint pursuant to
subsection (1) or an alleged offender has been apprehended and the matter of the
information or complaint or other charge is before a magistrate or Justice of the Peace for
trial or inquiry, any other police officer may appear and prosecute the matter with the
same right to address the court and to examine witnesses as if he or she were the police
officer who had laid the information or made the complaint or brought the charge, as the
case may be.
(3) Criminal proceedings shall not be dismissed in any court by reason only that the
Chief of Police is not present to prosecute a matter in person or by counsel or a solicitor,
or by reason only of a failure to appear of the police officer who laid the information or
made the complaint or brought the charge, but it shall be sufficient that the prosecution is
represented by any police officer for the time being present in the court.

### 79.

Member of other police force may attend duty in Grenada, and vice versa
If any goods or money charged to be stolen or fraudulently obtained shall be in the
custody of a police officer by virtue of a warrant of a magistrate or otherwise in the
execution of his or her duty and the person charged with stealing or obtaining possession
as aforesaid cannot be found or shall be duly convicted on such charge or charges, it shall
be lawful for the magistrate to make an order for the delivery of such goods or money to
the person who shall appear to be the rightful owner thereof, or if the owner thereof
cannot be ascertained, then to order that such goods within a reasonable time shall be sold
publicly and the proceeds thereof, together with any monies so in the custody of the
police officer, shall form part of the Police Reward Fund provided for in Part VI.

### 80.

Performance of duties by police officers of other CARICOM countries in
Grenada and vice versa
(1) Any member of the Police Force of any CARICOM country other than Grenada
(hereinafter in this section referred to as “any other CARICOM country”), who is directed
by his or her respective authorities to come to Grenada and does so come to Grenada for
the purpose of executing any writ or warrant or of performing any duties appertaining to
his or her office or imposed upon him or her by his or her superior officer shall, while on
duty within Grenada, be liable to perform the same duties and be subject to the orders and
directions of the Commissioner of Police of Grenada in the same manner and in all
respects as if he or she were a member of the Police Force of Grenada, and shall have the
same rights, powers, privileges and immunities, and be entitled to the same protection, as
are conferred, and be subject to the same liabilities as are imposed, by law and custom on
members of the Police Force of Grenada as may be necessary for the performance of such
duties.
(2) Where a member of the Police Force of any other CARICOM country who is
performing duties in Grenada pursuant to a direction given under this section becomes ill
or is injured while in Grenada, that member shall be entitled to the same medical care as
if he or she were a member of the Police Force of Grenada.

(3) It shall be lawful for the Governor-General of Grenada by direction in writing to
require any member of the Police Force of Grenada to proceed to any other CARICOM
country for the performance of any duties appertaining to his or her office under the
directions of the officer in charge of the Police Force of that other CARICOM country.
(4) Nothing in this section shall be construed as affecting the prerogative power of the
Crown to seek or accept assistance from the Police Force of any country outside
CARICOM.
(5) In this section—
“Caribbean Community” means the Caribbean Community established by the
Treaty establishing the Caribbean Community, which was signed at Trinidad and
Tobago on the 4th July, 1973, and to which Grenada is a party;
“CARICOM country” means a country that is a member of the Caribbean
Community.

POLICE (PROMOTIONS) REGULATIONS
[SRO 17 of 1959. Section 66.]

[1st June, 1959.]
1\. Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Police (Promotions) Regulations.
2\. Recommendations
Recommendations for promotion up to and including the rank of Inspector shall be
made in accordance with the provisions of these Regulations.
3\. Selection of Candidates
Candidates for promotion up to and including the rank of Inspector shall first pass
through the following stages, namely—
(a)

Field of Promotion; and

(b)

Zone of Selection.

4\. Field of Promotion
This shall include—
(a)

Constables of not less than four years continuous service; and

(b)

Subordinate officers of not less than two years service in their rank,

who shall be recommended as suitable for promotion by the Inspector or officer in charge
of their Division, hold the First Aid Certificate of the Saint John’s Ambulance
Association, and who shall be approved by the Chief of Police.
5\. Zone of Selection
(1) Candidates who qualify in the appropriate promotion examination shall enter the
corresponding Zone of Selection and shall remain therein for a period of not more than
four years from the date of passing the examination.
(2) If at the expiration of such period of four years as aforesaid a candidate has not
been promoted, he or she shall be required to pass the appropriate promotion examination
before re-entering the Zone of Selection.
6\. Grades of Examinations
Promotion examinations shall be in five grades, namely—
(a)

Grade I — open to all Constables in the Field of Promotion;

(b)

Grade II — open to all Grade I Constables in the Field of Promotion;

(c)

Grade III — open to all Corporals in the Field of Promotion;

(d)

Grade IV — open to all Sergeants in the Field of Promotion;

(e)

Grade V — open to all Cadet Officers in the Field of Promotion.

7\. Promotion Examinations
(1) Promotion examinations shall be based on—
(a)

knowledge of general Police duties;

(b)

knowledge of local laws which particularly concern the Police;

(c)

knowledge of Police procedure; and

(d)

an essay.

(2) In order to gain a pass at any examination, a candidate shall be required to obtain
at least fifty per cent of the total maximum marks for the entire examination.
(3) The names of successful candidates at examinations shall be published in Force
Orders.
(4) No Constable shall enter the Zone of Selection for promotion to Corporal until he
or she has qualified at the Grades I and II examinations.
(5) No Corporal shall enter the Zone of Selection for promotion to Sergeant until he
or she has qualified at the Grade III examination:
Provided that where promotion to Corporal is made under the provisions of these
Regulations, such subordinate officer shall enter the Zone of Selection for Sergeant upon
completing the required service and without undergoing a further qualifying examination.
(6) No Sergeant shall enter the Zone of Selection for promotion to Inspector until he
or she shall have qualified at the Grade IV examination.
(7) No Cadet Officer shall enter the Zone of Selection for promotion to Inspector until
he or she shall have qualified at the Grade V examination:
Provided that where promotion to Sergeant is made under these Regulations, such
non-commissioned officer shall enter the Zone of Selection for Inspector upon
completing the required service and without undergoing a further qualifying examination.
8\. Promotions Selection Board
All subordinate officers and Constables in the Zone of Selection shall appear each
year before the Promotions Selection Board who shall compile a Promotions List in order
of merit which shall remain in force until a new list shall have been compiled.
9\. Composition of Promotions Selection Board
The Promotions Selections Board shall consist of the Chief of Police, the Deputy
Chief of Police and an Assistant Chief of Police.
10\. Examinations and meetings of Board
Examinations and meetings of the Promotions Selection Board shall normally be held
annually, but the Chief of Police may hold them more or less frequently as he or she
thinks fit according to the exigencies of the service.
11\. Register of entrants for promotions examinations
A Register shall be kept at each Police Station, the Traffic Office, the Fire Brigade
Office, the Criminal Investigation Office and the Immigration Office, in which any
Constable or sub-ordinate officer wishing to enter for one of the promotions examinations
shall enter his or her number, rank, name, last date of passing the Saint John Ambulance
Association examination and grade of examination for which he or she wishes to sit.
12\. Submission of names of candidates
The Inspector or officer in charge of each Division shall submit to the Promotions
Selection Board a list of candidates whose names are entered in the Register as being
willing to sit the examination.

13\. Exemption due to ability
The Chief of Police may allow any Constable or sub-ordinate officer who shows
marked ability or aptitude for special work to sit for a promotion examination irrespective
of his or her length of service.
14\. Exemption in special cases
In cases of special employment or in special circumstances the Chief of Police may
recommend for promotion any subordinate officer or Constable without requiring him or
her to qualify under these Regulations.

POLICE REGULATIONS
[Section 66. SRO 26 of 1960 amended by SRO 6 of 1963, SRO 2 of 1969, SRO 16 of 1973, SRO
2 of 1990, SRO 33 of 1998.]

[6th August, 1960.]

---

### 1.

Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Police Regulations.

### 2.

Rank and designation
(1) The Force shall consist of the following ranks in the following order of
seniority—
(i) Gazetted Officers—
Commissioner of Police
Deputy Commissioner of Police
Assistant Commissioner of Police
Superintendent of Police
Assistant Superintendent of Police;
(ii) Inspector of Police;
(iii) Subordinate Officers—

Cadet Officers
Police Sergeant
Police Corporal
(iv) Police Constable.
(2) The designation “Commissioner of Police” shall apply to the Officer holding
appointment as Chief Officer of Police in the State.
(3) The designation “Non-Commissioned Officer” or “N.C.O.” means a subordinate
officer.
(4) The Deputy Commissioner of Police or other senior police officer shall, in the
absence or incapacity of the Commissioner of Police, or during any vacancy, have all the
powers and perform the duties of the Commissioner of Police.

### 3.

Police Divisions
(1) The State shall be divided into Police Divisions comprising Police Districts as
prescribed in the First Schedule to these Regulations.
(2) Each Police Division shall be under the supervision of an Officer, or Inspector.
(3) Police Districts shall be grouped to form Divisions and shall include the villages,
estates and other places falling within the areas allocated to the various stations as
prescribed in the Second Schedule to these Regulations.

### 4.

Strength
(1) The authorised establishment of the several ranks of the Police Force and any
changes thereof shall be as the Governor-General shall from time to time approve.
(2) The number shall be sufficient to provide men for the carrying out of patrols,
station duty, reserve and any other appointed duties under responsible supervision in each
tour of duty.

### 5.

Appointment of constables
A candidate for appointment as constable shall—
(i) produce satisfactory references as to his or her character including a
certificate given by the Head Teacher of the School which he or she last
attended and not less than two references given either by a Magistrate or
Justice of the Peace or by a responsible householder or former employer who
has known him or her personally for a continuous period of not less than
three years immediately preceding the date of application and, if he or she
has served in any branch of Her Majesty’s Naval, Military or Air Force, or in
the Civil Service or in any Commonwealth Police Force, produce
satisfactory proof of his or her good conduct while in such Service or Force.
A candidate dismissed from any such Service or Force shall not be eligible
for appointment;
(ii) produce a birth certificate, or other satisfactory evidence of his or her age:
Provided that a candidate over the age limit specified in section 16(1)(a) of
the Act may, for special reasons or in special circumstances approved by the
Commissioner of Police be appointed;
(iii) save in special cases approved by the Commissioner of Police conform with
the requirements of the Third Schedule to these Regulations:

Provided that the Commissioner of Police may appoint a candidate
possessing special qualifications for service in the Police Force whose height
is less than the standard but not less than five feet seven inches;
(iv) be certified by a Medical Officer appointed by the Chief Medical Officer for
the purpose to be in good health, of sound constitution and fitted both
physically and mentally to perform the duties of a police constable:
Provided that no candidate shall, except as otherwise provided in these
Regulations, be accepted whose physical and mental condition falls below
the standard set out in the Third Schedule to these Regulations;
(v) satisfy the Commissioner of Police that he or she is sufficiently educated by
passing such examination, together with an intelligence test, as may be
prescribed by the Commissioner of Police or produce satisfactory evidence
that he or she has passed another examination which, in the opinion of the
Commissioner of Police, is of an equivalent or higher standard;
(vi) give such information as may be required as to his or her previous history or
employment or as to any other matter relating to his or her appointment to
the Police Force; and if any candidate makes in his or her application for
appointment or on his or her attestation sheet any false or misleading
statement and is subsequently appointed to the Police Force, the making of
such false or misleading statement shall render him or her liable to
peremptory dismissal;
(vii) consent to be vaccinated and inoculated by a Medical Officer appointed for
the purpose.

### 6.

Eligibility for appointment
(1) No person shall be eligible for appointment to, or be retained in, the Police Force
if—
(a)

without the consent of the Commissioner of Police he or she carries on any
business or holds any other office or employment for hire or gain; or

(b)

he or she holds, or his wife or her husband, or any of his family living with
him or her holds, any licence granted under the Liquor Dealers’ Licences
Act, Chapter 174, the Sale of Produce Act, Chapter 296, or any Act
regulating places of public entertainment, or if he or she has any pecuniary
interest in such licence; or

(c)

without the consent of the Commissioner of Police, he or she resides at any
premises where his wife or her husband or any member of his or her family
keeps a shop or carries on any business; or

(d)

his or her wife, without the consent of the Commissioner of Police, keeps a
shop or carries on any like business in the section to which he or she has
been assigned for duty:

Provided that in any case of refusal of consent by the Commissioner of Police there
shall be right of appeal to the Governor-General whose decision shall be final.
(2) For the purpose of this Regulation, the expression “member of his or her family”
shall include parent, son or daughter, brother or sister and any female with whom he or
she may live as man and wife.

### 7.

Debts
No member of the Force shall contract a debt which he or she is unable to discharge.
No senior officer shall borrow money from anyone below him or her in rank nor traffic
with him or her. No member of the Force shall lend money to a senior.

### 8.

Re-employment
Every constable shall enrol for a period of three years, at the end of which period he or
she shall have the option of re-enrolling for a further period of three years.

### 9.

Period of Probation
For the first six months of his or her original enrolment a constable is deemed to be on
probation, and he or she may be dismissed by the Commissioner of Police during that
period should he or she be found wanting in intelligence or otherwise unfit for service in
the Police Force.

### 10.

Application
Personnel who apply—
(a)

to join the Police Force of another Government;

(b)

to join any of Her Majesty’s Forces; or

(c)

for other Government employment,

must submit their applications through the Commissioner of Police. It will be a
disciplinary offence for such application to be submitted through any other channel.

### 11.

Discharge
(1) Police officers of and below the rank of Sergeant may be discharged from the
Police Force by Order of the Commissioner of Police—
(a)

on the termination of any period of enrolment;

(b)

on certificate of the Medical Authority;

(c)

for misconduct.

(2) A discharge certificate shall be given to every such officer on his or her discharge.
(3) Such officers shall not be at liberty to withdraw themselves from the Police Force
until the expiration of the period of their enrolment unless special permission is given by
the Commissioner of Police.
All applications for employment outside the Police must be submitted through
Divisional Officers and the Commissioner of Police.

### 12.

Attestation
Every candidate on enrolment will be required to sign an attestation paper in the
prescribed form before the Commissioner of Police, binding himself or herself to serve
under certain conditions laid down, and subject to the regulations of the Police Force.

### 13.

General duties
(1) It shall be incumbent on every constable to acquire, as soon as possible after his or
her enrolment, a knowledge of the laws of Grenada, especially the provisions of the
Police Act.
(2) Whilst these laws invest a police constable with the power to deprive another
person of his or her liberty for offences, and under circumstances that are clearly defined
in the various laws, on the other hand, the law is properly zealous of the liberty of the
subject being unnecessarily interfered with. As it is of first importance that a police
constable should possess a full knowledge of the law which gives him or her the power
referred to, he or she shall exercise the powers with which he or she is vested without

abuse, but with discretion, judgement, coolness, and above all, with perfect command of
temper.
(3) The Commissioner of Police shall be kept promptly and fully informed of every
occurrence calculated to effect the discipline of the Police Force, or of any unusual
occurrence throughout the State. Although every occurrence will be reported, and when
possible, dealt with by the Commissioner of Police, they are likewise to be reported to the
next senior officer available who will, in the absence of the Commissioner of Police from
Headquarters, and, if the matter is urgent, give the necessary directions, so as to prevent
inconvenience to the public, reporting the circumstances to the Commissioner of Police in
due course.
(4) Among the important duties of a police constable shall be the following—
(a)

perfect obedience;

(b)

respectful and deferential demeanour to his or her superiors;

(c)

sober and orderly habits; abstaining from the excessive use of alcohol; and

(d)

according ready assistance to all persons needing it, cultivating the good
opinion of all classes in the community.

### 14.

Grievances
The Commissioner of Police will afford to non-commissioned officers and men, who
may desire to bring to his or her notice any grievance, an opportunity for doing so.

### 15.

Daily routine of drills, lectures, etc.
Drills, lectures and parades shall be carried out as detailed from time to time by the
Commissioner of Police. Training programmes shall be drawn up monthly and circulated
to all Stations. No deviation from them will be permitted without the prior sanction of the
Commissioner of Police.

### 16.

Non-Commissioned Officers
Non-commissioned officers should never forget the position they hold, and must do all
in their power to maintain their authority.

### 17.

Undue familiarity
Undue familiarity with constables is forbidden as tending to interfere with impartiality
in the performance of their duty.

### 18.

Knowledge of police duties
They must make themselves well acquainted with Police duties and drill.

### 19.

Appearance
They should be well dressed, smart in appearance, and in all respects examples to their
juniors.

### 20.

Reporting misconduct
They should at once report any man they may see misconducting himself or herself, or
improperly dressed, or any man leaving the station without permission.

### 21.

Disorderly conduct

Fighting or disorderly conduct on the part of the men, in or out of quarters, reflects on
the non-commissioned officers themselves, showing that they possess little or no
influence among those below them.

### 22.

Attention to recruits
They must be kind and attentive to the recruits, show them how to fit and clean their
appointments, and teach them their duties.

### 23.

Impartiality
They are cautioned to be impartial and just in their estimate of each man’s
performance of the duties entrusted to him or her.

### 24.

Correct language
They are on no account to use improper language in the presence of the men, and will
check the use of improper language on the part of the men.

### 25.

Prohibition on loans
Lending money to each other, or to the men, is strictly forbidden.

### 26.

Acceptance of drinks forbidden
Accepting drinks from the men is also strictly forbidden.

### 27.

Constables
All constables should take pride in belonging to the Force and ought at all times to do
it credit by their conduct and behaviour.

### 28.

Cleanliness
They must always be clean and leave their arms and accoutrements in proper order.

### 29.

Alert on duty
They should be alert on duty, and at all times smart and soldier-like in their
movements, and respectful in bearing and manner.

### 30.

Prevention of crime
Constables should remember that absence of crime is indicative of Police efficiency
and the prevention of crime is as important as its detection.

### 31.

Vigilance
Each individual constable should endeavour to distinguish himself or herself by such
vigilance and energy as to render it difficult for persons to commit offences.

### 32.

Protection for the public
Every member of the community has a claim to the constable’s full consideration, and
the property of the poorest individual is entitled to equal protection with that of the rich.

### 33.

Duties of the Inspector

The Inspectors are the seniors of the non-commissioned officers of the Police Force.

### 34.

Form of address
Their position being one of importance and responsibility they will be addressed by
N.C.O.s. and men by the appellation “Sir” and when they speak to N.C.O.s and men on
duty, such N.C.O.s, and men will stand at attention.

### 35.

Assistance to Commissioner
They must assist the Commissioner of Police in every possible way; be present at all
parades, being particular that punctuality is observed. They are expected to possess such
knowledge of the various duties of a police constable, as to be able to impart instructions
to members of the Force, and to correct all errors that may come under their notice.

### 36.

Duty rosters
It is their duty to see that Duty Rosters are carefully kept and the men equitably
detailed for duty daily; and all ranks are dressed at all times according to regulation. The
appearance, carriage, drill and dress of the Police on or off duty, coupled with a
knowledge of their duties as constables, is the best test of the Inspectors’ efficiency, and
they must always consider themselves on duty.

### 37.

Orderly room
They will attend at the Orderly Room of the Commissioner of Police at the hearing of
all complaints against defaulters and examination of witnesses, etc.

### 38.

Dress of prisoners, etc.
They will see that all prisoners and witnesses are at the Orderly Room at the proper
time, dressed according to Force Orders.

### 39.

Orders of Commissioner
They must be thoroughly conversant with every Order issued by the Commissioner of
Police, and it will be their special duty to see that those Orders are published to, and
obeyed by the rank and file of the Force.

### 40.

Improper language
They will check all loud, coarse and improper language on the part of the noncommissioned officers and men, and whilst avoiding undue familiarity, will associate
themselves with them to the extent necessary to enable them to obtain such knowledge of
their character and dispositions, as may prove to be of advantage to the Service; and
whilst being conciliatory, they must be firm in exacting from all a strict performance of
their duties, never overlooking any irregularity.

### 41.

Inspection of beats
They will visit and inspect the beats (in uniforms) regularly by day and night, and
enter such visits in the Station Diary.

### 42.

Checking of station books
They will check and initial all books kept in the stations of their Divisions at least
once every fortnight, except in the case of Carriacou, once each month.

### 43.

Duties of Orderly N.C.O.
The Orderly N.C.O. will inspect all parts of the barracks at Fort George daily.

### 44.

Cleanliness of barracks
He or she will see that orderlies thoroughly sweep out the barrack rooms daily, and
that the rooms are thoroughly scrubbed on the days appointed, that the shelves are dusted,
bedding neatly folded, and belts and truncheons hanging on the pegs; and that due
attention is paid to the cleanliness of the latrines and urinals. He or she will ensure that
when rifles are not in use they are secured in the room provided.

### 45.

Reserve
He or she will see that whenever the constables of the Reserve are called out, that the
barrack rooms are cleared of these men.

### 46.

Supervision
He or she will supervise the men doing fatigue duty and see that the barracks are kept
weeded and free from paper and rubbish of all sorts.

### 47.

Parades
He or she will attend all parades, and carry out punishment or extra drills when
required.

### 48.

Drills
He or she will drill the recruits when the Inspector is unable to do so.

### 49.

Prisoners in cells
He or she will be responsible for all prisoners confined in the cells and will see that
they get their rations at the proper times.

### 50.

Duties of Constables at Sally Port
The Constable on guard at the Sally Port will dress in accordance with the Regulations
in force.

### 51.

Entry of civilians
He or she will see that no unauthorised civilians enter the Fort without permission
from the Commissioner of Police.

### 52.

N.C.O.s in charge of beats
The non-commissioned officers in charge of beats will be responsible to see that the
men on beat carry out their duties satisfactorily and they will patrol the whole town.

### 53.

Inspections
They will constantly visit and inspect the men.

### 54.

Unusual occurrences
They will make enquiries and report on any unusual occurrence that comes to their
notice.

### 55.

Disturbances in town
It will be their duty to proceed at once to any part of the town where it is alleged that
any disturbance has taken or is taking place.

### 56.

No smoking on duty, etc.
Non-commissioned officers and men on beat duty are strictly forbidden to smoke or
drink and anyone doing so will be severely dealt with.

### 57.

Use of whistles
On all occasions on which it is necessary for Police to use their whistles for the
purpose of summoning assistance, a very long and a very short blast are to be blown
alternatively, in order that the signal may be recognised as that of a Police Officer.

### 58.

Beat duty
A constable on beat duty will not leave his or her beat except in cases of emergency
unless properly relieved. If circumstances require him or her so to vacate the beat
temporarily, the fact should be reported by street phone to the Police Station.

### 59.

Patrol
It will be his or her duty to patrol his or her beat at a slow pace (about two miles an
hour).

### 60.

Watch on premises
It is the constable’s duty to keep close to premises by night.

### 61.

No loitering
He or she must not loiter or stand in an idle or listless manner, but must visit all parts
of his or her beat, unless otherwise ordered.

### 62.

Inspection of doors, etc.
When going around, he or she should make a careful inspection of the doors and
windows of premises, testing all locks and bars, and should any be left insecure, he or she
is to rouse the occupants and report the same.

### 63.

Arrests
He or she may arrest (or report, as the case may be) persons offending against the
laws, and when called upon to act, he or she must do so unhesitatingly, with vigour,
energy and firmness. He or she will find, as a rule, the respectable portion of the public
willing to assist him or her in an emergency; and the uniform he or she wears will give
him or her great moral power and weight against lawless characters he or she may be
dealing with.

### 64.

Entries in note book
Every constable on patrol will have with him or her, his or her note book and pencil,
in which book he or she is to enter the names of persons he or she may arrest, or
respecting whom he or she may be called upon to report, any statement that may be made,
the place or residence of the person reported, the time and place at which the offence was
committed and all matters of importance.

### 65.

Use of truncheon
He or she should never use his or her truncheon, except in cases of extreme necessity;
for example—
(a)

to prevent the escape of a prisoner; or

(b)

when his or her (the Constable) or any other person’s life is in danger, and
then he or she should be careful that he or she strikes about the shoulders,
arms or legs and not the head.

### 66.

Use of whistle
He or she is to blow his or her whistle only when he or she requires assistance and in
cases of fire.

### 67.

Instructions for street duty
(1) When on street duty allow nothing but your duty to occupy your thoughts.
(2) Always hold yourself erect and be alert.
(3) Keep your head up and look about you and observe all that is going on.
(4) Keep your eyes and ears open.
(5) Avoid all gossiping.
(6) Salute smartly those you are expected to salute.
(7) When you see a visiting Non-commissioned Officer coming up—go to him or her
smartly and report your beat.
(8) Be civil and listen respectfully to everybody who has a complaint to make.
(9) Endeavour by your vigilance to prevent crime.
(10) When standing still always choose a point of vantage, preferably Cross Roads,
where you can see what is happening on the different roads and can also better regulate
and control traffic.
(11) Do not let people congregate in the middle of the road, on pavements or at
dangerous corners—ask them politely but firmly to move on and not obstruct the
thoroughfare.
(12) Do not allow children to run about all over the road and get in everybody’s way.
(13) If you see any children playing about in the streets during school hours find out
why they are not at school, take their names and addresses and report them.
(14) Do not allow porters and others to obstruct the thoroughfare with handcarts,
barrels, bags of flour or any other object.
(15) Remember that all vehicles must keep on the left side of the road—take name
and address of offenders and report them.
(16) Remember that all licensed liquor shops must close from 9 p.m. to 6 a.m., and all
day on Sundays and Christmas Day, Good Friday, and any day appointed for Public Fast.
(17) Look carefully at all animals in carriages and carts and report if any have sores,
taking the name and address of the owner or driver.
(18) If you see a person driving a motor car whom you reasonably suspect of not
having a licence, stop the car by putting up your right hand and ask him or her to show
you his or her licence, and if he or she has not got one or has one and refuses to show it;
take his or her name and address and number of the motor car and report him or her when
you return to the station.

(19) Report anyone you see driving a motor car at an excessive speed or in a reckless
and dangerous manner—hold up your hand and when he or she stops take full particulars.
(20) Remember that all carriages must have lights at night time.
(21) Be impartial in the discharge of your duties.
(22) Do not lose your temper no matter how much you are provoked.
(23) On no account allow any disorderly conduct or indecent language on your Beat.
(24) Make a note in your Note Book of any well-known thieves and bad characters
that may pass you, in what direction going, and the time, and report it to the beat N.C.O.,
and also to the N.C.O. in charge when you return to the station.

### 68.

Duties of N.C.O.s in charge of C.I.D.
He or she will take charge and enter in his or her property book, a correct list of all
property, weapons, watches, jewellery, and valuables of every description that may have
to be produced at trials before the Magistrate, and if the case be sent on for trial to the
High Court, all valuable articles are to be lodged in a safe place.

### 69.

Responsibility for detailing duties
He or she will be responsible for detailing the duties of all members of his or her
Department.

### 70.

Duties of detectives, etc.
Detectives are selected from among the most trustworthy, intelligent, quick and
shrewd men to be found in the Police Force, due regard being had to the knowledge they
possess of all parts of the island.

### 71.

Tracing felonies, etc.
The detectives are specially charged with the duty and responsibility of tracing
felonies and other serious offences; and the value of each man will be estimated
according to the skill he or she evinces and the integrity with which he or she discharges
his or her duty.

### 72.

Serious crimes
When a serious crime is committed and no person in particular is suspected, they
should refer to their record of old criminals, and give their special attention to the
movements and actions of these, as well as of all parties with whom the police ought to
be personally acquainted by reason of their previous misdeeds and former convictions.
Habitual criminals and other known thieves, their associates and friends, their residences
and haunts, must be under the special supervision of the detectives, and they must
frequently report to the Commissioner of Police the movements of all such persons.

### 73.

Secrecy about movements
Detectives are not to communicate their movements or proceedings except to their
superiors under whom they have directly to act.

### 74.

Entry in memorandum book
Every detective is supplied with a memorandum book in which he or she is required to
enter all suspicious circumstances and characters which may come under his or her
notice, whilst on duty, also particulars of arrests made and crime reported.

### 75.

Secrecy about detectives
Members of the Force should never speak to or appear to recognise detectives unless
the latter first address themselves to them.

### 76.

Keeping aloof
In many cases for obvious reasons, the detectives will receive instructions to keep
aloof from the constables on patrol duty.

### 77.

Visits to prisons
The detectives having obtained the usual authority, must periodically visit the prison
while the prisoners are being paraded, so as to obtain a good knowledge of their features
and personal appearances.

### 78.

Discretion at prisons
The detectives employed on this duty are not to make observations respecting
prisoners within their hearing. They must be careful to make no noise within the prison,
the officer in charge being responsible that there is no unnecessary talking and that the
prison officials are treated with the greatest courtesy and respect.

### 79.

Use of reasonable means
It cannot be too strongly impressed on the minds of the men selected as detectives
that, however anxious the Government may be to prevent the commission of crime and
other offences against the law, not even the most culpable should be proceeded against
from questionable motives, or by unjustifiable means. Should any detective therefore be
guilty of conduct derogatory to the character of an honest man, however valuable his or
her service may be, he or she will be liable to exemplary punishment, and to be dismissed
from the Force.

### 80.

Duties of N.C.O.s in charge of stations
The non-commissioned officers in charge of stations will be responsible for the
appearance of the men, and barracks, all appointments, and every article of public
property committed to their charge.

### 81.

Leaving stations
Whenever they leave their stations for any purpose whatever they are to give the
charge thereof to the next senior officer.

### 82.

Entries in station diary
They are to make true entries in the Station Diary at their stations of all their
patrolling.

### 83.

Visits, etc., to produce and liquor dealers
They are to ensure that Police visits and inspections are paid monthly to produce and
liquor dealers in their districts.

### 84.

On and off duty inspection
They are to inspect men coming off or going on duty.

### 85.

Record of offences
It is their duty to report to their Divisional Inspector records of—
(a)

all serious crimes;

(b)

all accidents;

(c)

fires; and

(d)

serious and unusual occurrences that come to their notice.

### 86.

Patrols
They are expected to patrol their districts regularly by day and by night and to visit
and inspect any constables that may be on duty at the time.

### 87.

Visits by constables
Every place in the District will be visited by a police constable not less than twice
every month by day, and not less than once every month by night. For the purpose of

### 88.

Constables in plain clothes
When a non-commissioned officer in charge of a Station finds it necessary to send a
constable on patrol in plain clothes for some special reason, whether in town or country
he or she will make an entry accordingly in the Station Diary.

### 89.

Lectures
Non-commissioned officers in charge of out-Stations will give not less than one
lecture weekly to their men, of not less than one hour, on Police work, Law, or
Magistrate’s Court Work. Subject, time and date of such lectures and names of those
attending will be entered in the Station Diary and shown on the Weekly Report.

### 90.

Squad drill
There shall be Squad Drill with rifles once a week for an hour and a note made thereof
in the Station Diary and the Weekly Report.

### 91.

Inspection of buildings, etc.
(1) Not later than a quarter to nine o’clock in the forenoon on every week day, and
not later than ten o’clock in the forenoon on Sundays, Christmas Day and Good Friday,
an inspection shall be carried out by the non-commissioned officer for the time being in
charge, at every station, Barrack and other building in which Police are quartered.
(2) The non-commissioned officer conducting the inspection shall satisfy himself or
herself that the police quarters, charge room, offices, cells, kitchens, baths, latrines,
passages, yards, out buildings and every part of the premises are thoroughly clean and in
an orderly state. He or she shall take notice to ensure that every fixture and every item of
furniture and equipment is in the proper place assigned to it and in serviceable condition.
Every defect shall be noted and the cause of damage to any article promptly investigated.
All such defects and damage shall be reported as hereinafter prescribed.

### 92.

Cleanliness of buildings

The non-commissioned officers and constables of each station and of every other
building in which Police are quartered shall keep every part of such building, its
approaches, outbuildings, passages, and yards in a clean and orderly state.

### 93.

Nails, etc., in walls
(1) Nails and hooks shall not be driven into the walls, either within or without the
buildings, without the authority of the Divisional Inspector and then only provided that
the walls are not thereby defaced or injured.
(2) Lines or wires shall not be placed across rooms or passages for clothing or other
articles to hang or dry on, nor shall such articles be displayed in any front windows.

### 94.

Animals, etc., at stations
Dogs shall not be kept at Police Stations except by permission granted in writing by
the Divisional Officer; fowls may be kept at a Station provided that such accommodation
is furnished for them as to prevent them entering the Station buildings and fouling the
approaches and entrances thereto. No other livestock shall be kept, except that
impounded, without the permission of the Commissioner of Police.

### 95.

Spitting prohibited
Spitting on the walls, floors or through doors and windows of stations, barracks and
other Police Buildings is forbidden.

### 96.

Furniture, etc.
No article in a station, barrack or other building occupied by Police shall be out of its
appointed place except when in actual use; no trunk, box, furniture or other article except
those listed on the inventories of each station, barrack or other building shall be permitted
to be brought into or to remain in any such station or other building except in a noncommissioned officer’s quarters. Constables shall be permitted to keep their plain clothes
in a room or store cupboard which shall be set aside for the purpose in every station,
barrack or other building occupied by Police.

### 97.

Arrangement of furniture and effects
(1) Bedsteads, cots and regulation boxes in the Police quarters in every station,
barrack or other building in which Police are quartered shall be so arranged as to provide
the maximum degree of comfort and convenience to the whole party occupying each
room.
(2) Each man’s bedding shall be neatly folded and arranged on the bed or cot in the
manner prescribed in Force Standing Orders. Every spare article of bedding, clean
underclothing, cleaning kits, small items of Police appointments and small articles shall
be kept in the owner’s regulation box.
(3) Every item of uniform clothing and headgear shall be kept on the shelf provided
for each man for the purpose; garments shall be neatly folded and placed on the shelf in
the manner prescribed in Force Standing Orders.
(4) Waterproof coats shall not be hung up about any room or left on verandas except
to dry, when dry they shall be placed on the coat hanger issued with each such coat and
placed in the hanging space provided for the purpose.

### 98.

Keys of public offices
The keys of the various public offices are brought to the Police Stations every night.
Each key is labelled with the name of the office to which it belongs, and hung on a peg

contained in a press in the Station, the key of which is kept on his or her person by the
non-commissioned officer in charge.
No one but the non-commissioned officer in charge of headquarters guard or N.C.O.
in charge of out-station will give out the keys.
All receipts and deliveries are to be entered in the book provided for the purpose.

### 99.

Regulation boxes
(1) Every non-commissioned officer and every constable not occupying separate
quarters shall at all times when he or she is absent from his or her quarters keep his or her
regulation box securely locked and shall retain the keys of the lock in his or her own
possession.
(2) The regulation box of every constable, and of every non-commissioned officer not
occupying separate quarters, who is absent from his or her quarters for longer than
twenty-four hours shall be placed in the storeroom.

### 100.

Charge room
(1) A Charge Room shall be provided in each Police Station for the purpose of
conducting police business with the public. Unless a separate office is provided for the
non-commissioned officer in charge, the Charge Room shall be used by him or her as an
office.
(2) The part of the Charge Room behind the bar or Charge Desk shall be reserved for
the use of the Police on duty in the Charge Room and no unauthorised person shall be
permitted to enter or remain therein.
(3) Police are forbidden to smoke in the Charge Room.
(4) Loud and vociferous talking shall be prohibited in the Charge Room. Persons who
persist in talking or otherwise behaving in a disorderly, insulting or indecent manner after
being warned to desist shall be dealt with under section 136(2) of the Criminal Code Act,

## Chapter 72A.

(5) Police shall not permit persons to loiter in the Charge Room, the entrances thereto,
or in the street or on footways near to and opposite to the entrance thereof.

### 101.

Notice-boards
All proclamations and notices required by law to be posted at Police Stations, notices
of rewards for lost property and official notices of similar import and all Government
Notices shall be posted on the Notice Board furnished at each Police Station for the
purpose. No notice, advertisement or paper of any description other than those
hereinbefore specified shall be posted on any such Notice Board or on any part of any
Station, Barrack or other Police Building.

### 102.

Inventories
(1) Inventories of every article of Government property provided and appointed to be
kept in each room of every station, barrack, quarters or other building occupied by the
Police Force shall be compiled and entered upon the form approved for the purpose,
verified and signed by the Divisional Officer or the non-commissioned officer in charge,
and shall be kept in each such room. Each such inventory form shall be placed on an
Inventory Board and hung in a convenient place in the room.
(2) Inventories shall be checked once quarterly at least by the non-commissioned
officer in charge and by the Divisional Officer who shall initial and date the inventories at
the times the surveys are carried out. Such checking shall be carried out by such officers
on separate occasions.

(3) Any damage to, or loss of, Government property shall be investigated forthwith
and a report forwarded by the non-commissioned officer in charge to the Divisional
Officer for transmission to the Commissioner of Police.

### 103.

Reporting defects
Any defect to any part of a building, to any fixture, or to any article of furniture, shall
be reported on the form approved for the purpose, and shall be forwarded through the
Divisional Officer to the Commissioner of Police.

### 104.

Petty repairs
Petty repairs and simple work that can be done by any handy man, such as cleaning
gutters, white washing, replacing window hooks, and oiling door locks, latches and
hinges, shall be done as a fatigue by the police themselves.
Barrack damages
105 (1) For the purposes of this Regulation, the expression “Barrack Damages” shall
include all damage to any part of any police building, any fixture, article of furniture, or
other Government property which, in the opinion of the Divisional Officer, has been
caused by carelessness, negligence, misuse, or wilfully, by an individual member of the
Force, or by any group of members, and is not due to fair wear and tear, climate, or
weather.
(2) The cost of making good Barrack Damages shall be deducted by the
Commissioner of Police from the pay of such individual member of the Force or group of
members as the Commissioner of Police after due enquiry, shall deem to have caused the
damage, as follows—
(a)

when the individual who committed the damage is known, or in the case of
damage in quarters occupied by one member of the Police Force, the charge
shall be defrayed by that individual or occupier;

(b)

when the individual is unknown, all the occupants of the room, station,
barrack or other police building shall be equally charged with the cost of
making good the damage.

(3) Every deduction made on account of Barrack Damages shall be entered on the
monthly pay sheet as a stoppage of pay, and the Commissioner of Police shall pay every
amount so deducted to the Permanent Secretary (Finance) obtaining a receipt for the total
amount of the deductions so made, and such amount shall be utilised in repairing or
replacing the property so damaged as aforesaid.

### 106.

Inspection by Divisional Officers
Every Divisional Officer shall for the purpose of ensuring that all the provisions of

performance of duty in the remaining portion of the Western Police Division will permit.

### 111.

Set of Laws
A complete set of all Laws and Force Orders must be kept at each station. The Laws
and Gazettes for the current year must be kept and filed.

### 112.

Force Orders
All Force Orders must be read out to the men on parade by the non-commissioned
officer in charge. Non-commissioned officers will be held responsible that every police
constable under them is acquainted with all Force Orders issued and also with any new
Laws passed that affect the Police.

### 113.

Complaints by public—procedure
All complaints and reports will be entered in the Station Diary as soon as possible
after they are made. They will also be entered in the Complaint Book, numbered
consecutively and cross-referenced to the Diary entries that apply.

### 114.

Results of court cases
The results of all cases taken to Court are to be entered in red ink in the Complaint
Book.

### 115.

Accidental fires, etc.
Accidental fires and deaths, cases of still births, etc., in which it is quite evident that
no crime is committed will be entered in Station Diary and not in Complaint Book.

### 116.

Statements
In all cases, even the most trivial, one short statement at least is to be taken. In
important cases as many statements as possible which bear on the case must be taken.

### 117.

Medical reports
A report from a Medical Officer is always to be obtained, and filed with statements in
cases of homicide, rape, serious assaults and wounding (which are taken up by the
Police).

### 118.

Case files

A case file (Form 1.17) is to be started in all important cases, whether taken to Court
or not, and all statements and other papers connected therewith are to be filed therein.

### 119.

Reasons for not taking case to court
The reason for not taking up a case or for not prosecuting is to be fully given in the
result column of Complaint Book.

### 120.

Enquiry before prosecution
Cases to be taken to Court must be thoroughly enquired into before being taken before
the Magistrate.

### 121.

Committal
When a case is committed to the High Court the File (Form 1.17) and all papers
connected therewith are to be at once forwarded under covering letter to the
Commissioner of Police.

### 122.

Appeal
This is also to be done in all cases where accused has appealed against the sentence of
the Magistrate.

### 123.

Particulars of accused persons
In all cases, names, ages, occupations, and addresses of accused persons, complainants
and witnesses are to be shown on the Case Files.

### 124.

Sleeping at police stations
No person is to be allowed to sleep at the Police Station without special permission of
the Commissioner of Police.

### 125.

Prohibition on family members at police stations
N.C.O.s and Constables are strictly forbidden to bring their wives or any relatives to
stay or sleep at a Police Station.

### 126.

Sleeping at out-stations
The N.C.O.s and men at the out-Stations are to sleep under mosquito nets if required.

### 127.

Blankets, etc.
When cells are not being used, the blankets must be folded and placed on the pillows.

### 128.

Offences against discipline
Constables, for any offence purely against discipline and not in violation of Public
Order, should not be arrested in a public manner if it can be avoided, but should be
ordered by non-commissioned officers to return to barracks; their places, if on duty, being
taken by other constables.

### 129.

Ignorance of Orders
Ignorance of published Orders cannot be admitted as an excuse for their nonobservance.

### 130.

Publication in Force Orders
Every circumstance which in any way affects a man’s pay or service is to be published
in Force Orders for the information of all concerned.

### 131.

Prohibition on gambling
All gambling is strictly prohibited, and any non-commissioned officer or constable
proved guilty of gambling will be severely punished.

### 132.

Obedience of orders
If a constable refuses to obey any order distinctly given or resists the authority of a
non-commissioned officer, he or she is to be confined without altercation, and
immediately reported to the Commissioner of Police.

### 133.

Confinement for offence
When a non-commissioned officer has occasion to confine a constable for any
offence, he or she should invariably obtain the assistance of one or more constables to
conduct the offender to the Guard Room or Barracks and should himself or herself avoid
in any way coming into contact with him or her, except under unavoidable circumstances.

### 134.

Drunkenness
When a constable is charged with drunkenness, care is to be taken that he or she is
perfectly sober before he or she is brought before an officer for the investigation of his or
her case.

### 135.

Disciplinary procedures
(1) Charges against defaulters will be made out on Charge Sheets, and presented to
defaulters to plead in writing.
(2) Defaulters may submit reports accompanying their pleas, and name witnesses to
be called in their defence.
(3) In cases of pleas of “not guilty”, no defaulter shall be punished unless he or she is
found “guilty” in Orderly Room, where witnesses will testify under oath in his or her
presence, and where he or she will be allowed the privilege of cross-examining witnesses.
(4) In cases of pleas of “guilty”, the Commissioner of Police may award punishments
without hearing evidence in Orderly Room.
(5) Defaulters in Orderly Room will be permitted to take notes of evidence adduced.

### 136.

Leave regulations
(1) Leave will be granted to members of the Police Force at the discretion of the
Commissioner of Police as follows—
(a)

Not exceeding thirty days per annum to members of the Police Force with
less than five years’ service. Such leave may be accumulated for three years
so that ninety days leave may be granted to a member of the Police Force
who has had no ordinary leave for thirty-six months.

(b)

Not exceeding thirty-six days per annum to members of the Police Force
with five years’ service and less than fifteen years’ service. Such leave may
be accumulated for four years so that one hundred and forty-four days leave
may be granted to a member of the Police Force who has had no ordinary
leave for forty-eight months.

(c)

Not exceeding forty-five days per annum to members of the Police Force
with fifteen years’ service or over. Such leave may be accumulated for four
years so that one hundred and eighty days leave may be granted to a member
of the Police Force who has had no ordinary leave for forty-eight months.

(2) Notwithstanding anything contained in this Regulation a member of the Police
Force holding the rank of Inspector may accumulate leave to a maximum of one hundred
and eighty days, whatever the length of his or her service.
(3) When sick leave granted to any member of the Police Force exceeds twenty-eight
days in any one year, the excess will be deducted from his or her ordinary leave
entitlement.
(4) All applications for leave under this Regulation shall be made in writing through
the usual channels to the Commissioner of Police.

### 137.

Address while on leave
The place where the leave will be spent must always be stated on the pass.

### 138.

Prohibition on wearing uniform on leave
Non-commissioned officers and men are forbidden to wear uniform when on leave
without first obtaining permission from the Commissioner of Police for the purpose.

### 139.

Custody of kit
On no account is the kit of a non-commissioned officer or constable proceeding on
leave to be left in the barrack-room. At St. George’s it will be handed to the Orderly
N.C.O., and at the out Stations to the non-commissioned officers in charge. It will be
carefully checked by the non-commissioned officers and at Headquarters it will be placed
in the storeroom.

### 140.

Medical and surgical
Under the relevant provisions of the Police Act, the following regulations apply—
(a)
(i) All medicines and supplies prescribed by the appropriate District
Medical Officer in the course of the treatment of members of the Force
shall be supplied from the Dispensary at the Grenada General Hospital
or the district Dispensaries free of charge. If these medicines or
supplies are not available at the Grenada General Hospital or
Dispensaries then they may be obtained from private chemists, but the
cost will be borne by Government.
(ii) The District Medical Officer shall supply only those medicines or
articles which in his or her opinion are absolutely necessary for the
treatment of the individuals concerned.
(b)

All members of the Force shall be admitted to hospital free of charge.

(c)

All members of the Force shall be provided with free dental treatment in
respect of extractions. In the case of other dental treatment fifty per cent of
the costs will be borne by Government.

(d)

District Medical Officers shall recommend all members of the Force
suffering from eye disease to be sent to a Government Eye Specialist for
treatment. Cost to be borne by Government.

(e)

All members of the Force shall on the prescription of a Government Eye
Specialist be supplied with spectacles, cost to be borne by Government, but
in the event of their requesting frames of a more expensive type than those

normally supplied by the Optician under the Government Contract they shall
be required to pay the difference in cost.

### 141.

Treatment
Medical and surgical treatment will be limited to such treatment as is available in the
State.

### 142.

Sickness at home
Whenever a police constable becomes indisposed when at home he or she will at once
proceed to the nearest police station and report to the non-commissioned officer in charge
that he or she is sick and unable to return to his or her duty. Should the constable be too
ill to move, it will be his or her duty at once to send, or cause his or her friends to send, a
written report to the non-commissioned officer of the nearest police station, who will
communicate with the nearest Government Medical Officer and request him or her to
attend such sick policeman. No certificate from any other medical practitioner will be
accepted. If the Government Medical Officer is of opinion that the health of the constable
was such that he or she could have complied with the rule and join the station, the
constable so offending will be charged.
Whenever an N.C.O. or Constable is excused duty by the District Medical Officer he
or she shall remain in barracks for the period excused unless admitted to hospital or
special permission is obtained from the Commissioner of Police.

### 143.

Venereal disease
Constables being at any time infected with venereal disease are to report themselves
sick without delay as treatment, if taken in time, may save a constable much misery. For
their own sakes, therefore, men should never hesitate for a moment to comply with this
order, and those who fail to comply, on discovery, will be treated as defaulters. This order
will be specially brought to the notice of all recruits on joining.

### 144.

Hospitalisation through misconduct
Non-commissioned officers or constables admitted into Hospital and incapacitated
through their own misconduct shall, at the discretion of the Commissioner of Police,
receive only half pay while in Hospital:
Provided that this rule shall not apply in the case of any such officer or constable
who is incapacitated by any venereal disease and who has not concealed the same.

Marking of articles
Every article of uniform clothing, equipment and necessaries shall be marked in the

### 163.

Damage to accoutrements
When any article of uniform or equipment is damaged or lost by a member of the
Police Force whilst in the execution of his or her duty it shall be replaced at the public
expense; and if damaged or lost owing to the fault or neglect of the wearer he or she shall
renew it at his or her own expense.

### 164.

Deficiency of kit
(1) Any non-commissioned officer or constable who fails to produce an article of kit
shall pay the cost of replacement, unless the deficiency is satisfactorily accounted for.
(2) No non-commissioned officer or constable shall lend to another, without
permission, any article of his or her uniform clothing or equipment.

### 165.

Kit inspections
The kit of each member of the Force below the rank of Sergeant shall be inspected
once each month and reported to the Commissioner of Police—
(i) plain clothes shall not be worn on duty except by detectives, or when
specially ordered;
(ii) non-commissioned officers or constables wearing plain clothes shall not
wear any article of uniform.

### 166.

Allowance to plain clothes Detectives
Detectives shall supply themselves with plain clothes out of the allowance granted to
them for that purpose. They shall not receive the authorised issues of uniform clothing,
equipment and necessaries, but shall be required to maintain a complete kit in serviceable
order.

### 167.

Canes
Non-commissioned officers and constables off duty in uniform shall walk out from
their Stations carrying the Regulation cane.

### 168.

Handing over of arms, etc.

When a non-commissioned officer or constable proceeds on leave or to hospital or is
suspended from duty he or she shall hand over his or her arms, equipment, accoutrements,
uniform clothing, headgear and footwear to the officer or non-commissioned officer
under whose immediate command he or she is serving, who shall be responsible for their
good order and safe custody. At St. George’s and Headquarters such articles shall be
handed over to the Orderly N.C.O. who will be responsible that such articles are handed
into the Stores and a note made of any deficiencies.

### 169.

Return of arms on discharge
On the discharge of any member of the Police Force, he or she shall return to store all
arms, accoutrements, equipment, clothing and other articles, the property of the
Government, before he or she receives his or her pay. The value of any article which he
or she may fail to produce shall be deducted from any pay due to him or her.

Mourning
Non-commissioned officers and constables in uniform when in mourning are to cover

Making good lost articles, etc.
Should any non-commissioned officer or constable lose by neglect or make away with

### 180.

Unserviceable equipment
When from any cause an article of equipment becomes unserviceable, the fact should
be brought to the notice of the Commissioner of Police, who may order a fresh article to

### 181.

Exchange of clothing, etc.
Non-commissioned officers and men are strictly forbidden to exchange amongst
themselves any of their clothing or equipment without the special authority of the
Commissioner of Police, and any one doing so will be dealt with for disobedience of
orders.

### 182.

Prohibition on issue of old clothing
No old clothing will be issued to the tailor, or to any non-commissioned officer or
constable to be used for repairing purposes, except such as will from time to time be
specially set aside for the purpose, on the authority of the Commissioner of Police.

### 183.

Report of loss or damage to arms, etc.
Non-commissioned officers and police constables must at once report in writing any
damage or loss of their arms, clothing and equipment, giving full particulars.

### 184.

Dress for court
All non-commissioned officers (below the rank of Inspector) and men (excluding
detectives of all ranks) will, when appearing in Magistrates’ Courts for any purpose
whatsoever, wear white tunics and helmets. If the weather is very stormy and wet they

tunics and caps without white covers.

### 185.

Dress for N.C.O.s

Non-commissioned officers in charge of guards and beats must invariably wear the
same uniform as the men.

### 186.

Improvements in general turn out
Non-commissioned officers in charge of stations must always do their best to improve
the general turn out of the men thus making the Force a credit to the State.

### 187.

Inspection of kit on transfer
When a constable is being transferred from one station to another, his or her kit will be
carefully inspected by the non-commissioned officer in charge of the station he or she is
leaving (at Headquarters by the Orderly N.C.O.) and on his or her arrival at the new
station by the non-commissioned officer in charge of that station. Any deficiencies found
will be at once reported to the Commissioner of Police.

### 188.

Kit boxes
Kit boxes are invariably to be kept locked. They are to be uniformly painted with the
number, rank and name.

### 189.

Wearing of belt
All non-commissioned officers of whatever rank must wear their belt when attending
Court, either to prosecute or give evidence or for any purpose whatsoever, when
appearing before the Commissioner of Police, and when out of their stations in uniform
for any purpose whatsoever.

### 190.

Dress of Station Orderly
The Station Orderly at all out-stations will be properly dressed and wear also belt and
be ready to receive any complaints or attend to anyone coming to the station.

### 191.

Lost property found in barracks
Articles of clothing or equipment found by any member of the Force lying about the
barracks unclaimed, are to be taken to the Orderly N.C.O. (if at a District Station to the
non-commissioned officer in charge) and persons who retain such articles in their own
possession are liable to severe punishment.

### 192.

Loss of articles, etc., by constables
Whenever a police constable loses any article of his or her clothing or equipment he or
she is immediately to report such loss, if at Headquarters, to the Orderly N.C.O. and if at
a District Station to the non-commissioned officer in charge of the station.

### 199.

Promotion
As every meritorious member of the Force may look forward to eventual promotion, it
is necessary to state the principles upon which it will be regulated. While due regard will
always be paid to long service and respectability of conduct, the efficiency of the Force
requires something more than this in making selections for promotion, and preference
will always be given to those who have manifested superior intelligence, zeal and energy
in the discharge of their duties. Non-commissioned officers and constables ought
therefore to understand distinctly that there will be no hesitation in selecting the
meritorious and efficient for promotion, however low down on the seniority list they
appear. Promotion examinations will be held in accordance with the Police (Promotions)
Regulations.

entering into unnecessary conversation with anyone. Non-commissioned officers and men
must always be polite and courteous in answering telephone calls.

Advance
So far as is practical, a member of the Police Force of or below the rank of Inspector,

### 207.

Communications
All constables wishing to communicate with the Commissioner of Police must do so
through the proper channel; that is, the non-commissioned officers in charge of stations
and the Divisional Inspectors.

### 208.

Complaints
If any member of the Force thinks himself or herself wronged in any matter by any
other member of the Force, other than the Commissioner of Police, he or she may
complain thereof to the Commissioner of Police, and if he or she thinks himself or herself
wronged by the Commissioner of Police, either in respect of his or her complaint not
being redressed, or in respect of any other matter, he or she may complain thereof, in
writing, to the Public Service Commission, forwarding his or her letter open, through the
Commissioner of Police.

### 209.

Complaints for private individuals
Constables are strictly forbidden to make out magisterial complaints for private
individuals.

### 210.

Parade for court evidence
Constables required to give evidence before the Magistrates or in other Courts, will be
duly warned and paraded for that duty, so as to ensure their being present when the courts
are opened.

### 211.

No talking in Court
They are not to disturb the Court by talking.

### 212.

Giving evidence
On being sworn, prior to giving evidence they should, first, state in a distinct tone of
voice their name and their number. They should be as concise as possible in relating
truthfully all they know, without exaggeration, with respect, impartiality, without fear or
reservation, and without any desire to influence the result for or against the prisoner.

### 213.

Summons as defendant
Whenever a member of the Force is summoned to appear before the Magistrate as a
defendant, a full report in writing must be submitted immediately to the Commissioner of
Police, by the Senior N.C.O. in charge of the Station.

### 214.

Reports
It is beyond the power of a non-commissioned officer or constable, without the
sanction of the Commissioner of Police, to withdraw, or cause to be withdrawn, any
complaint against an individual that may have been reported.

### 215.

Drunken persons
In cases where persons are found insensibly drunk in any of the streets or public
places, such drunken persons are to be removed for safety to the Police Station.

### 216.

Reporting destitutes
All cases of destitution in the streets coming under the notice of the Police are to be at
once reported to the visiting N.C.O. or the N.C.O. in charge of the Station.

### 217.

Bail
Non-commissioned officers in charge of Stations are empowered to take Bail (at
discretion) by recognisance, without fee or reward, from persons who are brought before
them. This power is, however, limited to certain cases and is regulated in the Police Act,
the provisions of which must be carefully studied and followed.

### 218.

Evidence
It is the duty of the Police to make full enquiry into and to collect the evidence relating
to all charges triable before the High Court, or before a Court of Summary Jurisdiction,
which have originated at their instance, and to present the cases to the Court in as full and
complete a manner as possible.

### 219.

Manner of giving evidence
The Police, when giving evidence, are to stand in an upright, respectful manner, speak
calmly and explicitly in a clear, distinct, and audible tone, so that the Court and Jury may
easily hear them, and they are to confine themselves strictly to evidence in the case before
the Court. When cross-examined by Counsel for the Defence, they are to answer with the
same readiness and civility as when giving evidence in support of the Charge.

### 220.

Truthfulness in giving evidence
The Police must give evidence with the strictest accuracy for the administration of
justice depends in a great measure on the trustworthiness of their evidence.

### 221.

Refreshing memory
Notes should be made at the time of observation of the particulars of a case to refresh
the memory, if necessary, when called on to give evidence, and the original notes should
always be kept and be ready for production in Court if asked for.

### 222.

Prohibition on suppressing or over stating cases
The Police are not to suppress or overstate the slightest circumstance, with a view to
favour the prosecution or to prejudice the prisoner.

### 223.

Accuracy of testimony
They are to endeavour, as far as possible, to feel indifferent as to the result of the case;
and they perform their duty best by stating accurately and without malice or favour, all
the particulars they know.

### 224.

Investigation of sudden deaths
N.C.O.s and men investigating cases of sudden deaths, etc., the facts of which have to
be placed before the Coroner, should not delay to write elaborate and careful reports,
before making the Coroner acquainted with the outlines of the case, for which purpose
concise and careful notes, made in a pocket book should be sufficient; should the coroner
desire a full report it will be submitted in due course. The reason for these orders is that
the delay necessitated by the writing of lengthy reports causes a considerable time to
elapse before a medical officer instructed to view the body can get on the spot during
which period the body may undergo radical changes.

### 225.

Pauper funerals

All N.C.O.s in charge of out-stations and at Headquarters before asking for funerals
for paupers, are, if the person concerned was not in receipt of poor relief, to obtain a
recommendation from a medical officer or a minister of religion, otherwise it will not be
granted. He or she will also make full enquiries as to the financial status of the relatives.

### 226.

Arrests
Mere words do not constitute an arrest, but the constable must actually touch the
person when he or she says “I arrest you”. He or she should avoid using any violence so
long as possible. When armed with a warrant to arrest, it will be his or her first duty to
keep himself or herself within the limits of the law, and to put forth every exertion in his
or her power to effect the arrest. In the event of the flight of the persons against whom the
warrants have been issued, the Constable may follow them; and should they take refuge
in their own house, he or she may, after demanding entrance, stating his or her object and
who he or she is, and being refused admission, forcibly enter such premises. He or she
will be equally safe to enter other premises in search of those “wanted”, provided he or
she is satisfied that they are therein. Should, however, the house of a stranger be forcibly
entered on mere suspicion, and the persons of whom he or she is in search are not to be
found therein, the law will not protect him or her.

### 227.

Arrest of watchman
Should a watchman or other person left in charge of premises or lands at night be
arrested by the Police, it will be the duty of the Police constable taking such person into
custody to provide for the safety of the premises or lands from which the watchman has
been taken by placing thereon, until morning, a policeman or other qualified constable.

### 228.

Executing a warrant
The constable or bailiff executing the warrant must have it in his or her possession,
and must, upon request by the person to be arrested, show him or her the warrant. He or
she must also, before making the arrest, inform the person to be arrested that he or she has
the warrant, unless there is reasonable cause for abstaining from giving the information
on the ground that it is likely to occasion escape, resistance, or rescue.

### 229.

Breaking and entering to execute warrant
Any constable or bailiff authorised to execute the warrant may, for the purpose of
executing it, either with or without assistance from any other person, break open and
enter any house, building, or enclosed place, if admittance cannot otherwise be obtained.
Before so doing he or she must, as far as practicable, notify his or her possession of the
warrant.

### 230.

Appearance in court of arrested person
Every person arrested for any cause, whether with or without a warrant, shall be
brought before the Judge, or the Magistrate, as the case may be, as soon as practicable,
and every person arrested under any of the provisions hereinbefore contained by any
person other than a constable or bailiff, shall as soon as possible be delivered into the
custody of a constable for that purpose.

### 231.

Arrested person not to be handcuffed
A person arrested, whether with or without warrant, shall not be handcuffed, or
otherwise bound, or searched, unless in case of necessity, or of reasonable apprehension
of violence, or of attempt to escape, or by Order of the Court.

### 232.

Fires
In the case of fire every constable must give the alarm to the Station, using the
quickest means possible and then proceed to the scene.

### 233.

Position of fire hydrants
Every constable on street duty must make himself or herself acquainted with the exact
position of the different fire hydrants on his or her beat.

### 234.

Dealing with fire
In dealing with a fire, constables must bear in mind the following—
(1)

Avoid, if possible, opening or breaking doors or windows, as a draught
makes a fire burn more fiercely.

(2)

Smoke is thinnest near the ground, so if you have to go into a room full of
smoke, do so on your hands and knees. A damp cloth held over your mouth
and nose will filter the smoke and enable you to breathe.

(3)

If a person’s clothing is on fire, lay him or her on the ground and cover him
or her or roll him or her up tightly in something heavy and woollen, such as
a rug, greatcoat, or tunic. Do not use anything light or flimsy as it will only
catch fire.

(4)

If curtains are on fire pull them down and crush them together.

(5)

Sand, earth, loose ashes or wet sacking put out burning oil or fat better than
water.

(6)

Constables not actually employed in working the fire appliances must do all
in their power to keep back the crowd and prevent obstruction. No one is to
be allowed to interfere in any way with the fire appliances, hydrants, etc.,
except members of the Police Force and the National Water and Sewerage
Authority. Constables must see that unauthorised persons do not enter the
premises in the confusion and should keep a sharp look out for thieves. All
property removed from buildings during a fire must at once be placed in
charge of a constable or constables.

### 237.

Riots
It is the duty of the Police on hearing of a riotous or tumultuous assembly to apply for
and to secure the attendance of a Magistrate or Justice of the Peace but if the occasion
demands immediate action and no opportunity is given for procuring such attendance,
then it is the duty of the Police to act for themselves and upon their own responsibility, in
dispersing such assembly.

### 238.

Proclamation in case of riot
(1) A copy of the Proclamation in subregulation (2) of this Regulation shall be taken
to the riot by each officer and non-commissioned officer proceeding thither.
(2) The Proclamation shall be in the following words—
“Our Sovereign Lady and Queen charges and commands all persons being
assembled, immediately to disperse themselves, and peaceably to depart to
their habitations or to their lawful business, under the pain of the penalties
contained in the Laws of Grenada. GOD SAVE THE QUEEN”.

### 239.

Firing on mobs, etc.
(1) Whenever the necessity for firing shall arise, the firing shall in order to obtain the
most effective results be directed at the leaders of a riot or the assailants of the Police.
Firing over the heads of rioters is expressly forbidden as this may give confidence to the
daring and the guilty and cause injury to innocent persons in their rear. Care shall be
taken not to fire upon persons separated from or not assisting the rioters or assailants, and
if time and circumstances permit, the rioters shall be warned that it is intended to fire
upon them unless they desist. Blank cartridges shall in no circumstances be used.
(2) Whenever members of the Police Force, acting under a Magistrate, shall receive
directions to fire, they shall not do so except by regular word of command from the senior
officer or non-commissioned officer in charge; and the officer or non-commissioned
officer shall not give word of command to fire unless distinctly required to do so by the
magistrate under whose authority he or she is acting. In cases where there is no magistrate
present and the police may be obliged to fire in their own defence, they shall not do so
without the express order of the officer or non-commissioned officer in charge. In all
cases the officer or non-commissioned officer in command shall exercise the utmost
forbearance consistent with the safety of his or her party before giving the order to fire,
and shall exercise a humane discretion as to the extent of such firing. No firing shall take
place after the necessity for it has ceased to exist, whether the magistrate orders the
cessation or not.
(3) In all cases in which the men are directed to fire, the officer or non-commissioned
officer in charge, if he or she is of the opinion that a slight effort will be sufficient to
obtain the object, shall order only one of the rifle party to fire and the fire of the others
shall be kept in reserve. He or she shall always state the number of rounds that are to be
fired.

### 240.

Unauthorised firing
If a shot shall be fired by a member of the firing party without being ordered to do so,
or after an order to cease firing shall have been given, the officer or non-commissioned
officer in command shall immediately ascertain by whom it was fired and place him or
her under arrest. A special report of the circumstances shall be made, as soon as possible
thereafter to the Minister.

### 241.

Justification
Members of the Police Force shall constantly bear in mind that however well justified
a constable may consider himself or herself to be in firing, his or her act, with all its
accompanying circumstances, whether attended by loss of life or not, may become the
subject of legal investigation, and he or she will be called upon to justify his or her action.

### 242.

Unlawful assembly
If the Commissioner of Police receives information on oath or shall have reasonable
cause to suspect that any meeting or assembly is being held for the purpose of inciting or
stirring up any persons to commit any act of insurrection or sedition, or to obtain
otherwise than by lawful means any alteration or change in the Constitution or
Government of the State as by law established or for any other seditious purpose, he or
she shall forthwith proceed to the place where the meeting is being held, accompanied if
possible by a magistrate and with such a force of Police as he or she shall deem advisable,
and shall order and direct in Her Majesty’s name, all persons he or she finds there
assembled peaceably to depart and disperse, and if any persons notwithstanding they have
been so ordered to disperse, continue together for fifteen minutes, they may be taken into
custody; and any persons resisting the Police in the execution of their duty or attempting
to do so may likewise be arrested and detained. A full report of all such meetings shall
immediately be made to the Minister.

Constitution of Police Welfare Association

### 243.

Police Welfare Association
(1) For the purpose of enabling non-commissioned officers and constables of the
Police Force to consider and bring to the notice of the Commissioner of Police all matters
affecting their general welfare and efficiency, other than questions of discipline,
promotion, transfer, leave or any other matter affecting individuals, there shall be
established an organisation to be called the Police Welfare Association (hereinafter
referred to as “The Association”) which shall act through Boards and a Central
Committee as provided in the rules.
(2) The Association shall be entirely independent of and unassociated with any body
outside the Police Force.

### 244.

Constitution
The Association shall consist of all members of the Police Force below the rank of
Assistant Commissioner, and the Association shall act through Branch Boards and a
Central Committee as hereinafter provided.

### 245.

Branch Boards
(1) There shall be three Branch Boards in the Police Force, one for Constables to be
known as the Junior Branch Board, one for Corporals, to be known as the Subordinate
Branch Board, and one for Sergeants, Cadet Officers and Inspectors, to be known as the
Senior Branch Board.
(2) The members of a Branch Board shall be elected as soon as may be after the
coming into operation of these Rules and thereafter in the month of January in each year
and shall hold office until the Branch Board for the succeeding year has been duly
constituted.
(3) The members of each Branch Board shall, by ballot, choose from among their
number a Chairperson and a Secretary.
(4) The Chairperson shall preside at all meetings of the Branch Board and shall have a
second or casting vote.
(5) Each Branch Board shall hold its first meeting (thereafter called “the annual
meeting”) as soon as may be after the coming into operation of these Rules and thereafter
in the month of February, each year. Other meetings may be held as often as
circumstances may require.
(6) The Quorum of a Branch Board at a meeting shall be three.

### 247.

Central Committee
(1) There shall be constituted a Central Committee consisting of three members from
the Junior Branch Board, two from the Subordinate Branch Board and two from the
Senior Branch Board. The members of each Branch Board shall at the annual meeting of
the Branch Board, by ballot, elect from among their number the members to be members
of the Central Committee.

(2) The Central Committee shall choose from among its members a Chairperson who
shall preside at all meetings of the Committee.
(3) The Central Committee shall choose from among its members a Treasurer and a
Secretary each of whom shall have a vote.
(4) The Central Committee shall hold at least one meeting in each year. Meetings of
the Central Committee may be held at the Headquarters of any of the Divisions and
subject to the approval of the Commissioner of Police as often as circumstances may
require.
(5) Members of the Central Committee shall hold office until the Central Committee
for the succeeding year has been duly constituted.

### 248.

Election to Branch Board
(1) In each Branch every member shall have the right to vote at the election of
members of the Branch Board.
(2) All such elections shall be by secret ballot under the supervision of a
commissioned officer of the Police Force.
(3) At any election under those Rules each voter may give one vote and no more for
each of any number of candidates not exceeding the number to be elected.

### 249.

Functions of Branch Boards and Central Committee
(1) It shall be the duty of a Branch Board to consider and make representations with
regard to all matters affecting the general welfare and efficiency of the members of the
particular Branch.
(2) It shall be the duty of the Central Committee to consider and make representations
with regard to all matters affecting the general welfare and efficiency of the Police Force
as a whole.

### 250.

Vacancies
On any vacancy occurring in a Branch Board during any year another member shall be
elected as herein provided to fill the vacancy so occurring.

### 251.

Representations
Representations may be made by resolution or petition in writing submitted by the
Secretary to the Commissioner of Police or the Minister through the proper channels.

### 252.

Meetings
Every opportunity and facility shall be given to members of the Police Force to attend
meetings of the Association and every attendance at a meeting shall, as regards
allowances and expenses, be deemed to be an occasion of Police Duty.

### 256.

Hours of duty
Police Women engaged wholly or mainly on patrol duty shall work for 7½ hours in
each 24, with one hour for refreshment. Police Women engaged on any other duties will
work 8 hours in each 24, with forty-five minutes for refreshments.

### 257.

General duties
Police Women are first and foremost Police Officers and may be employed on any
form of Police duty in which they may be useful. They should specialise, however, in
work for which they, as women, are best suited. These duties include—
(a)

uniform patrolling;

(b)

duties in connection with women and children reported missing or found
wandering, destitute or homeless;

(c)

duties in connection with girls and children who have been victims of sexual
offences or are in moral danger;

(d)

taking statements from women and children;

(e)

executing warrants upon women and girls;

(f)

escort and court duties in connection with women and girls;

(g)

searching and finger-printing women and children;

(h)

observation upon women prisoners detained in hospitals;

(i)

plain clothes duties and detective work.

### 258.

Juvenile offenders
(1) Police Women should regard themselves as specialists in all matters relating to
juvenile offenders, and should familiarise themselves with all the statutory provisions
relating to the prosecution, punishment, care and protection of children and young
persons.
(2) In this part of their work, Police Women must co-operate closely with Probation
Officers, Education Authorities and Social Welfare Workers.

### 259.

Powers of arrest
Police Women must exercise their powers of arrest with discretion when dealing with
male offenders, securing if possible, the aid of a male constable.

INCLUSION OF RANK OF ADJUNCT TO THE COMMISSIONER OF POLICE
NOTICE
[SRO 10 of 2007.]

[27th July, 2007.]
1\. Citation
This Notice may be cited as the Inclusion of Rank of Adjunct to the Commissioner of
Police Notice.
2\. Inclusion of Rank of Adjunct to the Commissioner of Police
The Governor-General in accordance with section 4 of the Police Act, Chapter 244,
which makes provision for him or her to amend the rank of the Police Force from time to
time by Notice published in the Gazette, hereby includes the rank of Adjunct to the
Commissioner of Police to be a rank which falls immediately below the rank of
Commissioner of Police.
