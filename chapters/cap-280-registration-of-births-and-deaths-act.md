## CHAPTER 280

REGISTRATION OF BIRTHS AND DEATHS ACT
An Act relating to the registration of births and deaths.
[Amended by Act No. 10 of 1967, Act No. 45 of 1991.]

[21st December, 1904.]

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Registration of Births and Deaths Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act—
“appointed fee” means the fee specified in the Second Schedule;
“general search” means a search during any number of successive hours not
exceeding six, without stating the precise object of search;
“house” includes a public institution as above defined;
“occupier” includes the keeper, matron, superintendent or other chief resident
officer of every public institution, and, where any house is let in separate apartments
or lodgings, includes the person under whom such apartments or lodgings are
immediately held, or his or her agent;
“particular search” means a search over any period not exceeding five years for any
given entry;
“prescribed” means prescribed by regulations made under section 7;
“public institution” includes any prison, lock-up, almshouse, house of refuge,
mental hospital, hospital, or any public or charitable institution;

“registrar”, used in connection with any birth or death or other event or any
register, means the registrar who is the registrar under this Act for the district in which
the birth or death or other event took place, or who keeps any register in which the
birth or death or other event is or is required to be registered, or who keeps the register
referred to;
“Registrar-General” means the Registrar-General of births and deaths in Grenada
for the time being;
“relative” includes a relative by marriage.

## PART I

Central Administration
The Registrar-General

### 3.

Appointment of Registrar-General and deputy Registrar-General
The Public Service Commission shall appoint—
(a)

a Registrar-General of births and deaths in Grenada and for the purposes of
this Act his or her office shall be called the Births and Deaths General
Register Office hereinafter referred to as the General Register Office; and

(b)

a deputy Registrar-General to act in the absence of the Registrar-General.

### 4.

Certified copies of entries to be sealed, etc.
(1) The Registrar-General shall cause to be made a seal of the General Register Office
and all certified copies of entries issued from that office shall be sealed or stamped
therewith.
(2) All copies of entries which shall be certified as true under the hand of the
Registrar-General and in Form 3 contained in the First Schedule and purporting to be
sealed or stamped with the seal of the said office (which seal it shall not be necessary to
prove) shall be admissible in all civil cases as evidence of the birth or death to which the
same relates, without any further or other proof of such entry; and no certified copy,
purporting to be issued from the said office, shall be of any force or effect which is not so
signed and sealed or stamped as aforesaid.
(3) In all criminal cases in which it may be necessary to use in evidence any entry or
entries contained in any of the said registers, such evidence shall be given by producing
to the court the original register.

### 5.

Power to alter contents of Schedules
(1) It shall be lawful for the Registrar-General, with the approval of the Minister to
alter from time to time or add to the forms and fees contained in the Schedules, regard
being always had to the objects and purposes of this Act and to rendering more effectual
the provisions of the same.
(2) Every such alteration or addition shall commence to operate upon its publication
in the Gazette.

### 6.

Powers of deputy Registrar-General in General Register Office
The deputy Registrar-General shall have all the powers and duties of the RegistrarGeneral and be subject to all the provisions of this Act save and except that he or she

shall have no power to make or declare any general rule or regulation or to rescind or
alter any order, rule or regulation made by the Registrar-General.

### 7.

Regulations for conduct of officers and offices
It shall be lawful for the Registrar-General with the approval of the Minister from time
to time to make regulations for the management, conduct and control of the General
Register Office and the District Register Offices, the discharge of the duties of RegistrarGeneral, and of registrars and their deputies hereinafter mentioned, and all other matters
concerning procedure under the provisions of this Act, and the regulations so made shall
be binding on the registrars and their deputies.

### 8.

Publishing information for ensuring due registration
The Registrar-General shall from time to time cause printed notices to be fixed or
placed on the outside of the several churches and chapel doors, or other public and
conspicuous buildings or places, within the respective registration districts, hereinafter
provided for, wherein shall be set forth the several acts to be done for the purpose of
registering any birth or death under the provisions of this Act.

### 9.

Register books and forms to be provided
(1) The Registrar-General shall cause to be provided, at the public expense, a
sufficient number of such register books and forms as may be necessary for the proper
carrying into effect of the provisions of this Act.
(2) The register books shall be of durable materials, and in them shall be printed upon
each side of every leaf the heads of information herein required to be known and
registered of births and deaths respectively; and every page of each of such books and
every place of entry therein shall be numbered progressively from the beginning to the
end of the book, beginning with the number one; and every entry shall be divided from
the following entry by a printed line.
(3) The Registrar-General shall furnish, for the use of the registrars, a sufficient
number of register books of births and of register books of deaths, and of forms for
certified copies thereof, as may from time to time be required for the purposes of this Act.

## PART II

Registration Districts

### 10.

Districts to be formed and power to alter same
(1) It shall be lawful for the Minister to divide Grenada into registration districts and
to add to or diminish the number of districts and to alter the limits thereof or any of them
by the formation of new districts or by the division of districts or otherwise and to take or
cause to be taken measures which seem to him or her necessary for carrying such
alterations into effect.
(2) The particulars of every such division when made and of any such addition,
diminution, or alteration shall be published in the Gazette and within the said several
districts as the Minister may direct.

### 11.

Appointment of district registrars
The Public Service Commission may appoint fit persons to be registrars of births and
deaths in such districts, and may remove any such persons from such office and, in every
case of vacancy therein, fill up the same. Such persons shall receive such remuneration as

the Public Service Commission with the approval of the House of Representatives may
determine.

### 12.

Certain public officers may be appointed as registrars
(1) The Public Service Commission may require any magistrate, revenue officer, or
senior police officer to act as a registrar under this Act without receiving additional
remuneration for the performance of such duty.
(2) In every case in which any such magistrate, revenue officer, or senior police
officer shall cease to hold his or her office he or she shall cease to be a registrar under this
Act and his or her successor in that office shall be and act as such registrar without the
necessity for any appointment to do so.

### 13.

Appointment of deputy registrars
(1) The Public Service Commission may appoint a fit and proper person to act as a
deputy registrar in case of the illness or unavoidable absence of a registrar, or in any
prescribed case.
(2) Every such deputy while so acting, shall have all the powers and fulfil all the
duties and be subject to all the obligations and penalties by this Act given to or imposed
on the registrar whose deputy he or she is, and, in case of a vacancy by death or otherwise
in the office of registrar, shall so act, if necessary, until such vacancy shall be filled.
(3) Every registrar shall be held civilly responsible for the acts and omissions of his or
her deputy.

### 14.

Death or resignation of registrar
If a registrar dies, or resigns, or is removed from, or otherwise ceases to hold his or her
office, all register books, documents and papers in his or her possession as such registrar,
or which may come to the hands of his or her representatives, shall be delivered, as soon
as conveniently may be, to his or her successor in office.

### 15.

Annual publication of list of registrars, their offices, etc.
The Registrar-General shall on or before the 31st January in each year cause a
statement to be printed and published in the Gazette containing the name, place of abode
and office and hours of business of every registrar.

### 16.

Registrar not to serve as juror
No registrar shall be liable to serve on a jury.

## PART III

Registration of Births and Deaths and Requirements for Burials
Births

### 17.

Information concerning birth to be given to registrar within forty-eight hours
In the case of every child born alive in Grenada it shall be the duty of the father and
mother of the child and, in default of the father and mother, of the occupier of the house
or place in which to his or her knowledge the child is born, and of each person present at
the birth and of the person having charge of the child, to give to the registrar, within
forty-eight hours after such birth, information of the particulars required to be registered

concerning such birth, and indicated in Form 1 in the First Schedule, and in the presence
of the registrar to sign the register.

### 18.

Avoidance of conviction for failure to register
No person shall be convicted for having failed to register a birth within forty-eight
hours if he or she satisfies the magistrate that he or she had reasonable grounds for not
doing so and registers it within seven days of the birth.

### 19.

Requisition by registrar of information concerning birth from person qualified
to give such information
Where a birth has, from the default of the parents or other persons required to give
information concerning it, not been duly registered, the registrar may, at any time after
the expiration of forty-eight hours from such birth, by notice in writing, require any of the
persons required by this Act to give information concerning such birth to attend
personally at the registrar’s office, or at any other place appointed by the registrar in his
or her district within such time (not less than seven days after the receipt of such notice
and not more than two months from the date of the birth) as may be specified in such
notice, and to give information to the best of such person’s knowledge and belief of the
particulars required to be registered concerning such birth, and it shall be the duty of such
person, unless the birth is registered before the expiration of the time specified in such
notice, to comply therewith.

### 20.

Information respecting finding of newborn child to be given to registrar
In case any living newborn child is found exposed, it shall be the duty of the person
finding such child, and of any person in whose charge such child may be placed, to give,
to the best of his or her knowledge and belief to the registrar, forthwith such information
of the particulars required to be registered concerning the birth of such child as the
informant possesses, and in the presence of the registrar to sign the register.

### 21.

Birth happening in a public institution
(1) When the birth of a child shall happen in any public institution, the occupier
thereof within the meaning of section 2 shall be liable in the first instance, and
notwithstanding the liability of the father and mother of such child, to report and give the
particulars of such birth to the registrar.
(2) In the event of any default on the part of any such occupier (being a public officer)
being discovered, after he or she has ceased to hold such office in any such public
institution, then and in such case proceedings under this Act in respect of such default
may be commenced and prosecuted against his or her successor after seven days’ notice
in writing has been given to such successor to have such birth registered according to law
and he or she has failed to do so.

### 22.

Duty of registrar to ascertain and register birth within three months
It shall be the duty of the registrar to inform himself or herself carefully of every birth
which happens within his or her district, and upon receiving personally from the
informant at any time within three months from the date of the birth of any child, or the
finding of any living newborn child, information of the particulars required to be
registered concerning the birth of such child, forthwith in the prescribed form and manner
to register the birth and the said particulars (if not previously registered) without fee or
reward from the informant, except that if in pursuance of a written requisition he or she
registers the same at the residence of the person making such requisition, or at the house
in which the birth took place (which he or she is thereby empowered to do), he or she
shall, unless the birth took place in a public institution, be entitled to the appointed fee.

### 23.

Registration after expiration of three months and less than twelve
months from birth
(1) After the expiration of three months next after the birth of any child, a registrar
shall not register such birth, except as in this section provided; that is to say, in case the
birth of a child has not been registered in accordance with this Act the registrar may, after
three and not later than twelve months next after the birth, by notice in writing require any
of the persons required by this Act to give information concerning the birth to attend
personally at the register office within such time (not less than seven days after the receipt
of the notice and not more than twelve months after the date of the birth) as may be
specified in the notice, and make before him or her a declaration in writing (which he or she
is hereby authorised to take) of the particulars required to be registered concerning the birth,
according to the best of the declarant’s knowledge and belief; and upon any of the said
persons attending before a registrar, whether in pursuance of a requisition or not, and
making such a declaration as aforesaid and giving information concerning the birth, the
registrar shall then register the birth according to the information contained in such
declaration.
(2) After the expiration of twelve months next after the birth of a child, that birth shall
not be registered except with the written authority of the Registrar-General for registering
the same, and except in accordance with the prescribed rules, and upon payment of the
appointed fee, and the fact of such authority having been given shall be duly entered in
the register.
(3) Every person who registers or causes to be registered the birth of any child in
contravention of this section shall be liable to a penalty of two hundred and fifty dollars.

### 24.

Inadmissibility of register in evidence
Save as in the last preceding section provided after the expiration of twelve months
following the birth of a child, it shall not be lawful for any registrar to register the birth of
such child, and no register of birth shall be admissible in evidence to prove the birth of
any child where it appears that twelve months had intervened between the day of the birth
and the day of the registration of the birth of the child, both days inclusive, and that no
such authority from the Registrar-General as is by the last preceding section required was
given.

### 25.

Registration of name or alteration of name of child
(1) If any child born in Grenada whose birth has been registered as aforesaid, shall,
after the registration of such birth, have any name given to him or her in baptism, or if the
name by which it was registered is altered, the parent or guardian of such child or other
person procuring such name to be given or altered shall, within twelve months after the
registration of birth, deliver to the registrar a certificate according to Form 4 given in the
First Schedule or to the like effect signed by such parent, guardian or person procuring
the name to be given or altered as aforesaid, and the registrar, on receipt of such
certificate shall, without any erasure of the original entry, forthwith enter in the register
the name mentioned in the certificate as having been given to the child and also the date
of the registration of such baptismal name or alteration of name.
(2) The registrar shall thereupon certify upon the said certificate the additional entry
so made and shall forthwith send the certificate, through the post or otherwise, to the
Registrar-General, together with a certified copy of the entry of birth with the name so
given or altered.
(3) Whenever any such certificate is received by the registrar after the register book
containing the original entry of birth as well as the certified copy thereof have both been
forwarded to the Registrar-General he or she shall forward the said certificate to the
Registrar-General who shall in like manner cause the same to be duly entered in the
register.

(4) If after the expiration of twelve months next after the registration of the birth of a
child the parent, guardian or such other person procuring the baptismal name or alteration
of name of such child to be given or altered as aforesaid shall request the registrar to
insert or alter such name in the register, such name shall only be so inserted or altered as
aforesaid upon the written authority of the Registrar-General in that behalf and upon
payment of the appointed fee and the fact of such authority having been given shall be
duly entered in the register.
(5) Every person required by this Act who, within the period specified in this section,
shall fail to comply with the provisions of this section shall be liable to a penalty of one
hundred dollars.

### 26.

Registering birth out of the district in case of removal
(1) A person required by this Act to give information concerning a birth who
removes, before such birth is registered, out of the district in which such birth has taken
place may, within one month after such birth, give the information by making and signing
in the presence of the registrar of the district in which he or she resides a declaration in
writing of the particulars required to be registered concerning such birth, and such
registrar shall receive and attest the declaration, and send the same to the registrar of the
district in which the birth took place; and the last mentioned registrar shall in the
prescribed manner enter the birth in the register; and the entry so made shall be deemed
for the purposes of this Act to have been signed by the person who signed the declaration.
(2) A person making a declaration in pursuance of this section in the case of any birth
shall be deemed to have complied with the provisions of this Act as to giving information
concerning that birth, and with any requisition of the registrar made under this Act within
the said three months to attend and give information concerning that birth.

### 27.

When name of father of child born out of wedlock may be registered
The registrar shall enter the name of a person as the father of a child born out of
wedlock—
(a)

at the joint request of the mother and the person acknowledging himself to
be the father of the child (in which case that person shall sign the register
together with the mother);

(b)

at the request of the mother on production of—
(i) a declaration in the prescribed form made by the mother stating that
the said person is the father of the child, and
(ii) a statutory declaration made by that person acknowledging himself to
be the father of the child;

(c)

where a declaration of paternity is made by the court under section 7 of the
Status of Children Act, 1991, Chapter 311A, declaring that a male person is
recognised in law to be the father of the child; or

(d)

where the mother is dead, then at the request of the person claiming to be the
father of the child on production of—
(i) a statutory declaration made by that person acknowledging himself to
be the father of the child, and
(ii) a statutory declaration made by some other person, who is a relative of
both the child and the deceased mother, affirming that this said other
relative has good reason to believe and showing the grounds of such
belief that the person acknowledging himself to be the father of the
child is indeed the father of the child.

Deaths

### 28.

Registration of death and cause of death
The death of every person dying in Grenada, and the cause of such death, shall be
registered by the registrar in the manner directed by this Act.

### 29.

Registration of death occurring in a house
When a person dies in a house it shall be the duty of the nearest relatives of the
deceased present at the death or in attendance during the last illness of the deceased and
in default of such relatives of every other relative of the deceased dwelling or being in the
same registrar’s district as the deceased, and in default of such relatives, of each person
present at the death, and of the occupier of the house in which, to his or her knowledge,
the death took place, and in default of the persons hereinbefore in this section mentioned,
of each inmate of such house, and of the person causing the body of the deceased person
to be buried, to give to the best of his or her knowledge and belief, to the registrar within
the five days next following the day of such death, information of the particulars required
to be registered concerning such death, and indicated in Form 2 in the First Schedule, and
in the presence of the registrar to sign the register.

### 30.

Information concerning death occurring elsewhere than in a house
When a person dies in a place which is not a house, or a dead body is found elsewhere
than in a house, it shall be the duty of every relative of such deceased person having
knowledge of any of the particulars required to be registered concerning the death, and in
default of such relative, of any person present at the death, and of every person finding,
and of any person taking charge of the body, to give to the registrar forthwith such
information of the particulars required to be registered concerning the death as the
informant possesses, and in the presence of the registrar to sign the register.

### 31.

Notice preliminary to information
If a person required to give information concerning any death sends to the registrar a
written notice of the occurrence of the death, accompanied by such medical certificate of
the cause of death as is required by this Act to be delivered to the registrar, the
information of the particulars required by this Act to be registered concerning the death
need not be given within the said five days, but shall, notwithstanding such notice, be
given within ten days after the day of the death by the person giving such notice, or by
some other person required by this Act to give the information.

### 32.

Requisition by registrar of information concerning death from qualified
informant
Where a death has, from the default of the persons required to give the information
concerning it, not been registered, or notice has not been given under the provisions of the
preceding section, the registrar may, at any time after the expiration of five days and
within six months from the day of such death or from the finding of the dead body, by
notice in writing require any person required by this Act to give information concerning
such death, to attend personally at the district register office or at any other place
appointed by the registrar within his or her district within such time (not less than seven
days after the receipt of the notice, nor more than twelve months after the death or finding
of the dead body) as may be specified in the notice, and to give the said information to the
best of the informant’s knowledge and belief, and to sign the register in the presence of
the registrar; and it shall be the duty of such person, unless the death is registered before
the expiration of the time specified in the notice to comply therewith.

### 33.

Duty of registrar to register death gratis except where required to register
elsewhere than at his or her office
(1) It shall be the duty of the registrar to inform himself or herself carefully of every
death which happens within his or her district, and upon receiving personally from the
informant at any time within twelve months after the date of any death, or of the finding
of any dead body, information of the particulars required to be registered concerning the
death from any person required by this Act to give the same, forthwith in the prescribed
form and manner to register the death and the said particulars (if not previously
registered) without fee or reward from the informant, except that if, in pursuance of a
written requisition, he or she registers the same at the residence of the person making
such requisition, or at the house where the deceased died (which he or she is hereby
empowered to do), he or she shall, unless the death took place in a public institution, be
entitled to the appointed fee.
(2) The registrar, upon being informed that a person has been found dead or that a
sudden, or suspicious, or unnatural death has taken place, shall at once report it to the
coroner, and, in default of his or her doing so, he or she shall be liable to a penalty of one
thousand dollars.

### 34.

Coroner to furnish information
(1) Where an inquest is held on a dead body, the jury shall inquire of the particulars
required to be registered concerning the death, and the coroner shall send to the registrar,
within five days after the finding of the jury is given, a certificate under his or her hand,
giving information concerning the death, and specifying any finding of the jury with
respect to the said particulars, and to the cause of death, and specifying the time and place
at which the inquest was held; and the registrar shall, if the death has not been previously
registered, register the same and enter in the register the date of the inquisition, the name
of the coroner or other officer holding the same, and the verdict of the jury; and he or she
shall forward the said certificate in due course to the Registrar-General.
(2) If the death has been previously registered, the registrar shall enter in the register
the above particulars and forward the certificate to the Registrar-General.
(3) Where an inquest is held on a dead body, no person shall, with respect to such
dead body, or death, be liable to attend on a requisition of a registrar, or be subject to any
penalty for failing to give any information in pursuance of any other provision of this Act.

### 35.

Registration of death after expiration of twelve months or after
discovery of the body
(1) After the expiration of twelve months next after a death or after the finding of a
dead body elsewhere than in a house that death shall not be registered except with the
written authority of the Registrar-General for registering the same, and except in
accordance with the prescribed rules, and upon payment of the appointed fee, and the fact
of such authority having been given shall be entered in the register.
(2) Every person who registers or causes to be registered any death in contravention
of this section shall be liable to a penalty of two hundred and fifty dollars.
Burials

### 36.

Coroner’s order and registrar’s certificate for burial
(1) Subject to the provisions of section 37 of the Coroners Act, Chapter 69, a coroner,
upon holding an inquiry or inquest upon a body, may, if he or she thinks fit, by order
under his or her hand, authorise the body to be buried before the registration of the death,
and shall give such order to the relative, friend or legal representative of the deceased
having the charge of or being responsible for the burial, or other person who causes the

body to be buried, or to the undertaker or other person having charge of the funeral; and
except upon holding an inquiry or inquest, no order, warrant, or other document for the
burial of any body shall be given by the coroner.
(2) The registrar, upon registering the death, or upon receiving a written requisition to
attend at a house to register a death, or upon receiving such written notice of the
occurrence of a death, accompanied by a medical certificate as is hereinafter provided by
this Act, shall forthwith, or as soon after as he or she is required, give, without fee or
reward, either to the person giving information concerning the death or sending the
requisition or notice to the relative, friend or legal representative of the deceased having
charge of, or being responsible for the burial, or to the undertaker or other person having
charge of the funeral, a certificate under his or her hand that he or she has registered or
received notice of the death, as the case may be.
(3) Every such order of the coroner and certificate of the registrar shall be delivered to
the person who buries or performs any funeral or religious service for the burial of the
body of the deceased; any person, to whom such order or certificate was given by the
coroner or registrar, who fails to deliver or cause to be delivered the same, shall be liable
to a penalty of one hundred dollars.
(4) It shall be the duty of every person who is about to bury or to perform a funeral or
religious service for the burial of a dead body to demand delivery of the order of the
coroner, or of the certificate of a registrar, relating to the burial or death, and where no
order or certificate under this section is delivered to him or her, such person shall, within
forty-eight hours after the burial, give notice thereof to the registrar of the district where
such burial has taken place, and in default of his or her giving such notice within the said
time he or she shall be liable to a penalty of two hundred and fifty dollars.

### 37.

Burial of child as stillborn: Penalty
(1) A person shall not wilfully bury or procure to be buried the body of any deceased
child as if it were stillborn.
(2) A person who has control over or ordinarily buries bodies in any burial ground,
shall not permit to be buried in such burial ground the body of any deceased child as if it
were stillborn, and shall not permit to be buried or bury in such burial ground any
stillborn child before there is delivered to him or her either—
(a)

a written certificate that such child was not born alive, signed by a registered
medical practitioner who was in attendance at the birth or has examined the
body of such child;

(b)

a declaration signed by some person who would, if the child had been born
alive, have been required to give information concerning the birth, to the
effect that no registered medical practitioner was present at the birth or
that his or her certificate cannot be obtained, and that the child was not born
alive; or

(c)

if there has been an inquest, an order of the coroner.

(3) A person who acts in contravention of this section shall be liable to a penalty of
two hundred and fifty dollars.

### 38.

Information as to burial of stillborn child
Every person who buries or causes to be buried the body of a stillborn child shall be
bound to inform the registrar of the district in which such burial has taken place and the
fact of such burial within five days after such burial; and a person who acts in
contravention of this section shall be liable to a penalty of two hundred and fifty dollars.
Medical Certificate of Death

### 39.

Provisions as to certificates of cause of death
With respect to certificates of the cause of death, the following provisions shall have
effect—
(1) The Registrar-General shall cause to be printed and forwarded from time to time
to every registrar to be delivered gratis to every registered medical practitioner in
Grenada forms of certificates of death in Form 6 given in the First Schedule.
(2) In the case of the death of a person who has been attended during his or her last
illness by a registered medical practitioner, that practitioner shall sign and give to some
person required by this Act to give information concerning the death a certificate in
Form 6 in the First Schedule, stating to the best of his or her knowledge and belief the
cause of death, and such person shall upon giving information concerning the death, or
giving notice of the death, deliver that certificate to the registrar, and the cause of death,
as stated in the certificate, shall be entered in the register together with the name of the
certifying medical practitioner.
(3) Where a coroner’s inquest shall have been held on the body of a deceased person,
a medical certificate need not be given to the registrar, but the certificate of the finding of
the jury, furnished by the coroner, shall be sufficient.
(4) If a person, to whom a medical certificate is given by a registered medical
practitioner in pursuance of this section, fails to deliver that certificate to the registrar, he
or she shall be liable to a penalty of one hundred dollars.
(5) Where a report of any post-mortem examination is sent to the coroner of a district
under and in pursuance of the Coroner’s Act, Chapter 69, and no inquest is subsequently
held touching the death of the person to whom such report relates, the coroner shall send a
copy of such report certified under his or her hand to the registrar of the district in which
such person died; and such report shall be treated by the registrar as equivalent to the
certificate touching the cause of death previously mentioned.
Correction of Errors

### 40.

Correction of errors in registers
With regard to the correction of errors in registers of births and deaths—
(1) No alteration in any such register shall be made except as authorised by this Act.
(2) Any clerical error which may from time to time be discovered in any such register
may be corrected by any person authorised in that behalf by the Registrar-General,
subject to the prescribed rules.
(3) An error of fact or substance in any such register may be corrected by entry in the
margin (without any alteration of the original entry) by the registrar, upon payment of the
appointed fee, and upon production to him or her by the person requiring such error to be
corrected of a statutory declaration setting forth the nature of the error and the true facts
of the case, and made by two persons required by this Act to give information concerning
the birth or death with reference to which the error has been made, or in default of such
persons then by two credible persons having knowledge of the truth of the case.
(4) When an error of fact or substance (other than an error relating to the cause of
death) occurs in the information given by a coroner’s certificate concerning a dead body
upon which he or she has held an inquest, the coroner, if satisfied by evidence on oath, or
statutory declaration that such error exists, may certify under his or her hand to the
registrar the nature of the error and the true facts of the case as ascertained by him or her
on such evidence, and the error may thereupon be corrected by the registrar in the register
by entering in the margin (without any alteration of the original entry) the facts as so
certified by the coroner.

(5) In every such case, the registrar shall sign his or her name against the alteration
and shall make and sign the like alteration in the certified copy of the register book
hereinafter prescribed:
Provided that in case such certified copy and the register book from which it has been
made shall have been already forwarded to the General Register Office, the registrar shall
forthwith make and forward thereto a memorandum of such error and of the correction to
be made, which memorandum shall be noted and kept as may be prescribed.
Miscellaneous

### 41.

Information concerning birth or death occurring on board ship
(1) In the case of any birth or death on board a vessel in any port or harbour in
Grenada, the master, chief officer or the person in command shall report the same in like
manner and subject to the like penalties in case of disobedience herein provided with
regard to the occupier of a house on shore.
(2) The master, chief officer or other person in command of any vessel shall, in like
manner and subject to the like penalties as aforesaid, report a birth or death on board any
such vessel whether moored or proceeding on any voyage coastwise or otherwise within
the territorial sea of Grenada:
Provided that nothing in this section shall be construed as absolving any of the persons
referred to in sections 17 and 30 from the duty of giving to the registrar the information
thereby required.

### 42.

When register is not evidence
Every person, by whom the information contained in any register of births or deaths
under this Act may have been given, except in cases in which such information shall have
been given by the Coroner, shall sign his or her name, description and address in the
register, and except as aforesaid, no register of birth or death according to this Act shall
be given in evidence which is not signed by some person professing to be the informant,
and to be such party as is hereby required to give information to the district registrar.

### 43.

Occupier of public institution giving information
Notwithstanding anything to the contrary hereinbefore contained, any person being a
public officer, who shall be liable under sections 21 and 30 to give information to a
registrar of a birth or death, shall do so by forwarding the same to him or her in such
form, at such time and in such manner as may be prescribed by the Registrar-General.

### 44.

Time for prosecution of an offence
A prosecution instituted against a person for the commission of any offence against
the provisions of this Act shall be commenced within three years from the time of the
offence committed, and not afterwards.

### 45.

Registration of baptism or burial by clergyman not affected by Act
Nothing in this Act shall affect any registration of baptisms or burials kept by any
clergyman or minister or officiating person, or the right of any such clergyman or
minister or other officiating person to receive any fee now usually paid for the
performance of registration of a baptism or burial.

## PART IV

Returns, etc.
Certified Copies of Registers

### 46.

Certified copies of registers to be sent quarterly to Registrar-General
(1) In the months of January, April, July and October in every year, on such days as
may from time to time be appointed by the Registrar-General, every registrar shall make
and transmit to the Registrar-General, on durable materials, a true copy certified under his
or her hand according to Form 7 given in the First Schedule of all the entries in the register
book kept by him or her of births and deaths made during the quarter of the year last
preceding the first day of the several respective months above-mentioned.
(2) If no birth or death shall have been registered since the transmission of the last
certificate, the registrar shall certify that fact, and such certificate shall be delivered to the
Registrar-General as aforesaid.
(3) The registrar shall safely keep each of the register books until it is filled, and shall
then forward it to the Registrar-General to be kept in the records of the General Register
Office:
Provided that the register books may be kept in the office of the registrar instead of
being forwarded to the Registrar-General, if the Registrar-General shall so direct.

### 47.

Duties of Registrar-General concerning certified copies sent by registrars
(1) The Registrar-General, if it appears, by interruption or the regular progression of
numbers or otherwise, that the copy of any part of a register has not been duly transmitted
to him or her shall, consistently as far as possible with the provisions of this Act, procure
the same to be amended and supplied.
(2) The certified copies so sent to the General Register Office shall be thereafter kept
in the said office in such order and manner as the Registrar-General under the direction of
the Minister may think best suited for facilitating perusal and examination of the same.
(3) The Registrar-General shall cause indexes of all the said certified copies herein
mentioned to be made and kept in the General Register Office.
(4) Every person shall be entitled, on payment of the appointed fees, to search the said
indexes between 10 a.m. and 3 p.m. of every day, except Sundays and holidays, and to
have a certified copy of any entry.

### 48.

Annual report, etc., to be furnished by Registrar-General
The Registrar-General shall once in every year, furnish to the Minister a report and a
general abstract of the births and deaths registered during the preceding twelve months in
such a form and at such date as the Minister may from time to time prescribe.

### 49.

Indexes of register books to be kept by registrars
If and as long as the Registrar-General shall direct that the register books be kept in
the offices of district registrars, every registrar shall cause indexes of the same to be made
and kept with them for reference at the times and in the manner provided in respect of the
General Register Office.

### 50.

Registrar-General to regulate permits, by registrar, for searching in registers
Every registrar shall, subject to such regulations as may from time to time be made by
the Registrar-General, allow searches to be made of any register book in his or her
keeping.

Transmission to General Register Office

### 51.

If certified copy of register is lost, registrar to make and certify a fresh copy
In case any certified copy of a register of births or deaths shall be lost in course of
transmission to the General Register Office, the registrar by whom the same may have
been forwarded shall forthwith procure fresh copies to be made and forwarded to the
General Register Office in lieu of those so lost.

### 52.

Original register and certified copy never to be transmitted at same time
(1) No original register book shall at any time be forwarded or transmitted from any
registrar to the General Register Office or otherwise at the same time or by the same
person by whom any certified copies are forwarded or transmitted.
(2) A registrar who carries forward or transmits either by post or otherwise the
original of any register at the same time as the certified copy thereof, or until the original
or such copy of the same register previously transmitted has been acknowledged to have
arrived at its place of destination, shall be liable to a penalty of two hundred and fifty
dollars.

## PART V

Offences and Procedure

### 53.

Penalty for false statement, etc.
A person who—
(a)

wilfully makes any false answer to any question put to him or her by a
registrar relating to the particulars required to be registered concerning any
birth or death, or wilfully gives to a registrar any false information
concerning any birth or death, or the cause of death;

(b)

wilfully makes any false certificate or declaration under or for the purposes
of this Act, or forges or falsifies any such certificate or declaration, or any
order under this Act, or, knowing any such certificate, declaration or order to
be false or forged, uses the same as true, or gives or sends the same as true to
any person;

(c)

wilfully makes, gives or uses any false statement or representation as to a
child born alive having been stillborn or as to the body of a deceased person or
stillborn child in any coffin, or falsely pretends that any child born alive was
stillborn; or

(d)

makes any false statement with intent to have the same entered in a register
of births or deaths,

shall for each offence be liable, on summary conviction, to a penalty of five hundred
dollars, and on conviction on indictment to a fine and to imprisonment for two years.

### 54.

Penalty for neglect of duty by registrar
Every registrar who refuses, or, without reasonable cause, omits to register any birth
or death whereof he or she has had due notice, or to make any addition to or alteration in
the register in accordance with the provisions of this Act, and every person having the
custody of any register book, or certified copy thereof, or of any part thereof, who
carelessly loses or injures or carelessly allows the same to be injured whilst in his or her
keeping, shall for every such offence be liable to a penalty of three thousand dollars.

### 55.

Penalty for withholding a register from Registrar-General
Every person who, after being required under the provisions of this Act to deliver a
register of births or deaths or copies thereof to the Registrar-General, shall refuse or
during fourteen days wilfully neglect to do so, shall for every such offence be liable to a
penalty of two hundred and fifty dollars.

### 56.

Consequences of wilfully destroying or injuring a register
Every person who shall wilfully destroy or injure, or cause to be destroyed or injured,
any register, shall be guilty of felony.

### 57.

Penalty on failure of qualified informant to notify registrar of birth, death or
burial
Every person required by this Act who, within the period specified by this Act, fails to
give notice of any birth, death or burial to the registrar of the district within which such
birth, death or burial has occurred or been performed, shall be liable to a penalty of one
hundred dollars.

### 58.

Penalty on failure or refusal of qualified informant to attend and sign register
Every person required by this Act who, within the period specified by this Act, shall
fail to attend personally at the place named by the registrar of the district within which a
birth or death has occurred and give information to such registrar of the particulars by this
Act required to be registered touching such birth or death, or refuses to sign the register in
the presence of the registrar, shall be liable to a penalty of one hundred dollars.

### 59.

Penalty on failure of qualified informant to notify registrar of the finding of a
newborn child or dead body
Every person required by this Act to give notice of finding exposed any living
newborn child or any dead body, and who does not forthwith give notice of such finding
and of the place where the same was found to the registrar shall be liable to a penalty of
one hundred dollars.

### 60.

Penalty for any other offence not hereinbefore specifically provided
If any person violates or is concerned in contravening any of the provisions of this Act
for the contravention of which no specific penalty is provided, he or she shall be liable to
a penalty of two hundred and fifty dollars.

### 61.

Prosecutions may instituted by Registrar-General
A person committing an offence under this Act may be prosecuted by the RegistrarGeneral or by any person authorised by him or her in writing in that behalf.

### 62.

Penalties to be recovered summarily, and power to award a portion thereof to
informer
Every penalty prescribed by this Act shall be recoverable on conviction of the offender
in summary proceedings, in a summary manner, before a magistrate; and the GovernorGeneral may award such proportion of the amount thereof as he or she may think proper
to the informer or other person through whose instrumentality the same has been
recovered.

### 63.

Power of magistrate to adjourn proceedings pending trial of indictable offence

Where the magistrate before whom a person is charged summarily with an offence
under this Act, which offence is also punishable on indictment, thinks that proceedings
ought to be taken against such person by indictment, he or she may again adjourn the case
to enable such proceedings to be taken.

REGISTRATION OF BIRTHS AND DEATHS (LATE REGISTRATION)
REGULATIONS
[Section 7.]

[10th November, 1956.]
1\. Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Registration of Births and Deaths (Late
Registration) Regulations.
2\. Definition
In these Regulations, “Act” means the Registration of Births and Deaths Act.
3\. Registration after expiration of twelve months
(1) After the expiration of twelve months next after the birth of any child or next after
any death or after the finding of any dead body elsewhere than in a house such birth or
death, as the case may be, shall be proved by means of a statutory declaration pursuant to
the provisions of the Oaths Act, Chapter 217, setting forth particulars relating to the birth
or death and made by two persons required by the Act to give information concerning the
birth or death or in default of such persons then by two credible persons having
knowledge of the truth of the case.
(2) The Registrar-General may require further particulars to be furnished, and upon
being satisfied of the truth of the particulars set forth in the declaration may in his or her
discretion if he or she considers it expedient to do so give written authority for registering
the birth or death, as the case may be.
