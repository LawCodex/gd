## CHAPTER 144

ILLITERATE PERSONS (PROTECTION) ACT
An Act for rendering certain formalities necessary to the validity of certain
documents executed by illiterate persons.
[1st July, 1909.]

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Illiterate Persons (Protection) Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act—
“document” means—
(a)

any deed whereby an illiterate person conveys land, or renders himself or
herself liable to any obligation;

(b)

any agreement in writing for the sale or purchase of land by an illiterate
person;

(c)

any agreement in writing made with an illiterate person for a lease;

(d)

any promise in writing by an illiterate person to answer for the debt, default
or miscarriage of another to an amount exceeding ten dollars;

(e)

any receipt or promissory note for an amount exceeding ten dollars made or
indorsed by an illiterate person;

“illiterate person” means a person who is unable to write his or her name;
“official attestor” means an official attestor under this Act.

### 3.

Official attestors, and appointment thereof
(1) The official attestors under this Act shall be the Registrar and the Deputy
Registrar of the Supreme Court, the magistrates and such of the Justices of the Peace or
other fit and proper persons as the Minister may, from time to time, appoint.

(2) The Minister may at any time revoke any such appointment.

### 4.

Invalidity of documents signed by illiterates, unless attested
(1) No document mentioned in section 2 shall be valid as against an illiterate person
unless it shall be signed by him or her in the presence of an official attestor, and attested
by such official attestor.
(2) Duty of official attestors.—The official attestor shall, before such document is
signed by the illiterate person, explain the same to him or her and shall refuse to attest the
same unless the illiterate person appears to understand the contents thereof.

### 5.

Application of Act
(1) The provisions of this Act do not apply to documents signed before the first day of
July, 1909.
(2) This Act does not apply to documents executed outside Grenada.
