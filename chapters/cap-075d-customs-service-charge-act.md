## CHAPTER 75D

CUSTOMS (SERVICE CHARGE) ACT
An Act to impose a Customs Service Charge.
[Act No. 4 of 1993 amended by Act No. 14 of 1995.]

[1st January, 1993.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Customs (Service Charge) Act.
2\. Interpretation
In this Act—
“Comptroller” means the Comptroller of Customs and Excise;
“Customs Laws” includes the Customs (Control and Management) Act,

## Chapter 75B, and any other enactment for the time being in force relating to Customs;

“goods” includes stores baggage and livestock;
“Minister” means the Minister charged with the responsibility for finance.
3\. Imposition of Customs Service Charge
Subject to section 5, there shall be levied on all goods imported under any Customs
law a charge to be known as the Customs Service Charge.

4\. Rate of Customs Service Charge
(1) The customs service charge shall be payable to the Comptroller and, subject to
section 6, shall be the equivalent of two and one half per cent of the CIF value of all
goods imported into the State under any Customs law whether or not the goods are
subject to Customs duties.
(2) All sums collected under this Act shall, as soon as practicable be paid by the
Comptroller to the Accountant General for the benefit of the general revenue of Grenada.
5\. Exempted goods
Notwithstanding the provisions of section 3, no Customs service charge shall be
payable under this Act in respect of the goods listed in the Schedule.
6\. Variation of service charge
The Cabinet may—
(a)

by Order made by SRO, vary the rate of service charge under this Act; and

(b)

upon application by an importer, vary the rate of the service charge payable
by the importer under this Act, if it is satisfied that it is just and necessary to
do so.

7\. Power to amend Schedule
The Minister may, with the approval of Cabinet, by Order made by SRO, add to,
delete or amend the provisions of the Schedule.
8\. Commencement
This Act shall be deemed to have come into force on the first day of January, 1993.

Schedule
CUSTOMS (SERVICE CHARGE) ACT
Exempted Goods
A. Goods imported by—
(1)

The Government for the public use of the State.

(2)

Public Libraries.

(3)

The Police including SSU and Coastguard.

(4)

Diplomatic Corps (Missions).

(5)

The personal and household effects including a motor car of all officers
(including Judges) in the Service of the Organisation of Eastern Caribbean
States transferred within such group who have already paid duty on such
effects in one of the Member States.

(6)

Exemptions granted under section X (Movement of Persons) of the List of
Conditional Duty Exemptions in the Fifth Schedule to the Caricom Common
External Tariff Orders 1991, SRO 8 of 1991.

(7)

Any other person, organisation or bodies approved by Cabinet.

(8)

Goods imported as direct inputs for the production, packaging of local
primary produce for export.

(9)

Goods imported as direct inputs in the manufacturing of commodities
exclusively for export.

B. Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG).
