## CHAPTER 255

PROBATE ACT
An Act to regulate the practice and procedure of the Supreme Court in its probate
jurisdiction.

[25th September, 1911.]

## PART I

Original Jurisdiction in Probate Matters

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Probate Act.

### 2.

Interpretation of Part
In this Part—
“administration” comprehends all letters of administration of the estate of deceased
persons whether with or without the will annexed and whether granted for general,
special or limited purposes;
“common form business” means the business of obtaining probate and
administration when there is no contention as to the right thereto, including the
passing of probates and administrations through the Court in contentious cases when
the contest is terminated, and all business of a non-contentious nature to be taken in
the Court in matters of testacy and intestacy, not being proceedings in any suit, and
also the business of lodging caveats against the grant of probate or administration;
“Court” means the Supreme Court in its probate jurisdiction;
“matters and causes testamentary” comprehends all matters and causes relating to
the grant and revocation of probate of wills or of administration;
“Registrar” means the Registrar of the Supreme Court.

### 3.

Rules as to exercise of jurisdiction
Subject to the provisions of the Civil Procedure Act, Chapter 55, the jurisdiction in
respect of probates and letters of administration vested in the Court by section 6 of the
West Indies Associated States Supreme Court (Grenada) Act, Chapter 336, shall be
exercised (so far as regards procedure and practice) in the manner provided by this Act or
by such rules and orders of Court as may be made pursuant to this Act, and where no
special provision is contained in this Act or in any such rules or orders of Court with
reference thereto it shall be exercised as nearly as may be in the manner provided by the
rules and orders of Court made under the provisions of the Acts of the United Kingdom
relating to probates and letters of administration.

### 4.

Order to produce any instrument purporting to be testamentary
The Court may, on motion or petition, or otherwise, in a summary way, whether any
suit or other proceeding shall or shall not be pending in the Court with respect to any
probate or administration, order any person to produce and bring into the Registrar’s
office, or otherwise as the Court may direct, any paper or writing being or purporting to
be testamentary, which may be shown to be in the possession or under the control of such
person; and if it be not shown that any such paper or writing is in the possession or under
the control of such person, but it shall appear that there are reasonable grounds for
believing that he or she has knowledge of any such paper or writing, the Court may direct
such person to attend for the purpose of being examined in open Court, or upon
interrogatories respecting the same, and such person shall be bound to answer such
questions or interrogatories, and, if so ordered, to produce and bring in such paper or
writing.

### 5.

Administration pendente lite
(1) Pending any suit touching the validity of the will of any deceased person, or for
obtaining, recalling, or revoking any probate or any grant of administration, the Court
may appoint an administrator of the estate of such deceased person; and the administrator
so appointed shall have, with respect to the personal estate, all the rights and powers of a
general administrator, other than the right of distributing the residue of such personal
estate and, with respect to the real estate, power to receive all rents and profits of such
real estate, and such powers of letting and managing such real estate, as the Court may
direct.
(2) Every such administrator shall be subject to the immediate control of the Court,
and act under its direction.

### 6.

Remuneration to administrators pendente lite
The Court may direct that administrators appointed pending suits involving matters and
causes testamentary shall receive out of the estate of the deceased such reasonable
remuneration as the Court thinks fit.

### 7.

Power as to appointment of administrator
Where a person has died or shall die wholly intestate, or leaving a will, but wit hout
having appointed an executor thereof willing and competent to take probate, or where the
executor shall at the time of the death of such person be resident abroad and it shall
appear to the Court to be necessary or convenient in any such case, by reason of the
insolvency of the estate of the deceased, or other special circumstances, to appoint some
person to be the administrator of the estate of the deceased, or any part of such estate,
other than the person who if this Act had not been passed would by law have been
entitled to a grant of administration of such estate, it shall not be obligatory upon the
Court to grant administration of the estate of such deceased person to the person who if
this Act had not been passed would by law have been entitled to a grant thereof, but it
shall be lawful for the Court, in its discretion, to appoint such person as the Court shall
think fit to be such administrator upon his or her giving such security, if any, as the Court
shall direct, and every such administration may be limited as the Court shall think fit.

### 8.

After grant of administration no person to have power to sue as executor
After any grant of administration, no person shall have power to sue or prosecute any
suit, or otherwise act as executor of the deceased, as to the estate comprised in or affected
by such grant of administration, until such administration shall have been recalled or
revoked.

### 9.

Payments under revoked probates, etc., to be valid
Where any probate or administration is revoked under this Act all payments bona fide
made to any executor or administrator, under such probate or administration, before the
revocation thereof, shall be a legal discharge to the person making the same; and the
executor or administrator who shall have acted under any such revoked probate or
administration may retain and reimburse himself or herself in respect of any payments
made by him or her which the person to whom probate or administration shall be
afterwards granted might have lawfully made.

### 10.

Persons making payment under probates to be indemnified
All persons making or permitting to be made any payment or transfer bona fide, upon
any probate or letters of administration granted in respect of the estate of any deceased
person, shall be indemnified and protected in so doing, notwithstanding any defect or

circumstance whatsoever affecting the validity of such probate or letters of
administration.

### 11.

Rights of executor to cease upon renouncing probate
Where any person renounces probate of the will of which he or she is appointed
executor or one of the executors, the rights of such person in respect of the executorship
shall wholly cease, and the representation to the testator and the administration of his or
her estate shall and may, without any further renunciation, go, devolve, and be committed
in like manner as if such person had not been appointed executor.

### 12.

Giving of bond
(1) Every person to whom a grant of administration is made shall give a bond to the
Registrar and, subject to this section, if the Registrar requires, with one or more sureties
conditioned for duly collecting, getting in, and administering the real and personal estate
of the deceased. The bond shall be in such form as the Court shall from time to time by
any general or special order direct.
(2) Such bond shall be in a penalty of double the gross value of the personal estate
and the gross annual value of the real estate of the deceased, unless the Court shall in any
case think fit to direct the same to be reduced. The Court may also direct that more bonds
than one shall be given, so as to limit the liability of any surety to such amount as the
Court shall think reasonable.
(3) The Public Trustee appointed under the Public Trustee Act, 1906, of the United
Kingdom, if administration is granted to him or her, shall not be required to give such bond,
but shall be subject to the same liabilities and duties as if he or she had given such bond.
(4) It shall not be necessary for the Attorney-General obtaining administration to the
use and benefit of Her Majesty to give such bond.

### 13.

Executor not acting or not appearing on citation
Whenever an executor appointed in a will survives the testator, but dies without
having taken probate, and whenever an executor named in a will is cited to take probate,
and does not appear to such citation, the right of such person in respect of the
executorship shall wholly cease, and the representation to the testator and the
administration of his or her estate shall and may, without any further renunciation, go,
devolve and be committed in like manner as if such person had not been appointed
executor.

### 14.

Rules and orders
(1) The Chief Justice may from time to time make rules and orders for regulating the
procedure and practice of the Court and the duties of the Registrar, and for determining
what shall be deemed non-contentious business and, generally, for the carrying into effect
of this Act.
(2) Rules and orders so made shall not come into force until approved by the Minister
and published in the Gazette.

## PART II

Probates, etc., of Small Estates

### 15.

Administration of estates not exceeding $250 in value

(1) In any case in which it appears that the property, real and personal, of or to which
a person who dies intestate is possessed or entitled does not exceed in value the sum of
two hundred and fifty dollars, an application may be made to the Registrar for the
purpose of obtaining a grant of letters of administration of the said property as a “small
estate”.
(2) Such an application may be made at any time not earlier than three months after
the death of the deceased by any person being the husband, wife, issue, father, mother, or
issue of such father or mother of the deceased.
(3) The Registrar shall investigate any such application and shall lay his or her report
thereon before a Judge who shall, if he or she is satisfied that the applicant is the person
entitled to administer the estate of the deceased, direct letters of administration to issue to
the applicant.
(4) The entire fee payable in respect of the issue of such letters of administration shall
be two dollars and fifty cents, and there shall not be required—
(a)

any proceedings under section 4; or

(b)

any bond under section 12; or

(c)

any valuation of the estate other than that in the affidavit accompanying the
application; or

(d)

any affidavit or certificate required by any Act relating to estate duty; or

(e)

any payment other than that of the sum of two dollars and fifty cents
hereinbefore referred to, whether by way of estate duty, court fees,
registration fees, stamp fees on affidavits, or otherwise.

### 16.

Probate where estate does not exceed $250 in value
(1) Probate of the will of any person dying entitled to or possessed of property, real
and personal, not exceeding two hundred and fifty dollars in value may, upon production
of such will and of an affidavit verifying the due execution of such will and of an
affidavit verifying the due execution and publication thereof, be issued to the executor
named in such will, or, in the alternative an administrator of the estate may be appointed
pursuant to the power contained in section 7.
(2) Save as in this section is specially provided, and when not inconsistent therewith,
the provisions of the last preceding section shall apply to all applications for probate and
subsequent proceedings under this section.

### 17.

Registrar to fill up papers
In all cases coming within the provisions of the last two preceding sections, it shall be
the duty of the Registrar to whom application is made, to fill up the usual papers required
by the Court to lead to a grant of probate or letters of administration, without the payment
of any fees whatever.

## PART III

Sealing of Probates, etc., granted outside Grenada

### 18.

Interpretation of Part
In this Part—
“British Court in a foreign country” means any British Court having jurisdiction
out of Her Majesty’s dominions in pursuance of an Order in Council, whether made
under any Act or otherwise;

“Court of probate” means any Court or authority, by whatever name designated,
having jurisdiction in matters of probate;
“Her Majesty’s dominions” includes any British protectorate or protected state and
any territory in respect of which a mandate on behalf of the League of Nations has
been accepted by Her Majesty;
“probate” and “letters of administration” include confirmation in Scotland, and any
instrument having in any other part of Her Majesty’s dominions the same effect which
under English law is given to probate and letters of administration respectively;
“probate duty” includes any duty payable on the value of the estate and effects for
which probate or letters of administration is or are granted.

### 19.

Sealing of probates and letters of administration granted outside Grenada
Where a Court of probate in any part of Her Majesty’s dominions, or a British Court
in a foreign country has, either before or after the passing of this Act, granted probate or
letters of administration in respect of the estate of a deceased person, the probate or letters
so granted may, on being produced to, and a copy thereof deposited with, the Supreme
Court, be sealed with the seal of that Court, and thereupon shall be of the like force and
effect, and have the same operation in Grenada as if granted by that Court.

### 20.

Conditions to be fulfilled before sealing
The Court shall, before sealing a probate or letters of administration under this Act, be
satisfied—
(a)

that probate duty has been paid in respect of so much, if any, of the estate as
is liable to probate duty in Grenada; and

(b)

in the case of letters of administration, that security has been given in a sum
sufficient in amount to cover the property, if any, in Grenada to which the
letters of administration relate; and may require such evidence, if any, as it
thinks fit as to the domicile of the deceased person.

### 21.

Security for payment of debts
The Court may also, if it thinks fit, on the application of any creditor, require, before
sealing, that adequate security be given for the payment of debts due from the estate to
creditors residing in Grenada.

### 22.

Duplicate or copy admissible
For the purposes of this Act, a duplicate of any probate or letters of administration
sealed with the seal of the court granting the same, or a copy thereof certified as correct
by or under the authority of the court granting the same, shall have the same effect as the
original.

### 23.

Rules of Court
The Chief Justice may make rules of court for regulating the procedure and practice,
including fees and costs, in the Supreme Court, on and incidental to an application for
sealing a probate or letters of administration under this Act.

[Section 14.]

### 1.

Citation
These Rules may be cited as the Small Estates (Fees) Rules.

### 2.

Waiver of fees
In the case of applications made for Letters of Administration or for Probate under
section 15 or 16 of the Act, where the deceased died entitled to or possessed of property,
real and personal, not exceeding ten dollars in value, the Court may forego the demand
for any fee or payment whatsoever.
