## CHAPTER 243

POISON SALES ACT
An Act to regulate the sale of poison.
[15th February, 1908.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Poison Sales Act.
2\. Substances in the First Schedule to be deemed poisons
In this Act, the word “poison” means any one of the substances named or described in
the First Schedule.
3\. Power of Minister to declare poisons and to amend the First Schedule
(1) The Minister may at any time hereafter declare, by Order, that any other article
not mentioned or included in the First Schedule shall be deemed to be a poison within the
meaning of this Act; and such article shall be accordingly added to that Schedule and
classified in such Part thereof as the Minister may, by such Order, direct.
(2) The Minister may, by Order, amend the First Schedule by declaring that any
article included in that Schedule shall thenceforth be excluded therefrom, or by altering or
modifying the description of any article included in that Schedule.
4\. Proprietor to be deemed seller

For the purposes of this Act the proprietor of the business in which or the person on
whose behalf any sale is made by an assistant, apprentice or employee shall be deemed to
be the seller:
Provided that nothing in this Act shall be construed as interfering with or as
affecting the liability of a person who sells drugs or poisons in contravention of the
provisions of the Licences Act, Chapter 172, or the Pharmacy Act, Chapter 241.
5\. Regulations on sale of any poison
(1) It shall be unlawful for any person to sell any poison, either by wholesale or by
retail, unless the box, bottle, vessel, wrapper or cover in which such poison is contained
be distinctly labelled with a red label bearing the name and address of the seller of the
poison, with the word “POISON” and with the name of the poison, and in the case of a
preparation which contains a poison as one of the ingredients thereof, with such
particulars as to the proportion which the poison contained in the preparation bears to the
other ingredients as may be prescribed, by Order, by the Minister.
(2) The poisons, which are mentioned or described in Part I of the First Schedule, or
which may hereafter be added thereto under section 3, shall not be sold to any person
unknown to the seller, unless introduced by some person known to the seller; and on
every sale of any such poison the seller shall, before delivery, make or cause to be made
an entry in a book to be kept for that purpose stating, in the form set forth in the Second
Schedule, the date of the sale, the name and address of the purchaser, the name and
quantity of the article sold, and the purpose for which it is stated by the purchaser to be
required, to which entry the signature of the purchaser and of the person, if any, who
introduced him or her shall be affixed:
Savings.—Provided that nothing in this subsection shall apply to sales by wholesale
to retail dealers in the ordinary course of wholesale dealing or to medicine supplied by a
medical practitioner to his or her patient or supplied by a duly registered pharmacist on
the prescription of a medical practitioner: but only if such medicine be labelled as
required by the first subsection hereof and the ingredients of such medicine be entered
with the name of the person to whom it is sold or delivered in a book to be kept by the
seller for that purpose.
6\. Restriction as to sale of arsenic
No person shall sell arsenic to any person other than to a person of full age.
7\. Provision for colouring arsenic
No person shall sell any arsenic unless the same be, before the sale thereof, mixed
with soot or indigo in the proportion of one ounce of soot or half an ounce of indigo at the
least to one pound of the arsenic, and so in proportion for any greater or less quantity:
Provided that where arsenic is stated by the purchaser to be required, not for use in
agriculture, but for some other purpose for which such admixture would according to the
representation of the purchaser render it unfit, such arsenic may be sold without such
admixture in a quantity of not less than ten pounds at any one time:
Provided also that this Act shall not extend to the sale of arsenic when the same
forms part of the ingredients of any medicine required to be made up or compounded
according to the prescription of a duly registered medical practitioner or veterinary
surgeon, or to the sale of arsenic by wholesale to retail dealers upon orders in writing in
the ordinary course of wholesale dealing.
8\.

“Arsenic” to include arsenious compounds

In the construction of the last two preceding sections, the word “arsenic” shall include
arsenious acid and the arsenites, arsenic acid and the arseniates, and all other colourless
poisonous preparations of arsenic.
9\. Penalty for selling poison otherwise than as authorised by this Act
If any person shall sell any poison save as authorised by this Act, or on any sale of
poison shall deliver the same without having made and signed the entries hereby required
on such sale or without having obtained such signature or signatures to such entries as
required by this Act, or if any person purchasing any poison shall give false information
to the person selling the same in relation to the particulars which such last person is
hereby authorised to inquire into of such purchaser, or if any person shall sign his or her
name as aforesaid as a witness to the sale of poison to a person unknown to the person so
signing as witness, every person so offending shall for such offence be liable, on
summary conviction, to a fine of five hundred dollars for his or her first offence and to a
fine of one thousand dollars for his or her second or any subsequent offence.
10\. Books to be open to inspection and penalty for obstructing inspection
The book, to be kept in the form set forth in the Second Schedule and as required by
section 5(2), shall be open at all times to the inspection of any District Medical Officer or
of the Chief of Police, or to any public officer or police officer specially authorised by
either of them to inspect the same, and if any such person is refused admittance to the
premises of the seller or is unable to obtain admittance, or inspection of the book, or to
make extracts therefrom, the seller shall be guilty of an offence and liable to the penalty
provided by section 9.

Oxalic acid.

Mercuric sulphocyanide.
