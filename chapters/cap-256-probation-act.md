## CHAPTER 256

PROBATION ACT
An Act to provide for the probation of criminal offenders.
[Act No 13 of 1960 amended by Act No. 23 of 1960, Act No. 27 of 1968.]

[28th May, 1960.]

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Probation Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
(1) In this Act—
“district” means parish, as declared and defined in the Parish Boundaries Act, also
the Carriacou area meaning the islands of Carriacou and Petit Martinique and the
neighbouring islets lying to the North of the parallel twelve degrees and twenty
minutes north latitude within the territorial sea of Grenada;
“institution” means a probation hostel, a probation home or any other institution in
which a person may be required by a probation order to reside;
“Judge” means Judge of the High Court;
“offence the sentence for which is fixed by law” means an offence for which the
High Court is required to sentence the offender to death or imprisonment for life or to
detention during Her Majesty’s pleasure;
“probation case committee” means a probation case committee appointed under
section 19(c);
“probation officer” means a person appointed to be a probation officer under
section 19; and, in relation to a probationer, means the person for the time being
responsible for his or her supervision;
“probation order” means an order under this Act placing a person under the
supervision of a probation officer;
“probation period” means the period for which a probationer is placed under
supervision by a probation order;
“probationer” means a person for the time being under supervision by virtue of a
probation order;
“sum adjudged to be paid by a conviction” includes any costs, damages or
compensation adjudged to be paid by the conviction of which the amount is
ascertained by the conviction;
“supervising court” means a magistrate’s court acting for the district for the time
being named in the probation order in which the probationer resides or will reside.
(2) For the purpose of this Act, except section 4(3), where a probation order has been
made on appeal, the order shall be deemed to have been made by the court from which
the appeal was brought.

## PART I

Probation

### 3.

Probation

Where a court by or before which a person is convicted of an offence (not being an
offence the sentence for which is fixed by law) is of opinion that having regard to the
circumstances, including the nature of the offence and the character of the offender, it is
expedient to do so, the court may, instead of sentencing him or her, make a probation
order:
Provided that, before making a probation order, the court shall explain to the offender
in ordinary language the effect of the order and that, if he or she fails in any aspect to
comply therewith or commits another offence, he or she will be liable to be sentenced for
the original offence; where the offender is fourteen years of age or over the court shall not
make a probation order unless he or she expresses his or her willingness to comply with
the requirements of the order.

### 4.

Duration and requirement of probation order
(1) A probation order shall have effect for such period not less than one year and not
more than three years from the date of the order as shall be specified therein, and shall
require the probationer to submit during that period to the supervision of a probation
officer appointed for or assigned to the district, which shall be named in the order, in
which the probationer will reside during the probation period, and shall contain such
requirements as the court considers necessary for securing the supervision of the offender,
and such additional requirements as to residence, and other matters (including submission
by the probationer to medical treatment) as the court, having regard to the circumstances
of the case, considers necessary for securing the good conduct of the offender or for
preventing a repetition of the same offence or the commission of other offences:
Provided that, without prejudice to the power of the court to make an order under
section 5(1), the payment of sums by way of damages for injury or compensation for loss
shall not be included among the requirements of a probation order.
(2) Before making a probation order containing a requirement as to residence the
court shall consider the home surroundings of the offender; and where the order requires
the offender to reside in an institution—
(a)

the institution shall be an institution which is subject to inspection by a
Ministry or Department of the Government;

(b)

the name of the institution and the period for which he or she is so required
to reside shall be specified in the order, and that period shall not extend
beyond twelve months from the date of the order;

(c)

the court shall forthwith give notice of the terms of the order to the
Governor-General.

(3) The court by which a probation order is made shall forthwith give a copy of the
order to the probationer, to the probation officer under whose supervision he or she is
placed, and to the person in charge of any institution in which the probationer is required
by order to reside; and the court shall, except where it is itself the supervising court, send
to the court for the district named in the order in which the probationer will reside during
the probation period a copy of the order, together with such documents and information
relating to the case as it considers likely to be of assistance to the supervising court.

### 5.

Damages and compensation
(1) A court, on making a probation order, may, without prejudice to its power of
awarding costs against him or her, order the offender to pay such damages for injury or
compensation for loss as the court thinks reasonable, but, in the case of an order made by
a magistrate’s court the damages and compensation together shall not exceed five hundred
dollars or such greater sum as may be allowed by any enactment other than this section.
(2) An order for payment of damages or compensation as aforesaid may be enforced
in like manner as an order for the payment of costs by the offender; and where the court,

in addition to making such an order for the payment of damages or compensation to any
person, orders the offender to pay costs to that person, the orders for the payment of
damages or compensation and for the payment of costs may be enforced as if they
constituted a single order for the payment of costs.

### 6.

Security for good behavior
A court may, on making a probation order, if it thinks it expedient for the purpose of
the reformation of the offender, allow any person who consents to do so to give security
for the good behaviour of the offender.

### 7.

Breach of requirement of probation order
(1) If at any time during the probation period it appears to a Judge or magistrate that a
probationer has failed to comply with any of the requirements of a probation order, he or
she may issue a summons requiring the probationer to appear at the place and time
specified therein or may issue a warrant for his or her arrest:
Provided that a magistrate shall not issue such a summons or warrant, except on
information in writing and on oath.
(2) A summons or warrant issued under this section shall direct the probationer to
appear or to be brought before the supervising court.
(3) If it is proved to the satisfaction of the supervising court, that the probationer has
failed to comply with any of the requirements of the probation order, the court may,
without prejudice to the continuance of the probation order, impose on him or her a fine
not exceeding five hundred dollars, or may—
(a)

if the probation order was made by a magistrate’s court, deal with the
probationer, for the offence in respect of which the probation order was
made, in any manner in which the court could deal with him or her if it had
just convicted him or her of that offence;

(b)

if the probation order was made by the High Court, commit him or her to
custody or release him or her on bail (with or without sureties) until he or
she can be brought before such court.

(4) Where the supervising court deals with the case as provided in paragraph (b) of
the last foregoing subsection then—
(a)

the court shall send to the High Court a certificate signed by the magistrate
certifying that the probationer has failed to comply with such of the
requirements of the probation order as may be specified in the certificate,
together with such other particulars of the case as may be desirable; and a
certificate purporting to be so signed shall be admissible as evidence of the
failure before the High Court;

(b)

where the probationer is brought or appears before the High Court, and it is
proved to the satisfaction of that Court that he or she has failed to comply
with any of the requirements of the probation order, that Court may deal
with him or her, for the offence in respect of which the probation order was
made, in any manner in which the court could deal with him or her if he or
she had just been convicted before that court of that offence.

(5) A fine imposed under this section in respect of a failure to comply with the
requirements of a probation order—
(a)

shall be deemed for the purpose of any enactment to be a sum adjudged to be
paid by a conviction;

shall be taken into account in fixing any subsequent sentence passed upon
the probationer under the provisions of this section or of section 8.

(6) A probationer who is required by the probation order to submit to treatment for his
or her mental condition shall not be treated for the purposes of this section as having
failed to comply with that requirement on the ground only that he or she has refused to
undergo any surgical, electrical or other treatment if, in the opinion of the court, his or her
refusal was reasonable having regard to all the circumstances.
(7) Without prejudice to the provisions of section 8, a probationer who is convicted of
an offence committed during the probation period shall not on that account be liable to be
dealt with under this section for failing to comply with any requirement of the probation
order.

### 8.

Commission of further offence
(1) If it appears to a Judge or magistrate that a probationer has been convicted by a
court in any part of Grenada of an offence committed during the probation period and has
been dealt with in respect of that offence, he or she may issue a summons requiring the
probationer to appear at any place and time specified therein or may issue a warrant for
his or her arrest:
Provided that a magistrate shall not issue such a summons or warrant, except on
information in writing and on oath.
(2) A summons or warrant issued under this section shall direct the probationer to
appear or to be brought before the court by which the probation order was made, or
before the supervising court:
Provided that if a warrant is issued requiring him or her to be brought before the High
Court, and he or she cannot forthwith be brought before that Court because that Court is
not being held, the warrant shall have effect as if it directed him or her to be brought
before a magistrate’s court for the place in the district where he or she is arrested; and the
magistrate’s court shall commit him or her to custody or release him or her on bail (with
or without sureties) until he or she can be brought or appear before the High Court.
(3) If a person in whose case a probation order has been made by the High Court is
convicted and dealt with by a magistrate’s court in respect of an offence committed
during the probation period, the magistrate’s court may commit him or her to custody or
release him or her on bail (with or without sureties) until he or she can be brought or
appear before the court by which the order was made; and if it does so the magistrate’s
court shall send to the High Court a copy of the minute or memorandum of the conviction
entered in the court register signed by the magistrate together with such other particulars
of the case as may be desirable.
(4) Where it is proved to the satisfaction of the court by which a probation order was
made, or, if the order was made by a magistrate’s court, to the satisfaction of that court or
the supervising court, that the person in whose case that order was made has been
convicted and dealt with in respect of an offence committed during the probation period,
the court may deal with him or her, for the offence for which the order was made, in any
manner in which the court could deal with him or her if he or she had just been convicted
by or before that court of that offence.
(5) If a person in whose case a probation order has been made by a magistrate’s court
is convicted before the High Court of an offence committed during the probation period,
or is dealt with by the High Court for an offence so committed in respect of which he or
she was committed for sentence to that Court, the High Court may deal with him or her,
for the offence for which the order was made, in any manner in which the magistrate’s
court could deal with him or her if it had just convicted him or her of that offence.
(6) If a person in whose case a probation order has been made by a magistrate’s court
is convicted by another magistrate’s court of an offence committed during the probation
period, that court may, with the consent of the court which made the order or of the
supervising court, deal with him or her, for the offence for which the order was made, in

any manner in which the court could deal with him or her if it had just convicted him or
her of that offence.

### 9.

Effects of probation
(1) Subject as hereinafter provided, a conviction of an offence for which an order is
made under this Part placing the offender on probation shall be deemed not to be a
conviction for any purpose other than the purposes of the proceedings in which the order
is made and of any subsequent proceedings which may be taken against the offender
under the foregoing provisions of this Act:
Provided that where an offender, being not less than seventeen years of age at the time
of his or her conviction of an offence for which he or she is placed on probation, is
subsequently sentenced under this Part for that offence, the provisions of this subsection
shall cease to apply to the conviction.
(2) Without prejudice to the foregoing provisions of this section, the conviction of an
offender who is placed on probation shall in any event be discharged for the purposes of
any enactment which imposes a disqualification or disability upon convicted persons, or
authorises or requires the imposition of any disqualification or disability.
(3) The foregoing provisions of this section shall not affect—
(a)

any right of such offender as aforesaid to appeal against his or her
conviction, or to rely thereon in bar or any subsequent proceedings for the
same offence;

(b)

the revesting or restoration of any property in consequence of the conviction
of any such offender.

## PART II

Amendment, Review and Discharge of Probation Orders

### 10.

Amendment of probation order
(1) If the supervising court is satisfied that a probationer proposes to change, or has
changed, his or her residence from the district named in the probation order to another
district, the court may, and if the application in that behalf is made by the probation
officer shall, by order amend the probation order by substituting for the district named
therein the district where the probationer proposes to reside or is residing:
Provided that if the probation order contains requirements which, in the opinion of the
court, cannot be complied with unless the probationer continues to reside in the district
named in the order, the court shall not amend the order as aforesaid unless, in accordance
with the following provisions of this Part, they cancel those requirements or substitute
therefor other requirements which can be so complied with.
(2) Where a probation order is amended under the foregoing subsection, the
supervising court shall send to the magistrate’s court for the new district named in the
order a copy of the order, together with such documents and information relating to the
case as it considers likely to be of assistance to that court.
(3) Without prejudice to the preceding provisions of this section, the supervising court
may, upon application made by the probation officer or by the probationer, by order
amend a probation order by cancelling any of the requirements thereof or by inserting
therein (either in addition to or in substitution for any such requirement) any requirement
which could be included in the order if it were then being made by that court in
accordance with the provisions of sections 3 and 4:
Provided that—

(a)

the court shall not amend a probation order by reducing the probation period,
or by extending that period beyond the end of three years from the date of
the original order;

the court shall not so amend a probation order that the probationer is thereby
required to reside in an institution, or to submit to treatment for his or her
mental condition, for any period exceeding twelve months in all.

### 11.

Review of probation order
(1) Where a probation order, whether as originally made or as amended under this
Part, requires the probationer to reside in an institution (otherwise than for the purpose of
submitting to treatment for his or her mental condition as a voluntary or resident patient)
for a period extending beyond six months from the date of the order as originally made or
of the amending order, as the case may be, the probation officer shall, as soon as may be
after the expiration of six months from that date, report to the supervising court on the
case.
(2) On receipt of any such report, the supervising court shall review the probation
order for the purpose of considering whether to cancel the requirement as to residence or
reduce the period thereof, and may, if it thinks fit, amend the order accordingly without
the necessity of any application in that behalf.

### 12.

Discharge of probation order
(1) The court by which a probation order was made may, upon application made by
the probation officer or by the probationer, discharge the order, and where the application
is made by the probation officer, the court may deal with it without summoning the
probationer.
(2) Where a probationer is sentenced under section 7 or 8 for the offence for which he
or she was placed on probation, the probation order shall cease to have effect.

### 13.

When probationer must appear
Where the supervising court proposes to amend a probation order under this Part,
otherwise than on the application of the probationer, it shall summon him or her to appear
before the court; where the probationer is fourteen years of age or over the court shall not
amend a probation order unless the probationer expresses his or her willingness to comply
with the requirements of the order as amended:
Provided that this section shall not apply to an order cancelling a requirement of the
probation order or reducing the period of any requirement or substituting a new district
for the district named in the probation order.

### 14.

Copies of amending or discharging orders
(1) On the making of an order discharging or amending a probation order, the clerk of
the court shall forthwith give copies of the discharging or amending order to the
probation officer, and the probation officer shall give a copy to the probationer and to the
person in charge of any institution in which the probationer is or was required by the
order to reside.
(2) Where an order is made under this Part amending, inserting or cancelling a
requirement that a probationer shall reside in an institution, the court shall forthwith give
notice of the terms of the order to the Governor-General.

## PART III

Arrangements for Probationers

### 15.

Supervising probation officer
(1) The probation officer who is to be responsible for the supervision of a probationer
shall be the officer for the district for the time being named in the order in which the
probationer resides, or will reside, and if that probation officer dies or is unable for any
reason to carry out his or her duties, or if the probation case committee dealing with the
case think it desirable that another officer shall take his or her place, another probation
officer shall be selected by the supervising court.
(2) The probation officer under whose supervision a woman or girl is placed shall be
a woman.

### 16.

Duty of probation officers
It shall be the duty of probation officers to supervise the probationers and other
persons placed under their supervision and to advise, assist and befriend them, to inquire,
in accordance with any directions of the court, into the circumstances or home
surroundings of any person with a view to assisting the court in determining the most
suitable method of dealing with his or her case; to advise, assist and befriend, in such
cases and in such manner as may be prescribed, persons who have been released from
custody; and to perform such other duties as may be prescribed by rules made under this
Act or as may be imposed by any enactment.

### 17.

Contributions towards institutions
Such contributions may be made out of the Consolidated Fund towards the
establishment or maintenance of institutions for the reception of probationers as the
Governor-General may approve.

### 18.

Assistance by probation case committee
A probation case committee may, in such cases and in such manner as may be
prescribed by rules made under this Act, give financial and other assistance to probationers.

### 19.

Appointments
The Governor-General on the advice of the Public Service Commission shall
appoint—
(a)

a principal probation officer, who shall organise and supervise the probation
service in accordance with rules made under this Act;

(b)

a sufficient number of probation officers, qualified by character and
experience to be probation officers, who shall perform such duties as may be
prescribed by this Act or by rules made under this Act;

(c)

a probation case committee or probation case committees which shall review
the work of probation officers in individual cases and perform such other
duties in connection with probation as may be prescribed by rules under this
Act.

### 20.

Rules
The Governor-General, on the advice of the Public Service Commission, may make
rules prescribing—
(a)

the duties of a principal probation officer;

the duties of probation officers;

(c)

the constitution and duties of a probation case committee;

(d)

the regulation, management and inspection of institutions;

(e)

the form of records to be kept under this Act;

(f)

the remuneration of any person appointed to carry out any duties under this
Act and the fees and charges to be made for any act, matter or thing under
this Act to be done or observed;

(g)

generally for carrying the purposes or provisions of this Act into effect.

PROBATION OF OFFENDERS RULES
[Section 20.]

[26th May, 1960.]

---

### 1.

Citation
These Rules may be cited as the Probation of Offenders Rules.
Appointment, Resignation and Removal

### 2.

Appointment
The appointment of probation officers for the district of a Magistrate’s Court shall be
made under the hand of the Governor-General on the advice of the Public Service
Commission.

### 3.

Probationary period
Subject to the provisions of rules 4 and 5, every probation officer shall be appointed in
the first instance for a period of one year, and if at the end of this period his or her service
has proved satisfactory, his or her appointment may be confirmed by the Public Service
Commission, and shall thereafter continue.

### 4.

Office accommodation
A probation officer shall be provided with suitable office accommodation and
supplied with such office furniture and stationery as may be necessary for the proper
performance of his or her duties.

### 5.

Resignation
A probation officer may resign his or her appointment on giving not less than one
calendar month’s notice in writing to the Public Service Commission.

### 6.

Dismissal
A probation officer may be dismissed by the Public Service Commission for
misconduct without notice. His or her appointment may be determined by not less than
one calendar month’s notice in writing, given by direction of the Public Service
Commission.
Duties

### 7.

Preliminary inquiries
A probation officer shall make such preliminary inquiries as the Court may direct in
respect of any offender in whose case the question of the making of a probation order is
under consideration, notwithstanding that no order has been made.

### 8.

Visits to probationer

(1) When any person (hereinafter referred to as the “probationer”) is placed under the
supervision of a probation officer, the probation officer shall, subject to any directions
given by the Court (whether in the probation order or otherwise) visit the probationer and
make inquiry as to his or her conduct, mode of life and employment. Such visits shall for
the first month be made (unless the Court otherwise directs) at least once a week; and
afterwards their number shall depend on the conduct and mode of life of the probationer,
but shall in all cases be at least once a month:
Provided that in the case of persons under sixteen years of age, regard shall be had to
the desirability of such visits being made at least once a fortnight during the first six
months of the order.
(2) The parent or guardian shall forthwith report to the probation officer any
misconduct on the part of a probationer under twenty-one years of age.

### 9.

Visits to schools
(1) In the case of any probationer attending school, the probation officer shall visit the
school and make inquiry of the head teacher as to the child’s attendance and progress.
(2) The head teacher shall forthwith report to the probation officer any misconduct on
the part of a probationer at school. This shall not be deemed to in any way affect the right
of the teacher to punish a probationer for any such misconduct.

### 10.

Conditions of recognisances
The probation officer shall ascertain that the probationer understands the conditions of
the recognisance, and shall by persuasion and warning endeavour to ensure his or her
observance of them. If the probationer fails to obey the conditions after due warning, the
probation officer shall report as hereinafter directed.

### 11.

Employment for probationers
The probation officer shall, as occasion may arise, advise, assist and befriend the
probationer, and when necessary endeavour to find him or her employment. For these
purposes he or she shall secure as far as possible the co-operation and assistance of social
workers and agencies.

### 12.

Mode of dress
A probation officer shall not wear any uniform or badge distinctive of his or her
office.

### 13.

Diary
(1) A probation officer shall keep a diary in which he or she shall record each day the
time of his or her arrival at, and departure from, his or her office, and the character of the
work upon which he or she has been engaged. He or she shall submit his or her diary to
the Committee hereinafter constituted at the first meeting of the Committee in each
month.
(2) In the performance of his or her duties generally a probation officer shall be
guided by a Committee consisting of not less than three nor more than five persons
appointed by the Public Service Commission, one of whom shall be the person
performing the duties of Magistrate of the Southern District who shall be Chairperson of
the Committee. The Chairperson shall have an original as well as a casting vote.
(3) The members of the Committee shall hold office during the pleasure of the Public
Service Commission and any three members thereof, including the Chairperson, shall
constitute a quorum.

(4) The Public Service Commission may appoint any person to be Chairperson of the
Committee in case of the temporary absence due to illness or otherwise of the person
performing the duties of Magistrate of the Southern District.
(5) The Committee shall meet at least once a month, and a record of the proceedings
of such meetings shall be kept by the probation officer who shall be the secretary of the
Committee.
(6) A probation officer shall carry out the instructions of the Committee, except
where such instructions are in conflict with any order of the court.

### 14.

Knowledge of Act, etc.
Every probation officer shall make himself or herself conversant with the provisions
of the Act and of these Rules.
Reports

### 15.

Observance of probationer
The probation officer shall report as to the conduct and mode of life of the probationer
and his or her observance of the conditions of his or her recognisance to such Court at
such intervals and in such a manner as the Court making the order may direct: When the
order has been made by the High Court directions for this purpose, when the Court is not
sitting, may be given by the Judge. In the absence of any directions, the probation officer
shall report in writing once a month to the Magistrate for the District to which he or she is
appointed.

### 16.

Compliance with recognisance
(1) If the probationer fails to observe any of the conditions of his or her recognisance,
the probation officer shall make a report to the Court before which the probationer is
bound by his or her recognisance to come up for judgement or sentence, if that Court be
then sitting or will sit within a reasonable time. In any other case the probation officer
shall, subject to the direction of the Court making the order, make the report to the
Magistrate for the district to which he or she is appointed.
(2) Where the probation officer is satisfied that the conduct of the probationer is such
that it is unnecessary that he or she should remain longer under supervision, he or she
shall report the same to the Court which made the order.

### 17.

Reports to Court
The reports to the Court shall be made orally or in writing as the Court may direct; in
the absence of direction they shall be in writing. They shall not, unless the Court specially
directs, be made in open Court or published.
Records

### 18.

Records of probationers
The probation officer shall keep records of all probationers placed under supervision,
and such records shall include the following particulars: name and address, sex, age,
offence, previous offences, if any, date and duration of order, conditions attached to
order, employment and, in the case of children attending school, name of school and
attendance during probation, number of visits and result.

### 19.

Results

Under the heading “Result” it shall be stated in general terms whether the period of
probation has been completed to the satisfaction of the probation officer and of the Court.
When the probationer’s behaviour has not been satisfactory, the records should include a
statement of the nature of the misconduct and of the action taken, for example, committal
to Industrial School or Prison.

### 20.

Permanent records
These records shall form part of the permanent records of the Court and shall be kept
at the Court or in such other place and in such custody as the Court may determine.
