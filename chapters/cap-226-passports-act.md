## CHAPTER 226

PASSPORTS ACT
An Act to provide for the issuing and renewal of passports, and for connected
matters.
[Amended by Act No. 52 of 1991, Act No. 15 of 1993, Act No. 6 of 2001, Act No. 6 of 2011.]

[8th August, 1980.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Passports Act.
2\. Interpretation
In this Act—
“Minister” means the Minister responsible for home affairs;
“overseas representative” means a diplomatic or consular representative of the
Government of Grenada in a foreign country;
“passport officer” means the Chief Passport Officer or a police officer designated
by the Minister pursuant to section 8.

3\. Issue and renewal of passports and grant of visas, cancellation, etc.
(1) The Minister may, in his or her discretion, issue passports in Grenada to citizens
of Grenada.
(2) Passports shall remain the property of the Government and may be cancelled or
withdrawn at any time by the Minister. A passport so cancelled or withdrawn shall
thereupon become void.
(3) The Minister may, in his or her discretion, endorse passports and grant visas on
travel documents requiring Grenadian visas.
(4) Any endorsement of a passport issued, and any visa granted, pursuant to this Act
may be cancelled by the Minister, whereupon the endorsement or visa shall be void.
4\. Power to issue and cancel permits, certificates of identity and travel documents
The Minister may, in his or her discretion, grant—
(a)

permits to leave Grenada to persons who are not citizens of Grenada; and

(b)

certificates of identity and travel documents,

in such form as may be prescribed, and may cancel permits, certificates or identity and
travel documents so issued.
5\. Powers of passport officers
Anything authorised under sections 3 and 4 to be done by the Minister may be done by
a passport officer authorised in that behalf by the Minister.
6\. Restrictions on validity
(1) The validity of a passport may be restricted to any named country or countries as
the Minister thinks fit.
(2) A passport may remain valid for a period of five years from the date of issue.
7\. Minister may authorise overseas representatives to issue passports, etc.
(1) The Minister may, from time to time, by writing under his or her hand, authorise
an overseas representative in any country to exercise in that country all or any of the
powers that the Minister is authorised by this Act to exercise in Grenada, and in very such
case the provisions of this Act shall, so far as they are applicable, apply with any
necessary modifications.
(2) In the exercise of powers pursuant to this Act, an overseas representative shall act
in accordance with such directions, general or special, as may be given to him or her by
the Minister.
(3) Any authorisation or directions under this section may be given to a specified
person or to persons of a specified class, or may be given to the holder or holders for the
time being of a specified office or appointment or class of offices or appointments.
8\. Designation of passport officers
The Minister shall designate a Chief Passport Officer and passport officers for the
purposes of this Act. Officers so designated shall be serving police officers and shall be
subject to the directions of the Minister as to the performance of their duties under this
Act.
9\. Power of the Minister to make regulations

The Minister may make regulations generally for carrying into effect the purposes of
this Act and in particular for the purpose of—
(a)

prescribing anything which under this Act is required or permitted to be
prescribed;

(b)

regulating the imposition and collection of fees for the issue of passports, the
grant of visas, permits, certificates of identity, travel documents and the
doing of any other things under this Act.

10\. Offences and penalties
(1) A person who contravenes a direction given or any restriction, condition or
requirement imposed under this Act, or contravenes any regulation made thereunder, shall
be guilty of an offence.
(2) Every person who—
(a)

alters or tampers with a passport;

(b)

is found in unlawful possession of a passport;

(c)

on finding a passport, neglects or fails, within seven days of the finding, to
send or give it to a passport officer or to the officer in charge at the nearest
police station;

(d)

by personation or false representation holds himself or herself out as being
the holder or the rightful possessor of a passport when he or she is not;

(e)

by personation or false representation procures or attempts to procure the
issue of a passport to himself or herself or to any other person;

(f)

parts with the possession of a passport to another person without lawful
authority or for an unlawful purpose;

(g)

destroys or makes away with a passport or by wilful neglect allows any
person to obtain possession of a passport;

(h)

without having actual personal knowledge of the applicant for a passport,
makes a declaration verifying the declaration of the applicant or vouching
for the fitness of the applicant to receive a passport, or makes such a
declaration merely on information obtained,

shall be guilty of an offence.
(3) A person who aids or abets any other person in the commission of an offence
against this Act, or who harbours a person whom he or she knows or has reasonable
grounds for believing to have committed an offence against this Act, shall be guilty of an
offence.
(4) Every person who is guilty of an offence under this section shall be liable, on
summary conviction, to imprisonment for one year and to a fine of four hundred dollars,
and in default of payment of any fine to imprisonment for up to one year.
10A. Time for making complaint
A complaint for an offence under this Act may be made at any time within two years
from the time when the matter of the complaint arose.

PASSPORTS (FEES) REGULATIONS
[SRO 6 of 2001 amended by SRO 7 of 2001, SRO 22 of 2001, SRO 1 of 2007.]

[2nd March, 2001.]
1\. Citation
This Regulation may be cited as the Passports (Fees) Regulations.
2\. Fees
The fees specified in the Schedule are payable to the Consolidated Fund for the
services respectively specified in the Schedule.

3\. Replacement of passport
(1) The holder of a Caribbean Community Grenada Passport which is due to expire
may apply for a new passport at any time after six months before the date of expiry, but
not earlier.
(2) The holder of a Caribbean Community Grenada Passport which has been lost or
stolen, or which is damaged so as to be unusable, may apply for a replacement passport
on furnishing evidence of the loss, theft or damage and paying the fee for a replacement
passport.
4\. Emergency passport
A person who is eligible for a Caribbean Community Grenada Passport and who
requires one to be issued in an emergency may apply to the Chief Passport Officer who
may issue a travel document valid for a single exit from and entry into Grenada.
5\. Repeal
The Passports and Visas (Fees) Regulations are repealed.
5A. Validity of Grenadian Passport
A Grenadian passport issued under these Regulations prior to 23 January, 2007, shall
continue to be valid pursuant to the provisions of these Regulations until the date of
expiry of the said passport.
