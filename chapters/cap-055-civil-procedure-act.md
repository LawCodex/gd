## CHAPTER 55

CIVIL PROCEDURE ACT
An Act relating to the practice in civil proceedings in the High Court and in the
Magistrates’ Courts.
[Amended by Act No. 25 of 1969, SI No. 2 of 1970, Act No. 5 of 1971, SI No. 1 of 1972, Act No.
15 of 1990.]

[27th November, 1882.]

## PART I

Preliminary

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Civil Procedure Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act—
“Court” includes the High Court and the Judges thereof, and in those parts of this
Act which apply to Magistrates’ Courts it includes also a magistrate’s court;

“decree holder” and “judgement creditor” include the person entitled to enforce a
decree or order;
“judgement debtor” includes the person against whom any decree or order may be
enforced;
“rules” means rules made by the Chief Justice, or by an authority that includes the
Chief Justice, for regulating the procedure and prescribing the forms to be adopted and
used in civil causes in the High Court and in the Magistrates’ Courts;
“seal” includes stamp.

### 3.

Rules
(1) The provisions of this Act may be amended, or the effect of any provision may be
modified, by rules.
(2) In all cases not expressly provided for, the practice and forms shall as nearly as
possible be in conformity with the practice for the time being in force in the High Court
of Justice in England; and the Orders and Rules of the High Court of Justice in England
shall, so far as they may be applicable and convenient, be in force in the High Court.

## PART II

### 4.

Costs of service
Whenever the service of process by the Registrar is attended with expense, he or she
shall not (except by order of the Court) be bound to effect it unless the reasonable
expenses thereof shall have been previously tendered to him or her by the party requiring
the service; and such expenses shall be costs in the cause.

### 5.

Undertaking by solicitor to appear
A solicitor not entering an appearance in pursuance of his or her written undertaking
to do so on behalf of any Defendant, shall be liable to attachment, as in case of
disobedience to an order of the Court.

### 6.

Bills and notes
The holder of a dishonoured bill or note shall have the same remedies for the recovery
of the expenses incurred in the noting of it for non-acceptance or non-payment, or
incurred otherwise by reason of the dishonour, as for the recovery of the amount of the
bill or note.

### 7.

Court may forbid certain questions
The Court may forbid any questions or inquiries which it regards as indecent or
scandalous, although such questions or inquiries may have some bearing on the questions
before the Court, unless they relate to facts in issue, or to matters necessary to be known
in order to determine whether or not the facts in issue existed.

### 8.

Questions intended to insult or annoy
The Court shall forbid any question which appears to it to be intended to insult or
annoy, or which, though proper in itself, appears to the Court needlessly offensive in
form.

### 9.

Entries in books of account

The Court may permit a party to use his or her books of account as evidence in
support of his or her claim or defence if they appear to be kept in the course of business
with a reasonable degree of regularity.

### 10.

May be obtained on interlocutory order
A mandamus or an injunction may be granted or a receiver appointed by an
interlocutory order of the Court, in all cases in which it appears to the Court to be just or
convenient that such order should be made; and any such order may be made either
unconditionally or upon such terms as the Court thinks just; and if an injunction is asked
either before, or at, or after the hearing of any cause or matter, to prevent any threatened
or apprehended waste or trespass, it may be granted if the Court thinks fit, whether the
person against whom it is sought is, or is not, in possession under any claim of title or
otherwise, or (if out of possession) does or does not claim a right to do the act sought to
be restrained under any colour of title; and whether the estates claimed by both or by
either of the parties are legal or equitable.

### 11.

Different modes of trial
Actions shall be tried and heard either before the Court or before the Court sitting with
assessors, or before the Court and a jury.

### 12.

Rights of parties to trial by jury of issues of fact
Either party may, within four days after the action has been set down for trial, by
notice in writing to be filed in Court and served on the other party, declare that he or she
desires to have the action or any issue of fact therein tried before the Court and a jury, and
he or she shall thereupon be entitled to have it so tried.

### 13.

Subject thereto, mode of trial to be in discretion of Court
Subject to the last preceding section the Court shall direct by which of the modes of
trial specified in section 11 the action shall be tried, and may at any time order that
different questions of fact arising therein be tried by different modes of trial, and that one
or more issues of fact be tried before any other or others.

### 14.

Power of Court at the hearing to order a jury
If it appears at or before the hearing of any action, or of any issue of fact therein, that
the same should be tried with a jury, the Court may make such order for the trial of the
action or issue of fact with a jury, and for the adjournment thereof in the meanwhile, as
may be necessary.

### 15.

Assessors
Trials with assessors shall take place in such manner and upon such terms as the Court
shall direct.

### 16.

Written judgement to be filed
Whenever the Court shall deliver a written judgement, the original or a copy thereof
signed by the Judge shall be filed in the suit or other proceeding.

### 17.

Payment by instalments
The Court may at any time order that any judgement debt be paid by instalments, and
may at any time rescind or vary any such order.

## PART III

Execution in General

### 18.

Writ of execution, particulars to be specified
(1) Every writ of execution shall bear date of the day on which it is issued, and shall
specify the name and place of abode or office of business of the solicitor actually suing it
out; and if no solicitor is employed to sue out the writ, then it shall specify that it has been
sued out by the Plaintiff or Defendant in person, as the case may be, and mention his or
her address.
(2) Every writ of execution against goods shall bind the property in the goods of the
execution debtor as from the time when the writ is delivered to a bailiff to be executed;
and, for the better manifestation of such time it shall be the duty of the bailiff, upon the
receipt of any such writ to endorse upon the back thereof the hour, day, month and year
when he or she received the same:
Provided that no such writ shall prejudice the title to such goods acquired by any
person in good faith and for valuable consideration before seizure by the bailiff, unless
such person had at the time when he or she acquired his or her title notice that such writ
or any other writ by virtue of which the goods of the execution debtor might be seized or
attached had been delivered to the bailiff and remained unexecuted.

### 19.

Execution may issue within six years
As between the original parties to a judgement, execution may issue at any time within
six years from the recovery of the judgement.

### 20.

Execution against sureties
Whenever a person has become liable as surety for the performance of a decree, or of
any part thereof, the decree may by leave of the Court be executed against him or her to
the extent to which he or she has rendered himself or herself liable, in the same manner as
a decree may be enforced against a Defendant.

### 21.

Costs of execution
The party enforcing a decree shall be entitled to recover the costs of execution, unless
the Court shall otherwise order in cases where costs have been needlessly incurred.

### 22.

When payment ordered in instalments
Where a decree directs payment of money by instalments, execution shall not issue
until after default in payment of some instalment; and execution or successive executions
may then issue for the whole of the money and costs then remaining unpaid.

### 23.

Judgements to be charge on land
A decree for the payment of money shall bind the land (not being a small holding as
defined by section 2 of the Land Settlement Act) of the judgement debtor within Grenada
to the extent of his or her beneficial interest therein, and the same shall be deemed to be
attached by virtue of the decree, in satisfaction thereof, from the date of the judgement,
subject to Government debts and to any prior bona fide mortgage, charge, or
encumbrance thereon. Upon the filing of the decree, as aforesaid, the Registrar shall
forthwith record it in the Decree Book.

## PART IV

Execution of Decree for Money by Seizure and Sale

### 24.

Issue of writ
When the property consists of goods in the possession of the judgement debtor, the
Registrar shall, on filing of a praecipe for that purpose, issue a writ of seizure and sale
under Form 5 of the Schedule.

### 25.

Endorsements thereon
Every writ of seizure and sale shall be endorsed with a direction to the Provost
Marshal to levy the money due and payable, and sought to be recovered under the
judgement, stating the amount together with interest thereon, if sought to be recovered, at
the rate of six per cent per annum from the time when the judgement was entered up, and
also the costs of execution.

### 26.

Mode of execution
The writ shall be executed after sunrise and before sunset, and the officer executing
the writ shall in the first instance, if practicable, seize, take and levy upon the goods of
the judgement debtor (except his or her wearing apparel and bedding, and that of his or
her family, and the tools and implements of his or her trade to the value of twenty-five
dollars or such greater value as may be specified by the written law in any particular
case), and shall also seize and take any money or bank notes, and cheques, bills of
exchange, promissory notes, bonds, specialties, or guarantees for money belonging to the
judgement debtor, and shall hold the same as a security for the amount directed to be
levied by the execution, or so much thereof as shall not have been otherwise levied or
raised for the benefit of the judgement debtor; and the decree-holder may sue in the name
of the judgement debtor, or in the name of any person in whose name the judgement
debtor might have sued, for the recovery of the sums secured or made payable thereby,
when the time of payment thereof shall have arrived.

### 27.

Appraisement and sale
No goods and chattels taken in execution shall be sold until the expiration of seven
days at least next following the day on which they were taken, unless they are of a
perishable nature, or upon the request in writing of the party whose goods have been
taken; and, until sale, the goods shall be deposited by the Registrar in some fit place, or
they may remain in the custody of any fit person whom he or she shall put in possession
thereof.

### 28.

Claims to property seized
Where any property is seized in execution which is claimed by any person other than
the judgement debtor, the claim may be determined by the Court in a summary way, upon
an interpleader summons taken out by the Claimant against the party prosecuting the
decree or order, or by the Registrar against the Claimant:
Provided always that the Court may on the hearing of the summons make such
order for the trial and determination of the rights of the parties as it thinks expedient, and
for the custody in the meanwhile of the property in dispute, and the costs.

### 29.

Proceeds of execution to be paid out
All monies received by the Registrar under a writ of seizure and sale, shall be paid out
by him or her without any order for the purpose.

## PART V

Execution of Decree for Money by Attachment of Debts and other Property

### 30.

Debts and property in custody of third parties, writ of attachment
Where the judgement debtor is beneficially interested in any monies, securities for
money, goods, chattels or other property whatsoever, other than land or any interest
therein, in the custody or under the control of any other person within Grenada, or where
such other person (hereinafter called “the garnishee”) is indebted to the Defendant, the
Registrar shall, on the filing of an affidavit to that effect, and the filing of a praecipe for
that purpose, issue a writ of attachment which shall bind the property of the judgement
debtor in the hands of the garnishee, as hereinafter mentioned, in satisfaction of the
decree.

### 31.

Copy to be filed
The writ shall be prepared by the party applying for it, and shall be entitled in the
action, and a copy signed by him or her or his or her solicitor shall be delivered to the
Registrar to be filed in Court.

### 32.

Service of writ
Subject to any order of the Court as to the mode of service the writ shall be served on
the garnishee:
Provided that the garnishee may accept service personally or through his or her
solicitor by indorsing on the writ a memorandum or acceptance of service and of the date
thereof, and in that case service shall be unnecessary.

### 33.

Memorandum of service
The Registrar shall within three days at most after service by him or her of the writ,
endorse thereon the day of the month and week of the service thereof.

### 34.

Effect of writ
From the time of the service upon the garnishee of a writ of attachment all property
within Grenada, other than land or any interest therein, to which the judgement debtor is
beneficially entitled, whether solely or jointly with others, and which at the time of the
service of the writ, or at any time before the writ is dissolved, is or shall be in the custody
or under the control of the garnishee, and all debts due or accruing due by the garnishee
to the judgement debtor at or during such time as aforesaid, shall to the extent of the
judgement debtor’s interest therein, and subject to Crown and Government debts and to
any bona fide prior title thereto, or lien or charge thereon, be attached in the hands of the
garnishee, to satisfy the claim of the decree-holder.

### 35.

Penalty for disposing of property attached
A garnishee who shall, without leave or order of the Court, at any time after the
service of the writ, and before the attachment is dissolved, knowingly and wilfully part
with the custody or control of any property attached in his or her hands, or remove it out
of Grenada, or sell or dispose of it, or pay over any debt due by him or her to the
judgement debtor, except only to or to the use of the decree-holder, shall be liable to
attachment, and shall pay such damages to the decree-holder as the Court shall award:
Provided that the Court shall not award a larger amount of damages than it is
competent to decree in an action for damages, and such award shall bar any suit for such
damages.

### 36.

Court may order seizure and detention of movable property
In all cases where it is made to appear to the satisfaction of the Court by affidavit or
otherwise that there is reasonable cause to believe that any movable property attached is
in danger of being removed out of Grenada, or of being sold, or otherwise disposed of,
without the leave of the Court, the Court may, by an order in writing, direct the Registrar
to seize such property and detain it subject to the order of the Court, or may make an
order for the delivery and custody of the property:
Provided that the Court may order the property to be released upon such terms as to
security and other matters as may seem just.

### 37.

Property in the hands of a public officer, or in custodia legis
Property in the hands or under the control of any public officer in his or her official
capacity shall be liable to attachment in execution of a decree with the consent of the
Attorney-General, and property in custodia legis shall be liable to attachment by order of
the Court. The attachment shall take effect from the service of the writ on the officer, or
in the case of property in custodia legis, from the date of the order of the Court.

### 38.

Appearance of garnishee for examination
Every garnishee shall appear before the Court on the day specified in the writ, or on
such subsequent day as the Court shall appoint and of which he or she shall have received
notice, to be examined touching the property which may have been attached in his or her
hands.

### 39.

Investigation as to property attached
Upon the day so appointed, and at any adjournment, the Court may, of its own motion,
or at the instance of any person interested in the inquiry, summon any person whom it
may think necessary and examine him or her in relation to the property, and may require
the garnishee as well as the person summoned as aforesaid, to produce all deeds and
documents in his or her possession or power relating to the property.

### 40.

Order thereon
The Court may upon the investigation order that any part of the property attached
which shall consist of money and banknotes, or a sufficient part thereof, be paid over to
the decree-holder, or that any part of the property so attached which may not consist of
money or banknotes, so far as may be necessary for the satisfaction of the decree, shall be
sold, and that the money which may be realised by the sale or a sufficient part thereof be
applied in satisfaction of the decree; and that the writ be dissolved.

### 41.

Personal liability of garnishee
If the garnishee does not dispute his or her liability and fails to comply with the order
of the Court, the Court may order execution to issue against him or her for the amount of
the property attached in his or her hands, or for such part thereof as shall be sufficient to
satisfy the decree, and all costs of the proceedings and execution may issue accordingly.

### 42.

Where liability disputed
If the garnishee disputes his or her liability, the Court may order that any issue or
question necessary for determining his or her liability be tried or determined in any
manner in which an issue or question in any action may be tried or determined; or with
the consent of the parties may dispose of the question between them in a summary
manner.

### 43.

Where several Claimants to property attached
Whenever there are several Claimants to any property attached or to any interest
therein, the Court may, in its discretion, summon before it all the Claimants, and may
make such orders as in the last preceding section mentioned, for the ascertaining of their
rights and for the custody of the property in the meanwhile, or, with the consent of the
parties, may dispose of the adverse claims in a summary manner.

### 44.

Attachment book
There shall be kept by the Registrar an attachment book, and in it entries shall be
made of the attachment and proceedings thereon, with names, dates and statements of the
amount recovered, and otherwise; and copies of entries made therein may be taken by any
person upon application to the proper officer.

### 45.

Order of Court to discharge garnishee
The payment of debts or the delivery of property by the garnishee under the order of
the Court, or execution levied upon him or her in respect thereof, shall be a valid
discharge, as against all Claimants, of the debts or property, although the attachment may
be set aside or the decree reversed.

### 46.

Attachment may be dissolved at any time
The attachment may be dissolved at any time by order of the Court, or, if the decreeholder consents, it shall be dissolved ipso facto on the filing in Court of a memorandum
of consent signed by him or her in the presence of the Registrar.

### 47.

Stay of proceedings against garnishee
The Court may stay proceedings in any suit commenced against a garnishee in respect
of property attached in his or her hands upon such terms as it shall think fit.

### 48.

Allowance to garnishee for attendance
The Court may allow a reasonable sum to a garnishee for his or her attendance and
loss of time, not exceeding the scale of allowances to witnesses, and the amount so
allowed shall be paid by the person enforcing the attachment, and may be recovered by
him or her as costs of execution, unless the Court shall otherwise order.

## PART VI

Execution of Decree for Money against Land

### 49.

Application for sale of debtor’s land
(1) In this section, “land” does not include a small holding as defined by section 2 of
the Land Settlement Act, Chapter 161.
(2) When the holder of a decree for money desires to enforce it against the judgement
debtor’s land, he or she may apply to the Court for that purpose. Applications for the sale
of land in execution under this section, and applications for directions as to the sale of
land pursuant to any judgement or order of the Court, may be made by summons in
chambers.
(3) It shall be necessary to take out one summons only in respect of each sale. Any
application subsequent to the original summons for any directions in the matter, except an

application properly made ex parte, shall be made under the summons by two clear days’
notice to the other party or parties stating the grounds of the application.
(4) On the hearing of the summons the party taking out the same and any party to
whom the summons is addressed shall, so far as practicable apply for all orders and
directions relating to the sale and the application of the proceeds thereof which he or she
may desire, and the Judge shall, so far as practicable, make such order and give such
directions as may be just with respect to all matters connected with the sale and the
application of the proceeds thereof.
(5) An application by a party which might have been made at the hearing of the
original summons shall, if granted on any subsequent application, be granted at the cost
of the party applying unless the Judge shall be of opinion that the application could not
properly have been made at the hearing of the original summons.
(6) If land directed to be sold shall fail to realise the reserve price the Registrar shall,
unless the Judge on the application of a party otherwise orders, without further direction
again advertise and put up the same for sale at a reserve price ten per cent lower than that
fixed for the preceding sale.
(7) It shall not be necessary to apply to the Judge for an order confirming any sale of
land made pursuant to an order or direction. All sales of land carried out by the Registrar
shall for all purposes be deemed to have been confirmed at the expiration of fourteen days
from the date of the sale unless the Judge shall in the meantime otherwise direct.

### 50.

Order for sale, and mode of sale
(1) In this section, “land” does not include a small holding as defined by section 2 of
the Land Settlement Act, Chapter 161.
(2) If the Court is satisfied by oath or affidavit that the decree remains wholly or in
part unsatisfied, and that the judgement debtor is beneficially entitled to any land in
Grenada and has no other property in Grenada against which the decree can be enforced,
the Court may order his or her interest in the land or any part thereof to be sold.
(3) The judgement debtor shall be served with notice of the application for sale; and,
before the order is made, such evidence as the Court thinks sufficient shall be given as to
the boundaries and area and value of the land, and the extent and value of the debtor’s
interest in it.
(4) Where the judgement debtor is absent from Grenada and it appears to the Court
that personal service of the notice of the application for sale would in the circumstances
of the case be too difficult or too costly, the Court may make such order for substituted or
other service, or for the substitution of notice for service by letter, public advertisement,
or otherwise, as may be just.
(5) The sale shall be free from all judgements against the judgement debtor.
(6) Before the land is advertised for sale, the Registrar shall, on the application of the
judgement creditor, search for prior unsatisfied judgements in the High Court against the
judgement debtor, and shall file a certificate of the result of the search. The holder of
every judgement mentioned in the certificate shall be served by the judgement creditor
with notice of the order for sale, and shall thenceforth be at liberty to attend the
proceedings and be bound thereby.
(7) Notice of the sale shall be given by advertisement. The advertisement shall be
prepared by the judgement creditor and approved by the Registrar and shall state—
(a)

what is believed to be the extent of the judgement debtor’s interest;

(b)

that the land is sold free from all judgements against the judgement debtor,
so far as they are known;

(c)

that the sale is subject to the usual conditions, a copy of which may be seen
at the Registrar’s office;

(d)

the reserve price.

(8) After the completion of the purchase the Court shall upon the purchaser’s
application grant him or her a certificate that he or she has purchased the interest of the
judgement debtor in the land sold. The certificate in Form 13 of the Schedule shall be
liable to the same stamp duty as a conveyance or assignment of the same property, and
when duly stamped shall be taken to be a valid transfer of the said interest and may be
registered in the same way as any deed of conveyance or assignment.
(9) The proceeds of sale shall be applied by the Registrar, without any order for the
purpose, first in paying the judgement creditor’s costs of procuring and carrying out the
order for sale, and then in satisfying the judgements mentioned in the Registrar’s
Certificate in the order of their priority, and then in satisfying the judgement creditor’s
debt and costs and any subsequent judgement; and the balance, if any, shall be paid to the
judgement debtor.

### 51.

For possession of land or chattel
If the decree be for the possession of land the Registrar may on the filing of a praecipe
for that purpose issue a writ of possession in Form 12 of the Schedule, and the decreeholder shall be put in possession, if necessary by the proper officer. If the decree be for
the delivery of any specific chattel, the Registrar may issue a writ of delivery, and, if
necessary, the chattel shall be seized by the proper officer and delivered to the party to
whom it has been adjudged, or alternative damages, if decreed, may be levied.

### 52.

Sale to be by auction, etc.
(1) Every sale of land under an order of the Court shall be carried out by the Registrar
and shall be conducted according to such orders, if any, as the Court may make.
(2) The sale shall be by public auction, unless the Court otherwise directs, and any
party shall be at liberty to bid.

### 53.

Reserve price and conditions of sale
The sale shall be subject to a reserve price to be fixed by the Judge, and to the
common conditions of sale which shall be, from time to time, approved by the Court. The
party prosecuting the decree may, in any case in which he or she thinks fit, apply to the
Court to approve of special conditions of sale, and, if the Court approves them, the sale
shall be subject thereto, and the advertisement shall so state.

## PART VII

Disobedience to a Decree: Attachment: Sequestration

### 54.

Application for order to show cause
Where the decree is one directing money to be paid into Court, or some other act to be
done in a limited time, and the person directed to do the act refuses or neglects to do it,
the person prosecuting the decree may apply to the Court for a writ of attachment for an
order on the person disobeying the decree, to show cause why he or she should not be
punished for the disobedience. The Court may in its discretion, issue the writ at once, or
in the first instance make such order as aforesaid.

### 55.

Service of order

A certified copy of the order shall be served on the party to whom the order is
directed. The service shall be personal.

### 56.

Issue of writ of attachment in default of appearance
On the return-day of the order, if the person to whom it is directed does not attend, and
if the Court is satisfied that the order has been duly served, the Court may order that a
writ of attachment be issued against him or her.

### 57.

Enlargement of time for return to order
The Court may enlarge the time for the return to the order, or may on the return of it
direct that the writ of attachment shall issue only after a certain time and in the event of
his or her continued disobedience at that time to the decree in respect of which he or she
has been guilty of disobedience.

### 58.

Proceedings on appearance
If the person against whom the order is made does not establish a sufficient excuse for
not attending, or if he or she attends and does not show cause to the satisfaction of the
Court why he or she should not be punished for the disobedience, the Court may order
him or her to pay such fine or to be committed to prison for such time as the Court
directs.

### 59.

Conditional order of commitment
The Court may order that a person committed to prison for disobedience to a decree
shall be detained in custody until he or she has obeyed the decree in all things that are to
be immediately performed, and given such security as the Court thinks fit, to obey the
other parts of the decree, if any, at the future times thereby appointed.

### 60.

Warrant of commitment
Whenever any order of commitment is made, a warrant of commitment under the seal
of the Court shall issue directed to the Registrar, who shall thereby be empowered to take
the body of the person against whom the order is made; and every police officer and all
other peace officers, within their several jurisdictions, shall aid in the execution of the
warrant; and the gaoler or keeper of every gaol or prison mentioned in any such order
shall be bound to receive and keep therein the person against whom the warrant is issued
until he or she is discharged by due course of law.

### 61.

Powers of Commissioners
A writ of sequestration shall be directed to three or more Commissioners, to be
appointed by the Court for the purpose, who shall be commanded and empowered to enter
upon all the land of the person against whom the writ shall issue, and to collect, take and
get into their hands, not only the rents and profits of his or her land, but also his or her
goods, and detain and keep the same under sequestration in their hands until he or she
shall appear before the Court and clear his or her contempt, or the Court shall make other
order to the contrary; and the Court may order the payment out of the proceeds of such
sequestration of all charges attending the execution thereof, including such reasonable
remuneration to the Commissioners as the Court shall think fit to allow.

## PART VIII

### 62.

Orders may be enforced in like manner as decrees

Any order of the Court made in any action or proceeding may be enforced and any
disobedience thereto may be dealt with and punished, in the manner provided in this Act
or by rules for the enforcement of decrees and for the punishment of disobedience thereto.

### 63.

Order for payment of money a charge on land
Every order of the Court for the payment of money shall bind the land of the person
against whom it is made, in the same manner and to the same extent in every respect as a
decree for money.

### 64.

Saving
Nothing hereinbefore contained shall take away or curtail any right heretofore existing
to enforce or give effect to any judgement or order in any manner or against any person or
property whatsoever.

### 65.

Imprisonment where debtor is able to pay and neglects or refuses
(1) The Court or magistrate may commit to prison for a period not exceeding six
weeks, unless the sum ordered to be paid is sooner paid, any person who makes default in
payment of any debt or instalment of any debt due from him or her in pursuance of an
order or judgement, if it is proved to the satisfaction of the Court or magistrate that the
person making default has, or since the date of the order or judgement has had, the means
to pay the debt or instalment and has neglected or refused to pay it.
(2) Proof of the means of the person making default may be given in such manner as
the Court or magistrate thinks just, and for that purpose the debtor and any witness may
be summoned and examined.
(3) Notice of the application for the order of committal shall be given to the debtor,
who shall be at liberty to attend and be heard on the hearing of the application.
(4) Any person imprisoned under this section shall be discharged as soon as he or she
has satisfied the debt or instalment in respect of which he or she was imprisoned, and the
costs.

### 66.

Imprisonment for non-payment in special cases
In the following cases the High Court may commit to prison for a period not
exceeding one year, namely—
(a)

for default in payment of a penalty or sum in the nature of a penalty, other
than a penalty in respect of a contract;

(b)

for default by a trustee or person acting in a fiduciary capacity in payment of
a sum in his or her possession or under his or her control which he or she is
ordered to pay;

(c)

for default by a solicitor in payment of costs which he or she is ordered to
pay for misconduct as a solicitor or in payment of a sum which he or she is
ordered to pay in his or her character as an officer of the Court;

(d)

for default by a bankrupt or person in insolvent circumstances in payment of
any portion of a salary or income which he or she is ordered to pay for the
benefit of his or her creditors.

### 67.

Imprisonment not to extinguish the debt
No imprisonment under either of the two last preceding sections shall operate as a
satisfaction or extinguishment of any debt or demand or cause of action, or deprive any

person of any right to take execution against the lands or goods of the person imprisoned
in the same manner as if the imprisonment had not taken place.

### 68.

Private agreement to refer
When any persons shall by an instrument in writing agree that any differences
between them, or any of them, shall be referred to the arbitration of any persons named in
the agreement, application may be made by the parties thereto, or any of them, that the
agreement be filed in the Court. On such application being made, the Court shall direct
such notice to be given to any of the parties to the agreement as it may think necessary,
requiring them to show cause, within a time to be specified, why the agreement should
not be filed. The application shall be numbered and registered as an action between the
parties interested as Plaintiffs and Defendants. If no sufficient cause be shown against the
filing of the agreement, the agreement shall be filed and an order of reference to
arbitration shall be made thereon. The provisions of the rules relating to arbitration, so far
as they are not inconsistent with the terms of an agreement so filed, shall be applicable to
all proceedings under the order of reference and to the award of arbitration and to the
enforcement of such award.

### 69.

Arbitration without the intervention of the Court
When a matter has been referred to arbitration without the intervention of the Court,
and an award has been made, any person interested in the award may, within six months
from the date of the award, make application to the Court that the award be filed in Court.
The Court shall direct notice to be given to the parties to the arbitration other than the
applicant, requiring them to show cause, within a time to be specified, why the award
should not be filed. The application shall be numbered and registered as a suit between
the applicant as Plaintiff and the other parties as Defendants. If no sufficient cause be
shown against the award, the award shall be filed and shall thereupon have the same force
and effect for all purposes as a judgement.

## PART IX

Actions in General

### 70.

Actions by the Government against private parties
An action by the Government against any private person shall be brought in the name
of the Attorney-General, and, subject to the provisions of the Crown Proceedings Act,

## Chapter 74, shall be carried on in the same manner in every respect as an action between

private parties.

### 71.

Judgement and proceedings thereon
Whenever in any such action a decree shall be made against the Government, no
execution shall issue thereon, but a copy of the decree under the seal of the Court shall be
transmitted by the Court to the Minister of Finance who, if the decree shall be for the
payment of money, shall have power by warrant under his or her hand to direct the
amount awarded by the decree to be paid by the Permanent Secretary (Finance), and in
case of any other decree, to take such measures as may be necessary to cause the same to
be carried into effect.

### 72.

Costs
In all actions by or against the Government, costs may be awarded in the same manner
as in actions between private parties, but no personal liability shall attach to the AttorneyGeneral in respect thereof.

### 73.

Petition for leave to sue or defend in forma pauperis
Any poor person, before commencing or defending any action in the Court in his or
her own right, or becoming poor during the progress thereof, may apply to the Court by
petition, for leave to sue or defend as a pauper, which petition shall be supported by an
affidavit of the petitioner, and by such further evidence as the Court may think necessary,
that he or she is not possessed of property to the amount of fifty dollars in value,
excepting wearing apparel and the matter or thing claimed by him or her if he or she be
the Plaintiff in the action, and thereupon it shall be referred to a barrister to consider the
case; and upon the petitioner producing a certificate, signed by the barrister, that he or she
has considered the case, and believes him or her to have good cause of action or defence,
as the case may be, it shall be lawful for the Court to admit the petitioner to sue or defend,
as the case may be, in forma pauperis, and also to appoint a barrister or solicitor, or both,
to appear for him or her.

### 74.

Affidavit of facts
No person shall be admitted to sue or defend in forma pauperis unless he or she has
filed in Court an affidavit containing a full statement of all the material facts of the case
to the best of his or her belief.

### 75.

Court may assign barrister or solicitor
If in any case the Court thinks fit to assign a barrister or solicitor, or both, to assist a
person admitted to sue or defend in forma pauperis, or to consider the case and give such
certificate as aforesaid, the barrister or solicitor so assigned may not refuse his or her
assistance, unless he or she satisfies the Court that he or she has some good reason for
refusing.

### 76.

No fees shall be paid: Exception
No fee shall be taken by a barrister or solicitor so assigned, nor shall any fees of Court
be demanded by an officer of the Court from any person applying or admitted to sue or
defend as a pauper, but if he or she succeed and costs should be awarded to be paid by his
or her opponent, then the barrister and the solicitor so assigned shall be entitled to and
shall receive all such fees as the Registrar shall allow to them on taxation, and such Court
fees as would in other cases be chargeable shall be charged and recovered.

### 77.

Loss of privilege by misconduct
Any person having been admitted to sue or defend as a pauper, and becoming of
ability during the progress of the action, or misbehaving himself therein by any vexatious
or improper conduct or proceeding, or wilfully delaying the action, shall, on the same
being shown to the Court, be deprived of all the privileges of such admission.

## PART X

Defendant Absconding or Removing Property

### 78.

Arrest of Defendant
If it is shown to the satisfaction of the Court that the Plaintiff has good cause of action
against the Defendant to the amount of not less than fifty dollars, or in the case of a
Magistrate’s Court to the amount of not less than ten dollars, and that there is probable
cause for believing that the Defendant is about to quit Grenada, and that his or her
absence will materially prejudice the Plaintiff in the prosecution of the action, the Court
may issue a warrant to arrest the Defendant in Form 15 of the Schedule and deliver him

or her to the keeper of the prison, there to be kept for a period not exceeding two months,
unless and until he or she gives security to the Plaintiff in such sum (not exceeding the
probable amount of the debt or damages and costs) as the Court thinks fit, that he or she
will not quit Grenada without the leave of the Court. The Court may at any time on
reasonable cause being shown release him or her from arrest in Form 16 of the Schedule.

### 79.

Removal of property to delay execution
If it is shown to the satisfaction of the Court that the Defendant with intent to obstruct
or delay the execution of any judgement which may be passed against him or her, is about
to remove any of his or her goods out of Grenada, it shall be lawful for the Court, in its
discretion, on the application of the Plaintiff, to order that the goods of the Defendant, or
any part thereof, be forthwith secured and taken into the custody of the Registrar or other
proper officer in Form 17 of the Schedule, as a pledge or surety to answer the just
demands of the Plaintiff until the trial of the action and judgement thereon:
Provided that the Court may at any time, upon reasonable cause being shown, and
upon such terms, if any, as to security or otherwise as may seem just, release the property
seized and order the same to be returned.

### 80.

Compensation for needless arrest or seizure
If it appears to the Court that the arrest of the Defendant or the seizure of his or her
goods was applied for on insufficient grounds, or if the action of the Plaintiff is
dismissed, or judgement is given against him or her by default or otherwise, and it
appears to the Court that there was no probable ground for instituting the action, the
Court may, on application of the Defendant, award against the Plaintiff such amount as it
may deem a reasonable compensation to the Defendant for the expense or injury
occasioned to him or her by the arrest or seizure:
Provided that the Court shall not award a larger amount of compensation under this
section than it is competent to the Court to decree in an action for damages. An award of
compensation under this section shall bar an action for damages in respect of the arrest or
attachment.

### 81.

Costs of subsistence of persons arrested
The expenses incurred for the subsistence in prison of the person arrested shall be paid
by the Plaintiff in the action at the rate of fifteen cents per day or such other sum as the
Court may, from time to time, direct, and such payment shall be paid to the keeper of the
prison by monthly payments in advance before the first day of each month, the first
payment to be made for such portion of the current month as may remain unexpired
before the Defendant is imprisoned. The amount so disbursed may be recovered by the
Plaintiff in the action unless the Court shall otherwise order. The Court may release the
person so imprisoned on failure by the Plaintiff to pay the subsistence money, or in case
of serious illness order his or her removal to hospital.

## PART XI

Business in Chambers

### 82.

What business may be transacted in Chambers or in private
Motions and petitions shall be heard in Court, and summonses and other applications
shall be heard in Chambers:
Provided that any business except the trial of an action may be transacted in
Chambers if the Court shall so direct; and the Court shall in each case decide whether the

application is a proper one to be made by motion in Court, or by summons in Chambers,
and may, at or before the hearing if it shall think fit, remove the same into Court or into
Chambers as the case may be. The Court may order any proceedings except the trial of an
action to be heard in private.

### 83.

Force of order in Chambers
Every order made in Chambers shall have the same force and effect as an order of
Court, and the Court sitting at Chambers shall have the same power to enforce, vary or
deal with any such order, as if sitting in Court.

### 84.

Where time expires on Sunday or holiday
Where the time for doing any act or taking any proceeding in a Court or public office
expires on a day on which the Court or office is closed, the act or proceeding shall, so far
as regards the time of doing or taking it, be held to be duly done or taken if done or taken
on the day on which the Court or office is next open.

### 85.

Enlargement of time for doing any act
The Court shall have power to enlarge or abridge the time appointed by this Act or
fixed by any order enlarging time for doing any act or taking any proceeding, upon such
terms, if any, as the justice of the case may require, and any such enlargement may be
ordered although the application for it is not made until after the expiration of the time
appointed or allowed.

### 86.

Enlargement of time by consent
The time for delivering, amending or filing any pleading or other document may be
enlarged by consent in writing without application to the Court.

### 87.

General power of Court not affected
Nothing in this Act shall affect the power of the Court to defer or adjourn the hearing
or determination of any action, matter, proceeding or application, for such time and on
such terms as justice requires.

### 88.

General power of Court not affected
Nothing in this Act shall affect the power of the Court to order or allow any
amendment of any writ, petition, answer, notice or other document whatever, at any time,
on such terms as justice requires.

### 89.

When offices to be closed
The offices shall be open on every day of the year, except Sunday, Good Friday,
Monday and Tuesday in Easter week, White Monday, Christmas Day, and the next
following working day, and all days appointed by Proclamation to be observed as days of
general fast, humiliation, or thanksgiving or declared to be holidays by the Bank Holidays
Act, Chapter 25.

### 90.

To be in writing
In all cases in which notice is required to be given, it shall be in writing; and in all
cases in which the publication of any notice is required, it may be made by advertisement
in the Gazette, unless otherwise provided in any particular case by this Act, or otherwise
ordered by the Court.

### 91.

Forms in Schedule
The forms contained in the Schedule to this Act, with such variations as circumstances
require, may be used for the purpose of carrying out the provisions of this Act.

### 92.

Saving
Nothing in this Act shall be deemed to curtail or abridge any power or authority now
vested in the High Court or in any court whose jurisdiction has been transferred to the
High Court.

## PART XII

Summary Procedure

### 93.

Rules applicable generally in summary jurisdiction
The provisions of this Act and of the rules apply to actions instituted in the summary
jurisdiction of the Court so far as they can be made applicable thereto, unless the Court in
any case, to avoid delay or for any other reason, thinks fit to direct otherwise, and except
as herein or by the rules otherwise provided.

### 94.

In what cases summary jurisdiction exercisable
The summary jurisdiction shall be exercised—
(a)

in actions by creditors, legatees (whether specific, pecuniary or residuary),
devisees (whether in trust or otherwise), heirs-at-law or next-of-kin, in which
the personal or real or personal and real estate against or for an account or
administration of which the demand may be made shall not exceed in
amount or value the sum of seven thousand dollars;

(b)

in actions for the execution of trusts in which the trust estate or fund shall
not exceed in amount or value the sum of seven thousand dollars;

(c)

in actions for foreclosure or redemption or for enforcing any charge or lien,
where the mortgage charge or lien shall not exceed in amount the sum of
seven thousand dollars. In such actions, other than those for redemption, the
jurisdiction shall extend to the granting of orders for personal payment of
any sum not exceeding seven thousand dollars due under the mortgage
charge or lien;

(d)

in actions for specific performance of or for the reforming, delivering up, or
cancellation of any agreement for the sale, purchase, or lease of any
property, where in the case of a sale or purchase the purchase money, or in
the case of a lease, the value of the property shall not exceed the sum of
seven thousand dollars;

(e)

in an action or matter under the Trustee Act, Chapter 329, in which the trust
estate or fund to which the action or matter relates shall not exceed in
amount or value the sum of seven thousand dollars;

(f)

in matters relating to the maintenance or advancement of infants in which
the property of the infant shall not exceed in amount or value the sum of
seven thousand dollars;

(g)

in actions or matters for the dissolution or winding up of any partnership in
which the whole property, stock, and credits of such partnership shall not
exceed in amount or value the sum of seven thousand dollars;

(h)

in actions for relief against fraud or mistake in which the damage sustained
or the estate or fund in respect of which relief is sought shall not exceed in
amount or value the sum of seven thousand dollars;

(i)

in proceedings for partition under the Partition Act, Chapter 225, in cases
where the whole of the property to which the proceedings relate shall not
exceed in value the sum of seven thousand dollars;

(j)

in all proceedings for orders in the nature of injunctions where required for
granting relief in any matter in which the Court has summary jurisdiction.

### 95.

Register of Civil Actions
The Registrar shall keep a book called the “Register of Civil Actions (Summary
Jurisdiction)”. Every action or other proceeding instituted in the summary jurisdiction
shall be numbered in each year according to the order in which it is commenced.

### 96.

No formal appearance or pleadings
The Defendant need not enter an appearance to the writ, but after service thereof the
action shall be heard and determined in a summary way without pleadings, but the Judge
may order the Plaintiff to file within such time as he or she shall direct a written statement
of his or her claim, and may likewise order the Defendant within such time as aforesaid to
file a written answer to the statement and may permit any defence on equitable grounds.

### 97.

Review
The Judge may, upon such grounds as he or she in his or her discretion thinks
sufficient, review any judgement or decision given by him or her within one month from
the date thereof (unless either party has appealed to the Court of Appeal and the parties
do not agree to the withdrawal of the appeal); and upon the review he or she may re-open
and re-hear the case wholly or in part and take fresh evidence and reverse, vary, or
confirm his or her previous judgement or decision.

### 98.

Setting down for hearing, notice of special defence
Except by consent or leave of the Court no action or other proceeding in the summary
jurisdiction of the Court shall be set down for hearing before at least eight clear days from
the service of the writ of summons; and, except by consent or by leave of the Court, it
shall not be competent for the Defendant to rely on any set-off, counterclaim, release or
discharge, or on any defence of fraud, illegality, want of consideration, the statute of
limitations, infancy or coverture, or in any action for libel or slander on the defence that
the libel or slander is true, unless at least two clear days’ notice thereof has been first
given to the Plaintiff or his or her solicitor and filed with the Registrar.

### 99.

Juries
In the summary jurisdiction the Court may in its discretion, on the application of either
party or of its own motion, order that a common or special jury be empanelled for the trial
of any action.

### 100.

Transfer of actions from or to the summary jurisdiction
If in the opinion of the Court an action has been commenced in the wrong jurisdiction
the Court may order the entry of it in the Register of Civil Actions (Summary
Jurisdiction), or in the principal registry, as the case may be, to be cancelled and the
action to be transferred to the principal registry or the Register of Civil Actions
(Summary Jurisdiction), as the case may be; and thereupon the Registrar shall endorse on

the writ a memorandum of the order of transfer, and the action shall then proceed as if it
had been commenced in the jurisdiction to which it has been transferred.

### 101.

Defence or counterclaim before Court in summary jurisdiction
Where in any action before the Court in its summary jurisdiction any defence or
counterclaim of the Defendant involves matter beyond the jurisdiction of the Court, such
defence or counterclaim shall not affect the competence of the Court to dispose of the
whole matter in controversy so far as relates to the demand of the Plaintiff and the
defence thereto, but no relief exceeding that which the Court has jurisdiction to
administer shall be given to the Defendant upon such counterclaim:
Provided that it shall be lawful for the Court, at any stage of the proceedings upon
the application of any party thereto, to order that the action be transferred to the superior
jurisdiction of the Court and the principal registry therein, and the same shall thenceforth
be continued or prosecuted as if it had been originally commenced in the superior
jurisdiction.

### 102.

Sittings
Sittings of the Court for the hearing of actions and other matters in the summary
jurisdiction shall be held at least once in each month, except August and September, on
days to be fixed by the rules.

## PART XIII

Procedure in Magistrates’ Courts

### 103.

Procedure in Magistrates’ Courts
The procedure in Magistrates’ Courts in civil cases shall be the same as in actions in
the summary jurisdiction of the High Court, subject to the following special rules.

### 104.

Certain sections not applicable to Magistrates’ Courts
The following sections of this Act do not apply to Magistrates’ Courts, namely—
Sections 5, 6, 10 to 15, 23, 63, 66, 68 to 77, 82 and 83.

### 105.

The summons
(1) The Plaintiff on applying for a summons shall lodge with the magistrate or his or
her clerk two copies of his or her claim, one of which shall be annexed to the summons.
The summons shall be signed and issued in Form 2 of the Schedule by the magistrate or
his or her clerk, and shall, unless in any particular case the magistrate otherwise directs,
be served in the manner in which personal service is now made.
(2) When judgement by confession or by consent of parties is entered up in a civil
case in a magistrate’s court, such confession or consent may be taken by the magistrate’s
clerk at any time before the hearing of the case, and the magistrate’s clerk shall thereupon
enter in the book kept for the purpose the judgement, the costs, and the date of the said
confession or consent.

### 106.

Extension of time in case of Carriacou
Where a person resident in Carriacou is summoned to attend in Grenada as Defendant
or witness, or a person resident in Grenada is summoned to attend in Carriacou, the
magistrate may extend the time allowed for appearance.

### 107.

Service on Defendant absent from Grenada
(1) Where by reason of the absence of a Defendant from Grenada personal service of
a summons issued in a magistrate’s court on a claim for a debt contracted within Grenada
cannot be effected, the magistrate may (whether such Defendant was within Grenada at
the time of the issue of the summons or not) upon an affidavit proving the necessary facts
and that the Defendant has property within Grenada against which any judgement given
can be enforced, order that service be effected as hereinafter provided, and such service
shall be equivalent to personal service.
(2) Such service may be ordered to be effected—
(a)

by delivery of the summons to some adult occupant at the usual or last
known place of abode or business within Grenada of the person to be served;
or

(b)

by advertisement in the Gazette or in some newspaper circulating within
Grenada and by a notice put up at the court house where the case is to be
heard.

## PART XIV

### 108.

Review
The magistrate may, upon such grounds as he or she in his or her discretion thinks
sufficient, review any judgement or decision given by him or her within one month from
the date thereof (unless either party has appealed and the parties do not agree to the
withdrawal of the appeal); and upon the review he or she may re-open and re-hear the
case wholly or in part, and take fresh evidence, and reverse, vary, or affirm his or her
previous judgement or decision.

### 109.

Payment of debt by instalments
The magistrate may at any time order that any judgement debt be paid by instalments
and may at any time rescind or vary any such order.

### 110.

Record to be kept by magistrate
The magistrate shall enter or cause to be entered in a book kept for the purpose the
names of the complainant and Defendant, the date of entry of the complaint, the date of
hearing, the judgement, the costs, the date of judgement, the date of issuing the writ of
execution or commitment for such debt or instalment as may be due, and the date when
the judgement is wholly satisfied.

### 111.

Execution against goods
Whenever the court shall have given or made a judgement or order for the payment of
money, the amount may be recoverable, in case of default or failure of payment thereof
forthwith or at the time or times and in the manner thereby directed, by execution against
the goods and chattels of the party against whom such judgement or order shall be given
or made; and the magistrate, at the request of the party prosecuting such judgement or
order, shall issue a writ of execution in Form 6 of the Schedule to the bailiff of the court,
who, by such writ, shall be empowered to levy by distress and sale of the goods and
chattels, such sum of money as shall be so ordered, and also the costs of the execution;
and the bailiff shall sell the same, or so much thereof as may satisfy the debt and all costs,
at the expiration of seven days after levy, or before, if the goods be of a perishable nature
or upon the request in writing of the party whose goods are levied upon. Until the sale the
goods shall remain in the custody of the bailiff by whom the levy is made; and forthwith
after the sale the bailiff shall pay the proceeds thereof into court, to be applied by the

magistrate towards satisfaction of the judgement creditor’s claim and costs; and the
surplus, if any, shall be paid to the judgement debtor on demand.

### 112.

Execution against land
(1) If the magistrate is, on the application of the judgement creditor, satisfied by oath
or affidavit that the judgement remains wholly or in part unsatisfied, and that the
judgement debtor is beneficially entitled to any land in Grenada, and has no other
property in Grenada against which the judgement can be enforced, he or she may issue a
praecipe to the Registrar in Form 11 of the Schedule to sell the interest of the judgement
debtor in the land, or so much thereof as may be sufficient to satisfy the judgement debt
and costs.
(2) In subsection (1), “land” does not include a small holding as defined by section 2
of the Land Settlement Act, Chapter 161.

### 113.

Notice to debtor and evidence as to value of land
The judgement debtor shall be served with notice of the application for sale; and
before the order is made such evidence as the magistrate thinks sufficient shall be given
as to the boundaries and area and value of the land and the extent and value of the
debtor’s interest in it; and the magistrate shall inform the Registrar of the result of the
evidence.

### 114.

Order for substituted service
Where the judgement debtor is absent from Grenada and it appears to the magistrate
that personal service of the notice of the application for sale would in the circumstances
of the case be too difficult or costly, the magistrate may make such order for substituted
or other service, or for the substitution of notice for service by letter, public
advertisement, or otherwise as may be just.

### 115.

Registration of order for sale in High Court
The Registrar upon receiving the praecipe shall enter it on the same day in the
Register of the High Court (Summary Jurisdiction), and from the date of the entry the
land of the judgement debtor shall, subject to any previous judgement by which it is
bound, be bound to the extent of the debt and costs and expenses referred to in the
praecipe, and of the costs of the sale.

### 116.

Registrar to carry out sale
The Registrar shall then proceed to carry out the sale in the same way in all respects as
if the order for sale had been made in an action in the High Court.

### 117.

Application of proceeds of sale
The Registrar shall apply the net proceeds of the sale in or towards satisfaction of any
previous unsatisfied judgement mentioned in his or her certificate, and shall forward the
balance, if any, to the magistrate, who shall apply it in or towards satisfying the debt and
costs in respect of which his or her praecipe was issued and shall pay the balance, if any,
to the judgement debtor or other person entitled thereto.

### 118.

Stay of sale on payment of debt
If at any time before the sale the judgement debtor pays to the Registrar the amount
shown to be due by the praecipe, and the costs incurred by the Registrar with a view to
the sale, the sale shall be at once stopped, and the Registrar shall forward to the

magistrate the amount shown to be due by the praecipe, and the magistrate shall apply it
in satisfying the debt and costs in respect of which the praecipe, was issued.

### 119.

Record of result of sale
The Registrar, after forwarding to the magistrate the net proceeds of the sale, or the
amount paid to him or her by the judgement debtor, shall enter a note thereof in the
Register in which the praecipe, is entered.

## PART XV

Miscellaneous

### 120.

Application of money paid into court under judgements
All money paid into court under a judgement, whether levied by execution or not,
shall be applied by the magistrate in satisfaction of the judgement in respect of which it is
paid in.

### 121.

Claim by third party to property taken in execution
Where any property taken in execution is claimed by any person other than the
judgement debtor, the claim may be determined by the magistrate upon an interpleader
summons taken out by the Claimant against the party prosecuting the judgement, and his
or her determination shall have the effect of a judgement.

CIVIL PROCEDURE (FOREIGN TRIBUNALS PROCESS) RULES

[Section 3.]

---

### 1.

Citation
These Rules may be cited as the Civil Procedure (Foreign Tribunals Process) Rules.

### 2.

Service of process of foreign court
For serving process of foreign courts or tribunals—
Where in any civil or commercial matter pending before a court or tribunal of a
foreign country a letter of request from such court or tribunal for service on any person in
Grenada, of any process or citation in such matter is transmitted to the High Court by Her
Majesty’s Secretary of State for Foreign and Commonwealth Affairs, with an intimation
that it is desirable that effect should be given to the same, the following procedure shall
be adopted—
(a)

the Letter of Request for service shall be accompanied by a translation
thereof in the English language, and by two copies of the process or citation
to be served, and two copies thereof in the English language;

(b)

service of the process or citation shall be effected by the process server,
whom the Chief Justice may appoint from time to time for the purpose (or
his or her authorised agent);

(c)

such service shall be effected by delivering to and leaving with the person to
be served one copy of the process to be served, and one copy of the
translation thereof, in accordance with the rules and practice of the High
Court of Grenada regulating service of process;

(d)

after service has been effected the process server shall return to the Registrar
of the High Court one copy of the process, together with the evidence of
service by affidavit of the person effecting the service verified by notarial
certificate, and particulars of charges for the cost of effecting such service;

(e)

the particulars of charges for the cost of effecting service shall be submitted
to the Registrar of the High Court, as Taxing Officer, who shall certify the
correctness of the charges or such other amount as shall be properly payable
for the cost of effecting service. A copy of such charges and certificate shall
be forwarded to the Governor-General;

(f)

the Registrar shall transmit through the Governor-General to Her Majesty’s
Secretary of State for Foreign and Commonwealth Affairs the Letter of
Request for service received from the foreign country, together with the
evidence of service, with a certificate appended thereto duly sealed with the
seal of the High Court. Such certificate shall be in the form in the Schedule
to these Rules.

### 3.

Court may order substituted service
Upon the application of the Attorney-General, with the consent of the GovernorGeneral, the Court or the Chief Justice may make all such orders for substituted service or
otherwise as may be necessary to give effect to these Rules.
