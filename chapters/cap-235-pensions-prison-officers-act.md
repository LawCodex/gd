## CHAPTER 235

PENSIONS (PRISON OFFICERS) ACT
An Act to regulate the grant of pensions and other allowances to prison officers.
[Amended by Act No. 35 of 1981, Act No. 24 of 1983, Act No. 28 of 1995.]

[9th December, 1935.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Pensions (Prison Officers) Act.
2\. Interpretation
In this Act—

“pay” includes the personal, house and lodging allowances (including the estimated
value of free quarters) which a Prison Officer is receiving at the time when he or she
ceases to be a member of the Prison Staff:
Provided that the amount to be allowed for house and lodging allowance shall not
exceed one-sixth of the pay of such Prison Officer;
“Prison Officer” includes Chief Officer, matron, steward, kitchen officer, master
baker, gatekeeper, transport officer and any other person employed in connection with
a prison whom the Governor-General may declare to be a prison officer within the
meaning of this Act, but does not include the Superintendent or Assistant
Superintendent of Prisons or any Prison Medical Officer;
“Prison Staff” means the whole body of persons employed as Prison Officers.
3\. Pensions to be charged on the Consolidated Fund
There shall be charged on and paid out of the Consolidated Fund as hereinafter
provided all such sums of money as may from time to time be granted by way of pension,
gratuity or other allowance in accordance with this Act to persons who have been on the
Prison Staff.
4\. Grant of pension
Subject to the provisions of this Act, a Prison Officer who has served on the Prison
Staff for ten years or upwards, may be granted on his or her retirement a pension at the
rate of fifteen-sixtieths of his or her pay with an addition of one-seven-hundred-andtwentieth of final pensionable emoluments in respect of each completed month of such
service in excess of ten years:
Provided that no pension granted by virtue of this section shall exceed two-thirds of
the average pay which shall include personal, house and lodging allowances (including
the estimated value of free quarters) of such Prison Officer during the three years
immediately preceding his or her retirement:
Provided further that where the Cabinet considers it just and equitable to do so, the
Cabinet may direct that any break in the service of a Prison Officer on the Prison Staff
shall be disregarded and the whole of his or her service shall be taken as pensionable
service including service after the 4th day of April, 1983.
5\. Cases in which a pension may be granted
No pension shall be granted—
(a)

to any Prison Officer below the rank of Chief Officer who has not attained
the age of fifty years or served on the Prison Staff for twenty years or
upwards;

(b)

to any Chief Officer who has not attained the age of fifty-five years or
served on the Prison Staff for twenty years or upwards,

unless it is established on medical evidence to the satisfaction of the Governor-General
that such Prison Officer is incapacitated by reason of some infirmity of mind or body for
further service on the Prison Staff and that such infirmity is likely to be permanent and on
the certificate of the Commissioner of Prisons that the Prison Officer has discharged his or
her duties with diligence and fidelity so as to justify the grant to him or her of a pension.
6\. Gratuity
A Prison Officer who has not completed ten years service on the Prison Staff may be
granted a gratuity at the rate of half a month’s salary for each complete six months of
service:

Provided that the Governor-General is satisfied upon medical evidence that such
Prison Officer is incapacitated by reason of some infirmity of mind or body for further
service on the Prison Staff, and that such infirmity is likely to be permanent:
Provided further that the Commissioner of Prisons has certified that the Prison
Officer has discharged his or her duties with diligence and fidelity so as to justify the
grant to him or her of a gratuity.
7\. Pension or gratuity may be reduced or withheld
Where it is established to the satisfaction of the Governor-General that an officer has
been guilty of negligence, irregularity or misconduct, the pension or gratuity for which
the officer may be eligible under this Act may, with the concurrence of the Public Service
Commission, be reduced or altogether withheld.
8\. Allowance in case of injury
Where upon medical evidence the Governor-General is satisfied that a Prison Officer
has been permanently injured—
(a)

in the actual discharge of his or her duty;

(b)

without his or her own default; and

(c)

by some injury specifically attributable to the nature of his or her duty,

and the retirement of such Prison Officer is thereby necessitated or materially accelerated,
the Governor-General may, in addition to the pension, if any, receivable by such Prison
Officer under this Act, grant to him or her such annual allowance as the GovernorGeneral shall think fit, but so that any allowance hereby granted together with the pension
awarded, shall not exceed two-thirds of his or her pay.
9\. Compulsory retirement
The Governor-General upon the advice of the Public Service Commission or, as the
case may be, the Chief of Police, may require a Chief Officer to retire at any time after he
or she attains the age of fifty-five years, and any member of the Prison Staff below the
rank of Chief Officer to retire after he or she attains the age of fifty years.
10\. Right to take reduced pension and gratuity
(1) Every Prison Officer who shall hereafter become eligible for pension under the
provisions of this Act may elect to take on his or her retirement a reduced pension and
gratuity in lieu of the pension provided for by this Act.
(2) Every Prison Officer who shall become eligible for pension may notify the
Governor-General of his or her election at any time during the period in which he or she
is becoming eligible for pension and not later than one month after the date when he or
she shall have so become eligible.
(3) An election once exercised shall be irrevocable.
11\. Definitions of reduced pension and gratuity
Reduced pension shall be a pension equal to three-fourths of the amount of pension
which would be payable under this Act including any allowance granted under section 8,
and a gratuity shall be the amount which one-fourth of the pension payable under this Act,
including such allowance when multiplied by twelve and one-half, shall represent.
12\. Gratuity in case of death

Subject to the provisions of section 13, when a Prison Officer who is not serving on
probation and who has served on the Prison Staff for not less than five years dies while
still in the service of Government it shall be lawful for the Governor-General to grant to
his or her legal personal representative a gratuity of an amount not exceeding one year’s
pensionable emoluments.
13\. Gratuity when death due to injuries
When a Prison Officer dies as a result of injuries received in the execution of his or
her duties the Governor-General may at his or her discretion grant to the legal personal
representative of such Prison Officer a gratuity of an amount to be at the discretion of the
Governor-General but not exceeding one year’s pensionable emoluments of such Prison
Officer:
Provided that if a gratuity of an amount equal to or exceeding one year’s
pensionable emoluments of such Prison Officer is granted under the provisions of this
section no gratuity shall be payable under the provisions of section 12.
14\. Pension not of right
No Prison Officer shall have an absolute right to compensation for past services, or to
any pension, gratuity or other allowance under this Act, nor shall anything herein limit
the right of the Crown, the Governor-General or the Public Service Commission to
dismiss any such Prison Officer without compensation.
15\. Pension not assignable, etc.
No pension granted under this Act shall be assignable or transferable or liable to be
attached, sequestered, or levied upon, for or in respect of any debt or claim whatsoever.
16\. Pension to cease on conviction
If any Prison Officer to whom a pension has been granted under this Act is convicted
before any court of any crime or offence for which he or she is sentenced to death or any
term of imprisonment exceeding twelve months, and does not within two months after
such conviction receive a free pardon of Her Majesty, or the Governor-General on Her
Majesty’s behalf under section 72(1)(a) of the Constitution, then in any such case such
pension shall forthwith cease:
Provided always that in the case of a Prison Officer who after conviction as above
described receives such a free pardon, at any time, the Governor-General may, if he or
she thinks fit, restore the pension.
17\. Pension to cease on bankruptcy
If any Prison Officer to whom a pension has been granted under this Act becomes
bankrupt, then such pension shall forthwith cease:
Provided that in any case where a pension ceases by reason of the bankruptcy of the
pensioner the Governor-General may from time to time during the remainder of such
pensioner’s life, or during such shorter period or periods, either continuous or
discontinuous, as the Governor-General shall think fit, pay all or any part of the moneys
to which such pensioner would have been entitled by way of pension had he or she not
become a bankrupt, to, or apply the same for, the maintenance and personal support or
benefit of all or any, exclusive of the other or others, of the following persons, namely,
such pensioner and any wife, child or children of his or her, in such proportions and
manner as the Governor-General thinks proper.
18\. Application of Act

This Act shall apply to all Prison Officers serving on the Prison Staff at the date of
commencement of this Act or who shall thereafter be appointed to or promoted on the
Prison Staff:
Provided that, in the case of a Prison Officer serving on the Prison Staff at the date
of commencement who has notified the Governor-General in writing within six months
thereafter that he or she does not desire this Act to apply to him or her, unless and until
such Prison Officer shall be promoted the provisions of this Act shall not apply to him or
her, and the provisions of the Prison Officers Pensions Act, Chapter 173 of the 1934
Edition of the Revised Laws of Grenada, shall be deemed to apply to his or her case.
19\. Saving
The pension granted to any Prison Officer who had retired from the Prison Staff
before the date of commencement of this Act, or the pension rights of any Prison Officer
not coming under the provisions of this Act by reason of an election exercised by him or
her under the provisions of section 18, shall not be affected by the repeal of the Prison
Officers Pensions Act, Chapter 173 of the 1934 Edition of the Revised Laws of Grenada.
20\. Disqualification
No pension, gratuity or other allowance under this Act shall be granted to any Prison
Officer first appointed to the Prison Staff on or after the 4th April, 1983.
