## CHAPTER 313

STREET COLLECTIONS CONTROL ACT
An Act to regulate and control the collection of money, and the sale of articles for
charitable or other purposes in streets and other public places.
[1st March, 1952.]

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Street Collections Control Act.

### 2.

Purposes for which street collections are permitted
(1) Subject to the provisions of subsection (2), this Act shall apply to the collection of
money and to the sale of articles in any street or other public place for the benefit of
charitable or other purposes, and any person who acts in contravention of any provision
of this Act shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of
one hundred dollars or in the case of a second or subsequent offence to a fine of five
hundred dollars.
(2) This Act shall not apply to—
(a)

the selling of articles in a street or other public place when the articles are
otherwise lawfully sold in the ordinary course of trade and no representation
is made by or on behalf of the seller that any part of the proceeds of sale will
be devoted to any other purpose;

(b)

any collection made in a street or other public place when the collection is
made by or with the prior approval and on behalf of any religious
denomination, trade union, or friendly society.

### 3.

Permit to collect

(1) No collection of money or sale of any article shall be made in a street or other
public place unless the person, society, committee or other body of persons responsible
for such collection or sale shall have obtained from the Chief of Police a permit for such
collection or sale and any such permitted collection or sale shall be conducted in
accordance with the conditions contained in the permit.
(2) A person aggrieved by the refusal of the Chief of Police to grant a permit or by
any conditions imposed by the Chief of Police when granting a permit may appeal to the
Minister.

### 4.

Application for permit
Application for a permit shall be made in writing to the Chief of Police not later than
one month before the date on which it is proposed to make the collection or sale.

### 5.

Conditions of grant of permit
The Chief of Police may, in granting a permit—
(a)

fix the day on which and the hours during which a collection or sale shall be
made;

(b)

limit a collection or sale to such area or to such street or public place or such
part thereof as he or she thinks fit;

(c)

impose such conditions as he or she considers necessary in order to avoid
any interference with traffic or annoyance to members of the public, and to
ensure the due accounting of the monies collected for the purposes for which
a collection or sale was made.

### 6.

Assistant collectors or sellers to be duly authorised
No person may assist or take part in any collection or sale without the written
authority of the person, society, committee or other body of persons, to whom a permit
has been granted. Such written authority shall indicate the area allotted to the holder
thereof, and every person acting as a collector or seller shall remain within his or her
allotted area. Every person so authorised shall produce such written authority forthwith
for inspection by a police officer on demand.

### 7.

Traffic not to be obstructed
(1) No collection or sale shall be made in any part of the carriageway of a street and
no person shall, for the purpose of making a collection or effecting a sale, do any act
which may cause the person in charge of a vehicle in motion to stop such vehicle.
(2) No collection or sale shall be made on the footway or in any public place to the
obstruction of any person and no collector or seller shall importune any person to the
annoyance of such person.

### 8.

Provisions with regard to collecting boxes
(1) Every collector or seller shall carry and present to all contributors or purchasers
for the reception of money a box or other receptacle securely closed and sealed in such a
way as to prevent the same being opened without such seal being broken, and into this
box or other receptacle all shall be immediately placed. All such boxes or receptacles
shall be numbered consecutively. Every collector or seller shall deliver his or her boxes or
other receptacles with the seals unbroken to one of the persons responsible for the proper
application of the money received.
(2) A collector or seller shall not carry or use any collecting box, receptacle, or tray
which does not bear displayed prominently thereon the name of the fund for which the

collection or sale is being made, nor any box or other receptacle which is not duly
numbered.

### 9.

Collector not to receive reward
No payment or reward shall be made or given either directly or indirectly to a
collector or seller or other person concerned with the promotion or conduct of a collection
or sale for or in respect of services rendered in connection therewith.

### 10.

Accounting for money collected
Within one month after the date of a collection or sale the person, society, committee
or other body of persons responsible therefor shall forward to the Chief of Police a
statement in such form as shall be approved by the Chief of Police certified by the auditor
of the society or by some independent responsible person, with vouchers showing in
detail the amount collected and the expenses incurred in connection with such collection
or sale, and shall if required by the Chief of Police satisfy him or her as to the due and
proper application of the proceeds of the collection or sale. The person, society,
committee or other body shall also, within the same period, at his, her or their own
expense and after audit, publish in such newspaper or newspapers as the Chief of Police
may direct a short statement showing the name of the person, or society or other body
responsible for the collection or sale, the name of the charity or fund which is to benefit,
the date of the collection or sale, the amount collected, the amount of the expenses and
the amount distributed to the charity or fund.
