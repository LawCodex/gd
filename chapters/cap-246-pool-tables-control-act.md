## CHAPTER 246

POOL TABLES CONTROL ACT
An Act to control the use, and to provide for the registration, of pool tables, and for
matters connected therewith.
[Act No. 7 of 1977.]

[12th September, 1977.]
1\. Short title
This act may be cited as the Pool Tables Control Act.
2\. Interpretation
In this Act—
“Minister” means the Minister of finance;
“operator” means a person who holds an operator’s licence issued under
section 5(2);
“pool table” includes a billiard table, bagatelle board and any other instrument used
in any game of like kind, and any coin-operated or disc-operated machine for games
of skill or chance;
“public place” includes any building, room or place to which the public have
access whether on payment or otherwise, and includes a club.

3\. Owner of pool table to register
(1) The owner of a pool table which is kept in a public place shall register such pool
table with the Comptroller of Inland Revenue in accordance with the provisions of this
Act.
(2) On a change of ownership of a pool table the new owner shall, within fourteen
days, apply for a certificate of registration in his or her name to the Comptroller of Inland
Revenue.
(3) A person who contravenes this section shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on
summary conviction, to a fine of two hundred dollars and in default of payment to
imprisonment for three months.
(4) The Minister shall cause a register to be kept of the name of the owner of each
pool table, a description of the pool table, and the location of the public place in which it
is kept.
(5) The Minister shall, upon payment of the appropriate fee specified in the First
Schedule, issue a certificate of registration in the form set out in Form A in the Second
Schedule.
4\. Licence to operate a pool table
No person shall operate a pool table in a public place unless he or she is the holder of
an operator’s licence for the time being in force authorising him or her to operate that
table; a person who contravenes this section shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on
summary conviction, to a fine of six hundred dollars and in default of payment to
imprisonment for three months and for a second or subsequent conviction to
imprisonment for six months and a fine of one thousand dollars.
5\. Application for operator’s licence
(1) An application for an operator’s licence under section 4 shall be made to the
Minister who, having had regard to the general nature and character of the locality, may
grant or refuse the application, but no such application shall be granted unless there is in
force a certificate of registration relating to the pool table in respect of which the
application is being made.
(2) If the application is granted the Minister shall, upon payment by the applicant of
the appropriate fee set out in the First Schedule, issue to him or her an operator’s licence
in the form set out in Form B in the Second Schedule.
(3) An operator’s licence shall, unless previously forfeited, expire on the 31st day of
December in each year.
6\. Permitted hours of use of pool tables
A pool table which is kept in a public place shall not be used or operated except
between the following times—
(a)

Monday to Friday inclusive from 4.00 p.m. to midnight;

(b)

Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays from 9.00 a.m. to midnight,

and a person who knowingly permits a pool table to be used or operated in contravention
of this section shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of
five hundred dollars and in default of payment to imprisonment for three months and the
licence of the operator of the pool table shall be liable to forfeiture.
7\. Persons under sixteen years prohibited from playing, etc.

An operator who knowingly permits a person under the age of sixteen years to use or
operate a pool table in a public place or in any way to assist in the use or operation of the
pool table, or to play, or assist in the playing of, a game of pool or to remain
unnecessarily by the pool table, shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary
conviction, to a fine of one thousand dollars and in default of payment to imprisonment
for six months and his or her licence shall be liable to forfeiture.
8\. Police may demand production of operator’s licence for inspection
A member of the Police Force may enter any public place in which there is a pool
table and demand, from the person in charge of the public place, production of the
operator’s licence for inspection, and if the person in charge fails to produce it or to cause
it to be produced for inspection he or she shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on
summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand dollars and in default of payment to
imprisonment for six months.
9\. Offence to keep unregistered pool table
A person who keeps an unregistered pool table in a public place shall be guilty of an
offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand dollars and in
default of payment to imprisonment for six months.
10\. Regulations
The Minister may make regulations to provide for any matter or thing which may be
necessary to give effect to the provisions of this Act; such regulations may create
offences and provide penalties on summary conviction not exceeding two hundred dollars
and, in default of payment, imprisonment for three months.
11\. Minister may vary fees
The Minister may, by Order, vary the fees set out in the First Schedule.
12\. Exemption
The Minister shall, by Order, exempt from the provisions of this Act a pool table if he
or she is satisfied that it is owned by and operated in a bona fide guest house, hotel or
members’ club.
