## CHAPTER 323

TOWNS BOUNDARIES ACT
An Act to ascertain and fix the boundaries of the several towns.
[Amended by Act No. 30 of 1966.]

[12th August, 1885.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Towns Boundaries Act.
2\. Appointment of commissioners for towns in Grenada and for Hillsborough in
Carriacou
It shall be lawful for the Governor-General to appoint in writing three or more
commissioners for all or each or any of the towns of St. George, Gouyave, Grenville,
Sauteurs, St. David and Victoria in the island of Grenada and for Hillsborough in the
island of Carriacou, for the purpose of ascertaining and fixing the boundaries thereof.

3\. Duties of commissioners and power to summon witnesses for the purpose of
giving information
The commissioners shall ascertain, fix and lay out all the boundaries of the said towns,
or what in their opinion ought to be the boundaries, and for that purpose they shall hold
public meetings and have full power to summon and call before them witnesses and
examine them upon oath, and to take any evidence that they may consider useful and
necessary; and any person refusing to obey any such summons shall be guilty of an
offence and liable to a fine of one hundred dollars.
4\. Power of commissioners to enter upon lands, etc.
The commissioners for the purposes of this Act shall have full power to enter upon,
view and pass over all plantations, lands and enclosures, if they shall deem it necessary,
to enable them more accurately to ascertain and fix the boundaries with respect to which
their inquiry is being made.
5\. Commissioners may employ a sworn surveyor
The commissioners shall have power to employ a sworn surveyor, who shall in all
matters respecting the boundaries obey the instructions of the commissioners; and he or
she shall cause boundary marks to be placed or planted at such points as they shall direct,
and he or she shall thereafter prepare a plan in accordance with the instructions of the
commissioners, setting out the boundaries of the town as by the commissioners
ascertained and fixed.
6\. Plan to be preserved in Minister’s office
The plan as soon as completed shall be forwarded by the commissioners to the
Minister, in whose office it shall be preserved and remain upon record.
7\. Governor-General to declare by Proclamation the fixed boundaries
If the Governor-General shall approve the boundaries as set out in the plan he or she
may, by Proclamation, declare that the boundaries as so fixed are, and the same shall be
for all purposes, the limits of the town as represented and described by the plan, and he or
she may by the same Proclamation declare that the area included in the plan is, and the
same shall be for all purposes, the town which the plan purports to represent.
8\. Copy of plan to be evidence
After the Proclamation has been duly published in the Gazette any copy of the plan
certified by the Minister shall be taken as evidence in any court and shall be conclusive
with respect to the boundaries of the town which it represents, unless and until the
boundaries shall have been altered in the manner hereafter provided.
9\. Penalty for removing or injuring boundary marks
Whosoever without the sanction of the Governor-General removes or injures, or
causes to be removed or injured, any boundary mark planted or placed by the
commissioners shall be guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of five hundred dollars.
10\. Governor-General may by Proclamation alter and amend the boundaries of a
town

It shall be lawful for the Governor-General, with the approval by resolution of the
House of Representatives, at any time after the publication of his or her Proclamation
aforesaid, from time to time by further Proclamation to alter and amend the boundaries of
any town as fixed and set out in the plan thereof; and the boundaries as defined in such
further Proclamation shall be the proper boundaries of such town for the time being.

TOWNS BOUNDARIES PROCLAMATION
[Sections 7 and 10.]

[12th August, 1885.]
1\. Citation
These Proclamations may be cited as the Towns Boundaries Proclamation.
2\. Boundaries of St. George’s, Gouyave (Charlotte Town), Sauteurs and Victoria
The areas shown in plans of the city of St. George’s, and the towns of Gouyave
(Charlotte Town), Sauteurs and Victoria (formerly Grand Pauvre) as recorded in the
office of the Governor-General and declared as from 20th October, 1886, are to be for all
purposes, the areas of the said city and towns respectively.
3\. Amendment of Boundaries of St. George’s

The following boundaries shall be substituted for the Northern and Western
boundaries of the said city of St. George’s as fixed in and by the said plan, that is to say—
The Northern boundary shall commence at the Tempé Bridge and follow the left bank of the
Tempé Ravine to its junction with the St. John’s River and thence follow the left bank of
the said St. John’s River to its junction with the sea.
The Western boundary shall be extended in a Northerly direction along the seashore to its
junction with the Northern boundary at the mouth of the St. John’s River.

4\. Amendment of Boundaries of Grenville
The Western boundary shall commence at a concrete mark on a track South of lands of
Virginia Morrison 150 feet from the Western side of the Grenville Main Road, and continue
through lands of Virginia Morrison, Charles Modeste, Alexander McNeilly, Thomas
Lowhar, Jane Dottin, John Striker, Chrissie Striker, Beatrice Striker, Virginia Striker,
Edward Blair, F.A. Cape, Margaret Davy, Julia Douglas, James Lewis, Annie Fletcher,
Sophia Seabright, in a line parallel to the Grenville Main and Salisbury Roads,
terminating at an iron mark 150 feet West of the junction of the Salisbury Road with the
Grand Bras-Cadrona By-way, the said boundary line being duly shown by a plan thereof
prepared by William Erling Dolly bearing date 25th November, 1922, and lodged in the
office of the Governor-General.
5\. Amendment of Boundaries of Hillsborough, Carriacou
The boundaries of the town of Hillsborough, Carriacou, shall commence at a concrete
mark on the sea coast West of the Roman Catholic Church and run along a straight line in
a South-Easterly direction to the boundary mark stone on the Beausejour Estate Road,
thence in a North-Easterly direction along the Beausejour Estate Road to its junction with
the Brunswick-Top Hill Road, thence along the Brunswick-Top Hill Road in a SouthEasterly direction to its junction with the Beausejour Land Settlement Road, thence in a
North-Easterly direction along the Beausejour Land Settlement Road to its junction with
Second Avenue to its junction with the Beausejour Top Hill Road, thence along the
Beausejour Top-Hill Road in a North-Westerly direction to its junction with the Island
Road continuing in a straight line to the boundary mark stone on the sea coast and thence
along the sea coast in a South Westerly direction terminating at the said stone West of the
Roman Catholic Church at point of commencement.
