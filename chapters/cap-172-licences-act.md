## CHAPTER 172

LICENCES ACT
An Act relating to licences (other than auctioneers’, liquor dealers’, and excise
licences, or licences relating to firearms, motor vehicles, or for the purchase of
nutmegs and other goods).
[Amended by Act No. 11 of 1962, Act No. 18 of 1967, Act No. 12 of 1968, Act No. 2 of 1970, Act
No. 15 of 1970, Act No. 21 of 1972, Act No. 37 of 1975, Act No. 8 of 1988, Act No. 36 of 1990,
Act No. 25 of 1992, Act No. 26 of 1994, Act No. 3 of 1995, SRO 22 of 1997, Act No. 13 of 2006.]

[1st January, 1907.]
Preliminary

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Licences Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act—
“boat” includes any boat, lighter or canoe, whether propelled by oars or by sails or
by motor engine, used for hire or for carrying passengers or freight along the coast of
Grenada or within the limits of any harbour in Grenada;
“boatman” means an individual who, for fee or reward, offers his or her services
for hire, or is engaged, in carrying by boat or in rowing or assisting in the navigation
and propulsion of a boat for the conveyance of any passengers, goods, wares,
merchandise or baggage;
“carriage” includes gig, buggy, dog-cart or other vehicle on springs, usually drawn
by animals or propelled by any mechanism or engine;
“cart” means any dray, wagon, or drag usually drawn by an animal or animals;
“coasting vessel” means any steam or sailing vessel registered in Grenada as a
“coaster” under any law relating to customs;
“commercial traveller” includes any person not resident in Grenada but who visits
Grenada for the purpose of soliciting orders for goods on a commission basis or otherwise;

“commission agent” includes any person, partnership, firm or company, (whether
incorporated or not) resident in Grenada and engaged in the business of soliciting
orders for the importation of goods or of importing goods of any kind on a
commission basis;
“Conference” means the Conference of Heads of Government, the organ of the
Community established under paragraph 1(a) of Article 10 of the revised Treaty of
Chaguaramas establishing the Caribbean Community including the CARICOM Single
Market and Economy;
“estate agent” includes any person who—
(a)

carries on the business of buying or selling, or buying and selling;

(b)

offers to buy or sell, or to buy and sell;

(c)

lets, rents, or hires, or offers to let, rent, or hire,

land for or on behalf of any other person;
“livery stable keeper” means any person who shall, for the time being, be
registered as such under this Act;
“Member of the Community” means
(a)

Antigua and Barbuda
Barbados
Belize
Commonwealth of Dominica
Grenada
Guyana
Jamaica
St. Christopher and Nevis
Saint Lucia
St. Vincent and the Grenadines
Suriname
Trinidad and Tobago;

(b)

any other State or Territory of the Caribbean region that is admitted to
membership of the Community by the Conference;

“Member State” means a Member State of the Community excluding an associate
member;
“national” means a person who—
(a)

is a citizen of a Member State;

(b)

has a connection with a Member State of a kind that entitles him or her to be
regarded as belonging to or, if it be so expressed, as being a native or
resident of that Member State for the purposes of the laws of that Member
State relating to immigration; or

(c)

which is a company or other legal entity that is constituted in an Member
State in conformity with its laws and which that State regards as belonging
to it—
(i) where that company or other legal entity has been formed for gainful
purposes,

(ii) has its registered office and central administration, and carries on
substantial activity within the Community, and
(iii) is substantially owned and effectively controlled by persons
mentioned in paragraph (a) or (b).
For the purposes of this definition, a company or other legal entity is—
(i) substantially owned if more than fifty per cent of the equity interest of
the company is beneficially owned by persons mentioned in paragraph
(a) or (b),
(ii) effectively controlled if the persons mentioned in paragraph (a) or (b)
have the power to name a majority of directors or otherwise legally to
direct its actions;
“owner” means the person in actual possession of any animal or article liable to be
licensed under this Act, but does not include a person who, being the employee or
agent of a licensed owner, shall, upon request of a revenue officer or police officer,
give the true names and addresses of himself or herself and such licensed owner;
“Permanent Secretary” means the Permanent Secretary (Finance) and, in matters
relating to a district revenue office, includes every officer in charge of such office;
“porter” includes a stevedore, and means any person who, not being permanently in
the employ or any other person, offers his or her services for hire, or is engaged for fee
or reward in the removal or conveyance of goods, wares, merchandise or baggage
from or to any vessel within the limits of any harbour in Grenada;
“revenue officer” means any revenue or excise officer, and includes all persons
whose right or duty it may be to exact the performance of, or to perform, any duty or
service relating to the Customs and Excise or Inland Revenue Departments.

### 3.

Appropriation of monies
All monies received under this Act, whether in respect of licences issued, or of any
order to pay penalties, forfeitures and sums of money, or the net proceeds of anything
seized and sold under this Act, shall, where not otherwise provided, be paid into the
Treasury for the credit of the Consolidated Fund.
Licences and Fees

### 4.

Licences
(1) Every owner of an animal or article specified in Part I or Part II of the First
Schedule shall take out a licence to own, use or keep the same and shall pay therefor the
fee set out therein.
(2) Every person (not being disqualified in that behalf under any provision of this
Act) who carries on or follows a trade, profession, business or calling mentioned in Part I,

## Part II or Part III of the First Schedule shall take out a licence to do so which shall

continue in force for the period prescribed and he or she shall pay therefor the prescribed
fee.
(3) Licences under Part I of the First Schedule shall be issued yearly or half-yearly at
the option of the licensee and shall continue in force from the date of grant thereof until
the following thirty-first day of December in the case of yearly licences, or in the case of
half-yearly licences, until the following thirtieth day of June or the thirty-first day of
December, whichever shall first ensue:
Provided that where a person becomes the owner of an animal or article or desires to
carry on or follow any trade, profession, business or calling within three months of the
end of the first half of the year he or she shall be required to pay—

(a)

in respect of a yearly licence, only seventy-five per cent of the full yearly
duty; and

(b)

in respect of a half-yearly licence, only fifty per cent of the first half-year’s
duty:

Provided also that where a person becomes the owner of an animal or article or desires
to carry on or follow any trade, profession, business or calling within three months of the
end of the second half of the year, he or she shall be required to pay, in respect of the said
half-year, only twenty-five per cent of the yearly duty.
(4) Licences under Part II of the First Schedule shall be yearly licences and shall
expire on the thirty-first day of December in each year:
Provided that where the license is taken out in respect of a period commencing on or
after the first day of July only one-half of the full license fee shall be paid. In all other
cases the full license fee shall be paid.
(5) Every licence shall be in the form prescribed in the Second Schedule.
(6) A person who sells or otherwise transfers to any other person the ownership of an
animal or article for which a licence has been taken out hereunder, shall give notice
thereof in writing to the Permanent Secretary within seven days after such sale of transfer,
and shall in such notice state the description of such animal or article and the name and
address of the person to whom the same has been sold or transferred. A person who fails or
neglects to comply with the provisions of this subsection shall be guilty of an offence.

### 5.

Exemptions
(1) No licence is required under this Act in respect of an animal or article—
(a)

which is expressly exempted in the First Schedule; or

(b)

which, being the property of the Government, shall be employed in the
service of the Government.

(2) No licence under this Act is required by—
(a)

a person licensed as a chemist or druggist under the repealed Act formerly
numbered 178 and bearing date 12th April, 1876;

(b)

a public officer in respect of his or her being employed—
(i) to dispense drugs at any Government institution,
(ii) to conduct a sale of property carried out under the authority of a court,
(iii) to conduct a sale pursuant to the provisions of any written law; or

(c)

a barrister or solicitor who buys or sells, on buys and sells, land for or on
behalf of a client.

### 6.

Establishments kept for carrying on licensed calling, to have name, etc., of
licensee painted over main entrance
A licensed person keeping an establishment for the conduct of any business or calling
required to be licensed under the preceding section shall have over the principal entrances
of such establishment painted in legible characters at least one inch in length, his or her
name with the addition of such other words as shall denote that he or she is licensed to
carry on such business or calling; and a person contravening the provisions of this section
shall be guilty of an offence.

### 7.

Licences not transferable

No licence granted under this Act shall be transferable to any person other than the
widow, executors or executrices, administrators or administratrices or assignees in
bankruptcy, of the person to whom such licence shall have been originally granted.

### 8.

Licences to be taken out within seven days
Every person liable to take out a licence under this Act shall do so at the Treasury or at
the revenue office of the district in which he or she resides—
(a)

within the first seven days of the months of January and July, in the case of a
half-yearly licence;

(b)

within the first seven days of the month of January, in the case of a yearly
licence; or

(c)

within seven days of the day upon which he or she first becomes liable to
take out a licence.

### 9.

Permanent Secretary may issue duplicates of licences where originals are lost,
etc.
It shall be lawful for the Permanent Secretary, on satisfactory proof that a licence
issued under this Act has been mislaid or destroyed, to issue a duplicate of such licence,
which duplicate shall operate in all respects as the original licence; and the holder thereof
shall be subject to all the provisions of this Act as if such duplicate licence had been the
original licence.

### 10.

Penalty for refusing to produce licence on demand
If a person who has taken out any licence under this Act does not produce and deliver
such licence to be examined and read by a revenue officer or police officer within a
reasonable time after such officer has requested production of the same, he or she shall be
guilty of an offence.

### 11.

Penalty for neglect to duly take out licence
A person liable to take out a licence under this Act who neglects to do so in the
manner prescribed by this Act shall be guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of five
hundred dollars, and where at the time of conviction he or she had not paid for or taken
out the required licence he or she shall also pay into court, in addition to such fine, a sum
equal to the fee payable for the licence neglected to be taken out; and upon payment of
such licence fee the magistrate shall notify the Permanent Secretary thereof as soon as
practicable thereafter.

### 12.

Minister may alter contents of the First Schedule
The Minister may by Order alter, conditionally or otherwise, the fees for licences
mentioned in the First Schedule, either by way of increasing, decreasing or annulling the
same or any of them, and may likewise alter the said Schedule by adding thereto any
animal, article, trade, profession, business or calling, and settle and prescribe the fee
payable for a licence in respect of such animal, article, trade, profession, business or
calling:
Provided that before any such Order comes into effect it shall have been approved by
the House of Representatives.
Special Trade Provisions
Boatmen and Porters

### 13.

What persons may be licensed as boatmen or porters
No person shall be permitted to take out a licence to act as a hawker, pedlar, boatman
or porter unless he or she shall in the first instance have satisfied the Permanent Secretary
as to his or her being of good character, and is otherwise a fit and proper person to be
licensed as such; and for that purpose such person shall, before making his or her first
application for a licence, obtain and present at the Treasury or at the revenue office of the
district in which he or she resides, a certificate testifying to his or her character, signed by
two well-known and respectable persons.

### 14.

Lists of boatmen and porters to be exhibited at Treasury
A list of the persons for the time being licensed to act as hawkers, pedlars, boatmen or
porters shall be kept exposed to public inspection at the Treasury.

### 15.

Registration badges to be issued to each licensed boatman or porter and to be
worn by him or her when at work
(1) To each person taking out a licence to act as a boatman or porter shall be issued by
the Permanent Secretary a badge made of metal or some other durable material with a
number marked thereon, also the name of the licensee’s occupation; such badge shall not
be transferable, and the person to whom the same shall be issued shall wear the same
exposed to view at all such times as he or she is plying for hire or work, and shall at any
time produce such badge for inspection by any person who may demand such inspection.
The badges issued hereunder during the first half of the year shall be of a different
description or colour from those issued in the second half of the year.
(2) Any boatman or porter who refuses to produce his or her badge to a person
lawfully demanding the same shall be guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of two
hundred and fifty dollars.
(3) Every boatman or porter, to whom a badge shall be delivered, shall, at the
expiration of his or her licence, return the badge to the Permanent Secretary, and if the
same is not returned within forty-eight hours after the licence has expired, the person to
whom it was delivered shall be guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of fifty dollars.
(4) Every person, who uses or wears any such badge after the expiration of the licence
in respect of which the badge was delivered, shall be guilty of an offence and liable to a fine
of two hundred and fifty dollars.

### 16.

Minister may fix tariffs of charges for services of boatmen and porters
(1) It shall be lawful for the Minister from time to time to frame or amend a tariff of
fares to be charged, and charges to be made respectively by boatmen and porters licensed
under this Act, and such tariff shall, subject to the provisions of this Act, be binding on all
parties after publication in the Gazette.
(2) A person who neglects to pay to a boatman or porter the legal fare or charges to
which the boatman or porter is entitled according to such tariff, shall be ordered by the
magistrate to pay to the complainant (in addition to the legal fare or charge to which he or
she shall be entitled as aforesaid) a sum not exceeding five dollars for his or her expenses
and loss of time in prosecuting the offence.

### 17.

Penalty for certain offences by boatmen or porters
A boatman or porter who—
(a)

extorts, demands or takes any hire or reward greater than the sum to which
he or she shall be entitled under any tariff then in force;

(b)

being called upon at any time between the hours of six in the morning and
six in the evening refuses, wilfully avoids or attempts to avoid any fare,
passenger, hire or job, by misrepresenting that he or she is hired or engaged,
or without reasonable cause refuses or neglects to ply or carry;

(c)

does not answer when called upon by the number of his or her boat or of his
or her badge;

(d)

accepts any order or commission, fare or passenger and afterwards refuses to
execute such order or commission, or to take such fare or passenger to such
place as he or she shall lawfully direct;

(e)

in any manner unnecessarily delays any fare or passenger; or

(f)

does not proceed with due diligence and without any wilful delay to such
place as shall be lawfully directed,

shall on conviction be guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of two hundred and fifty
dollars.

### 18.

Penalty on boatmen for overloading boats, losing passengers’ goods, etc.
Every boatman who commits any of the following offences—
(a)

in any way whatsoever overloads a boat or suffers the same to be
overloaded;

(b)

carries or allows to be carried in a boat a greater number of passengers than
is specified in the licence for such boat;

(c)

through carelessness or negligence loses any goods whatsoever entrusted to
his or her care or the property of any passenger travelling by his or her boat,
and who on demand by the owner or agent of such goods or property refuses
or neglects to make satisfaction for the same,

shall be guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of one thousand dollars.
Boats and Coasting Vessels

### 19.

Coasting vessels and boats to be measured and particulars thereof recorded
before licence issued
Before a licence is granted for a coasting vessel or boat, the Permanent Secretary shall
cause the same to be inspected and measured, and shall keep a record of its carrying
capacity, also the tonnage of each vessel and the measurement of each boat so that the
same may be correctly specified in the licence:
Provided that the Permanent Secretary may at his or her discretion accept for the
purposes of this section the description as to the tonnage of any coasting vessel specified
in the certificate of registry thereof, issued under any customs law of Grenada.

### 20.

Carrying capacity, as specified on licence, to be written on bow of each vessel or
boat used for passenger hire
(1) It shall be unlawful for a boatman plying for hire to use a coasting vessel or boat
for the carriage of any passenger for fee or reward, unless the carrying capacity specified
on the licence of such vessel or boat shall have been written or painted on the bow of the
vessel or boat as required by section 22(1).
(2) No licence shall be issued in respect of a coasting vessel or boat which shall be
used by a boatman plying for hire, unless the Permanent Secretary shall be satisfied that it
is in a seaworthy and proper condition and if such vessel or boat shall become out of
repair, unseaworthy or otherwise unsafe, the Permanent Secretary shall cause a notice in

writing to be served on the owner of such vessel or boat, requiring him or her to repair
and not use the same until it shall be repaired and made seaworthy and safe for use; and
every person who shall after such notice use such vessel or boat and ply for the letting of
the same on hire, before it has been made seaworthy and safe to the satisfaction of the
Permanent Secretary on inspection, shall be guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of
fifty dollars for every day that he or she shall so use and let such vessel or boat for hire.

### 21.

Permanent Secretary may require vessels or boats to be brought up for
inspection at any time
The Permanent Secretary may at any time require any coasting vessel or boat to be
brought up for inspection and measurement; and the person in whose name such vessel is
registered or the owner of any boat shall produce the same for inspection at the time and
place appointed by the Permanent Secretary and, in default of so doing, shall be guilty of
an offence and liable to a fine of two hundred and fifty dollars.

### 22.

Licence number to be painted on bow of vessel or boat, also carrying capacity
as stated in licence
(1) The owner of every coasting vessel or boat used or plying for hire shall within
seven days after he or she obtains his or her licence cause to be legibly written or painted
on the bow of such vessel or boat the carrying capacity thereof (as specified in its licence)
and the number of such licence, in figures not less than two inches in length, and shall
keep the same legible at all times; and every owner who fails to comply with the
provisions of this section shall be guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of one hundred
dollars.
(2) Any person in charge of a coasting vessel or boat used or plying for hire who
refuses to permit, or in any manner hinders or prevents or attempts to hinder or prevent, any
person from reading the number painted on his or her boat shall be guilty of an offence.
Slaughterers

### 23.

Only persons licensed as slaughterers to be entitled to trade as such in St.
George’s
(1) Within the boundaries of the town of St. George, no person shall carry on the
business of a slaughterer unless he or she is duly licensed under this Act. As to other parts
of Grenada the Minister may, if and when satisfied that it is in the public interest to do so,
make rules under this Act making it unlawful for any person to carry on the business of a
slaughterer within the boundaries of any town or within a fixed distance or limit from the
boundaries of any town or within any other area, unless such person is duly licensed
hereunder; and such rules shall control and settle the terms and conditions upon which
licences may be issued for the purpose aforesaid, and upon which any persons licensed
according to this section shall carry on such business as aforesaid.
(2) A licence issued for the purpose mentioned in subsection (1) may be revoked if
the owner carries on business in contravention of any rules made hereunder, or if upon
the report of any person appointed by the Minister to inspect the premises named in the
licence it shall appear that such premises are no longer suitable for use as a place for the
slaughter of animals for butchers’ meat:
Provided that nothing herein contained shall be deemed to affect any of the several
powers or provisions created by or contained in the St. George’s Public Slaughter-House
Act, Chapter 294.
Pharmacists

### 24.

Duly qualified persons alone to be licensed as pharmacists
No person shall be licensed to carry on the business of a pharmacist unless he or she
shall satisfy the Permanent Secretary that such business is bona fide conducted by a
person registered as a pharmacist.

### 25.

Penalty for selling or dispensing drugs and poisons except in presence of
registered pharmacist
(1) No drug or medicine containing any poison within the meaning of the Poison
Sales Act, Chapter 243, shall be retailed, dispensed or compounded, unless there shall be
actually present at the time a registered pharmacist.
(2) If any such drug or medicine shall be retailed, dispensed or compounded in
contravention of this section, the person carrying on the business and also any assistant
actually concerned in such retailing, dispensing or compounding shall be guilty of an
offence and liable to a fine of five thousand dollars.
Livery Stable Keepers

### 26.

Application to be made for registration as a livery stable keeper
(1) Upon the application of any person who shall carry on the business of keeping and
driving public stage coaches or conveyances or of letting out animals and vehicles on hire
to the public, the Minister may authorise the Permanent Secretary to register such person
as a livery stable keeper.
(2) Every person registered as a livery stable keeper hereunder shall be exempt from
payment of such licence fees, and shall be subject and liable to such duties and
obligations, as are specified in the next four succeeding sections.

### 27.

Livery stable keeper to be relieved of payment of a moiety of usual licensing
fees for his or her stock
Any livery stable keeper may, in respect of such of his or her vehicles and animals as
are used for his or her business exclusively, take out licences therefor upon payment of
one-half of the licence fees ordinarily payable under this Act.

### 28.

Tariff of charges taken by livery stable keepers to be fixed by Minister
(1) The Minister may, from time to time, fix and settle or amend a tariff of the
charges or fares which may be taken by or on behalf of livery stable keepers.
(2) After any such tariff shall have been published in the Gazette it shall be binding
on all parties:
Provided that no livery stable keeper shall be compelled to let out on hire any of his or
her vehicles or animals for the charges and fares specified in such tariff.
(3) If and whenever a passenger in any conveyance or a person hiring a vehicle or
animal belonging to a livery stable keeper shall not have made any special agreement
with such livery stable keeper or his or her agent or driver before engaging his or her seat,
or before completing any contract for such hiring, it shall be unlawful for such livery
stable keeper or his or her agent or driver to demand from any such passenger or hirer, in
respect of any such engagement or contract, a larger sum than the charge or fare specified
in or to be calculated according to the tariff of charges and fares for the time being in
force; and any livery stable keeper or other person on his or her behalf demanding or
taking any charge, fare, or sum in contravention of this section shall be guilty of an
offence and liable to a fine of one hundred dollars.

### 29.

Tariffs of charges and fares to be printed on cards for distribution amongst all
livery stable keepers
(1) Whenever and so often as a tariff of charges and fares shall have been settled or
amended by the Minister, the Permanent Secretary shall cause such tariff to be distinctly
printed on cards in legible letters and figures, and shall furnish each livery stable keeper
(at the prescribed price for such cards) with as many copies thereof as he or she may
require for the purposes of complying with the provisions of this section.
(2) Every livery stable keeper shall obtain from the Permanent Secretary at least one
of the said cards for each of his or her business establishments and shall have one such
card affixed in a conspicuous place near to each of the principal entrances of each of his
or her business establishments, so that the contents of such card can be distinctly seen by
every customer coming thereto; and every livery stable keeper shall, under a like penalty,
obtain from the Permanent Secretary one of the said cards for each of the vehicles used in
his or her business and require the drivers of his or her vehicles to carry and keep the said
card for production to any passenger or hirer on demand; and every such driver who
omits to produce such card for the inspection of any passenger or hirer who may require
to see it shall be guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of one hundred dollars.
(3) If a livery stable keeper contravenes any of the provisions of subsection (2) he or
she shall be guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of one hundred dollars.

### 30.

Minister may cause inspection to be made of livery stables
It shall be lawful for the Minister to cause an inspection to be made by any fit and
proper person or persons if and whenever he or she may deem it necessary, of all vehicles
and animals used by a livery stable keeper in his or her business, and if any such vehicle
or animal shall at any time be in a condition unfit for public use, the fact shall be notified
to such livery stable keeper in writing specifying in what respect it is so unfit; and if, after
such notice, a livery stable keeper shall let to hire any such vehicle or animal whilst in a
condition unfit for public use, he or she shall be guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of
fifty dollars for every day that he or she shall so use or let the same for hire.
Commercial Travellers

### 31.

Fee for commercial traveller’s licence
(1) Every commercial traveller whether resident or non-resident in Grenada shall,
before commencing business in Grenada, obtain a licence from the Permanent Secretary
who is hereby empowered to issue the same on payment into the Treasury of the sum set
out in Part II or, as the case may be, Part III of the First Schedule.
(2) The licence shall be in the form prescribed in the Third Schedule.
Supplemental
General Powers and Provisions

### 32.

Rules and forms to be made by Minister
(1) The Minister may, from time to time, as occasion may require and as it may be
deemed expedient for the better carrying into effect of the provisions of this Act—
(a)

make, alter or rescind rules;

(b)

add to, revoke or alter the forms to be used under this Act (including those
prescribed in the Schedules); and

(c)

direct that any forms and books be printed and distributed.

(2) A person who offends against any rules made hereunder shall be liable to a fine of
five hundred dollars.

### 33.

Forms of declaration to be issued from Treasury
(1) The Permanent Secretary shall cause to be prepared and issued such forms of
declaration as may be approved by the Minister and as may be required for carrying out
the purposes of this and the next succeeding section.
(2) Every person applying to take out any licence under this Act may be required by
the Permanent Secretary to fill up and sign a declaration or declarations in the prescribed
form, wherein shall be stated—
(a)

whether or not such person keeps any of the animals or articles mentioned in

## Part I of the First Schedule and, if he or she does, the number, particulars,

and description thereof;

(b)

whether or not such person carries on or follows any business or calling
mentioned in Part II of the First Schedule and, if he or she does, the
particulars of every such business or calling, and the name of the town
wherein the same is carried on; and

(c)

if a licence is unnecessary in respect of any of the above particulars under
the provisions of this Act such person shall also state in his or her
declaration in what respect and upon what ground, the licence is
unnecessary.

### 34.

Special notice requiring declaration may be served on any person whomsoever
(1) The Permanent Secretary may direct a special notice to be served upon any person
whomsoever, requiring such person to fill up, sign, and deliver to the officer named in
such notice a form of declaration to be left with such notice, stating therein the several
particulars required by the preceding section, and to pay the duties by which he or she
shall appear by such declaration to be chargeable to the officer named therein within
fourteen days from the date of the service of such special notice.
(2) Such notice may be served either personally or by being left at the usual or last
known place or abode of the person so served.
(3) Every person, who shall neglect or refuse to deliver any declaration in conformity
with the provisions of this or the preceding section, and every person who shall deliver a
declaration wherein the particulars required to be set forth shall not be fully and truly
stated, shall be guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of one thousand dollars, and that
over and above any duties to which he or she may be liable.

### 35.

Licence to be produced on demand or name and address declared: power to
arrest person refusing to give information
A revenue officer or police officer, who has reason to suppose any person to be liable
to take out a licence or licences under this or any other Act, may demand from such
person the production and delivery of such licence or licences, and if the person upon
whom the demand is made shall not produce any licence or licences within a reasonable
time or shall not permit the officer to read and examine the same when produced, it shall
be lawful for such officer to require such person to declare immediately his or her
Christian name and surname and place of residence; and if such person shall refuse to do
so he or she shall for such refusal forfeit a penalty not exceeding five hundred dollars
over and above any other penalty to which he or she may be liable under this or any other
Act; and it shall be lawful for such officer to arrest such person so refusing and convey
him or her before the magistrate having jurisdiction to hear the case.

### 36.

Authorised officer may enter upon land or premises
It shall be lawful for a revenue officer or police officer, who may require the
production of any licence, to enter and remain so long as may be necessary upon any land
or upon any premises (other than a dwelling-house) for the purpose of making the
demand specified in the preceding section.

### 37.

On reasonable suspicion that no licence has been duly taken out for an animal
or article, the same may be seized and, if unclaimed within ten days, sold
A police officer may seize and detain any animal or article specified in the First
Schedule for which animal or article he or she reasonably suspects no licence has been
taken out; and, if no person claims such animal or article within ten days after notice of
such seizure given by advertisement posted publicly at the nearest police station in the
district in which the seizure is made, the same shall be sold at auction at such nearest
police station in such manner as the magistrate may direct:
Provided that the officer in charge of any police station may kill or cause to be
destroyed any dog which has been seized under this section and the owner of which
cannot be found within ten days after its seizure.

### 38.

Power of Minister to order restitution of anything seized, or, after sale,
payment of proceeds, to persons entitled thereto
The Minister may direct a stay of any proceedings for the sale of anything seized
under this Act or for the recovery of any penalty or sum of money, or, in cases not
previously heard and adjudged by a magistrate, may order the restitution of the whole or
any part of anything seized as aforesaid or, after any sale thereof, may order the whole or
any part of the net proceeds of such sale to be paid to any person who appears entitled
thereto or shows good cause to invalidate the seizure.

### 39.

Power to suspend or revoke a licence to follow business or calling
(1) If a person who has taken out a licence to follow any business or calling under this
Act—
(a)

becomes bankrupt, or insane; or

(b)

has been found guilty of any conduct which in the opinion of the Minister
renders him or her unfit to continue to hold such licence,

such licence may be suspended or revoked by Order of the Minister.
(2) Every such Order shall be published in the Gazette.

### 40.

No licence to be issued to person, after suspension or revocation of a licence
issued to him or her, without leave of Minister
Whenever the Minister shall have caused a licence, issued to any person hereunder, to
be suspended or revoked, such person shall not be permitted to take out a licence for the
like purpose at any future time unless or until the consent of the Minister shall have been
first obtained; and any licence issued in contravention of this section shall be null and
void.
Penalties and Procedure

### 41.

Obstruction of public officer or lawful authority

Every person in any manner hindering, molesting or obstructing a revenue officer or
police officer or any other person in the exercise of the power or duties conferred or
imposed on him or her by this Act shall be guilty of an offence against this Act.

### 42.

Penalty for forging or counterfeiting any licence, badge or certificate
A person who forges or counterfeits any licence, badge or certificate issued under this
Act or who knowingly uses any forged licence, badge or certificate, shall be guilty of a
misdemeanour and liable to imprisonment for twelve months.

### 43.

General penalty
A person who is guilty of an offence against this Act in respect of which no specific
penalty is provided, shall be liable to a fine of one thousand dollars.

### 44.

Procedure for recovery of penalties, etc.
All offenders against this Act or against any rules made hereunder may be prosecuted
by the Permanent Secretary, the Comptroller of Inland Revenue, the Chief of Police or by
any police officer for the time being in charge of a police station, before the magistrate of
the district in which the offence occurs; and the procedure in such complaint shall be in
accordance with the procedure prescribed by any Act now or hereafter to be in force
regulating the duties of and procedure before magistrates.

### 45.

Minister may reward witnesses, in prosecutions hereunder, with a portion of
penalties recovered
If in any proceeding under this Act a person gives evidence therein whereby any other
person is ordered to pay a fine or penalty or whereby any forfeiture is ordered under this
Act, the Minister may if he or she sees fit direct payment to the person giving such
evidence of a sum not exceeding one-half of the amount recovered in respect of such
order.

### 46.

Onus probandi
Whenever in any prosecution under this Act the defendant claims to have been
licensed, or claims any qualification or exemption from liability, the burden of proving
such licence, qualification or exemption shall lie on him or her.

### 1.

Citation
These Rules may be cited as the Licences Rules.
Licensed Boats

### 2.

Name of boat to be painted
Every owner of a licensed boat shall cause the number of his or her boat to be painted
on both sides of the bow of his or her boat in figures of not less than four inches in height
and the name, if any, of his or her boat to be conspicuously and legibly painted on the
stern, and not on a board removable at pleasure, in letters not less than two inches in
height. Such figures and letters shall be black, if the boat is painted of a light colour and
white if the boat is painted of a dark colour.

### 3.

Boatman to wear badge
Every boatman shall wear his or her badge on his or her belt in front and shall keep a
printed card containing these Regulations and the tariff of boat fares, which shall be
produced on the requisition of the port officer or of a passenger.

### 4.

“Port officer”
In the above rules the term “port officer” includes any officer, non-commissioned
officer or private of the Police Force on duty on board, or told off to attend the arrival and
departure of any vessel.

### 5.

Disorderly conduct
If any licensed boatman, when pursuing his or her calling as such, is drunk or behaves
himself or herself in a disorderly manner, or refuses to obey the orders of the port revenue
or police officers, such boatman on conviction before a Magistrate shall be liable to a
penalty not exceeding one hundred dollars to be recovered in such manner as the Minister
may direct.

### 6.

Interpretation
In these Rules, unless the context otherwise requires, “motor boat” includes any boat
powered with an outboard engine.

not exceeding fifty dollars.
