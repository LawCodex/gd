## CHAPTER 51

CINEMATOGRAPH ACT
An Act to regulate and control cinematograph and other exhibitions, and to
provide for connected matters.
[Act No. 25 of 1963 amended by SRO 16 of 1964, Act No. 1 of 1967, Act No. 14 of 1973.]

[25th January, 1964.]

## PART I

Preliminary

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Cinematograph Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act—
“Board” means the Board of Film Censors constituted under section 10;
“censor” means a member of the Board;
“cinematograph exhibition” means any exhibition of pictures or other optical
effects produced by means of film in a projector or other similar apparatus;
“exhibitor” means a person licensed under this Act to hold cinematograph
exhibitions;
“film” means cinematograph film;
“Licensing Authority” means the Minister or such person as may be appointed
Licensing Authority under section 4;
“poster” includes any printed pamphlet, placard or other printed matter used for
the purpose of advertising a film or the holding of a cinematograph exhibition, but
does not include a newspaper or magazine article being a description of or a
comment upon a film;
“theatre” includes any premises, whether or not covered or enclosed, and any
other place whatsoever, which is used or intended for the holding of cinematograph
exhibitions.

## PART II

Licensing

### 3.

Exhibitions not to be held unless in licensed premises by a licensed person

(1) It shall not be lawful to hold any cinematograph exhibition except in a theatre
for the time being licensed under this Act and in accordance with the regulations
contained in the Schedule to, or made under, this Act.
(2) No person shall carry on the business of holding cinematograph exhibitions for
public entertainment unless he or she is the holder of an exhibitor’s licence.

Licensing Authority, kinds of licence, etc.
(1) Licences under this Act shall be issued by the Licensing Authority.

(2) The Licensing Authority shall be the Minister or such person as he or she may
appoint as the Licensing Authority for the purposes of this Act.
(3) Licences issued under this Act are of two kinds—
(a)

a theatre licence which specifies a place and authorises its use as a
theatre; and

(b)

an exhibitor’s licence which authorises a person to hold cinematograph
exhibitions.

(4) Licences shall, unless earlier cancelled or suspended, expire after one year
from the date of issue or on such earlier date as the Licensing Authority may therein
specify.
(5) A person, having made application in accordance with the provisions of this
Act, shall be granted a licence only if he is considered by the Licensing Authority to be
fit to be the holder of the kind of licence for which he or she has applied.

### 5.

Application for theatre licence

(1) (a) On making application for a theatre licence, the applicant shall state his or
her name, residential address and occupation, the situation of the theatre to be
licensed, and any other facts to support his or her application that he or she considers
relevant. If, at the date of the application, the erection of the theatre has not been
commenced or completed, the applicant shall furnish a proper and sufficient plan
thereof and a specification of the furnishings and fittings proposed for installation
therein.
(b) On receipt of the application the Licensing Authority shall fix a date for the
hearing of the application (being a date not earlier than twenty-one days following
such receipt) and, at least fourteen days before the hearing, shall—
(i) publish in the Gazette and in a daily newspaper in circulation in
Grenada, and
(ii) deliver to the Chief of Police and to the Comptroller of Inland
Revenue,
a notice stating the nature of the application and specifying the date, time and place
fixed for the hearing.
(2) At the hearing—
(a)

the applicant may appear and be heard in person or by counsel or a
solicitor and he or she may adduce evidence in support of his or her
application;

(b)

the following may appear and be heard in person or by counsel or a
solicitor and may adduce evidence in support of an objection to the
application—
(i) an owner or occupier of any property situate within a quarter of a
mile of the theatre with which the application is concerned,
(ii) the Comptroller of Inland Revenue or some other officer of the
Department of Inland Revenue duly authorised by him or her in
writing, and
(iii) the police officer in charge within whose police district the theatre
lies.

(3) The procedure in respect of every application shall be the same as in a
magistrate’s court.

Application for exhibitor’s licence

(1) On making application for an exhibitor’s licence an applicant shall state his
name, residential address and occupation.
(2) On receipt of the application the Licensing Authority shall fix a date for the
hearing, and give notice thereof, in accordance with the requirements of section
5(1)(b).
(3)
Section 5(2) applies with respect to attendance at the hearing of an
application for an exhibitor’s licence with the following modifications, namely, the
deletion of sub-paragraphs (i), (ii) and (iii) and the substitution of the following—
(i) the Chief of Police or some other police officer duly authorised by him or
her in writing,
(ii) the Comptroller of Inland Revenue or some other officer of the
Department of Inland Revenue duly authorised by him in writing,
and section 5(3) applies with respect to the procedure at such a hearing.

### 7.

Matters for the Licensing Authority to consider

(1) For the purpose of deciding whether or not to grant an application, the
Licensing Authority shall consider—
(a)

the evidence given and the arguments adduced at the hearing;

(b)

in the case of an application for a theatre licence, the proximity of the site
in relation to the nearest residential area and to local amenities such as
any school, hospital, place of religious worship, and any private or public
institution;

(c)

in the case of an application for an exhibitor’s licence—
(i) any conviction of the applicant for an offence under this Act or the
regulations,
(ii) whether or not the application is for the grant of a licence to the
applicant or in reality to some other person.

(2) (a) A theatre licence shall not be issued unless the Licensing Authority is
satisfied that adequate provision has been or will be made to ensure—
(i) that the sound generated by any performance at the theatre will not be a
source of undue annoyance or discomfort to the occupiers of buildings in
the vicinity,
(ii) the existence of facilities for the parking of vehicles so as to avoid undue
traffic congestion and of sufficient means of access to the theatre,
(iii) that any cinematograph exhibition or other performance at the theatre is
unlikely in any manner to constitute a public nuisance;
(b) a theatre licence shall not be issued unless the Licensing Authority is
satisfied by the certificate of a duly qualified architect or engineer or by other sufficient
evidence that adequate measures have been or will be taken to ensure the safe and
proper accommodation of persons attending a cinematograph exhibition at the theatre,
and to eliminate the risk of harm to them arising from any structural defect or in the
event of an outbreak of fire;
(c) a licence shall not be issued to an applicant in respect of a mobile theatre or
for the holding of cinematograph exhibitions by means thereof unless the Licensing
Authority is satisfied that the applicant is in possession of a certificate of approval
issued by the Chief of Police and the Chief Medical Officer and has paid both the fees
prescribed in respect of a theatre licence and in respect of an exhibitor’s licence.

Appeals to Board of Appeal

(1) The applicant and any person whose objection to an application has been heard
by the Licensing Authority may appeal to a Board of Appeal against the decision of the
Authority.
(2) The Governor-General may, from time to time, appoint a Board of Appeal,
consisting of two fit and proper persons as members of the Board of Appeal and a
magistrate as Chairperson of the Board of Appeal.
(3) The procedure in respect of every appeal shall be the same as in a magistrate’s
court.

### 9.

Court may cancel or suspend licence

If a licensee is convicted of an offence under this Act, the court before which he or
she has been convicted shall have power to cancel, or suspend for such period as may
seem just, the licence or licences held by him.

## PART III

Censorship

### 10.

Appointment of Board of Film Censors

(1) The Minister shall appoint annually five persons, or such greater number as he
or she considers necessary, to constitute a Board of Film Censors, one of whom he or
she shall nominate to act as chairperson of the Board, and he or she shall cause a
notice of every such appointment and nomination to be published in the Gazette.
(2) The chairperson may, with the approval of the Minister, appoint a secretary to
the Board, at such remuneration and upon such conditions as the chairperson shall
determine.

### 11.

Offence to exhibit film or poster without a certificate of approval

(1) A film shall not be exhibited unless a certificate of approval in respect of it has
been issued by the Board under section 12.
(2) A person who exhibits a film or portion of a film in contravention of
subsection (1) is guilty of a summary offence and liable to a fine of four thousand
dollars in respect of each occasion upon which such exhibition is proved to have
occurred. The court before which a person is convicted of an offence under this
subsection may order the forfeiture of the film or portion thereof in respect of which
the offence has been committed, whereupon the Chief of Police shall have authority to
dispose of the same in such manner as he or she thinks fit.
(3) A poster shall not be exhibited, displayed, published or distributed unless a
certificate of approval in respect of it has been issued by the Board under section 12.
(4) Any person who exhibits, displays, publishes or distributes a poster in
contravention of subsection (3) is guilty of a summary offence and liable to a fine of
two thousand dollars.

### 12.

Censorship and issue of certificates of approval

(1) The Board in its discretion may, without examination by any censor, issue a
certificate of approval in respect of a film or poster if satisfied, by the production of a
certificate of censorship, that the film or poster has been censored and approved by
the competent authority under the laws of Trinidad and Tobago.
(2) Subject to subsection (1), the duty of a censor is to examine every film and
poster submitted to him or her for examination and to report thereon to the
chairperson of the Board.

(3) A certificate of approval shall be in the prescribed form and may be revoked at
any time by notice in writing given by the Board.
(4) The Board shall not issue a certificate of approval in respect of a film or poster
the exhibition of which, in the opinion of the Board, is for any reason undesirable in
the public interest and, in particular, which in the opinion of the Board depicts any
matter that is contrary to public order or decency.
(5) A certificate of approval in respect of the exhibition of a film may be issued
unconditionally, or with either or both of the following conditions attached, namely—
(a)

that some specified portion of the film shall not be exhibited;

(b)

that the film or any portion thereof shall not be exhibited except to
persons of a specified age or sex at certain times.

(6) A film to which any matter has been added after the certificate of approval in
respect of the film has been issued shall be again submitted for approval, and the
entire film shall be deemed not to have been approved until the Board has issued a
certificate of approval in respect of the original film together with the added matter.

### 13.

Appeal to Minister

A person who is aggrieved by a decision of the Board may appeal against the
decision to the Minister.

## PART IV

Miscellaneous

### 14.

Offence to exhibit certain types of film

(1) An exhibitor who exhibits any film which—
(a)

portrays an act of sexual intercourse or, unless the film is of historical or
educational value, depicts scenes of excessive violence;

(b)

contains spoken or written words, or gestures, that are obscene; or

(c)

principally depicts the use of any controlled drug within the meaning of
the Misuse of Drugs Act, unless there has been issued in respect of the
film a certificate of approval with a condition that it shall not be exhibited
except to persons over the age of twenty-one years,

is guilty of a summary offence and liable to a fine of one thousand five hundred dollars
and to imprisonment for twelve months, and the court shall suspend his or her
exhibitor’s licence for a period not exceeding one year.
(2) Proceedings for an offence under subsection (1) shall not be instituted without
the written authority of the Director of Public Prosecutions.

### 15.

Exhibitor to maintain a record of exhibitions and to furnish a return

(1) An exhibitor shall, in respect of every theatre where he or she carries on
business, maintain a register in the form prescribed and he or she shall, as soon as
practicable after the occasion of each cinematograph exhibition at the theatre, record
in the register the title, origin, length, and such other particulars as the regulations
may prescribe, of every film exhibited on that occasion. For the purposes of this
subsection, an exhibitor whose theatre consists of a tent or other movable structure
shall not be required in respect of that theatre to maintain more than one register. The
Chief of Police or a police officer duly authorised by him or her may at any reasonable
time enter a theatre and require the production of, and inspect, the register.
(2) An exhibitor shall, not later than the fifteenth day of every month, furnish the
chairperson of the Board with a return in such form as may be prescribed containing,

in respect of each theatre under his or her control, the details recorded under
subsection (1) concerning every cinematograph exhibition held during the immediately
preceding month.
(3) An exhibitor who fails to maintain a register, or to record therein particulars, as
required by subsection (1), or who fails to furnish a return as required by subsection
(2), or who makes a false entry in any register or return, is guilty of a summary
offence and liable to a fine of one thousand dollars.

### 16.

Offences generally

If a person—
(a)

being the owner of apparatus capable of exhibiting film, uses that
apparatus or permits it to be used;

(b)

supplies illumination for any such apparatus knowing or having reason to
believe that it is being or will be used; or

(c)

being the owner or occupier of any building or place whatsoever, whether
he or she is the holder of a theatre licence or not, uses the building or
place or permits the same to be used,

in contravention of any of the provisions of this Act or of a condition attached to a
certificate of approval or of any condition of a licence, he or she is guilty of a summary
offence and liable to a fine of two thousand dollars and, in the case of a continuing
offence, to a further fine of one thousand dollars in respect of each day during which
the offence is proved to have been continued.

### 17.

Power of police or censor to enter

(1) The Chief of Police or a police officer duly authorised by him or her or a censor,
for the purpose of ascertaining whether or not the provisions of this Act or the
regulations or a condition attached to a certificate of approval or the conditions of a
licence are being or have been complied with, may enter any building or place where
he or she has reasonable grounds to believe that a cinematograph exhibition is being
or is about to be held.
(2) If any person prevents or obstructs the entry of the Chief of Police or such
police officer or a censor acting in accordance with the provisions of subsection (1),
such person is guilty of a summary offence and liable to a fine of one thousand dollars.

### 18.

Power of Minister to make regulations

The Minister may make regulations, not inconsistent with this Act, generally for
giving effect to the provisions of this Act and, in particular but without prejudice to the
generality of the foregoing, for prescribing—
(a)

fees or charges payable in respect of any application, licence, certificate or
other document under this Act;

(b)

the form or content of any application, licence, register or other document
required for the purpose of this Act;

(c)

the duties, and regulating the procedure of, the Licensing Authority, the
Board of Appeal and the Board of Film Censors;

(d)

the conditions subject to which licences may be issued;

(e)

offences against the regulations made under this section and penalties
therefor not exceeding in respect of any one offence a fine of ten
thousand dollars and imprisonment for twelve months or in respect of a
continuing offence a fine of one thousand dollars for each day of
continuance;

(f)

any other matter required or authorised to be prescribed by this Act.

CINEMATOGRAPH REGULATIONS
[Section 18.]

---

### 1.

Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Cinematograph Regulations.

### 2.

Definitions
In these Regulations—
“building” means any structure used or intended to be used for the exhibition of
films to the public and includes a yard, external passage-way or open space
connected therewith;
“enclosure” means that part of a building in which cinematograph apparatus is
installed and operated;
“exit” includes emergency exit and passage-way;
“new building” means a building erected or adapted for use for the exhibition of
films to the public and brought into use after the commencement of these
Regulations;
“rewinding room” means a room designed and constructed with the approval of
the Chief Technical Officer (Works) and used for the rewinding of film;
“theatre” means a building or place in respect of which a theatre licence has
been issued under the Act;
“vault” means a vault designed and constructed with the approval of the Chief
Technical Officer (Works).

## PART I

Provisions for the Safety of the Audience and Prevention of Fire

### 3.

Design and construction

The design and construction of all new buildings shall be subject to the approval of
the Chief Technical Officer (Works).

### 4.

Exits, etc., to be clearly indicated

(1) All exits from a building provided for members of the public or the staff
employed in connection with the building or with cinematograph exhibitions in the
building shall be suitably indicated by notices bearing the word “EXIT” in six-inch
letters and all such notices shall be tinted green and illuminated by green lights.
(2) All other doors and openings leading from the auditorium, gallery or any
passageway into a dead end or such other places as are unsafe or unsuitable for the
purpose of egress shall be indicated by notices bearing the words “NO
THOROUGHFARE” in six-inch letters and all such notices shall be tinted red and
illuminated by red lights.

(3) Where doubt may arise as to the direction of exit, or where persons might
unwittingly move into danger, a notice bearing the words “TO EXIT” in six-inch letters
and tinted green or illuminated by green lights shall be provided.
(4) All notices required by the three foregoing paragraphs shall be illuminated in
such manner as may be specified by the electricity inspector of the Government.
(5) Wherever possible all such notices shall be placed above the doors to which
they relate, but in any case they shall not be less than nine inches above floor level.

### 5.

Gangways, etc., not to be obstructed

No chairs, seats or other obstructions shall be placed or allowed to remain in
gangways or in any place used for the safe and speedy egress of the public or
members of the staff employed in connection with any building or cinematograph
exhibition in a building.

### 6.

Standing permitted in certain places

Members of the audience shall be permitted to stand in any theatre during any
cinematograph exhibition in places set aside for that purpose and provided that ingress
and egress of other persons are not hindered. Any person who continues to stand
when requested not to do so shall be guilty of an offence and liable on summary
conviction to a fine of two hundred dollars.

### 7.

Exits to be kept clear

Whenever the public is present at any cinematograph exhibition exits shall be kept
free of inflammable tapestries or any other object likely to restrict egress by the public
from the building in which such cinematograph exhibition is being held.

### 8.

Shutters and gates to be left open

All sliding or folding shutters or roller or collapsible gates of a building shall be left
fully open whilst the public is present at any cinematograph exhibition.

### 9.

Provision in connection with floor coverings and curtains

(1) Floor coverings of buildings shall be fixed in such a way as to prevent rucking
or obstruction, and shall be sunk to floor level. All floors shall be maintained in a nonskid condition.
(2) All curtains shall be hung not less than two inches clear of floors, and shall
slide freely.

### 10.

Provisions in connection with enclosures

(1) Ventilation in enclosures shall be as approved by the Chief Technical Officer
(Works).
(2) Two openings for each projector in an enclosure shall be provided and the
construction thereof shall be subject to the approval of the Chief Technical Officer
(Works).
(3) All furniture and fittings within an enclosure shall be of non-combustible material.
(4) No film cement shall be kept in an enclosure.
(5) No winding or rewinding of films shall take place in an enclosure whilst
members of the public are present on the premises or the projectors are in use. In
new buildings separate rewinding rooms shall be provided and shall be subject to the
approval of the Chief Technical Officer (Works).
(6) No smoking shall be permitted in enclosures or rewinding rooms. Notices to
this effect shall be permanently affixed in a prominent position.

(7) Films other than those being projected, and being prepared for projection, shall
be kept in a non-combustible container.
(8) Barriers and notices shall be provided to prevent members of the public coming
into contact with enclosures.

Apparatus and installations

All electrical apparatus and installations shall be of a standard and specification
approved by the electricity inspector of the Government.

### 12.

Projectors

Projectors shall be of a type approved by the electricity inspector of the
Government and shall be placed on firm fire-resisting supports.

### 13.

Film exceeding one hundred thousand feet to be stored in vault

Not more than five hundred pounds (one hundred thousand feet) of film shall be
permitted to be kept in any building unless stored in a vault and in any case not more
than one hundred and twenty-five pounds (twenty-five thousand feet) shall be kept
exposed at a time.

### 14.

Cementing of films

(1) No collodion, amyl acetate or other similar flammable substance for the
cementing of film shall be kept other than in rewinding rooms or approved fireresisting cabinets or safes, or in any quantity exceeding one Imperial pint.
(2) Splices in film shall be made by a cutting and splicing machine approved by the
Chief of Police.
(3) Motion picture projectors shall be operated by and be in charge of a
projectionist to be licensed by the electricity inspector of the Government.

### 15.

Provisions as to the auditorium

The auditorium and exits therefrom to the outside building, including any external
courts, passage ways, stairways, or ramps, the notices indicating position of exits, and
all parts of the building to which the public is admitted shall throughout be adequately
illuminated during the whole time the public is present at a cinematograph exhibition.
In the event of the failure of the main lighting the auditorium shall be at once
illuminated by a secondary lighting service, and remain so illuminated until the main
lighting is repaired or until all members of the public have left the premises.

### 16.

Residence in building prohibited

No person shall be permitted to reside in any building used for the purpose of the
holding of cinematograph exhibitions.

### 17.

Fires to be reported

All fires occurring in a building during the holding of any cinematograph exhibition
shall be immediately reported by the exhibitor to the nearest fire or police station.

### 18.

Fire equipment to be provided

Fire equipment shall be provided and maintained
accordance with the directions of the Chief of Police.

### 19.

Attendants at exhibitions

throughout

buildings

in

(1) In order to secure the safety of the audience the exhibitor or some responsible
person designated by him or her shall be in attendance throughout cinematograph
exhibitions. He or she shall be assisted by such staff as the Chief of Police shall deem
necessary.
(2) All such persons shall be fully instructed in their duties and all attendants shall
wear a distinctive uniform or armband.
(3) During cinematograph exhibitions at which children are present the number of
attendants shall be such as may be required by the Chief of Police.

### 20.

Notices to be affixed in building for action in event of fire

There shall be in the enclosure, rewinding room, pay-office and manager’s office of
every building a prominent notice detailing action in the event of fire or any other
unusual occurrence.

### 21.

Exhibition of film may be stopped by police

The Chief of Police or any member of the police force may at any time without prior
notice require a cinematograph exhibition which is being held in a building to be
stopped if in his opinion circumstances exist which would constitute a danger to the
public should a fire or other unusual happening occur.

### 22.

Person aggrieved may appeal to Minister

Whenever any person shall withhold his or her approval of the doing of or omission
to do anything in connection with a building or the furniture, fittings, electrical
apparatus or installations thereof, which is subject to his or her approval under these
Regulations, the person aggrieved by his or her decision may, within seven days of the
date of receipt of notification that such approval has been withheld, appeal against
such decision to the Minister who may, in his discretion or her, authorise the doing of
or the omission to do such thing either unconditionally or subject to such conditions as
he or she may think fit to impose or may confirm such decision:
Provided that if the person whose approval is withheld is of the opinion that the
doing of or omission to do any such thing would constitute a danger to persons
attending any cinematograph exhibition in such building he or she shall so notify the
person seeking his or her approval and forward a certificate to that effect to the
Minister; and thereupon such building shall cease to be used for the holding of any
cinematograph exhibition until the Minister shall so authorise and, if the Minister shall
so authorise, such building shall only be used for the holding of cinematograph
exhibitions subject to such conditions as the Minister may impose.

### 23.

Unauthorised person not allowed in enclosure

No unauthorised person shall be allowed in an enclosure.

### 24.

Contraventions liable to fine or imprisonment or both

If the occupier of the theatre shall contravene any of the provisions of
regulation 3(1) or any exhibitor shall contravene any of the provisions of regulation 5,
8, 10(6), 13 or 15 such occupier or exhibitor, as the case may be, shall be guilty of an
offence and liable on summary conviction to a fine of eight thousand dollars and to
imprisonment for twelve months, and, in the case of a continuing offence, to a further
fine of one thousand dollars for each day during which the offence continues.

### 25.

Contraventions liable to fine

If the occupier of the theatre shall contravene any of the provisions of
regulation 3(2), 4, 9, 10(1), 10(2), 10(3), 10(5), 10(8), 11 or 12 or any exhibitor shall
contravene any of the provisions of regulation 6, 7, 10(4), 10(5), 10(7), 14, 16, 17,
18, 19, 20 or 23 such occupier or exhibitor, as the case may be, shall be guilty of an
offence and liable on summary conviction to a fine of two thousand dollars, and in the

case of a continuing offence, to a further fine of five hundred dollars for each day
during which the offence continues.

## PART II

Censorship

Definitions

In the following regulations—
“Board” means the Board of Film Censors;
“chairperson” means the chairperson of the Board.

### 27.

Chairperson to keep book for entry of films for exhibition

The chairperson shall keep a book in which shall be entered particulars of all films
submitted for examination, such particulars to include the names of such films, the
names of exhibitors of such films and the names of the censors by whom such films
were examined and their decision regarding them.

### 28.

Trailer to be submitted for examination

The separate film used for advertising purposes and commonly called “a trailer”
must be submitted for examination and shall be subject to the provisions of the Act.

### 29.

Vacation of post by censor

Unless he or she has been absent through ill-health or with the leave or under the
direction of the Minister, any censor shall be considered to have vacated his or her
post as censor who has not on the last day of any quarter attended at least one-third
of the censorship meetings for the examination of films or film posters which he or she
had been deputed by the chairperson to attend, or who had not on the last day of any
year attended at least one-third of the censorship meetings of the Board for other
purposes held during the preceding twelve months or during his or her tenure of office
during such months.

### 30.

Minister may fill vacancy

The Minister may grant leave of absence to any censor and may temporarily fill the
vacant place or places during such absence.

## PART III

Licences and Fees

### 31.

Fees

The following annual fees shall be paid to the Permanent Secretary (Finance) in
advance for the following licences, namely—
In respect of a theatre in the town of Saint George .................................... $100.00
In respect of a theatre in the town of Grenville ............................................ $50.00
In respect of a theatre elsewhere ............................................................... $25.00
In respect of an exhibitor .......................................................................... $25.00
Provided that in respect of any application made on or after the first day of July in
any year, the fee payable shall be one-half of the annual fee.

Duration

All licences shall commence on the first day of January or from the date of issue is
issued subsequent to the first day of January in any year, and shall expire on the
thirty-first day of December in that year unless previously cancelled or suspended.

Forms

A licence for a theatre shall be in Form A and a licence for an exhibitor shall be in
Form B subject to such modifications or other conditions as may be required in the
circumstances of each particular licence.
