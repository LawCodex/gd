## CHAPTER 253

PRISON (EXTRA-MURAL SENTENCES) ACT
An Act to provide for the employment of persons sentenced to short terms of
imprisonment on public works outside the prison.
[Amended by Act No. 7 of 1960, Act No. 53 of 1979, Act No. 66 of 1979 and Act No. 36 of
1980.]

[19th December, 1953.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Prison (Extra-Mural Sentences) Act.
2\. Interpretation
In this Act—
“offender” means a person falling within the category of persons described in
sections 3(1)(a), (b) and (c) who is employed in public work outside a prison in
accordance with the provisions of that section;
“public work” means work prescribed as public work.
3\. Public work in lieu of imprisonment
(1) Notwithstanding any law to the contrary any of the following persons, namely—
(a)

a person sentenced by a court to imprisonment for a term not exceeding six
months;

(b)

a woman convicted of an offence which in the opinion of the court is not a
grave offence;

(c)

a person liable to be committed to prison for non-payment of a fine or of a
sum of money ordered to be paid which fine or sum of money does not
exceed two hundred and fifty dollars,

may be ordered by the court on the advice of the Commissioner of Prisons, and with his
or her own consent, at the time of passing sentence, to be employed, in lieu of
imprisonment, on public work outside the prison under the supervision and control of a
public body as hereinafter provided.
(2) Any person serving a term of imprisonment not exceeding six months may, with
his or her own consent, be ordered by the Commissioner of Prisons to be employed, in
lieu of serving the remainder of the sentence in prison, on public work outside the prison
under the supervision and control of a public body as hereinafter provided.
(3) Every person shall be employed on a daily task which shall be such as can
normally be completed in the usual working day and at the usual working hours as the
Commissioner of Prisons shall direct and while not so employed shall not be subject to
any prison rules.
(4) Every person employed on such labour as aforesaid shall be required to report
daily during the term of his or her sentence to the Commissioner of Prisons or to such
persons as he or she shall nominate and at such time and place as he or she shall direct.
(5) If any such person fails to present himself or herself at the appointed time and
place or absents himself or herself from his or her task without permission, or works or
conducts himself or herself in a manner which the Commissioner of Prisons shall
consider unsatisfactory, the Commissioner may forthwith order that such person shall
undergo the confinement to which he or she is liable, subject nevertheless to a deduction
of the number of days on which he or she has completed his or her daily task, and he or
she shall thereupon be committed to prison accordingly.
4\. Regulations
(1) The Minister may make regulations—
(a)

prescribing the nature of the work which is to be regarded as public work for
the purposes of this Act;

(b)

providing for the issue of free food to offenders so far as appears to the
Minister to be necessary;

(c)

providing, in the case of an offender employed as provided in section 3 for
non-payment of a debt, for crediting him or her with wages in respect of
public work performed by him or her under this Act and for the application
of sums so credited in or towards the payment of such debt;

(d)

providing for the remission of part of the period of employment in public
works;

(e)

providing for any other matter to be prescribed under this Act;

(f)

generally for carrying the provisions of this Act into effect.

(2) Without prejudice to the power of the Minister to make regulations under
subsection (1), until such regulations are made by him or her the regulations in the
Schedule shall have effect.

### 1.

These Regulations may be cited as the Prison (Extra-Mural Sentences) Regulations.

### 2.

An offender may be assigned by the Commissioner to public work under the
supervision and control of a public body and be ordered to perform duties normally
carried out by that body.

### 3.

The public body to whom an offender is assigned shall provide the offender with
free food or, if free food is not provided, he or she shall be paid an allowance of three
dollars daily in lieu of free food.

### 4.

If an offender is employed on an extra-mural sentence for non-payment of a debt,
he or she shall be paid a wage for a normal working day, but one-half of each such wage
shall be deducted, and credited towards the payment of the debt, by the Commissioner of
Prisons.

### 5.

An offender employed on an extra-mural sentence shall be entitled to the same
remission of sentence as a prisoner serving his or her sentence in custody.

### 6.

An offender employed on an extra-mural sentence shall, at the time of commencing
the employment, notify his or her place of residence to the officer-in-charge of the police
station in the district in which he or she resides; any change of residence shall be similarly
notified.

### 7.

If the Commissioner of Prisons is satisfied that an offender has contravened
section 3(5) of the Act, he or she may, by written direction addressed to the Chief of
Police, require that the offender be delivered to the prison at Richmond Hill, and the
Chief of Police shall act accordingly.
