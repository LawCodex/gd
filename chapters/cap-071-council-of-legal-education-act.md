## CHAPTER 71

COUNCIL OF LEGAL EDUCATION ACT
An Act to give legislative effect to certain provisions of an agreement establishing
the Council of Legal Education and to provide for the incorporation in Grenada
of the Council.
[Act No. 19 of 1972 amended by Act No. 48 of 1972, SRO 10 of 1973, SRO 19 of 1985, Act No.
10 of 1990.]

[20th September, 1971.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Council of Legal Education Act.
2\. Interpretation
In this Act—
“Agreement” means the agreement, as from time to time amended, entered into by
the Governments of Bahamas, Barbados, Dominica, Grenada, Guyana, Jamaica,
Trinidad and Tobago, and by the Universities of Guyana and the West Indies, which
came into force on the 17th March, 1971 and provided for the establishment of the
Council;
“Council” means the Council of Legal Education established by the Agreement.
3\. Incorporation of the Council

The Council is deemed to be a body corporate established by this Act.
4\. Certain provisions of the Agreement to have the force of law
The provisions of Articles 1.2, 5, 6 and 9.6 of the Agreement have the force of law as
set forth in the Schedule.
5\. Power of Minister to amend the Schedule, etc.
(1) With effect from 17th March, 1973, the Minister responsible for education may,
by Order, amend the Schedule in a manner that corresponds to an amendment of the
Agreement if that amendment of the Agreement has been accepted by the Government.
(2) An Order under subsection (1) may contain such consequential, supplemental and
ancillary provisions as the Minister thinks are necessary or expedient for the giving of
effect to the amendment of the Schedule including any necessary amendment of a
reference in this Act to a particular provision of the Agreement.

ARTICLE 1.2
The Council shall possess full juridical personality and in particular full capacity—
(a)

to contract;

(b)

to acquire and dispose of property; and

(c)

to institute and defend legal proceedings.
ARTICLE 5

A person who holds a Legal Education Certificate fulfils the requirements for practice
in Grenada so far as institutional training and education are concerned. Subject to
paragraph 3 and to any other written law whereby reciprocal arrangements are made
between Grenada and a foreign country concerning admission to the practice of law in
Grenada, a person shall not be admitted to such practice unless he or she is the holder of a
Legal Education Certificate.
ARTICLE 6

---

### 1.

The following persons are recognised as being professionally qualified for
admission to practice in Grenada—
(a)

a national who was, on 1st October, 1972, qualified to be admitted to
practice as a barrister or solicitor in Grenada;

(b)

a national who before 1st January, 1985 had undergone or was undergoing or
had been accepted for a course of legal training leading to, and who has
since obtained, a qualification which, had it been obtained prior to 1st
October, 1972, would have been recognised by all of the participating
territories to the Agreement as being a qualification to be admitted to
practice as a barrister or solicitor in those territories.

### 2.

In paragraph 1, “national” means a person who—

(a)

is a citizen of any of the participating territories; or

(b)

is regarded as belonging to any of the participating territories under the law
in force in that territory.
ARTICLE 9.6

The Council, its assets, property, income, operations and transactions, are exempt
from direct taxation other than that which is no more than a charge for a public utility
service, and the Council is further exempt from customs duties on goods imported for its
official use.
