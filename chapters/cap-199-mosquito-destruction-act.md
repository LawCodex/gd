## CHAPTER 199

MOSQUITO DESTRUCTION ACT
An Act to provide for the destruction of mosquitoes.
[28th June, 1952.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Mosquito Destruction Act.
2\. Interpretation
In this Act—
“operator” means any person employed under this Act to spray premises;
“premises” includes lands, buildings, vehicles, structures of any kind, streams,
drains, ditches or places open, covered, or enclosed, whether built on or not, and
whether natural or artificial, any ship lying in any port or harbour, and any aircraft, in
Grenada;
“sanitary officer” means a medical officer or a sanitary inspector;
“to inspect premises” means to inspect premises to ascertain the presence of
mosquitoes and to examine any water in or upon premises whether contained in a
receptacle or not, for the purpose of ascertaining whether it contains any of the immature
stages of the mosquito;
“to spray premises” means to treat all buildings, and any other structure, and their
contents with Dichloro Diphenyl Trichlorethane or any other form of insecticide ordered
by the Chief Medical Officer.
3\. Right to inspect premises

It shall be lawful for any person authorised in writing by the Chief Medical Officer to
enter and inspect any premises between the hours of seven in the morning and six in the
evening or, in the case of business premises, during such hours as the business is in
progress; and any owner, occupier, or other person in charge of the premises who after
the production of such written authority refuses the admission of, or obstructs, or hinders,
or prevents, any such person from making the entry or inspection shall be guilty of an
offence and, on summary conviction thereof, liable to a fine of five hundred dollars.
4\. Operators
(1) The Minister may, from time to time, upon the recommendation of the Chief
Medical Officer, employ any number of operators who shall be paid for their services
from money provided by the House of Representatives.
(2) It shall be lawful for any operator, accompanied by a sanitary officer or authorised
in writing by him or her, to enter any premises for the purpose of spraying such premises,
at all reasonable times, having due regard to the nature of the premises, except that no
spraying of premises shall be done otherwise than between the hours of seven in the
morning and six in the evening.
(3) A person who refuses the admission of, or obstructs, or hinders, an operator or
sanitary officer in the execution of his or her duties under this section shall be guilty of an
offence and, on summary conviction thereof, liable to a fine of two hundred and fifty
dollars.
5\. Regulations
(1) The Minister may make regulations, as respects the whole or any part of Grenada,
for all or any of the following matters, that is to say—
(a)

the measures to be adopted for the eradication of mosquitoes;

(b)

the collection of information relating to the destruction of mosquitoes; and

(c)

the enforcement of the regulations.

(2) A penalty not exceeding the sum of five hundred dollars may be attached to the
breach of any regulation made under this section.
(3) The regulations contained in the subsidiary legislation to the Act shall be deemed
to have been made under the provisions of subsection (1).
6\. Who may prosecute
Legal proceedings in respect of any breach of this Act or the regulations made
thereunder may be taken by a sanitary officer attached to the district within which the
premises are situate, or by any person authorised in writing by the Chief Medical Officer.

MOSQUITO DESTRUCTION REGULATIONS
[Section 5, SRO 22 of 2003.]

1\. Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Mosquito Destruction Regulations.
2\. Definition
In these Regulations, “owner” includes the person for the time being entitled to
receive the rent of premises, or who would receive it if the premises were let.
3\. Duty of owner
The owner of premises shall keep in good repair any tanks, vats, barrels, and other
vessels, used for storing water, and all gutters and water pipes, and shall keep them in
such a condition as not to breed mosquitoes.
4\. Duty of occupier
The occupier of premises shall prevent water being retained and becoming a breeding
place of mosquitoes in any bottle, tin, box, shell or other receptacle of a like nature, kept
on the premises.
5\. Evidence of offence
The presence of mosquito larvae in any collection of water on premises shall be
evidence that the provisions of the last two preceding regulations have not been obeyed.
6\. Penalty

An owner or occupier, as the case may be, of premises, who contravenes any of the
provisions of regulation 3 or 4 of these Regulations shall be guilty of an offence and
liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of two hundred and fifty dollars in the case of a
first offence and, in the case of a subsequent offence, to a fine of five hundred dollars.
