## CHAPTER 69

CORONERS ACT
An Act relating to Coroners.
[Amended by Act No. 1 of 1981, Act No. 10 of 1990.]

[2nd November, 1896.]

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Coroners Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act—
“Coroner” includes the deputy of any Coroner;
“Court” means the High Court;
“district” means police district.
Coroners and their Jurisdiction

### 3.

Chief Justice is the Supreme Coroner
(1) The Chief Justice shall be ex officio Supreme Coroner.
(2) During any period when the Chief Justice is absent from Grenada in the
performance of his or her duties, the office of Supreme Coroner shall devolve upon the
Registrar of the Supreme Court or upon such person as the Governor-General may think
fit to appoint.
(3) The person upon whom the said office devolves as aforesaid shall, during such
period, discharge the duties and have the powers imposed and conferred upon the Chief
Justice by this Act.

### 4.

Magistrates ex officio Coroners
Every magistrate within the limits of his or her district shall be ex officio Coroner.

### 5.

Appointment of other Coroners
(1) The Governor-General may, from time to time, appoint either in lieu of or in
addition to any magistrate, any fit and proper person either permanently or for a limited
time to act as Coroner of any district, and may also revoke such appointment.
(2) Any Magistrate may in cases of actual necessity, with the approval of the
Governor-General, appoint a fit and proper person to be his or her deputy, and may
revoke such appointment.
(3) Every person so appointed as Coroner or Deputy Coroner shall for all purposes
connected with any inquiry or inquest which he or she may make or hold as Coroner or
Deputy Coroner, have all the powers of a Justice of the Peace.

### 6.

Coroner and jury to be subject to jurisdiction of Court
Every Coroner and Coroner’s jury shall be subject to the jurisdiction of the Court in
the same manner as Coroners and Coroners’ juries in England are subject to the
jurisdiction of Her Majesty’s High Court of Justice.

### 7.

Application of common law of England
In matters not provided for or varied by or repugnant to this Act, every Coroner shall
have the jurisdiction and authority within Grenada of a Coroner under the common law of
England.

### 8.

Coroner’s jurisdiction in cases of death out of district or outside Grenada
When the dead body of any person is brought within any port or place in Grenada, the
Coroner of the district where the body may be, shall if necessary, inquire concerning the
death of the person in the same manner and with the same powers as if the person had

died within the Coroner’s district, although the death or the cause of death may have
occurred beyond the limits of the district or outside Grenada.
Duties of Coroners and others in cases of Sudden or Suspicious or Unnatural Death

### 9.

Inquiry into cause of death of persons confined in prisons, lunatic asylums, etc.
(1) The keeper or other person in charge of any prison, mental hospital, or other place
of lawful detention, shall forthwith report to the Coroner of the district the death of any
person confined in any such public institution.
(2) It shall be the duty of the Coroner to inquire into the cause of every such death
and, where there is reasonable cause to suspect that such death was occasioned by
accident or violence, or in the case of a sudden death of which the cause is unknown, to
hold an inquest.

### 10.

Sudden deaths, etc.
(1) It shall be the duty of every Coroner to inquire into the cause of death, and, if
necessary, to hold an inquest—
(a)

in the case of every person found dead within his or her district;

(b)

in the case of every sudden or other death occurring within his or her district
where there is reasonable cause to suspect that such death was occasioned by
either accident or violence;

(c)

in the case of every sudden or unnatural death occurring within his or her
district of which the cause is unknown.

(2) Duties of constables and others.—Every constable upon being informed that a
person has been found dead, or that any such death as aforesaid has taken place, shall at
once report it to the Coroner of the district; and the person in charge of any public
institution, or of any estate, and the head of any household, in which there shall take place
any such death as is specified in subsection (1)(b) and (c), shall at once report it to the
Coroner.

### 11.

Inquiry by Coroner
Upon receiving any such report as is required by section 10, or as is required by
section 33(2) of the Registration of Births and Deaths Act, Chapter 280, the Coroner shall
cause the facts and circumstances attending the death reported to be carefully inquired
into under his or her direction by the police, and report thereof in writing to be made to
him or her; and if as a result of such inquiry and report the Coroner be of opinion that the
cause of death is not sufficiently apparent, or that there is a reasonable probability of the
death having been occasioned by violence, he or she shall, or if it appears that the death
was caused by accident he or she may in his or her discretion, have the body of the
deceased person examined by a medical practitioner, with or without a post-mortem
examination of the body or analysis of the contents of the stomach and intestines, and
cause such medical practitioner to report to him or her the result of such examination or
analysis.

### 12.

Report of result of inquiry to Chief Justice
If as the result of any such inquiry and report the Coroner is of opinion that the cause
of death is sufficiently apparent and that no further light would be thrown upon the case
by an inquest, he or she shall instead of holding an inquest, immediately draw up his or
her report on the case with his or her opinion and reasons for the same, and forward it
forthwith to the Chief Justice together with the information and report furnished by the
police and the medical report if any.

### 13.

Report, if approved, to be recorded
Such report if approved by the Chief Justice shall be indorsed with such approval and
forwarded by him or her to the Registrar of the Supreme Court to be preserved in his or
her office and there remain upon record:
Provided that the Chief Justice may on receipt of such report direct that an inquest
shall be held in cases where it seems to him or her advisable to do so:
Provided also that nothing contained in this section or section 12 shall prevent the
Coroner from holding an inquest after making such report—
(a)

before the receipt by the Registrar of the report with the approval of the
Chief Justice indorsed thereon;

(b)

after such receipt, if fresh facts have come to his or her knowledge
necessitating in his or her opinion the holding of an inquest.
Jury

### 14.

Number of jury, etc.
The jury on a Coroner’s inquest shall consist of five persons and no more, and it shall
suffice if four of the jurors concur in a verdict.

### 15.

Persons liable to serve as jurors
Every person between the ages of twenty-one and sixty years of age, except those
persons who are exempted from serving as jurors in cases tried in the High Court, shall be
liable to serve on a Coroner’s jury.

### 16.

Payment as jurors
Every person duly empanelled to serve as a juror shall receive for his or her loss of
time and expense such a sum not exceeding five dollars for each day of his or her
attendance at an inquest as the Coroner thinks fit. Such sums shall be paid to the jurors
from the Treasury on the authority of a certificate from the presiding Coroner.

### 17.

If necessary, bystanders to serve as jurors
If at the time and place appointed for the inquest any of the jurors duly summoned fail
to appear, the Coroner may direct so many of the bystanders to be empanelled as will
complete the number of jurors required.

### 18.

Jurors how summoned
The jury shall be summoned verbally by any police officer duly authorised by written
precept signed by the Coroner and directed to any police officer; and for this purpose the
police officer may enter into any store, shop, or other place of business to which the
public have access.
Witnesses

### 19.

Summoning witnesses
(1) A police officer may by verbal notice call upon any person who appears to be able
to give material evidence to attend before the Coroner and his or her jury at the time and
place of inquiry. The Coroner may, if he or she thinks fit, summon any person to attend
before him or her to give evidence.

(2) On application by any Coroner it shall be lawful for the Chief Justice at any time
by warrant to require any prisoner to be taken from any prison to any court or place, to
give evidence before the Coroner and his or her jury.
(3) The Coroner, upon the application, made either at the time of the inquiry or within
a week thereafter, of any person who has been duly summoned or required to attend as a
witness or has given evidence at the inquiry, may allow to such person such sum, if any,
as shall seem fit, as well for defraying his or her reasonable expenses as for affording him
or her compensation for his or her trouble and loss of time. The sum so allowed shall be
paid by the Permanent Secretary (Finance) on the authority of the Coroner’s certificate,
and shall be a charge on the Consolidated Fund.

### 20.

Summoning medical practitioner
Whenever it appears necessary to the Coroner or to the majority of the jury
empanelled on any inquest that medical evidence should be given touching the death of
the deceased, or that a post-mortem examination should be made, without an analysis of
the contents of the stomach and intestines, or with such analysis should the making of the
same be sanctioned by the Governor-General, it shall be lawful for the Coroner to
summon the medical practitioner who attended the deceased during his or her last illness,
or some other medical practitioner, as a witness at the inquest; and the Coroner in his or
her summons for the attendance of the medical practitioner, or at any time between the
issuing of the summons and the termination of the inquest, may direct the performance of
a post-mortem examination or analysis of the contents of the stomach and intestines. The
summons may be served personally by a police officer or left at the place of residence of
the medical practitioner.

### 21.

Witness may give evidence on affirmation
If any witness refuses or is unwilling from alleged conscientious motives to be sworn,
the Coroner may, upon being satisfied of the sincerity of the objection, allow the witness
to make affirmation.
Procedure upon Inquests and Inquiries

### 22.

Oath of jurors
As soon as the Coroner and jurors at any inquest have assembled, the Coroner shall
administer to each of the jurors the following oath—
“You shall diligently inquire and true presentment make, on behalf of
Our Sovereign Lady the Queen, when, where, how, and after what manner
............................................................................................................
(here name the deceased, if known, or, if unknown, state a person unknown
now dead), came to his or her death, and of such other matters relating to
such death as shall be lawfully inquired of by you—SO HELP YOU
GOD”.

### 23.

View of body
The Coroner or any jury may view the body, but it shall not be necessary at any
inquest or inquiry for the Coroner or the jury to view the body:
Provided that where at any inquest it appears to the Coroner, or to the greater
number of the jury attending at the inquest, to be expedient to view the body, the jury
shall thereupon proceed to view the body, and, if the body has been buried, the Coroner
shall order its exhumation for the purpose, unless he or she certifies that, in his or her
opinion, the exhumation would be useless for the purposes of the inquest or dangerous to
the public health.

### 24.

Depositions of witnesses, etc.
(a)

On every inquest.—The Coroner shall take the sworn information of all
material witnesses in writing.

(b)

Inquisition how to be signed.—The Coroner and every juror who concurs in
a verdict shall sign the inquisition with their usual signatures; but if any juror
cannot write he or she shall affix his or her mark; and no seal shall be
necessary or affixed to any inquisition.

(c)

Adjournment.—The Coroner may adjourn the proceedings from place to
place and from time to time as often as necessary.

(d)

Disagreement of jury.—If at the end of one hour the jury do not agree on a
verdict, it shall be lawful for the Coroner to direct them to find whether any
fact or series of facts is proved, and thereupon the jury shall proceed to
consider the matters thus left to their consideration. If the jury (or the
majority mentioned aforesaid) agree as to the fact or series of facts being
proved, the fact or series of facts shall be written down and shall form part of
the inquisition and shall be signed by the jurors or the majority of them
aforesaid. In the event of such agreement it shall be lawful for the Coroner,
should he or she be of opinion that the fact or series of facts so proved
justifies his or her so doing, to interpret the finding as one of murder or
manslaughter, or accidental death or otherwise, and the inquisition shall be
drawn up to that effect and shall be signed by the Coroner.

(e)

If the jury cannot agree at the end of two hours, and there is then no prospect
of their coming to agreement, the Coroner may discharge them without any
verdict being returned, and the inquisition shall be drawn up to that effect.

(f)

Certificate of Coroner at close of each day’s evidence.—At the conclusion
of each day’s evidence the Coroner shall certify it in the following form—

“The foregoing depositions of ................................................. were taken and sworn before me and
the further inquiry was adjourned to ................................................. at ............................... o’clock.
Coroner ................................................ District”.

### 25.

Apprehension of witness not attending
Where any person summoned as a witness at any inquest or inquiry refuses or neglects
to appear at the time and place appointed by the summons and otherwise to comply with
it, and no just excuse is offered for the refusal or neglect, then, after proof upon oath of
the summons having been served upon him or her either personally or by leaving it for
him or her with some person at his or her place of abode or having been communicated to
him or her in any manner authorised by this Act, it shall be lawful for the Coroner to issue
a warrant under his or her hand to bring the person, at a time and place therein mentioned,
before him or her to testify as aforesaid.

### 26.

Committal of witness refusing to be examined, etc.
Where, on the appearance of any person who has been so summoned, either in
obedience to the summons or on being brought by virtue of a warrant, or where any
person present is called on by the Coroner to give evidence, then if such person—
(a)

refuses to be examined upon oath concerning the premises; or

(b)

refuses to take the oath, or, having taken it, refuses to answer such questions
concerning the premises as may then be put to him or her; or

(c)

refuses to produce any document which he or she may be summoned to
produce; or

(d)

refuses to subscribe his or her deposition,

the Coroner may, by warrant under his or her hand, commit the person so refusing to
prison for any time not exceeding seven days, unless he or she shall in the meantime
consent to be examined and to answer concerning the premises, or to produce the
documents, if any, or to subscribe his or her deposition, as the case may be.

### 27.

Committal of accused
Where a verdict or finding of murder or manslaughter is returned against any person,
the Coroner shall forthwith make out his or her warrant for his or her committal to prison
thence to be brought before the Court at the sitting to be holden next after the taking of
the inquisition, and thereupon such person, if then present, shall forthwith be taken and
conveyed to prison, or, if absent, may be apprehended under warrant and conveyed as
aforesaid.

### 28.

Power to take bail in case of manslaughter
Where a verdict or finding of manslaughter is returned against any person, it shall be
lawful for the Coroner before or by whom the inquest or inquiry is taken to accept bail, if
he or she thinks fit, with good and sufficient sureties for the appearance of the person so
charged before the next sitting of the Court or before a magistrate and thereupon such
person, if in custody, shall be discharged therefrom.

### 29.

Copies of depositions
Every person who has been committed to prison or held to bail, under and by virtue of
any verdict of a Coroner’s jury or any finding of a Coroner, may require, and shall be
entitled to, copies of the depositions and of the statement of the accused, if any, on
payment of the like fees as are by law payable for copies of other depositions.

### 30.

On verdict of murder, etc., Coroner to bind over material witnesses
On every inquisition where a verdict is passed against any person of murder or
manslaughter or as accessory before the fact to any murder, the Coroner shall bind by
recognisance all material witnesses on the inquisition to appear and give evidence against
the person charged at the Court to be holden next after the taking of the inquisition.

### 31.

No objection to deposition or inquisition if duly signed
No objection shall be allowed to any deposition taken before any Coroner because it is
unsigned by the deponent, but every such deposition shall be good and valid if the
Coroner’s signature appears thereto attesting the taking thereof. No objection shall be
allowed to any inquisition if it is signed at the foot by the Coroner and four jurors.

### 32.

Coroner’s court not an open court
The room, building, or other place in which a Coroner’s court is held shall not be
deemed an open court, and it shall be lawful for the Coroner in his or her discretion to
exclude any person therefrom. The Coroner may in his or her discretion allow any
member of the family of the deceased, or any other person who may be interested in the
inquiry or inquest, to take part therein either personally or by his or her counsel or
solicitor.

### 33.

Proceedings on Sunday valid
Every summons served and warrant issued and every inquisition held or verdict found
on a Sunday shall be as valid as if served, issued, held, or found on any other day.

### 34.

Coroner to send inquisition to Registrar
(1) Immediately after the taking of every inquest, the Coroner shall transmit to the
Registrar of the Supreme Court the inquisition together with the depositions taken and all
papers and documents connected therewith.
(2) Every inquisition and the documents connected therewith shall be forthwith laid
by the Registrar of the Supreme Court before the Attorney-General and subsequently
before the Chief Justice and shall thereafter be preserved in the Registrar’s office and
there remain upon record.

### 35.

Liability to indictment of person against whom verdict of murder or
manslaughter is returned
Where a verdict of murder or manslaughter is returned against any person, it shall be
lawful for the Director of Public Prosecutions to file such indictment against him or her,
founded upon the depositions or the further evidence hereafter mentioned, as to him or
her may seem fit:
Provided that, if no indictment is filed, such person shall nevertheless be liable to be
tried upon the inquisition, which shall for all purposes be regarded as an indictment.

### 36.

Power of magistrate to take further evidence on verdict of murder or
manslaughter
Where a verdict of murder or manslaughter is returned against any person, a
magistrate shall hear and take any further evidence against that person which may be
tendered to him or her. Such further evidence shall be taken and obtained in the same
manner and by the same means as evidence in indictable offences, and the magistrate
shall for this purpose have power to summon witnesses and shall by warrant require the
gaoler to bring before him or her the person against whom the verdict is returned, or, if
the latter is on bail, shall summon him or her to attend at the magistrate’s court, in order
that he or she may be present when the further evidence is taken.

### 37.

Such further evidence to be forwarded to Registrar with copy for Director of
Public Prosecutions
The further evidence taken as aforesaid shall, together with a copy thereof, be
forwarded forthwith by the magistrate to the Registrar of the Supreme Court, and the
latter shall deliver the copy to the Director of Public Prosecutions and shall file the
original further depositions and exhibits, if any, with the inquisition and the other
documents connected therewith.
Burials and Exhumations

### 38.

In case of sudden or unnatural death, body not to be buried without warrant of
Coroner or Justice of the Peace till after twenty-four hours
When any such death as is mentioned in section 9 has occurred, the body shall not be
buried, except by order of the Coroner or of a Justice of the Peace, till after the lapse of
twenty-four hours from the time of the death having taken place; and any person burying
any such body without such order shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary
conviction, to a fine of one thousand dollars.

### 39.

Coroner may order exhumation
Whenever it appears to a Coroner that the body of any person who has died under
circumstances requiring the holding of an inquiry or inquest has been buried without
notice having been given to the Coroner or without an inquiry or inquest having been

held, or in case the inquiry or inquest although held is insufficient, it shall be lawful for
the Coroner by his or her warrant to order the exhumation of the body; and the Coroner
shall proceed after the exhumation to hold an inquiry or inquest thereon, and the expense
of the exhumation and of the reinterment shall be paid on the Coroner’s order out of the
Consolidated Fund; but an exhumation shall not be ordered in any case where it would be
injurious to the public health or where there is no probability of a satisfactory inquiry or
inquisition resulting therefrom.
Fees of Medical Practitioners

### 40.

Remuneration of medical practitioners
(1) A medical practitioner, not being a medical officer as hereinafter mentioned, who
makes any report under the direction of a Coroner on any inquiry by him or her, or
attends at any Coroner’s inquest in obedience to a summons of the Coroner issued for that
purpose, or performs at or in connection with the inquiry or inquest any of the duties or
services specified in the Second Schedule shall be entitled to receive the remuneration
apportioned to those duties or services in that Schedule:
Provided that no remuneration shall be paid to any medical practitioner for the
performance of any post-mortem examination or analysis made without the previous
direction of the Coroner.
(2) It shall be lawful for the Governor-General from time to time to make regulations
fixing fees by way of remuneration for duties and services performed under this Act by
any medical practitioner who at the time of the performance is a medical officer within
the meaning of the Medical Officers Act, Chapter 188, and, except as to such fees, if any,
as may be allowed by the regulations, the medical officers shall perform the duties and
services aforesaid without remuneration.

### 41.

Payment of remuneration
All sums payable under the preceding section shall be a charge on the Consolidated
Fund to be paid on a certificate in writing under the Coroner’s hand setting forth the
services performed, the amount due, and the person entitled to receive it.
Offences

### 42.

Neglect of duty by Coroner
Every Coroner who refuses or neglects, without reasonable excuse, to hold any inquest
or inquiry which it is his or her duty to hold, or to perform any duty which he or she is
required by this Act to perform, shall be guilty of an offence and liable on conviction on
indictment to a fine of five thousand dollars.

### 43.

Neglect to give information of death
Whoever, being required by this Act to inform a Coroner or a Registrar or Deputy
Registrar of Deaths of any death, refuses or neglects to do so as required shall be guilty of
an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand dollars.

### 44.

Juror making default
Whoever, being summoned as a juror on any inquest, refuses or neglects to attend or
to be sworn or to serve as a juror; or, being called on to complete the number of jurors
required, refuses to be sworn or to serve as a juror, shall be guilty of an offence and
liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand dollars.

### 45.

Medical practitioner making default
Any medical practitioner who—
(a)

being directed by any Coroner to examine the body of any person and report
thereon;

(b)

being duly summoned to attend any Coroner’s court to give evidence; or

(c)

being directed to make any post-mortem examination or analysis,

refuses or neglects to do so shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary
conviction, to a fine of one thousand dollars.

### 46.

Witnesses making default
Whoever—
(a)

having been called upon or summoned to attend as a witness on any inquest,
refuses or neglects to attend; or

(b)

being present in court, refuses to be sworn or to give evidence; or

(c)

having given evidence, refuses to sign his or her deposition when required to
do so,

shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of two hundred
and fifty dollars.

### 47.

Refusing to enter into recognisance
Any juror or witness who refuses to enter into any recognisance by this Act required
shall be committed by the Coroner by warrant to the prison, there to be kept and
imprisoned until the return day of the recognisance, unless in the meantime he or she duly
enters into the recognisance either before the Coroner or before any Justice of the Peace;
and the Coroner or Justice taking the recognisance shall thereupon issue his or her order
to the keeper of the prison for the discharge of the prisoner from custody.

### 48.

Obstructing persons in execution of duties
Whoever—
(a)

obstructs the Coroner or his or her jury in any view or inquiry;

(b)

wilfully does any act with the purpose of defeating the object of any inquiry;

(c)

obstructs or annoys a medical practitioner in the discharge of any duty
imposed on him or her by this Act; or

(d)

impedes or obstructs the exhumation or burial of any body ordered by the
Coroner to be exhumed or buried,

shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of two thousand
five hundred dollars.

### 49.

Obstructing proceedings in Coroner’s court
Whoever obstructs or impedes the proceedings in any Coroner’s court, or wilfully
insults the Coroner or any juror or officer of the court, or wilfully misbehaves himself or
herself in such a manner as to interfere with the proceedings, shall be liable to be
committed by the Coroner for contempt for a period not exceeding fourteen days, or to
pay a fine to be imposed by the Coroner not exceeding one hundred dollars, or to be
imprisoned in default of payment for a period not exceeding fourteen days.

### 50.

Forging or uttering false certificate
Whoever forges any certificate mentioned in this Act, or utters any such certificate
knowing it to be forged, shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on conviction on
indictment, to imprisonment for three years.

### 51.

How offences prosecuted
Any person committing an offence under this Act may be prosecuted by the Coroner
in whose court or district the offence was committed, or by the Director of Public
Prosecutions, or by any person authorised by the Director of Public Prosecutions.

### 52.

Proceedings on forfeited recognisance
All forfeited recognisances shall forthwith be lodged by the Coroner with the Registrar
of the Supreme Court to be proceeded upon in like manner as other recognisances.
Miscellaneous

### 53.

Admissibility of deposition on trial
If, on the trial of any person against whom a verdict or finding of murder or
manslaughter has been returned at any inquest or inquiry, it is proved that any person
whose deposition has been taken at the inquest or inquiry is dead, or absent from Grenada
or so ill as not to be able to travel, then, if the deposition purports to be signed by the
Coroner before whom it purports to have been taken, it may be read as evidence without
any further proof thereof, unless it is proved that it was not in fact signed by the Coroner
purporting to sign it.

### 1.

Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Coroners (Medical Fees) Regulations.

### 2.

Medical fees
Where any medical officer has by the direction of a Coroner performed any service
under the Act out of his or her district or attends at any inquest out of his or her district in
obedience to a summons issued by the Coroner he or she shall be paid by the Permanent
Secretary (Finance) out of the Consolidated Fund of the State the actual out-of-pocket
expenses necessarily incurred in travelling.
