## CHAPTER 62A

CONSTITUTIONAL JUDICATURE (RESTORATION) ACT
An Act to restore the Judicature provided for by and under the Constitution, and
certain constitutional Orders in Council.
[Act No. 19 of 1991 amended by Act No. 37 of 1991.]

[16th August, 1991.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Constitutional Judicature (Restoration) Act.
2\. Interpretation
In this Act—
“Appointed day” means the day appointed by the Governor-General by
proclamation published in the Gazette for the coming into force of this Act;
“Constitution” means the Constitution of Grenada set out in Schedule 1 to the
Grenada Constitution Order, SI 2155 of 1973;

“Court of Appeal” means the Court of Appeal of the Supreme Court established by

## PART II of the West Indies Associated States Supreme Court Order, SI 223 of 1967,

and referred to in the Constitution, SI 2155 of 1973;
“Courts Order” means the West Indies Associated States Supreme Court Order,
SI 223 of 1967, as referred to in the Constitution, SI 2155 of 1973;
“Former Court of Appeal” means the Court of Appeal of the Former Supreme Court;
“Former High Court” means the High Court of the Former Supreme Court;
“Former Supreme Court” means the Supreme Court of Grenada established by the
Establishment of Supreme Court of Grenada Act, People’s Law No. 4 of 1979;
“Her Majesty in Council” means Her Majesty in Council as that term is used in the
West Indies Associated States (Appeals to Privy Council) Order, SI 224 of 1967, and
referred to in the Constitution, SI 2155 of 1973, and sometimes called the Judicial
Committee of the Privy Council or the Privy Council;
“High Court” means the High Court of Justice of the Supreme Court established by

## PART II of the Courts Order, SI 223 of 1967;

“Judicial Committee of the Privy Council” means Her Majesty in Council;
“Privy Council” means Her Majesty in Council;
“Privy Council Appeals Order” means the West Indies Associated States (Appeals to
Privy Council) Order, SI 224 of 1967 as referred to in the Constitution, SI 2155 of 1973;
“Supreme Court” means the Supreme Court established by PART II of the Courts
Order, SI 223 of 1967.
3\. Legal effect of the suspended provisions of the Constitution
(1) The provisions of the Constitution, SI 2155 of 1973, which were suspended by the
Suspension of Constitution Act, 1979, insofar as they have not yet been brought back into
force or have not yet been proclaimed once more to have full force and effect, shall, as
from the appointed day, have full legal force and effect.
(2) Notwithstanding the provisions of subsection (1), and without derogating from the
generality thereof, the following provisions of the Constitution which were suspended by
the Suspension of Constitution Act, 1979, in so far as they have not yet been brought
back into force or have not yet been proclaimed to have full force and effect, shall as
from the appointed day, have full legal force and effect, that is to say—
(i) section 16,
(ii) section 37,
(iii) section 39.
(3) Notwithstanding the provisions of subsection (2), the Governor-General may by
order published in the Gazette declare any law promulgated during the relevant period to
be no longer of any force and effect.
(4) In this Act, “relevant period” means that period commencing on the 13th day of
March, 1979, up to and including the 3rd day of December, 1984.
4\. Courts Order and Constitutional Supreme Court restored
(1) The Courts Order shall, in its entirety, be restored and shall have full force and
effect in so far as it is applicable to Grenada so that the High Court and the Court of

Appeal of the Supreme Court established by Part II of the said Courts Order, SI 223 of
1967, shall again have full jurisdiction in and over Grenada as from the appointed day,
and all decisions of the said Supreme Court, whether given before or after that day, shall
have full force and effect in Grenada.
(2) Subject to the provisions of this Act, the Acts establishing the Former High Court
and the Former Court of Appeal of the Former Supreme Court, in particular the
Establishment of Supreme Court of Grenada Act, 1979, the Supreme Court Jurisdiction
Act, 1979, and the Court of Appeal Act, 1979, shall no longer be of any force and effect
and the Former Supreme Court is accordingly abolished as from the appointed day.
(3) All the institutions together with all their powers such as are created by the Courts
Order, SI 223 of 1967, additional to the Supreme Court established by it and restored by
subsection (1), are restored with full force and effect in relation to Grenada as from the
appointed day.
5\. Privy Council Appeals Order and appeals to Privy restored
Subject as hereinafter provided—
(1) The Privy Council Appeals Order, SI 224 of 1967, shall, in its entirety, as from
the appointed day, be restored and shall have full force and effect in so far as it is
applicable to Grenada so that appeals shall again lie to Her Majesty in Council as
provided for in the Constitution, SI 2155 of 1973, and the Privy Council Appeals Order,
SI 224 of 1967.
(2) The Privy Council (Abolition of Appeals) Act, 1979, abolishing appeals to Her
Majesty in Council shall no longer be of any force and effect as from the appointed day,
and as from that day all decisions of Her Majesty in Council, whether given before or
after that day, shall have full legal force and effect in Grenada.
(3) There is hereby restored all jurisdiction in Her Majesty in Council in and over
Grenada, additional to the jurisdiction to hear appeals restored by subsection (1),
provided for in the Constitution, SI 2155 of 1973, the Courts Order, SI 223 of 1967, and
the Privy Council Appeals Order, SI 224 of 1967.
6\. How references in Constitution to High Court, Court of Appeal, Supreme Court
and Her Majesty in Council to be construed
All references in the Constitution, SI 2155 of 1973, to the High Court, the Court of
Appeal, the Supreme Court and Her Majesty in Council shall, as from the appointed day, be
construed as and deemed to be references to the High Court, the Court of Appeal, the
Supreme Court and Her Majesty in Council respectively as those terms are used in the
Courts Order, SI 223 of 1967, and the Privy Council Appeals Order, SI 224 of 1967.
7\. Transitional provisions
(1) All matters and proceedings commenced and pending in the Former High Court,
including matters ordered by the Former Court of Appeal to be reheard, retried or
determined howsoever by the Former High Court, shall be deemed to have been
commenced and may be continued in the High Court and, for such purpose only, nothing
contained in this Act shall invalidate any proceedings done in the Former High Court
prior to the appointed day.
(2) Subject to the provisions of this Act, from and after the appointed day the High
Court, and the Court of Appeal shall, subject to any orders and rules of Court, treat all
judgements, verdicts, rulings and orders of the Former High Court or any court other than

the Former Court of Appeal or any tribunal whatsoever in Grenada as judgements,
verdicts, rulings and orders of the High Court, such court or tribunal.
(3) All matters and proceedings pending in and not determined by the Former Court
of Appeal shall be determined and concluded as if pending in the Court of Appeal.
(4) Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act or any other law, no appeal
whatsoever shall at all lie to Her Majesty in Council from any decision whether final,
interlocutory or otherwise or from any thing or matter arising out of any such decision of
the Former Court of Appeal.
(5) Any person who on the appointed day holds or is acting in an office specified in
section 86 or 88 of the Constitution and—
(a)

who was appointed by the Governor-General acting in accordance with the
advice of the Judicial and Legal Services Commission established by the
Courts Order, SI 223 of 1967, may, if he or she so wishes, continue to hold
or act in that office as the case may be;

(b)

who was not appointed in the manner mentioned in paragraph (a) may, if he
or she so wishes, continue to hold or act in that office as the case may be
until an appointment is made in that manner.

8\. Repeal
The Acts listed in the First Column of the Schedule shall, to the extent listed opposite
in the Second Column, no longer be of any force and effect.
9\. Permitted reference to Supreme Court
The Supreme Court which by virtue of this Act Grenada now re-enters and which the
Constitution says shall be styled the Supreme Court of Grenada and the West Indies
Associated States is the regional Supreme Court established by the Courts Order which
said regional Supreme Court is in other States subject to the jurisdiction thereof referred
to as the Eastern Caribbean Supreme Court.
10\. Commencement
This Act comes into force on the 16th day of August, 1991.
