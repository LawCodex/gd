## CHAPTER 151

INTER-AMERICAN INSTITUTE OF AGRICULTURAL SCIENCES
(PRIVILEGES AND IMMUNITIES) ACT
An Act to give statutory effect to certain provisions of an agreement between the
Government and the Inter-American Institute of Agricultural Sciences
concerning the privileges and immunities of the Institute, and for connected
matters.
[Act No. 39 of 1982.]

[20th August, 1982.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Inter-American Institute of Agricultural Sciences
(Privileges and Immunities) Act.
2\. Interpretation
In this Act—
“Agreement” means the agreement in writing between the Government and the
Institute, the text of which is set out in the Schedule;
“Institute” means the Inter-American Institute of Agricultural Sciences.
3\. Powers of the Institute as a body corporate
The Institute is a body corporate with perpetual succession and with power to contract,
to sue and be sued in its corporate name, to acquire, hold, lease and dispose of real and
personal property, and to do all things that under the Agreement it is required or
empowered to do.

4\. Privileges and immunities of officials, etc.
The officials and staff of the Institute and other persons entitled under the Agreement
shall be accorded by the Government the privileges and immunities to which they are so
entitled subject to the terms of the Agreement.
5\. Privileges and immunities of the Institute
The Institute shall enjoy the privileges and immunities to which it is entitled in
accordance with the Agreement and the provisions of Articles 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12 and 13 of
the Agreement shall have the force of law in Grenada.
6\. Power of the Minister to amend the Schedule
(1) If, pursuant to Article 25 of the Agreement, an amendment enters into force the
Minister of Agriculture may, by Order, similarly amend the Schedule.
(2) An Order under subsection (1) may contain such consequential, supplemental or
ancillary provisions as appear to the Minister of Agriculture to be necessary or expedient
to give effect to the amendment and, without prejudice to the generality of the foregoing,
the Minister may, by such Order, amend references to the Agreement in this Act to
correspond with the text of the Agreement.

## PART I

Legal Status and Organisation
Article 1
The Institute is an inter-governmental organisation made up of American States,
which, in accordance with the provision of Chapter XXI of the Charter of the
Organisation of American States, signed on 30th April, 1948, amended by the Protocol of
Buenos Aires, and ratified by Grenada on 13th May, 1975, is recognised as an InterAmerican Specialised Agency that bears an international juridical personality.
Article 2
The Institute agrees to establish an Office in Grenada, called Representation of IICA
in Grenada, the major purpose of which will be to carry on the programmes approved by
the Board of Directors of the Institute.
Article 3
The Resident Representative of the Institute in Grenada shall be responsible for the
activities carried out by the Institute in Grenada. These activities may be part of its
regular programmes financed by funds from the quota contributions from the member
states; they may represent carrying out of the projects of the Programme of Technical Cooperation of the Organisation of American States in which the Institute is a co-operating

agency, or they may be the result of the responsibilities acquired by virtue of contracts
signed or donations received. In all cases the personnel participating in these activities
shall be considered personnel of the Institute.
The Office of the Institute in Grenada is a branch of the Inter-American Institute of
Agricultural Sciences and as such shall have the right to the legal status of, and the
privileges and immunities accorded to, the Institute under the basic Convention, and also
those privileges and immunities specified in this Agreement.
Article 5
To facilitate and strengthen the development of the activities, the Government,
represented by the Ministry for Agriculture, Forestry, and Fisheries, and the Institute shall
sign special agreements that shall specify the contributions and facilities that the former
shall provide and the obligations of the latter.
Article 6
For the purpose of attaining the objectives sought for, the Institute is empowered to
sign, in consultation with the Ministry for Agriculture, Forestry, and Fisheries, operations
agreements with other national institutions.

## PART II

Legal Capacity, Privileges and Immunities of the Institute
Article 7
The Institute, as an international entity, enjoys legal capacity throughout Grenada and
as such, is empowered as follows—
(a)

to make contracts;

(b)

to acquire real and movable property for the purposes of the Institute and to
dispose of such property freely;

(c)

to institute legal and administrative proceedings of its own interest, in doing
so expressly waiving the immunity from jurisdiction that pertains to it.
Article 8

The premises and files of the Institute shall be inviolable. These, and the properties
and goods of the Institute, wherever they may be located, shall enjoy immunity against
seizure, requisition, confiscation, expropriation, and any other form of intervention
whether of an executive, administrative, judicial or legislative nature.
Article 9
This Institute and its properties, income, and other assets shall be—
(a)

exempt from all direct taxes: it is understood, however, that it may not claim
exemption from taxes that are, in fact, charges for public services;

(b)

exempt from customs duties and other taxes, charges, or restrictions on
articles and vehicles imported or exported for its official use: it is
understood, however, that articles imported duty free shall not be sold in
Grenada except under conditions agreed with the Government, which
conditions shall not be lower than those set for diplomatic missions;

(c)

exempt from customs duties, prohibitions, and restrictions, on imports and
exports of its publications.

The Institute may hold its funds in foreign currency and may withdraw them in the
same currency in which they were brought into Grenada through authorised entities.
The Institute and its goods, income, and properties shall enjoy immunity from all
juridical or administrative proceedings, and shall not be subject to the jurisdiction of
national courts, except in particular cases in which that immunity is expressly waived. It
is understood that such waiver of immunity will not have the effect of making these
goods and properties liable to any form of attachment.
Article 12
The Institute shall enjoy favourable treatment for its official communications, similar to
that given to diplomatic missions, with respect to priorities, rates, and taxes on
correspondence, cable, telegrams, radiograms, telephone calls, telephotos, and other means
of communications, as well as press rates for information materials for the press and radio.
The correspondence or other official communications of the Institute shall not be censored.

## PART III

Immigration and Residence Facilities
Article 13
Subject to any written law restricting entry or movement of persons for reasons of
national security, all permanent or temporary staff members of the Institute and members
of their families living with them shall enjoy immunities from restrictions on immigration
and those with regard to registration of aliens, and their stay in Grenada shall be
facilitated for the performance of their missions.
Article 14
The Institute staff members and members of their families living with them mentioned
in the previous article whose status has been officially reported, with the required
information, to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, shall be provided by that Ministry with an
identification document which shall attest to their status before national authorities and
give them the right to remain in Grenada.
Article 15
The provisions of the preceding article do not free the Institute from the duty of
providing proof, when so required of it, to show that persons requesting the privileges
agreed to are entitled to them; nor do these provisions exonerate the Institute from the
application of health or quarantine regulations.
Article 16
The Director-General of the Institute, the Deputy Director-General, and the Regional
Director for the Antilles Zone when travelling to Grenada on official business shall enjoy
the same facilities accorded to chiefs of diplomatic missions, and these shall extend also
to members of their families living with them.

## PART IV

Privileges and Immunities of the Personnel

In all matters not set forth in the basic Convention, the Government shall apply to the
members of the international professional personnel of the Institute the provisions of
Articles 15 and 16 of the “Agreement between the General Secretariat of the Organisation
of American States and the Government of Grenada on the functioning in St. George’s of
the Office of the Secretariat in Grenada” signed at Washington, D.C., United States of
America, on 6th October, 1978.
Article 18
The Resident Representative of the Institute in Grenada and those persons included in
the previous article shall be granted, with respect to the foreign exchange system, the same
facilities and privileges enjoyed by the members of the diplomatic missions accredited to
Grenada.
Article 19
The Resident Representative of the Institute in Grenada, as well as members of his or
her family living with him or her, shall enjoy the privileges and immunities, exemptions,
and facilities granted to resident representatives of other international organisations
accredited to Grenada.
Article 20
The Director-General of the Institute or his or her duly authorised representative shall
provide the Government with the names of the international professional personnel of the
Institute who are entitled in Grenada to the privileges and immunities mentioned in this
Part.
Article 21
The Institute waives immunity from jurisdiction with reference to so-called general
services personnel, that is, employees and workers who work permanently in Grenada
and are not international professional personnel, in accordance with the regulations of the
Institute. The labour legislation of Grenada shall apply to general services personnel.

## PART V

Nature of the Privileges and Immunities
Article 22
Privileges and immunities are granted to officials and staff members of the Institute
exclusively in the interest of the Institute. Consequently, the Director-General of the
Institute shall waive the immunity of officials or staff members in any case in which he or
she, in accordance with his or her own criteria, considers that immunity impedes the
course of justice and that the waiver will not prejudice the interest of the Institute. That
waiver shall be made by the Director-General himself or (with his or her express
authorisation) by the Resident Representative of the Institute in Grenada.
Article 23
When the Government so requests, the Institute shall co-operate with the competent
authorities of Grenada to facilitate the proper administration of justice to see to
compliance with police ordinances, and to prevent abuses in connection with the
privileges and immunities granted in this Agreement.
The Institute shall take whatever measures may be necessary for the appropriate
settlement of—

(a)

disputes arising with regard to contracts or other matters of private law to
which the Institute is a party;

(b)

disputes to which any official or staff member is a party, regarding which he
or she enjoys immunity:

Provided that the Director-General has not waived this immunity in accordance with
Article 22.

## PART VI

Final Provisions
Article 25
Either of the parties may move to amend this Agreement and for this purpose they
shall consult each other with regard to any amendment proposed. Such amendment shall
enter into force when approved by both parties.
Article 26
Subject to prior approval by the Board of Directors of the Institute, this Agreement
shall enter into force on the date of the note by which the Government notifies the
Institute that the Agreement has been ratified in accordance with the legal procedures in
force in Grenada.
Article 27
Either of the parties may terminate this Agreement by notifying the other party six
months in advance.
