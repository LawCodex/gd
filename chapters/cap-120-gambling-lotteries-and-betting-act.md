## CHAPTER 120

GAMBLING, LOTTERIES AND BETTING ACT
An Act relating to gambling, lotteries and betting.

No. 11 of 2006.]

[22nd April, 1970.]

## PART I

Preliminary

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Gambling, Lotteries and Betting Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act—
“Authority” means the Gaming Authority established by section 3;
“Comptroller” means the Comptroller of Inland Revenue;
“Conference” means the Conference of Heads of Government, the principal organ
of the Community established under paragraph 1(a) of Article 10 of the Revised
Treaty of Chaguaramas establishing the Caribbean Community including the
CARICOM Single Market and Economy;
“Member of the Community” means—
(a)

Antigua and Barbuda
Barbados
Belize
Dominica
Grenada
Guyana
Jamaica
Saint Lucia
St. Christopher and Nevis
St. Vincent and the Grenadines
Trinidad and Tobago;

(b)

any other State or Territory of the Caribbean region that is admitted to
membership of the Community by the Conference;

“Member State” means a Member State of the Community excluding an associate
member;
“national” means a person who—
(a)

is a citizen of a Member State; or

(b)

has a connection with a Member State of a kind which entitles him or her to
be regarded as belonging to or, if it be so expressed, as being a native or
resident of the State for the purposes of its laws relating to immigration;

“pinball machine licence” means a licence in respect of premises on which a
pinball machine is kept under section 15;
“winnings” includes winnings of any kind, and any reference to amount and to
payment in relation to winnings shall be construed accordingly.

### 3.

Establishment of Gaming Authority

(1) There shall be a Gaming Authority consisting of the Chief of Police and three
other persons, appointed by the Minister, one of whom shall be the Comptroller of Inland
Revenue.
(2) The public officers appointed by the Minister under subsection (1) shall hold
office on the Authority during such period as the Minister shall appoint.
(3) The Minister shall nominate the chairperson of the Authority. The chairperson
shall preside at every meeting at which he or she is present.
(4) The Authority shall exercise the powers and shall fulfil the obligations assigned to
it under this Act.

## PART II

Gambling

### 4.

Interpretation of Part
(1) In this Part—
“common gaming house” means any premises kept or used for gambling;
“gamble” means to play at or to engage in any game of chance for winnings in
money or money’s worth;
“game of chance” includes a game of chance and skill combined and a pretended
game of chance or of chance and skill combined, but does not include any athletic
game or sport;
“instruments or appliances for gambling” includes all articles other than money
that are used in or for the purpose of gambling;
“occupier” means any person in actual occupation of the whole or any part of any
premises;
“owner” means the person for the time being receiving or entitled to receive the
rents of any house, building, yard, or other place, or his or her agent, and includes
joint tenants and tenants in common;
“premises” means any house, office, room, or building, and any place or spot,
whether open or enclosed, and includes a ship, boat or other vessel, whether afloat or
not, and any vehicle.
(2) For the purposes of this Part premises shall be deemed to be used for gambling
even if they are so used on one occasion only.

### 5.

Offences relating to common gaming house
A person shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of
three thousand dollars and to imprisonment for six months who—
(a)

being the owner or occupier or having the use temporarily or otherwise
thereof, keeps or uses premises as a common gaming house;

(b)

permits premises of which he or she is the owner or occupier, or of which he
or she has the use temporarily or otherwise, to be used as a common gaming
house;

(c)

has the control or management of, or in any manner assists or is engaged in
the management of, premises kept or used as a common gaming house;

(d)

announces or publishes, or causes to be announced or published, either orally
or by means of print, writing, design, sign, or otherwise, that premises are

opened, kept, or used as a common gaming house, or in any manner invites or
solicits a person to play in a common gaming house; or
(e)

advances, furnishes, or receives money for the purpose of establishing or
conducting the business of a common gaming house.

### 6.

Person gambling or being found in common gaming house
(1) Every person who gambles in a common gaming house is guilty of an offence and
liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand five hundred dollars and to
imprisonment for six months.
(2) Where a person is found in a common gaming house, or is found escaping
therefrom on the occasion of its being entered under the provisions of this Act, that
person shall be presumed, until the contrary is proved, to be or to have been gambling
therein.

### 7.

Gambling in public place
All persons found at any time gambling, or assembled together for such purpose, in a
public or open place are guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine
of one thousand five hundred dollars and to imprisonment for six months.

### 8.

Using premises for gambling without permission of owner
(1) Every person found using premises for the purpose of gambling without the
permission of the owner or occupier thereof is guilty of an offence and liable, on
summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand five hundred dollars and to imprisonment
for six months.
(2) For the purpose of subsection (1) the onus of proving such permission shall be
upon the person alleging the same.

### 9.

Presumption
It shall be presumed, unless the contrary is proved, that premises are a common
gaming house and that the same are so kept or used by the occupier or person having the
use temporarily or otherwise thereof if—
(a)

any instruments or appliances for gambling are found therein or upon any
person found escaping therefrom where such premises are entered under
section 44;

(b)

a police officer duly authorised or empowered under section 44 is wilfully
obstructed or delayed in entering or approaching the premises; or

(c)

there is adduced evidence that gambling took place on those premises in
circumstances other than those described in section 12(1)(a), (b) or (c).

### 10.

Penalty for obstructing police officer
Every person who wilfully obstructs or delays a police officer duly authorised or
empowered under section 44 in entering or approaching premises or uses any means or
contrivance whatsoever for the purpose of obstructing or delaying the entry of such an
officer into premises, is guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine
of three thousand dollars and to imprisonment for six months.

### 11.

Exempted premises
Notwithstanding anything contained in sections 5 to 16 but subject to section 12, the
following premises shall be deemed not to be common gaming houses and are exempt

from the provisions of this Act that render a person liable to be convicted of an offence
for gambling in or suffering any games or gambling in, or for keeping or using or being
concerned in, the management or conduct of a common gaming house, that is to say—
(a)

a private dwelling-house;

a members’ club registered under the Registration of Clubs Act,

## Chapter 282;

(c)

premises occupied by a society or an organisation established or conducted
wholly or mainly for any of the purposes mentioned in section 23(1).

### 12.

Conditions as to gambling in houses and clubs
(1) Subject to the provisions of this Act, gambling may be conducted on the premises
referred to in section 11 and if so conducted shall be conducted in accordance with the
following conditions, that is to say—
(a)

that either—
(i) the chances in the game are equally favourable to all the players, or
(ii) the gambling is so conducted that the chances therein are equally
favourable to all players;

(b)

that no money or money’s worth which any of the players puts down as stakes,
or pays by way of losses, or exchanges for tokens used in playing the game, is
disposed of otherwise than by payment to a player as winnings;

(c)

that, apart from any reasonable sum that may be added to the annual or other
subscription for membership of a member to meet the cost of procuring and
maintaining instruments and appliances for gambling, no other payment in
money or money’s worth is required for a person to take part in the
gambling;

(d)

that no person takes part in the gambling who is not—
(i) a member of the club in pursuance of an application made more than
twenty-four hours before the gambling began, or
(ii) a bona fide guest of such a member;

(e)

that no payment of money or money’s worth was required in order to obtain
access to the premises,

and if gambling takes place on premises referred to in section 11 contrary to
paragraphs (a) to (e) then it shall be held that the premises are a common gaming house,
unless it is proved that the gambling was being conducted in accordance with the
conditions set out in paragraphs (a) to (e).
(2) Nothing in this Part shall render a person liable to be convicted for gambling on
premises held in pursuance of subsection (1) to be a common gaming house by reason
only of an infringement of subsection (1)(c) if the person charged proves that he or she
was not required to make any payment such as is mentioned in the said paragraph (c) and
that he or she neither knew nor had reasonable cause to suspect that any other person was
so required.
(3) If gambling takes place on premises that are held in pursuance of subsection (1) to
be a common gaming house, a person concerned in the organisation or management of
the gambling is guilty of an offence; and for the purposes of this subsection a person who
took part in procuring the assembly of players shall be deemed to have been concerned in
the organisation of the gambling.

### 13.

Persons present at gambling on premises deemed a common gaming house

(1) A person who is present at gambling on premises held in pursuance of section
12(1) to be a common gaming house for the purposes of taking part therein is guilty of an
offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand five hundred dollars
and to imprisonment for six months, save that, for the purposes of proceedings under this
section, section 12(1)(c) shall be deemed to be omitted if the person charged proves that
he or she was not required to make, or to undertake to make, any payment such as is
mentioned in the said paragraph (c) and he or she neither knew nor had reasonable cause
to suspect that any other person was so required.
(2) For the purposes of subsection (1) proof that a person was present at any gambling
shall be evidence that he or she was present for the purpose of taking part therein unless
he or she proves that he or she was present neither for that purpose nor for any of the
following purposes, that is to say, taking part in the management of the gambling,
operating any instrument or other thing whatsoever used in connection with the gambling,
or making bets in connection with the gambling.

### 14.

Gambling machines
(1) Nothing in sections 11, 12 and 17 shall be construed as permitting gambling by
means of a gambling machine on premises exempted from certain of the provisions of
this Part by section 11 and, if gambling by means of a gambling machine takes place in
any such premises, the premises shall be deemed to be a common gaming house.
(2) In this section, “gambling machine” means a machine for playing a game of
chance, being a game which requires no action by a player other than actuation or
manipulation of the machine.

### 15.

Pinball machines
(1) Nothing in this Part, other than this section and section 16, shall apply to a game
of chance played by means of a pinball machine on premises licensed for the purpose in
accordance with the provisions of the First Schedule.
(2) No person shall use premises for the purpose of keeping a pinball machine unless
he or she is the holder of a permit granted under the First Schedule authorising him or her
to do so.

### 16.

Tax on pinball machines
(1) There shall be charged annually in respect of a pinball machine a tax of two
hundred dollars.
(2) Tax charged under subsection (1) shall be paid to the Comptroller by the occupier
of the premises on which the machines are kept.
(3) In this section, and in sections 2 and 15, “pinball machine” means the machine,
known as a pinball machine, for playing a game of chance, being a game which requires
no action by a player other than actuation or manipulation of the machine.

### 17.

Certain activities deemed not to be gambling
(1) For the purposes of this Act the word “bet” does not include a bet or stake at
gambling conducted in accordance with section 12(1) on premises exempted from certain
provisions of this Part by section 11.
(2) Subject to Part IV—
(a)

the making of a bet by way of pool betting, within the meaning of Part IV;
and

participation in a lottery which satisfies the conditions set out in
subsection (3),

shall be deemed not to be gambling for the purposes of this Part relating to gambling in,
or being present at any gambling on, or being concerned in the organisation or
management of, premises exempted from certain provisions of this Part by section 11, if
the gambling is conducted as is mentioned in section 12.
(3) The conditions referred to in subsection (2)(b) are—
(a)

that the lottery is a lottery deemed by section 21 not to be unlawful; and

(b)

that each winner of a prize is ascertained either by result of a draw or other
determination on the outcome of an event.

## PART III

Lotteries

### 18.

Interpretation of sections 17 and 19 to 28
(1) In sections 17 and 19 to 28—
“bingo” means the lottery commonly known as “bingo”;
“lottery” includes bingo and any game, method or device whereby money or
money’s worth is distributed or allotted in any manner depending upon or determined
by chance or lot;
“money” includes a cheque, promissory note, bank note, postal order or money
order;
“ticket” includes, in relation to a lottery or proposed lottery, any document or token
or other article whatsoever evidencing the claim of a person to participate in the
chances of the lottery.
(2) For the purposes of sections 19 to 28—
(a)

references to printing shall be construed as including references to writing
and other modes of representing or reproducing words or symbols in a
visible form;

(b)

documents shall be deemed to be distributed if they are distributed to
persons or places whether within or outside Grenada, and “distribution” shall
be construed accordingly; and

(c)

a lottery does not cease to be a lottery merely by reason that some condition
or requirement involving skill in addition to the happening or fall of chance
or lot is necessary to be fulfilled before the prizes are distributed or allotted.

### 19.

Conduct of lotteries
No person shall conduct a lottery except in accordance with the provisions of this Part.

### 20.

Offences in connection with lotteries
(1) Subject to the provisions of this section, every person who in connection with a
lottery promoted, or proposed to be promoted, in Grenada or elsewhere—
(a)

prints any ticket for use in the lottery;

(b)

sells or distributes, or offers or advertises for sale or distribution, or has in
his or her possession for the purpose of sale or distribution, any ticket or
chance in the lottery;

(c)

prints, publishes or distributes, or has in his or her possession for the purpose
of publication or distribution—

(i) any advertisement of the lottery,
(ii) any list (whether complete or not) of prize winners or winning tickets
in the lottery, or
(iii) any such matter descriptive of the drawing or intended drawing of the
lottery, as is calculated to act as an inducement to persons to
participate in that lottery or in other lotteries;
(d)

brings, or invites a person to bring or send, into Grenada any ticket or chance
in the lottery, any money or valuable thing received in respect of the sale or
distribution of any ticket in, or advertisement of, the lottery;

(e)

sends or attempts to send out of Grenada any ticket or chance in the lottery,
any money or valuable thing received in respect of the sale or distribution, or
any document recording the sale or distribution, or identity of the holder, of
any ticket or chance in the lottery;

(f)

uses premises, or causes or knowingly permits premises over which he or
she has care or control to be used, for purposes connected with the
promotion or conduct of the lottery;

(g)

draws, throws, declares or exhibits, expressly or other wise, the winner or
winning number, ticket, lot, figure, design, symbol or other result of a
lottery; or

(h)

causes, procures, or attempts to procure a person to do any of the foregoing
acts,

shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of three
thousand dollars or in default of payment to imprisonment for six months.
(2) In proceedings instituted under subsection (1) of this section it shall be a defence
to prove that the lottery to which the proceedings relate was not unlawful by reason of
some subsequent section of this Part and that at the date of the alleged offence the
defendant believed, and had reasonable ground for believing, that none of the conditions
required by that section to be observed in connection with the promotion and conduct of
the lottery had been broken.

### 21.

Exemption of lotteries incidental to certain entertainments
(1) Notwithstanding the provisions of this Act, a lottery promoted as an incident of
entertainment to which this section applies shall be deemed not to be unlawful and is
exempt from the requirements of this Act, except that the conditions specified in
subsection (2) of this section shall be observed in connection with the promotion and
conduct of the lottery, and if any of those conditions is broken, every person concerned in
the promotion or conduct of the lottery is guilty of an offence unless he or she proves that
the offence was committed without his or her consent or connivance and that he or she
exercised all due diligence to prevent it.
(2) The conditions referred to in subsection (1) of this section are that—
(a)

the whole of the proceedings of the entertainment (including the proceeds of
the lottery) after deducting the expenses of the entertainment, including
expenses incurred in connection with the lottery, and in particular—
(i) the expenses incurred in printing tickets in the lottery, and
(ii) such sum, if any, not exceeding fifty dollars as has been expended by
the promoters of the lottery on account of any expense incurred by
them in purchasing prizes in the lottery,
shall be devoted to purposes other than private gain;

none of the prizes in the lottery shall be money prizes;

(c)

tickets or chances in the lottery shall not be sold or issued, nor shall the
result of the lottery be declared, except in the premises on which the
entertainment takes place; and

(d)

the facilities afforded for participating in the lottery shall not be the only, or
the only substantial, inducement to persons to attend the entertainment.

(3) The entertainments to which this section applies are bazaars, sales of work, fetes,
dinners, dances, sporting or athletic events, and other entertainments of a similar
character, whether limited to one day or extending over two or more days.
(4) A person guilty of an offence under this section is guilty of an offence and liable,
on summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand five hundred dollars.
(5) It shall be a defence, for a person charged with an offence under subsection (1)
solely by reason of his or her being the promoter, to prove that the breach occurred without
his or her consent or connivance and that he or she exercised all due diligence to prevent it.

### 22.

Lotteries conducted for the promotion of certain objects by approved national
organisations
(1) For the purposes of this Act the Minister may, by Order—
(a)

declare an organisation to be an approved national organisation for the
promotion of sports and games;

(b)

declare an organisation to be an approved national organisation for the
promotion of religion, education, charity, or the arts and culture.

(2) An approved national organisation may organise a lottery for the raising of funds
for the promotion of any of the objects specified in subsection (1), or for the promotion of
the welfare of the whole or part of the community, on the following conditions—
(a)

the organisers of the lottery shall—
(i) notify the Authority of their intention to organise the lottery,
(ii) undertake to advise on the structure and operation of the lottery
including the specific purposes to which the proceeds are to be applied
and the duration of the lottery,
(iii) undertake to furnish the Authority with periodic returns, and a final
return, to enable the Authority to determine whether the lottery is
being, and has been, conducted in accordance with those purposes;

(b)

if the Authority are not satisfied concerning the operation of the lottery.
having regard to any return furnished pursuant to subparagraph (iii), the
Authority may direct its suspension for so long as may be necessary to
complete an inquiry into the manner in which it is being, or has been,
conducted. At such an inquiry an approved national organisation may be
legally represented.

(3) Upon the completion of an inquiry, the Authority shall furnish the Minister with a
report of the inquiry and with such recommendations, if any, as they think fit.

### 23.

Lotteries conducted for charitable and other purposes by individuals
(1) Any three or more individuals may together organise a lottery for the raising of
funds to be used for a charitable, religious, educational or recreational purpose, or for the
promotion of the welfare of the whole or part of the community, on the following
conditions—
(a)

the organisers of the lottery shall—
(i) obtain the approval of the Authority,

(ii) undertake to declare the purpose to which the proceeds of the lottery
are to be applied,
(iii) enter into a bond to secure payment to the Authority of such amount,
calculated as a percentage of the gross receipts from the lottery, as the
Minister of Finance may, by Order, prescribe. Such amount is in this
section referred to as “the duty payable on the lottery”, and
(iv) pay to the Authority, within fourteen days immediately following the
completion of the lottery, the duty payable on the lottery evidenced by
a statement of account certified by a duly qualified accountant.
(2) The Authority, after obtaining the approval of the Minister of Finance, may waive
or refund the whole or any part of the duty payable on the lottery and, for that purpose,
are empowered to and shall release the organisers from whichever of the conditions in
subsection (1)(a)(iii) and (iv) is appropriate and to such extent as is necessary.
(3) If the whole or some part of the duty payable on the lottery has been waived or
refunded the Authority may, in its discretion, collect from the organisers the full amount
of the duty and pay over to the person for whose benefit the lottery has been organised the
amount waived or refunded.
(4) The organisers of a lottery who wilfully fail to fulfil any one or more of the
conditions specified in subsection (1)(a), being a condition from which they have not
been released from compliance, or who wilfully fail to declare the purpose to which the
proceeds of the lottery are to be applied, or who procure or present a statement of account
knowing the same to be false in a material particular, shall be severally guilty of an
offence and liable, on. summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand five hundred dollars
and to imprisonment for twelve months.

### 24.

Foreign lottery tickets
(1) Notwithstanding the provisions of this Act, lottery tickets relating to a lottery
conducted or held outside Grenada may be sold or disposed of in Grenada if the following
requirements of the seller are fulfilled by him or her—
(a)

he or she shall make application in writing to the Comptroller and obtain
from him or her a licence permitting him or her to sell and dispose of, and to
make contracts for the sale or disposal of, the lottery tickets in which he or
she desires to deal for a period of twelve months from the date of issue of the
licence, and the licence shall remain in force for that period and no longer;

(b)

he or she shall, before the licence is issued to him or her, pay to the
Comptroller a licence fee of five hundred dollars, and if the application for
the licence is refused the fee shall be returned to him or her;

(c)

before he or she offers any ticket for sale or disposes of any ticket he or she
shall cause each of the same to be stamped with the appropriate amount of
stamp duty specified in the Second Schedule and he or she shall endorse
thereon his or her name and address:

Provided that a person who resells or otherwise disposes of a lottery ticket which he or
she has purchased or lawfully obtained from a person who has been duly licensed by the
Comptroller under this subsection shall not be required to pay a fee or any stamp duty.
(2) A person who contravenes the provisions of subsection (1) shall be guilty of an
offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of five hundred dollars, and lottery
tickets found in his or her possession to which this section applies shall be forfeited.
(3) In proceedings under subsection (2) for an offence, it shall be presumed until the
contrary is proved that the requirements of subsection (1) have not been complied with.

### 25.

Approved lotteries conducted by race club

(1) Nothing in this Act applies to a lottery organised and controlled by a race club in
connection with a race meeting held in Grenada, or in connection with a particular race
run at such meeting, where such lottery shall have been approved by the Minister.
(2) There shall be raised, levied and collected stamp duty as set forth in the Second
Schedule on every ticket sold or issued in connection with a lottery approved under subsection (1). The stamp duty shall be payable in such manner as the Minister may from
time to time prescribe by regulations made under this section.
(3) The Minister may from time to time by Order suspend or amend the provisions of
the Second Schedule.

### 26.

Promotion and conduct of lotteries by companies
(1) Notwithstanding any provision to the contrary in this Act, the Minister may grant
to a company registered in Grenada a licence to promote and conduct a lottery, and a
lottery promoted or conducted in accordance with the conditions of the licence granted in
respect of it shall be lawful.
(2) Stamp duty is not payable on tickets sold or issued in connection with a lottery
licensed pursuant to subsection (1), nor are the moneys derived from the sale of such
tickets to be treated as income of such a company for the purposes of any law relating to
income tax:
Provided that the conditions of the license granted in respect of the lottery are being,
or have been, duly fulfilled.

### 27.

Recovery of money paid; contracts for sale of lottery tickets void
(1) Any money or money’s worth paid or deposited for or in respect of the purchase
of a lottery ticket, other than a ticket sold in a lottery exempted from the provisions of this
Part, shall be recoverable as money had and received to the use of the person by whom
the same was paid or deposited.
(2) Subject to section 28, every sale or contract for sale of a ticket in a lottery is void,
and no action shall be maintained by a person in respect of such sale or contract except by
the purchaser for the return of the money or other consideration, if any, paid thereon.

### 28.

Action maintainable in respect of exempted lotteries
Notwithstanding common law and section 27 or any other written law, for the
purposes of the provisions of this Act an action shall lie upon a sale or contract for sale of
a lottery ticket in a lottery exempted from the provisions of this Part.

## PART IV

Betting

### 29.

Interpretation of Part
(1) In this Part—
“betting office licence” means a licence authorising the holder thereof to use
premises, in respect of which the licence is for the time being in force, as a betting
office;
“betting transaction” includes the collection of or payment of winnings on a bet
and any transaction in which one or more of the parties is acting in the manner
described in section 31(1)(a) or (b);

“football pools”, for the purposes of this subsection, means pool betting that is
effected on or by way of the result of football matches wherever played;
“foreign pools operator” means an agent or representative of a principal who, in his
or her own right and not as agent of another, carries on pool betting business in respect
of football pools outside Grenada;
“licensed betting office” means premises in respect of which a betting office
licence is for the time being in force;
“pool betting business” means business involving the receiving or negotiating of
bets made by way of pool betting;
“totalisator” means the contrivance for betting known as the totalisator or parimutuel,
or any other machine or instrument of betting of a like nature, whether mechanically
operated or not.
(2) For the purposes of this Act, bets shall be deemed to be made by way of pool
betting either—
(a)

whenever a number of persons make bets on terms that the winnings of such
of those persons as are winners shall be, or be a share of, or be determined
by reference to, the stake money paid or agreed to be paid by all those
persons, whether the bets are made by means of a totalisator or by filling up
and returning coupons or other printed or written forms, or otherwise
howsoever; or

(b)

whenever a number of persons make bets on terms that the winnings of such
of those persons as are winners shall be, or shall include, an amount (not
determined by reference to the stake money paid or agreed to be paid by
those persons) which is divisible in any proportions among such of those
persons as are winners.

### 30.

Prohibition on use of premises for betting
(1) Subject to the provisions of this Act, no person shall use, or cause or knowingly
permit any other person to use, any premises for the purpose of the effecting by that
person or, as the case may be, by that other person of betting transactions; and a person
who contravenes the provisions of this subsection is guilty of an offence and liable, on
summary conviction, to a fine of three thousand dollars and to imprisonment for six
months.
(2) Any person who, for any purpose connected with the effecting of a betting
transaction, resorts to any premises which are being used in contravention of subsection
(1) is guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand
five hundred dollars.
(3) For the purposes of subsection (2), proof that a person was on premises while they
were being used as mentioned in that subsection shall be evidence that he or she resorted
to the premises for such a purpose as is so mentioned unless he or she proves that he or
she was on the premises for some bona fide purpose not connected with the effecting of a
betting transaction.

### 31.

Prohibition of carrying on betting transactions except as provided
(1) Except in accordance with the provisions of this Act, no person shall—
(a)

whether on his or her own account or as employee or agent of any other
person carrying on the business of receiving or negotiating bets or
conducting pool betting operations; or

by way of business in any manner hold himself or herself out, or permit
himself or herself to be held out, as a person who receives or negotiates bets
or conducts such operations.

(2) Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act, an employee or agent of the
holder of a betting office licence may carry on such business as is mentioned in
subsection (1)(a) or (b) on the premises in relation to which that licence is for the time
being in force without being the holder of a permit to do so.
(3) A person who contravenes the provisions of subsection (1) is guilty of an offence
and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of three thousand dollars and to
imprisonment for six months.

### 32.

Betting office licences
(1) A person who is the holder of a betting office licence in respect of premises in
relation to which that licence is for the time being in force may use those premises for the
effecting of any betting transaction by or through that person or an employee or agent of
his or her.
(2) The following persons, and those persons only, may apply for the grant or
renewal, as the case may be, of a betting office licence in respect of premises—
(a)

a person who is for the time being the holder of or the applicant for a permit
authorising him or her to carry on such business as is mentioned in section
31(1)(a) or (b);

(b)

a club duly authorised to conduct a race meeting in Grenada.

(3) Subject to the provisions of this section, the rules contained in the First Schedule
shall have effect for the purposes of betting office licences, a permit to carry on such
business as is mentioned in section 31(1)(a) or (b), a pinball machine licence and a permit
to operate a pinball machine under section 15.
(4) An application for the grant of a betting office licence in respect of premises may
be made notwithstanding that the premises have still to be constructed or are still in the
course of construction.
(5) A betting office licence granted to a foreign pools operator entitles that person to
carry on pool betting business as a foreign pools operator only and no other betting
transaction may be effected by such person, and a betting office licence granted to any
other person does not entitle that person to carry on business as a foreign pools operator.

### 33.

Permit to carry on business
A person desiring permission to carry on such business as is mentioned
section 31(1)(a) or (b), may apply for the grant of a permit to do so in accordance with
the provisions of this Act.

### 34.

Conduct of licensed betting office
(1) A licensed betting office shall be managed in accordance with the rules contained
in the Third Schedule, and the provisions thereof apply, mutatis mutandis, to premises in
which pinball machines are kept; in the case of a contravention of any of those rules the
licensee and any employee or agent of the licensee by whom the contravention was
committed is guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of three
thousand dollars.
(2) Where any person is charged with an offence under subsection (1) by reason only
of his or her being a licensee, it shall be a defence to prove that the contravention took
place without his or her consent or connivance and that he or she exercised all due
diligence to prevent it.

(3) Without prejudice to any other right to refuse a person admission to premises or to
expel a person from premises, in the case of a licensed betting office the licensee or any
employee or agent of his or her may refuse to admit to, or may expel from, the licensed
premises any person who is drunken, violent, quarrelsome or disorderly, or whose
presence on those premises would subject the licensee or any employee or agent of his or
her to a penalty under subsection (1). If a person liable to be expelled from the licensed
premises under this subsection, when requested by the licensee, any employee or agent of
the licensee or a police officer to leave those premises, fails to do so, he or she is guilty of
an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of two hundred and fifty dollars.
(4) A police officer may, at the request of the licensee or an employee or agent of the
licensee, help to expel from a licensed betting office any person whom the officer has
reasonable cause to believe to be liable to be expelled therefrom under subsection (3) and
such officer may use such force as may be required for that purpose.
(5) A police officer may enter a licensed betting office for the purpose of ascertaining
whether the provisions of subsection (1) are being complied with, and any person who
obstructs a police officer in the exercise of his or her powers under this subsection is
guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand five
hundred dollars.

### 35.

Conduct of pool betting business
The Fourth Schedule shall have effect for the purpose of regulating the conduct of a
pool betting business.

### 36.

Action maintainable in respect of betting transaction
Notwithstanding any rule of law to the contrary, an action shall lie for the recovery of
an amount claimed in respect of a betting transaction conducted by the holder of a betting
office licence.

### 37.

Pool betting duty
(1) There shall be charged on all bets made by way of pool betting a duty to be known
as the pool betting duty equal to twelve and one half per cent of the amount of the stake
paid.
(2) The pool betting duty shall be paid by the promoter to the Comptroller.
(3) The provisions set out in the Fifth Schedule shall have effect with respect to the
pool betting duty.
(4) This section and the Fifth Schedule shall apply in relation to all bets, wherever
made, where the promoter of the betting is the holder of a betting licence.

### 38.

Interpretation of certain words and provisions
(1) In sections 37 and 39 and the Third and Fourth Schedules—
“bets” does not include the taking of a ticket or chance in any lottery which by
virtue of section 21, 22, 23, 24 or 25, is not unlawful;
“promoter” means, in relation to pool betting, the person to whom the persons
making the bets look for the payment of their winnings, if any, and includes a foreign
pools operator.
(2) For the purposes of this Act any bet that is not deemed to be by way of pool
betting shall be deemed to be a bet at fixed odds.

(3) For the purposes of the pool betting duty where a payment in respect of the duty is
made before a person is entitled to make a bet by way of pool betting that payment shall,
if he or she makes the bet, be treated as part of the bet.

### 39.

Tax on fixed odds betting
(1) There shall be charged on all bets at fixed odds a tax equal to five per cent of the
amount of the stake paid.
(2) All tax chargeable on bets under subsection (1) shall be paid by the promoter to
the Comptroller.
(3) The Fourth Schedule shall, mutatis mutandis, have effect with respect to the tax
chargeable on bets mentioned in subsection (1).

### 40.

Betting, etc., in streets and public places
(1) A person frequenting or loitering in streets or public places on behalf either of
himself or herself or of any other person for the purpose of contravening section 31, or
betting or wagering or agreeing to bet or wager, or paying or receiving or settling bets, is
guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand five
hundred dollars and shall be liable to the forfeiture of all books, cards, papers and other
articles relating to betting transactions that may be found in his or her possession.
(2) A police officer may take into custody without warrant any person found
committing an offence under subsection (1) and may seize and detain any article liable to
be forfeited under that subsection.
(3) For the purpose of this section, “street” includes any highway, public bridge, road,
land, footway, square, court, alley or passage, whether a thoroughfare or not; and “public
place” includes any public park, garden, or beach, and any unenclosed ground to which
the public for the time being have unrestricted access.
(4) Nothing in this section shall apply to a race club in respect of betting transactions
carried on, upon ground used by the race club for holding horse race meetings, on days on
which races take place.

### 41.

Betting with young persons
(1) If any person—
(a)

has any betting transaction with a young person;

(b)

employs a young person in the effecting of any betting transaction or in a
licensed betting office; or

(c)

receives or negotiates any bet through a young person,

and knows or ought to know that such person is a young person he or she is guilty of an
offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand five hundred dollars.
(2) A person shall not be guilty of an offence under subsection (1) by reason of the
carriage by a young person of a communication relating to a betting transaction for the
purpose of its conveyance by post.
(3) In this section, the expression “young person” means a person who is under the
age of eighteen years.
(4) Notwithstanding subsections (1) and (2), in proceedings for an offence under this
section in respect of a person apparently under the age of eighteen years it shall be a
defence to prove that at the time of the alleged offence he or she had in fact attained that
age or, where it is proved that he or she was under the age of eighteen years, that he or
she appeared to have attained that age.

### 42.

Cancellation of and disqualification for betting office licence
(1) If the holder of a betting office licence is convicted—
(a)

of an offence under section 30, 40 or 41; or

of an offence involving fraud or dishonesty,

in the case of an offence under section 30, 40 or 41, the court by or before which he or
she is convicted may, if the court thinks fit, in addition to any other penalty imposed for
that offence, order that his or her licence be forfeited and cancelled, and in the case of an
offence involving fraud or dishonesty shall so order that his or her licence be forfeited
and cancelled and, where the licence is ordered to be forfeited and cancelled, the court
shall order the payment of all duties and taxes payable under this Act and of valid claims
made under this Act.
(2) An order under subsection (1) shall be deemed for the purposes of any appeal to
be part of the sentence for the offence.
(3) A person whose betting office licence is forfeited and cancelled in pursuance of an
order under subsection (1) shall, in the case of forfeiture and cancellation for fraud or
dishonesty by virtue of that order, be disqualified for five years from holding or obtaining
a licence beginning with the date of the conviction which gave rise to the order.
(4) In the case of forfeiture and cancellation for any cause other than fraud or
dishonesty, a person whose betting office licence is so forfeited and cancelled shall, by
virtue of that order, be disqualified from holding or obtaining a licence for a period of
three years beginning with the date of the conviction which gave rise to the order.
(5) Where a betting office licence is forfeited and cancelled in pursuance of an order
under subsection (1), the clerk of the court by which the order was made shall send a
copy of the order to the Authority.

### 43.

Offences by holder of betting office licence
(1) The holder of a betting office licence who employs in his or her betting
transactions any person known to him or her to be for the time being disqualified under
section 42(3) or (4) is guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of
one thousand five hundred dollars.
(2) If the holder of a betting office licence, on being required by a police officer to
produce his or her licence for examination, refuses or without reasonable cause fails to do
so, he or she is guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of two
hundred and fifty dollars.

## PART V

Miscellaneous

### 44.

Enforcement and penalties
(1) If a magistrate is satisfied on information on oath that there is reasonable ground
for suspecting that an offence under this Act is being, has been or is about to be
committed on any premises, he or she may issue a warrant in writing authorising a police
officer to enter those premises, if necessary by force, at any time within fourteen days
from the time of the issue of the warrant and search them; and a police officer who enters
the premises under the authority of the warrant may—
(a)

seize and remove any document or exposed money or security for money, or
any instrument or appliances for gambling as defined by section 4, or other
thing whatsoever found on the premises which he or she has reasonable

cause to believe may be required as evidence for the purposes of proceedings
in respect of any such offence; and

arrest and search any person found on the premises whom he or she has
reasonable cause to believe to be committing or to have committed or to be
about to commit any such offence.

(2) On conviction for an offence under this Act all instruments and appliances for
gambling so defined shall be forfeited, and all such money or securities for money shall
be paid in to the credit of the Consolidated Fund.
(3) Where, owing to the lateness of the hour or other reasonable cause, it is
inconvenient to obtain a warrant, a police officer of or above the rank of inspector, may
by night or day, without warrant, enter any premises, other than a private dwelling, which
he or she has reasonable grounds for believing is being kept or used as a common gaming
house, and such officer shall, upon such entry, have the powers of arrest, search, and
seizure as a police officer duly authorised by warrant under subsection (1).

### 45.

Unclaimed winnings
(1) All money and other prizes, being the winnings in any lottery exempted from the
provisions of Part III or the winnings of any betting transaction conducted by the holder
of a betting office licence under Part IV, that remain unpaid to the winner thereof at the
end of one year after the declaration of the results of such lottery or betting transaction
shall be paid to the Treasury by the promoters thereof.
(2) For the purposes of subsection (1) where a prize in a lottery or a betting
transaction does not consist of money, the prize shall, after the period specified in
subsection (1), be sold in the open market by the promoter of the lottery or betting
transaction and the proceeds thereof, less the expenses of the sale, shall be paid to the
Treasury as aforesaid.
(3) Subject to the provisions of subsection (5) the Authority, on presentation of proof
to their satisfaction that a person is entitled to the money or proceeds representing any
such prize as is mentioned in subsections (1) and (2), shall authorise the payment and
distribution of the appropriate amount of such money or proceeds to the person so
entitled, and the same shall without further authority be paid by the Treasury.
(4) Where payment is made by the Treasury in accordance with subsection (3) the
validity thereof shall not be enquired into by a court and no action shall lie or be
maintained in respect of the lottery or betting transaction that resulted in the payment
aforesaid.
(5) At the end of three years after the proceeds or money representing prizes are
required to be paid to the Treasury under subsections (1) and (2) no application for the
payment of such proceeds or money shall, except with the approval of the Minister, be
entertained, and all rights, claims, actions or other causes in respect of such proceeds or
money shall, if not already pending, be barred, and such proceeds or money shall be
applied to the promotion of such form of sport or such charity as the Minister may direct.

### 46.

Offence of sending money abroad
Every person, other than a foreign pools operator, who sends or causes to be sent out
of Grenada any money or valuable thing in connection with a betting transaction is guilty
of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of one thousand five hundred
dollars.

### 47.

Postmaster General may open suspect postal packet on warrant of Minister
The Postmaster General may, on warrant in writing under the hand of the Minister,
open or cause to be opened a postal packet for transmission by post to a foreign

destination being a packet which the Postmaster-General suspects to contain any money
or valuable thing in contravention of section 46 and, on conviction of a person for an
offence under section 46 in respect thereof, the money or valuable thing found therein
shall be forfeited.

### 48.

Power to make rules and regulations
(1) The Minister may, for the purposes of giving effect to the provisions of this Act
and the Schedules, make such rules and regulations as may be necessary or expedient.
(2) The rules contained in the Schedules shall be deemed to be made under this section.

Third Schedule
GAMBLING, LOTTERIES AND BETTING ACT
Rules for Licensed Betting Offices and Premises Containing Pinball Machines
[Section 34.]

1\. The licensed premises shall be closed throughout Good Friday, Christmas Day and
every Sunday, and on such days as are prescribed in the Schedule to the Bank Holidays
Act (Chapter 25) and shall not be used for any purpose other than the effecting of betting
transactions.
2\. No person who is a young person within the meaning of section 41(3) and who is
known to be under the age of eighteen years by an individual connected with the
licensee’s business present on the licensed premises, shall be admitted to or allowed to
remain on those premises; so, however, that in any proceedings for a contravention of this
Rule, it shall be a defence to prove that at the time of the alleged contravention he or she
was a person who had in fact attained the age of eighteen years or had apparently attained
that age.

(a)

and shall also display a notice indicating that young persons are prohibited
from entering or remaining upon the licensed premises;

(b)

exhibit on the licensed premises such notices in such form and in such
locations as may be prescribed; and

(c)

comply with any prescribed restrictions with respect to the exhibiting of
written matter or signs of any description on the licensed premises.

Fourth Schedule
GAMBLING, LOTTERIES AND BETTING ACT
Requirements as to the Conduct of Pool Betting Businesses
[Section 35.]

1\. Conduct of pool betting business
(1) Subject to the provisions of this paragraph, pool betting business carried on by the
holder of a betting office licence shall comply with the following requirements—
(a)

the business shall take the form of the promotion of competitions for prizes
for making forecasts as to sporting or other events, the bets being entries in
the competitions and the winnings in respect of the bets being the prizes or
shares in the prizes;

(b)

each bet shall be an entry in a particular competition;

(c)

the stakes and the winnings shall be entirely in money;

(d)

in each competition, the prizes shall be equally available for all the bets and
accordingly the question which bets qualified for, or for shares in, the prize
or prizes and, save so far as it depends on the amounts staked, the amounts
of the respective shares in the prizes, shall be determined solely by the
relative success of the forecasts embodied in the respective bets;

(e)

the total amount payable by way of winnings shall, in the case of each
competition, be the total amount of the stakes (less the pool betting duty
payable in respect thereof) in respect of entries in that competition less such
percentage of that total amount as may be determined by the promoter, being
the same percentage in respect of all his or her competitions which depend
on the same events taking place on the same day (and being a percentage
which is determined and notified to the accountant appointed under
paragraph 4 hereof before that day);

(f)

the rules or any alteration thereof applying to the competition shall be posted
up on some conspicuous part of the licensed premises and notified to the
accountant at least one day before the first of the relevant sporting or other
events takes place.

(2) Notwithstanding anything in subparagraph (1)(d) or (e) if it is found that a bet
which ought to have been treated as a winning bet has been inadvertently overlooked, the
promoter shall pay to the person making that bet the sum paid by him or her to other
persons making comparable bets or, if there are no comparable bets, such sum as would
have been payable had such winning bet not been overlooked.
(3) The reference in paragraph 1(1)(e) to the day on which the events on which a
competition depends take place shall, where the events do not take place wholly on the
same day, be construed as a reference to the last day on which any of the events take
place.
2\. Rejected entries
References in this Schedule to stakes in, or in respect of entries in, any competition do
not include references to stakes in respect of rejected entries.
3\. Information to be supplied by promoters
(1) As soon as may be after the events to which any of his or her competitions relate
have taken place, and in any case not later than seven days after the announcement of the
results of the competition, every promoter shall send to the accountant a statement
showing—

(a)

the total amount of the stakes in respect of entries in the competition;

the total amount payable by way of winnings in that competition;

(c)

the total amount of the stakes in respect of winning bets in the competition,
and if there are more prizes than one in the competition, the total amount of
the stakes in respect of winning bets qualifying for or for shares in each of
the prizes; and

(d)

the amount payable in respect of each winning bet.

(2) In September in each year every promoter shall send to the accountant and to the
Comptroller a statement showing, as respects his or her pool betting business for the
twelve-month period ending on the 30th June in the same year—
(a)

the aggregate total stakes in all his or her competitions;

(b)

the aggregate prize money in respect of those competitions;

(c)

the amount of the promoter’s commission of fifteen per cent.

4\. Appointment, etc., of the accountant
(1) The Comptroller may in the case of each promoter appoint a person being
competent to be the accountant to exercise and perform, in relation to that promoter, the
powers and duties conferred and imposed by the provisions of this Schedule.
(2) The remuneration of the accountant in respect of each promoter shall be payable
out of the Consolidated Fund, but the amount so paid to the accountant shall be
recoverable as a debt jointly due to the Government from the promoters.
(3) A Magistrate’s Court shall have jurisdiction to hear and determine any claim for a
debt mentioned in subparagraph (2) without limit of jurisdiction and the provisions of the
Magistrates Act (Cap. 177) shall apply thereto.
(4) In this section, “remuneration” includes any sum paid or to be paid to the
accountant in respect of his or her expenses.
5\. Duties and powers of the accountant
(1) The accountant shall—
(a)

examine all statements submitted to him or her under paragraph 3 with a
view to determining whether there is cause to believe that any of the
provisions of this Schedule are being contravened in relation to the
competition in question;

(b)

retain all such statements for two years; and

(c)

at any time during that period give facilities for their examination by an
officer of the Inland Revenue Department authorised in that behalf by the
Comptroller.

(2) The accountant and any agent of his or her authorised in that behalf by him or her
in writing may at all reasonable times enter premises on which a promoter is carrying on
his or her business and enquire into the manner in which that business is being carried on,
and may require the promoter or any employee of his or her to give to the accountant or
agent all such information and produce all such accounts, books and other documents and
carry out such checks or additional checks of coupons or other entry forms as the
accountant or agent may think necessary for the purpose of determining whether there is
cause to believe that any of the provisions of this Schedule are being contravened. It shall
be the duty of the promoter to preserve in good condition all accounts, books or other
documents (including coupons or other entry forms) that relate wholly or partly to his or
her competitions for, in the case of coupons and other entry forms, at least two months,
and in the case of accounts, books or other documents, at least three years, from the day
on which the events take place on which each competition depends, or, where the events

do not take place wholly on the same day, from the last day on which any of the events in
relation to a competition take place.
(3) If at any time the accountant has cause to believe that any of the provisions of this
Schedule are being contravened in relation to any of the competitions of a promoter, he or
she shall report the matter to the Comptroller.
(4) Not later than the last day of November in each year the accountant shall furnish
to the Comptroller a report stating, if it be the fact, that he or she has examined all
statements submitted to him or her by a promoter under paragraph 3 for the period
mentioned in subparagraph (2) of that paragraph which ended in that year, has conducted
such enquiries into the manner in which the business of that promoter is carried on and
made such examination of that promoter’s records as he or she considers necessary, and
whether or not he or she has cause to believe that any of the provisions of this Schedule
have been contravened in relation to any of the competitions of that promoter which have
taken place during the said period.
6\. Offences
(1) Any person who—
(a)

obstructs the accountant or any agent of his or her in the execution of his or
her powers or duties under this Schedule; or

(b)

fails to comply with any requirement duly made of him or her by the
accountant or any agent of his or her, or, in response to such a requirement,
knowingly gives any information which is false or misleading,

is guilty of an offence.
(2) If any promoter fails to perform a duty imposed upon him or her by this Schedule,
or if any of the provisions of this Schedule, except so far as they impose duties on the
accountant or the Comptroller, are contravened by him or her in course of his or her
business as a promoter, he or she is guilty of an offence.
(3) The Comptroller shall have power to institute proceedings for offences under this
Schedule.
(4) A person guilty of an offence under this Schedule is liable, on summary
conviction, to a fine of five thousand dollars.

Fifth Schedule
GAMBLING, LOTTERIES AND BETTING ACT
Provisions as to Pool Betting Duty
[Section 37(3).]

1\. Pool betting duty shall be under the care and management of the Comptroller, and
shall be paid over at such times, by the persons by whom it is payable, as the Comptroller
may direct.
2\. In this Schedule, “officer” means an officer of the Department of Inland Revenue, and
includes any person who is expressly authorised by the Comptroller to perform the duties
of an officer for the purposes of this Schedule.
3\. Any person who carries on a business the carrying on of which involves or may
involve any sums becoming due from him or her by way of pool betting duty, shall—
(a)

not less than fourteen days before he or she begins to carry on the business,
notify the Comptroller that he or she intends to carry on that business;

not later than the date of the first user thereof for the purposes of the
business, make entry with the Comptroller, in such manner as the
Comptroller may require, of all premises and totalisators, if any, used by him
or her for the purposes of the business;

(c)

keep such books, records and accounts in relation to the business as the
Comptroller may direct and, for at least two years or such longer period as
the Comptroller may in any particular case direct, preserve on the premises
where entry has been made as aforesaid any books, records and accounts
directed to be kept by him or her under this paragraph and any other books,
records, accounts or documents relating to the business; and

(d)

permit an officer to enter premises used for the purposes of the business, to
inspect any totalisator used for those purposes, and to inspect and take copies
of any books, records, accounts or other documents in his or her possession
or custody or on any premises used for those purposes being books, records,
accounts or documents which relate or appear to relate to the business,

and any such person, and any person employed or having functions in connection with
any such business, shall if required to do so by the Comptroller or an officer produce, at a
time and place to be specified by the Comptroller or the officer, any such books, records,
accounts or documents relating to the business and make, at times and to persons to be so
specified, such returns relating to the business, as the Comptroller or the officer may
require.
4\. A person is guilty of an offence under this Schedule and liable, on summary
conviction, to a fine of five thousand dollars or treble the amount of the duty which is
unpaid or payment of which is sought to be evaded, as the case may be, at the election of
the Comptroller, who—
(a)

fails to pay any pool betting duty payable by him or her;

(b)

contravenes any of the provisions of paragraph 3;

(c)

obstructs an officer in the performance of his or her functions in relation to
pool betting duty;

(d)

in connection with pool betting duty, knowingly or recklessly makes any
statement which is false in a material particular, or, with intent to deceive,
produces or makes use of any book, account, record, return or other
document which is false in a material particular; or

(e)

is knowingly concerned in, or attempts fraudulent evasion of pool betting
duty by himself, herself or any other person.

5\. Where a person is convicted of an offence under paragraph 4(d) or (e) the court may,
in lieu of or in addition to ordering him or her to pay the fine, order him or her to be
imprisoned for a term not exceeding two years.
6\. Where a person is convicted of an offence under paragraph 4 in respect of a failure to
comply with any of the provisions of paragraph 3 and the failure continues after the
conviction, then, unless he or she has a reasonable excuse for the continuance of the
failure, he or she shall be guilty of an offence under this Schedule.
7\. Where an offence under this Schedule has been committed by a company, every
person who at the time of the commission of the offence was a director, general manager,
secretary or other similar officer of the company, or was purporting to act in any such
capacity, shall be deemed to be guilty of that offence unless he or she proves that the
offence was committed without his or her consent or connivance and that he or she
exercised all such diligence to prevent the commission of the offence as he or she ought
to have exercised having regard to the nature of his or her functions in that capacity, and
in all the circumstances.

8\. (1) Summary proceedings in respect of an offence under this Schedule may,
notwithstanding anything to the contrary in the Criminal Procedure Code Act, Chapter
72B, be taken at any time within twelve months from the date on which the commission
of the offence comes to the knowledge of the Comptroller, but so that proceedings shall
not be taken after the expiration of two years from the date of the commission of the
offence.
(2) For the purposes of this paragraph, a certificate of the Comptroller as to the date
on which the commission of the offence came to his or her knowledge shall be conclusive
evidence thereof.

### 1.

Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Gambling and Betting (Licences) Regulations.

### 2.

Definitions
(1) In these Regulations—

“Act” means the Gambling, Lotteries and Betting Act, Chapter 120;
“Authority” means the Gaming Authority established under section 3 of the Act;
“Schedule” means the Schedule to these Regulations.
(2) In these Regulations any reference to a Form in the Schedule shall include a
reference to a Form to the like effect with such variations as the circumstances may
require.

### 3.

Application for grant of permits and licences
An application made in accordance with paragraph 5 of the First Schedule to the Act
for the grant of a permit to carry on such business as is mentioned in section 31(1)(a) or
(b) of the Act or a permit to operate a pinball machine under section 15 of the Act or a
pinball machine licence or a betting office licence, shall be made in writing in Forms
numbered 1 and 2, as the case may be, in the Schedule and shall contain such particulars
and, if the application is for a permit, give such references as are required thereby.

### 4.

Application for renewal of permits and licences
An application made in accordance with paragraph 9 of the First Schedule to the Act
for the renewal of a permit to carry on such business as is mentioned in section 31(1)(a)
or (b) of the Act or a permit to operate a pinball machine under section 15 of the Act or a
pinball machine licence or a betting office licence shall be made in writing in Forms
numbered 3 and 4, as the case may be, in the Schedule and shall contain such particulars
as are required thereby.

### 5.

Form of permits and licences
A permit to carry on such business as is mentioned in section 31(1)(a) or (b) of the
Act or a permit to operate a pinball machine under section 15 of the Act or a pinball
machine licence or a betting office licence shall be granted in Form numbered 5 and
renewed in Form numbered 6 in the Schedule.

### 6.

Register of permits and licences
The Authority shall cause to be kept registers of permits to carry on business under
section 31(1)(a) or (b) of the Act and permits to operate a pinball machine under section
15 of the Act and betting office licences and pinball machine licences granted or renewed
by them, and containing inter alia the following particulars—
(a)

in the case of a permit to carry on such business as is mentioned in
section 31(1)(a) or (b) of the Act which is cancelled under section 42 of the
Act, particulars of the cancellation;

(b)

in the case of a betting office licence which by virtue of paragraph 18 of the
First Schedule to the Act ceases to be in force, particulars of the
circumstances in which it ceased to be in force.

### 7.

Fee for inspection of registers
The registers of permits and licences referred to in paragraph 6 hereof shall be opened
during office hours at the Inland Revenue Office, St. George’s, for inspection of permits
by persons other than constables on payment of a fee of two dollars and fifty cents.
