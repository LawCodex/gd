## CHAPTER 143

HUMAN REMAINS (IMPORTATION CONTROL) ACT
An Act to regulate and control the importation for anatomical examination of
human remains, and for connected matters.
[Act No. 36 of 1978.]

[29th September, 1978.]

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Human Remains (Importation Control) Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act—
“documents” means the documents referred to in paragraph 2 of the Schedule;
“Minister” means the Minister responsible for public health;
“Sanitary Authority” means the Sanitary Authority constituted under the Public
Health Act, Chapter 263;
“sanitary officer” means a sanitary officer under the Public Health Act, Chapter 263.

### 3.

Minister may grant licence
(1) The Minister, subject to regulations made under this Act and after receipt of copies
of the documents, may grant a licence to import human remains for the purpose of
anatomical examination—
(a)

to fellows or members of any college of physicians or surgeons;

(b)

to graduates or licentiates of medicine; or

(c)

to the Saint George’s University School of Medicine Limited.

(2) The Minister may, in a licence granted under subsection (1), specify such
conditions not inconsistent with the provisions of this Act or the regulations as he or she
may deem necessary in the interest of public health.

### 4.

Documents to be delivered with human remains, etc.
(1) Every person to whom a licence has been granted under this Act shall, on the
landing of the human remains, demand and receive the documents together with the
human remains, and he or she shall, in a book to be kept by him or her for the purpose,
enter or cause to be entered the particulars contained in the documents, and produce such
book for inspection whenever required to do so by a sanitary officer.
(2) The person receiving the documents shall within seven days transmit them to the
Sanitary Authority.

### 5.

Requirement of cremation and burial
After undergoing anatomical examination the human remains shall be cremated and
buried, and a certificate of the burial shall within seven days be transmitted to the
Sanitary Authority.

### 6.

Offences
A person who—
(a)

imports human remains for anatomical examination contrary to the
provisions of this Act or in breach of a condition in a licence issued under
section 3 is guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine
of ten thousand dollars and to imprisonment for twelve months;

(b)

contravenes any provision of section 4 or 5 is guilty of an offence and liable,
on summary conviction, to a fine of ten thousand dollars and to
imprisonment for six months.

### 7.

Power of Minister to make regulations and to amend Schedule
(1) The Minister may make and thereafter amend regulations providing for any matter
which in his or her opinion is necessary to give effect to the provisions of this Act in the
interest of public health.
(2) The regulations contained in the subsidiary legislation shall have effect as if made
by the Minister under this section.
(3) The Minister shall have power to amend the regulations contained in the
subsidiary legislation.

HUMAN REMAINS (IMPORTATION CONTROL) REGULATIONS
[Section 7.]

### 1.

Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Human Remains (Importation Control)
Regulations.

### 2.

Documents
The following documents shall accompany the shipment of human remains to
Grenada—
(a)

an official certificate of the cause of death issued by the local registrar of
death or similar authority; and

(b)

a statement by the person authorised to prepare the remains, certified by an
appropriate authority, indicating the manner in which the body was prepared,
and stating that the coffin contains only the body in question and the
necessary clothing; and

(c)

a transit permit specifying the name, age and sex of the deceased, issued by
the competent authority for the place of death, or the place of burial in the
case of exhumed human remains; or

(d)

a transit permit, issued by an appropriate authority, specifying the name, sex,
age and cause of death of the deceased and also the name of the embalmer,
the name of the coroner and the name of the appropriate public health
officer.

### 3.

Health measures
(1) Human remains shall be subject to the following measures before shipment to
Grenada—
(a)

thorough washing and disinfection of the body and all orifices followed by
the packing of the orifices with cotton soaked in disinfectant and wrapping
of the body in a sheet saturated in disinfectant and placement in an
impervious coffin;

(b)

proper embalming and placement in an impervious coffin;

(c)

proper embalming and encasement in a plastic container sealed by heat or
adhesive materials; or

(d)

proper embalming and encasement in an air disaster bag.

(2) For the purpose of this regulation, “impervious coffin” means—
(a)

a container or box, of whatever material, hermetically sealed and so
maintained by plastic or rubber gasket or by metal or similar material which
has been soldered or welded;

(b)

a plastic container sealed by heat or adhesive materials.
