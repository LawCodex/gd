## CHAPTER 22

AUCTIONEERS ACT
An Act to provide for the licensing and control of auctioneers.
[Cap. 22 of 1959 amended by Act No. 6 of 1962, Act No. 10 of 1990, Act No. 52 of 1991.]

[1st July, 1951.]
1\.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Auctioneers Act.

2\.

Interpretation
A person—
(a)

who exercises or carries on the trade or business of an auctioneer;

(b)

who acts in such capacity at any sale; or

(c)

who sells or offers for sale any goods or chattels, lands, tenements or
hereditaments, or any interest therein, at any sale where any person
becomes the purchaser of the same by competition and being the highest
bidder, either by being the single bidder, or by increasing upon the

biddings made by others or decreasing on sums named by the auctioneer
or person acting as auctioneer, or other person at such sale, or by any
other mode of sale by competition,
shall be an auctioneer within the meaning of this Act.

Licence necessary

(1) No person shall, except as hereinafter provided, carry on the business of an
auctioneer unless he or she has obtained from the Permanent Secretary (Finance) a
licence in the form in the First Schedule:
Provided that no licence shall be issued to any person until he or she gives to the
satisfaction of the Permanent Secretary (Finance) good and sufficient security in the sum
of fifteen hundred dollars for the faithful performance of the duties of his or her calling or
business and for the purpose of securing the payment of any fines, penalties,
judgements, damages or costs which may be imposed upon or recovered against him or
her as auctioneer.
(2) No licence shall be issued under this Act to a corporation, sole or aggregate, or
to a partnership.
4\.

Duration and conditions of licence

(1) A licence under this Act may be issued either in respect of any half-year ending
on the thirtieth day of June or thirty-first day of December, or in respect of any year
ending on the thirty-first day of December.
(2) Every licence shall continue in force from the date on which it is granted until
the end of the half-year or year in respect of which it is granted, and shall be subject
to such conditions as are therein specified.
5\.

Licence duty

(1) The amount payable to the Permanent Secretary (Finance) for a licence under
this Act shall be twenty-five dollars for a half-yearly licence or fifty dollars for a yearly
licence.
(2) Every such amount shall be paid into and form part of the Consolidated Fund.
6\.

Refusal and cancellation of licence

(1) Notwithstanding anything in the preceding sections contained, the Permanent
Secretary (Finance) may refuse any application for an auctioneer’s licence or to renew
any such licence, and may cancel the licence of any auctioneer who has—
(a)

been convicted of any offence under this Act;

(b)

failed to account for or pay over any monies or property which have come
into his or her possession or control as such auctioneer;

(c)

parted with any property entrusted to him or her for sale without
obtaining payment for the same and has failed to make good to his or her
employer the value thereof; or

(d)

otherwise misconducted himself or herself as an auctioneer.

(2) A refusal to issue or to renew, or the cancellation of, an auctioneer’s licence,
shall be notified to the person affected thereby under the hand of the Permanent
Secretary (Finance).
(3) A person aggrieved by any action of the Permanent Secretary (Finance) under
this section may appeal to the Governor-General whose decision shall be final.

List of licensed auctioneers to be published

The Permanent Secretary (Finance) shall, at the beginning of every month, cause to
be published in the Gazette and in at least one local newspaper, a list of the persons to
whom licences have been granted by him or her during the preceding month, and if at
any time he or she cancels a licence he or she shall publish in like manner the name of
the person whose licence shall have been so cancelled.

Penalty for selling without licence

A person who carries on the business of an auctioneer without being duly licensed
shall be guilty of an offence and liable—

9\.

(a)

for a first offence, to a fine of five hundred dollars; and

(b)

for a second or subsequent offence, to a fine of three thousand dollars.

Keeping of accounts by auctioneer

(1) Every auctioneer shall keep a regular and correct account of all property sold
by him or her at auction, such account to be written in a book to be kept for that
purpose, in the form contained in the Second Schedule, and every such account shall
be open to the inspection of every person interested:
Provided that it shall be a sufficient compliance with the provisions of this
subsection if an entry is made in the book of the name of the person to whom the
property sold belongs, together with a reference to some other book wherein is kept
an account containing the particulars mentioned in the form aforesaid.
(2) A person who fails to keep such account in the manner hereinbefore
mentioned, or hinders or prevents any person interested from inspecting any such
book, shall be guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of five hundred dollars.
10\.

Remuneration payable to auctioneers

(1) It shall be competent for an auctioneer to make an agreement with a client,
and for a client to make an agreement with an auctioneer, for the remuneration of the
auctioneer to such amount and in such manner as the auctioneer and the client think
fit, and it shall be competent for the auctioneer to accept and for the client to give
remuneration accordingly. Any such agreement shall be in writing and signed by the
person to be bound thereby or by his or her agent in that behalf.
(2) In the absence of any such agreement as is mentioned in subsection (1), on a
sale or attempted sale at auction of the properties referred to in the Third Schedule an
auctioneer is entitled in respect of such sale or attempted sale, to demand and to
receive the relevant commission or fee specified in that Schedule.
(3) Subject to the provisions of subsection (1), any auctioneer who demands,
receives or retains any commission or fee in excess of the commission or fee hereby
chargeable shall be guilty of an offence and liable to a fine of five hundred dollars.
11\.

Power to grant permission to sell at auction without licence

Notwithstanding the provisions of this Act, the Minister of Finance may, from time to
time, grant permission to a public officer to sell at auction any property on account of
the Government or of any Ministry or Department.
12\.

Exceptions

This Act does not apply to auction sales—
(a)

by the Registrar, bailiff or any other officer of a court on legal process in
the exercise of any powers conferred on them by law;

(b)

by any police officer or other public officer in any Ministry or Department
where such sales are held under the provisions of any written law or
departmental regulation.

Recovery of penalties

All penalties under this Act may be recovered on summary conviction in the name
of the Permanent Secretary (Finance) or some person authorised by him or her in
writing either generally or in a special case.

## PART II

Where a Sale is not Effected
The following fee in addition to all out-of-pocket expenses and storage fees, if any,
shall be chargeable in respect of every description of property—
ten per cent of the fee otherwise payable under Part I of this Schedule.
On a mortgagee’s sale where no reserve price is fixed, the reserve price shall be
deemed to be the amount secured by the mortgage. In other cases where no reserve
price is fixed, the reserve price shall be deemed to be the value of the property as
agreed between the person offering the property for sale and the auctioneer.
