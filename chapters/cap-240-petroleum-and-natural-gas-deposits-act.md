## CHAPTER 240

PETROLEUM AND NATURAL GAS DEPOSITS ACT
An Act to make provision with respect to petroleum exploration and production,
and for matters connected therewith.
[Act No. 22 of 1989 amended by Act No. 31 of 2007.]

[16th August, 1991.] *
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Petroleum and Natural Gas Deposits Act.
2\. Interpretation
(1) In this Act—
“Act” includes the regulations;
“conditions” includes terms, limitations and stipulations;
“development licence” means a licence authorising operations for and in
connection with petroleum production;
“Director” means the Director for Petroleum Affairs appointed under section 30;
“drilling” includes perforating the earth’s surface, whether the hole is vertical,
inclined or horizontal, and preventing the collapse of the sides of the hole or
preventing the hole from becoming filled by extraneous materials (including water),
and the filling of wellheads, coring and logging, and operations incidental to any of
the foregoing;
“exploration licence” means a licence authorising operations for and in connection
with petroleum exploration;
“land” includes land covered by water, marshes, coastal marine swamp lands and
the offshore area;
“licence” means an exploration licence or a development licence, or both, as the
context requires;
“licensee” and “grantee” mean a company to which a licence has been granted and
includes any person to whom the licence or an interest therein is lawfully transferred;
“Minister” means the Minister responsible for matters concerning petroleum
exploration;
“offshore area” means the area of the internal waters, the territorial sea, the
archipelagic waters, the continental shelf and the exclusive economic zone of Grenada
and the space above and underlying that area;
“permission” means permission granted under section 11;
“petroleum” means—
(a)

naturally occurring hydrocarbons, whether in a gaseous, liquid or solid state;

(b)

any naturally occurring mixture of hydrocarbons, whether in a gaseous,
liquid or solid state; or

(c)

any naturally occurring mixture of one or more hydrocarbons (whether in a
gaseous, liquid or solid state) and some other substance,

and includes petroleum described in paragraph (a), (b) or (c) that has been returned to
a natural reservoir, but does not include coal, shale or any substance that may be
extracted from coal or shale;
“petroleum agreement” means an agreement in respect of any of the matters
referred to in section 6;

\*

Notes.—(1) This Act, on commencement, repealed and replaced the Petroleum and Natural
Gas Deposits Act (14 of 1974) which would otherwise have been included in this Revision.
(2) The Petroleum and Natural Gas Deposits Act commenced by SRO 30 of 1991.

“regulation” means regulations made under section 29;
“well” means a hole in land made by drilling in connection with operations for
petroleum exploration or development but does not include a seismic shot hole.
(2) In this Act, “internal waters”, “territorial sea”, “archipelagic waters”, “continental
shelf” and “exclusive economic zone” have their respective meanings in section 2 of the
Territorial Sea and Maritime Boundaries Act, Chapter 318.
3\. Property in petroleum to continue to be vested in the Government
(1) Notwithstanding anything to the contrary in any written law, or the prerogative of
the Crown, or any grant or lease from the Crown, the property in all petroleum in land is,
and shall be deemed always to have been, vested in the Government.
(2) Fees, rents and royalties payable under this Act shall be paid to the Treasury.
4\. Licence for exploration or production of petroleum
(1) Subject to the provisions of this Act, no person shall carry out operations relating
to petroleum exploration or production except under and in accordance with a licence
granted under this Act.
(2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) shall be guilty of an offence and liable,
on summary conviction, to a fine of fifty thousand dollars and to imprisonment for two
years and, in the case of a continuing offence, to a further fine of five thousand dollars for
every day during which the offence continues.
5\. Persons to whom licence may be granted
(1) A licence may be granted—
(a)

in the case of an exploration licence, to a company whether incorporated in
or outside Grenada; but in the case of a company incorporated outside
Grenada, it shall have an established place of business in Grenada and be
registered as a foreign company in accordance with the provisions of the
Companies Act, Chapter 58A;

(b)

in the case of a development licence, to a body corporate which is a
company or a corporation incorporated and registered in Grenada.

(2) A licence may be granted to two or more persons associated together by any form
of joint arrangement, if each one of them is qualified to hold the licence under
subsection (1).
(3) Where at any time a licensee comprises two or more persons the obligations to be
observed and performed by the licensee under this Act shall be joint and several
obligations, but without prejudice to any right of contribution which may exist between
them.
6\. Agreement with respect to grant of licence, etc.
The Minister may enter into an agreement (not inconsistent with this Act) with any
person with respect to all or any of the following matters, namely—
(a)

the grant of a licence to that person or to any other person identified in the
agreement, including a body corporate yet to be formed;

(b)

the conditions to be included in a licence granted or renewed;

(c)

the procedure to be followed by the Minister while exercising any discretion
conferred upon him or her by or under this Act and the manner in which the
discretion shall be exercised;

(d)

any matter incidental to or connected with the foregoing.

7\. Application for licence
(1) An application for a licence shall be made to the Minister in the prescribed form
and notice of the application shall be published in the Gazette and in at least one daily
newspaper printed and circulating in Grenada and in some other manner the Minister
thinks proper.
(2) An application for a licence may be made by two or more persons jointly if the
agreement between the parties thereto as to the proposed joint operation is submitted with
such application to the Minister.
8\. Grant of licence
(1) The grant of a licence shall be in accordance with the provisions of this Act and,
subject thereto, may be upon such conditions as the Minister considers appropriate.
(2) The grant of a licence shall be subject to the rights of the owners of the surface of
the land in respect of which the licence is granted.
(3) The Minister may determine that the grant of a licence shall be subject to such
procedure of competitive bidding as may be prescribed.
9\. Assignment of licence with Minister’s consent
(1) Unless the licence otherwise provides, an assignment or transfer to another person
of the rights acquired or the obligations undertaken thereunder shall be null and void
unless the previous consent in writing of the Minister has been obtained.
(2) An application by the holder of a licence for consent to the assignment or transfer
of the licence or any rights or obligations thereunder shall be made to the Minister in the
prescribed form.
10\. Cancellation of licence
(1) The Minister may in writing at any time, subject to the provisions of subsection
(3) and without prejudice to the continuing validity of any obligations outstanding
thereunder, cancel a licence in any case where—
(a)

he or she is satisfied that the licence was obtained as a result of wilful
misrepresentation by the licensee in any material particular in the process of
applying for the licence; or

(b)

the holder of the licence is in default, that is to say, in breach of a provision
of this Act or of any condition of a licence or permission or any provision of
a petroleum agreement.

(2) Where a licence is cancelled pursuant to subsection (1), the Minister shall cause
notice of the cancellation to be sent by registered post to the registered office of the
licensee and to be published in the Gazette.
(3) The Minister shall not, under subsection (1), cancel a licence on the ground of
default unless—
(a)

he or she has, by notice served on the licensee, given not less than thirty
days’ notice of the intention to cancel the licence on that ground;

(b)

he or she has in the notice, specified a reasonable date before which the
licensee may in writing submit any representation which he or she wishes
the Minister to consider; and

(c)

he or she has taken into account—

(i) any action taken by the licensee to remove that ground or, where the
default cannot be remedied, any offer by the licensee to the Minister
of adequate compensation in respect thereof and any action by the
licensee to prevent the recurrence of similar grounds, and
(ii) any representation submitted to the Minister by the licensee pursuant
to paragraph (b).
(4) The Minister shall not, under subsection (1), cancel a licence on the ground that
the licensee has failed to pay an amount payable by him or her under this Act or the
licence if, before the date referred to in subsection (3)(b), and specified in a notice under
that subsection, the licensee shall have paid the amount, together with any additional
amount which may be payable under the regulations.
(5) The Minister may, by notice served on a licensee, cancel his or her licence, if, in
the case of a licensee that is a body corporate, an order is made or a resolution is passed
winding-up the affairs of the body corporate, unless the winding-up is for the purpose of
an amalgamation and the Minister has consented to the amalgation, or is for the purpose
of reconstruction and the Minister has been given notice of the proposed reconstruction.
(6) Subject to the provisions of a petroleum agreement, where two or more persons,
associated together by any form of joint arrangement, constitute a licensee, the Minister
shall not, under subsection (5), cancel the licence on the occurrence, in relation to one or
some only of those persons, of an event entitling the Minister to do so under that
subsection if any other person or persons constituting the licensee satisfies or satisfy the
Minister that he, she or they, as the case may be, is or are willing and would be able to
carry out the duties and obligations of the licensee.
(7) On the cancellation of a licence, the rights of the holder thereunder shall cease, but
the cancellation shall not affect any liability incurred by the holder before the cancellation
and any legal proceedings that might have been commenced or continued against him or
her may be commenced or continued against him or her notwithstanding the cancellation
of the licence.
11\. Permission for geological or geophysical survey, etc.
(1) The Minister may grant permission to a person to carry on geological, geophysical
and other surveys or investigations in, on or in relation to land, being surveys or
investigations which in his or her opinion are relevant for the identification of a
petroleum reservoir, or for petroleum exploration or development, on such conditions as
may be agreed to between him or her and the applicant for the permission.
(2) Every permission granted pursuant to subsection (1) shall be in writing and shall
include conditions relating to—
(a)

the period for which the permission is granted;

(b)

furnishing the Minister with information obtained as a result of survey or
investigation;

(c)

restrictions (if any) on the dissemination to any person other than the
Minister of information obtained as a result of survey or investigation; and

(d)

payment of fees to the Government for the grant of the permission.

12\. Directions
(1) The Minister may, by notice in writing served on the grantee of a licence or
permission, give to the grantee a direction not inconsistent with good oilfield practices as
to any matter with respect to which regulations may be made under section 29.

(2) A person who fails or neglects to comply with a lawful direction given under
subsection (1) shall be guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of
fifty thousand dollars and to imprisonment for two years.
(3) In proceedings for the prosecution of an offence under this section it shall be a
sufficient defence if the accused proves that he or she promptly took all reasonable steps
to comply with the direction.
13\. Compliance with directions
(1) Subject to section 12, where a person fails or neglects to comply with a lawful
direction given to him or her under that section, the Minister may cause to be done all or
any of the things required to be done by the direction.
(2) Costs and expenses incurred by the Minister under subsection (1) shall be a debt
due to the Government from the person to whom the direction was given and may be
recovered in a court.
14\. Royalty
(1) Subject to the provisions of this section, the holder of a development licence shall,
in accordance with his or her licence and the regulations, pay royalty in respect of
petroleum obtained by him or her in the area covered by his or her licence.
(2) Where provision is made in a licence for the payment of royalty in kind, the word
“pay” and cognate expressions in this Act shall be construed accordingly.
(3) The Minister may, after consultation with the Minister of Finance, remit the whole
or part of the royalty payable on petroleum obtained from an area subject to a
development licence, for such period as he or she may determine, if he or she considers it
expedient in the interests of the production of the petroleum to do so.
(4) The Minister may, on application made to him or her by the holder of a
development licence, defer or waive payment of any royalty due from the holder for such
period and subject to such conditions as he or she may determine.
(5) For the purposes of subsection (1), the value of petroleum shall be such value as
may be determined by mutual agreement between the Minister and the licensee and, in
the absence of agreement, the Minister shall determine the value.
15\. Ancillary rights
(1) Where proper and efficient work under a licence is, in the opinion of the licensee,
unduly hampered by the inability or failure of the licensee to obtain an ancillary right,
such a right may, subject to the provisions of this Act, be conferred on the licensee.
(2) In this Act, “ancillary right” means any facility, right or privilege required by a
licensee in order that petroleum may be searched for, drilled for, produced, stored,
treated, converted or carried away, and includes the right to—
(a)

enter land and to explore or geophysically examine the land for petroleum
and to sink boreholes and wells therein for the purpose of searching for and
producing petroleum, and the right to use and occupy land for the erection of
such buildings and tanks, the laying and maintenance of such pipes and the
construction of such other works as may be required for the purpose of
searching and boring for and producing, storing, treating, converting and
carrying away petroleum;

(b)

obtain a supply of water or other substance in connection with the working
of petroleum;

(c)

dispose of water or other liquid matter obtained from or on any land.

16\. Limitation of power of granting rights
(1) No ancillary right shall be granted unless it is shown that it is not reasonably
practicable to obtain the right in question by private arrangement for any of the following
reasons that the—
(a)

persons with power to grant the right are numerous or have conflicting
interests;

(b)

persons with power to grant the right, or any of them, cannot be ascertained
or cannot be found;

(c)

persons from whom the right must be obtained, or any of them, have not the
necessary powers of disposition, whether by reason of defect in title, legal
disability or otherwise;

(d)

person with power to grant the right unreasonably refuses to grant it or
demands terms which, having regard to the circumstances, are unreasonable.

(2) For the purposes of this Act, a person whose concurrence is necessary for the
exercise of an ancillary right shall be deemed to be a person having power to grant the
right, or a person from whom the right must be obtained, as the case may be.
17\. Application for ancillary rights
(1) A licensee who is desirous of obtaining an ancillary right may make application in
writing to the Minister for the grant of such a right.
(2) An application under this section shall set forth the circumstances alleged to
justify the grant of the right and shall be in such form as may be prescribed and be
accompanied by such information verified in such manner as the Minister may direct.
18\. Minister to consider application
The Minister shall consider the application and, if satisfied that in the case of the
applicant the requirements of this Act are being complied with, and that it is expedient in
the public interest that the ancillary right applied for should be granted, he or she shall
refer the matter to the High Court:
Provided that where it is alleged that the right in question cannot be obtained by
reason of a person not having the necessary powers of disposition or having unreasonably
refused to grant it or having demanded terms that are unreasonable, the Minister shall not
refer the application to the High Court without first having communicated with that
person.
19\. Persons interested to be heard
A person whose interest is or may be prejudicially affected by the grant of an ancillary
right shall be entitled to be heard by himself, herself or by an attorney before the High
Court.
20\. Grant of ancillary rights by High Court
(1) Where a reference is made to the High Court under section 18, the High Court, if
satisfied that it is expedient in the public interest that the ancillary right applied for should
be granted, shall grant the right upon such terms and subject to such conditions and for
such period as the Court thinks fit; but before granting such right the Court shall have
regard to the effect, on the amenities of the locality concerned, of the exercise of the right.
(2) The High Court may impose as a condition of the grant of an ancillary right that
any compensation to be paid in respect thereof shall be paid, or that security therefor to
the satisfaction of the High Court shall be furnished, before the right is exercised.

(3) Where the person to whom any such compensation is payable cannot be found or
ascertained the compensation shall be paid into the High Court.
21\. Compensation
(1) Where the High Court decides to grant an ancillary right in accordance with the
provisions of this Act, such compensation as, in default of agreement, may be determined
by the High Court shall be paid by the applicant in respect of the acquisition of the right
to such persons as the High Court may determine to be entitled thereto.
(2) Such compensation shall, in every case, be assessed on the basis of what would be
fair and reasonable between a willing grantor and a willing grantee having regard to the
conditions subject to which the right is or is to be granted.
22\. Effect of ancillary rights
An ancillary right granted under this Act shall not confer on the person to whom it is
granted any greater or other power than if the right had been granted by a person legally
entitled to grant it nor relieve the grantee from any obligation or liability to which he or
she would have been subject had the right been granted by such a person.
23\. Erection of survey-marks, etc.
A licensee, if called upon in writing to do so by the Minister, shall erect and maintain
at his or her own expense such survey-marks or monuments (of a form and type approved
by the Minister responsible for shipping and navigation) as the Minister first-mentioned
may consider necessary for the delimitation of any offshore area included in the licence.
24\. Illumination of installations
A licensee, if called upon in writing to do so by the Minister, shall illuminate between
the hours of sunset and sunrise—
(a)

with respect to the offshore area, in a manner satisfactory to the Minister
responsible for shipping and navigation; and

(b)

with respect to any other place in a manner satisfactory to the Minister
responsible for public works,

all derricks, piers, survey-marks and other installations erected in the offshore area or
other place included in the licence.
25\. Pollution
(1) A licensee shall adopt all necessary precautions to avoid pollution of the offshore
area and any land or water by petroleum, mud or any other fluid or substance which
might contaminate the offshore area, land or water or which might cause harm to or
destruction of marine life, and shall at his or her own expense remove all petroleum, mud
and any other fluid or substance causing such pollution.
(2) A licensee shall be absolutely liable for any loss, damage or injury caused by the
licensee or by the agents or employees of the licensee resulting from such pollution.
(3) Full details of any spill or leakage of petroleum, mud or any other fluid or
substance shall be recorded in writing by the licensee and shall forthwith be reported to
the Minister.
(4) If a licensee fails to take any step or action required of him or her under
subsection (1) or (3), the Minister shall have the right to take that step or action and to
recover from the licensee the costs or expenses reasonably and necessarily incurred
thereby.

(5) A person who contravenes the provisions of subsection (1) or (3) shall be guilty of
an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine of fifty thousand dollars and to
imprisonment for two years and, in the case of a continuing offence, to a further fine of
five thousand dollars for every day during which the offence continues.
26\. Licensee to keep Minister and Director indemnified
A licensee shall keep the Minister and the Director indemnified at all times against
any action, claim or demand of whatever nature brought against the Minister or the
Director by a third party in relation to any matter arising out of the exercise of the rights
granted by the licence.
27\. Licensee to take out insurance or enter into bond
(1) The Minister shall require every licensee to take out and maintain with an
insurance company acceptable to the Minister a policy of insurance against liability for
personal injury or damage to property to such amount as the Minister may determine, or
shall require the licensee to enter into a bond with one or more sufficient sureties for the
discharge of such liability to a like amount.
(2) Without prejudice to the provisions of subsection (1), the Minister may, from time
to time, make such arrangements as appear appropriate to him or her to secure that the
holder of a licence complies with this Act, and in particular may accept guarantees,
whether from shareholders or otherwise, in respect of that compliance.
28\. Exemption from customs duties, etc.
Notwithstanding the provisions of any other Act, no customs or excise duties, or levies
or taxes or charges of whatever nature shall be payable by the grantee of a licence or
permission, or by any contractor employed by him or her, in respect of the importation, or
the removal from bond, of plant, machinery, tools or equipment (in this section referred to
as “exempted materials”) which are peculiar to and necessary for the purposes of the
licence or permission:
Provided that—
(a)

exempted materials imported, or removed from bond, shall be used for the
purposes of the licence or permission and for no other purpose;

(b)

subject to a petroleum agreement, the holder of the licence or permission or
the contractor concerned shall at all times have and retain the beneficial
ownership of such exempted materials; and

(c)

the grantee of the licence or permission, or the contractor, shall enter into a
bond binding himself or herself in the event of any such exempted materials
being used otherwise than for the aforesaid purposes to pay to the
Accountant-General the amount of all customs or excise duties, levies, taxes
and charges payable on the value of such materials at the time such materials
ceased to be used for those purposes.

29\. Regulations
(1) The Minister may make regulations prescribing matters required or permitted to
be prescribed by this Act or necessary or convenient to be prescribed for carrying out or
giving effect to this Act.
(2) The Minister may make regulations with respect to—
(a)

exploration for petroleum, including the carrying on of operations and the
execution of works for that purpose;

(b)

production of petroleum, including the carrying on of operations and the
execution of works for that purpose;

(c)

conservation, and the prevention of waste, of petroleum and other natural
resources;

(d)

the manner in which applications for licences shall be made, the procedure
for issuing licences, and the procedure for inviting, receiving, evaluating and
accepting competitive bids;

(e)

the form and contents, and conditions with respect to the grant or renewal, of
licences;

(f)

fees, royalties and rents;

(g)

deposits to be made, and bonds to be provided, by the holders of licences as
a guarantee of due performance;

(h)

keeping records and books of account and furnishing the Minister with
reports, returns and other information;

(i)

the registration of instruments, the effect of registration and failure to
register instruments, and the assignment or transfer of licences or interests in
licences;

(j)

specified financial, technical, working and general obligations to be imposed
on the holders of licences, the manner in which such obligations are to be
performed, and the supervision and control of their performance;

(k)

the description, shape and extent of areas to be granted under licences;

(l)

imposing conditions concerning the relinquishment of areas held under
licences;

(m)

prevention and control of, and compensation for, pollution;

(n)

the division of the geographical area of Grenada into graticular units and
establishing a base map of Grenada in which each graticular unit or block
will be identified in order to impose a uniform system of geographical
identification of areas in each licence;

(o)

the construction, erection, maintenance, operation and use of installations,
pipelines, pumping stations, tank stations, valve stations, machinery, and
equipment;

(p)

controlling the flow, and preventing the escape, of petroleum, water, gases
(other than petroleum) and other noxious or deleterious matters (including
drilling fluid or a mixture of drilling fluid and water or any other matter);

(q)

conditions of use, and controlling tariffs charged for the use, of any pipeline;

(r)

the removal of structures, machinery, equipment and other property imported
in expressed or implied connection with exploration for, or the production or
conveyance of, petroleum, but not used or intended to be used in that
connection;

(s)

the maintenance of pressure in, or the repressuring of, a petroleum reservoir,
and the recycling of petroleum;

(t)

secondary or tertiary recovery of petroleum from a petroleum reservoir and
methods to be used in such recovery;

(u)

the use of wells and the use of subsurfaces for the disposal of petroleum,
water and substances produced in association with exploration for, or the
recovery of, petroleum;

(v)

methods to be used for the measurement of petroleum, water and other
substances from a well;

(w)

rates or the method of setting the rates at which petroleum and water may be
recovered from a well or petroleum reservoir;

(x)

safety and welfare standards and the health and safety of persons employed
in or in connection with exploration for, or the production or conveyance of,
petroleum;

(y)

taking logs or directional surveys or making other down-hole investigations
and taking, preserving and furnishing cores, cuttings and samples of
petroleum and water;

(z)

restrictions on the exercise of rights by the holder of a licence.

(3) Regulations made under subsection (2) may, notwithstanding the provisions of
any other Act, provide for the imposition of fines not exceeding five hundred thousand
EC dollars or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding five years for any contravention
of the regulations.
30\. Appointment and powers of Director, etc.
(1) The Minister may appoint a person to be Director for Petroleum Affairs with
responsibility for the administration of this Act.
(2) Whatever may be required or permitted by this Act to be done by the Director
may be done by a public officer specially or generally authorised in that behalf in writing
by the Director, or by a person specially or generally authorised in that behalf in writing
by the Minister, and for the purposes of doing anything that is properly so authorised that
officer or person shall be deemed to be the Director.

PETROLEUM AND NATURAL GAS DEPOSITS REGULATIONS
[SRO 14 of 2007.]

[23rd November, 2007.]

## PART I

Preliminary

---

### 1.

Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Petroleum and Natural Gas Deposits
Regulations.

### 2.

Definitions
In these Regulations—
“Act” means the Petroleum and Natural Gas Deposits Act, Chapter 240, and
includes these Regulations;
“block” means a block constituted as provided in regulation 4, and the term
“blocks” shall have a corresponding meaning;
“companies” means a corporate body incorporated under the Companies Act, 1994,
or a corporate body incorporated outside Grenada;
“development area” means an area subject to a development licence;
“discovery block” in relation to an exploration area, means a block in the
exploration area comprising the geological feature, as outlined by the relevant
geological or geophysical date, in which there is a possibility bared in research that a
discovery of petroleum may be found;
“exploration area” means an area subject to an exploration licence;
“good oilfield practices” means all those things that are generally accepted
internationally as good, safe and efficient in the carrying on of exploration of
petroleum or development operations in the oil field;
“petroleum reservoir” means a naturally occurring discreet accumulation of
petroleum in any form whatsoever.

(2) A reference in these Regulations to Grenada, includes a reference to the
continental shelf and the exclusive economic zone.

### 3.

Application to continental shelf and Exclusive Economic Zone
These Regulations shall apply in relation to the continental shelf and the exclusive
economic zone.

## PART II

Constitution of Blocks

### 4.

Reference map showing blocks
(1) The Minister shall cause to be prepared a reference map of Grenada showing the
geographical areas where petroleum and natural gas may be found.
(2) Subject to subregulation (3) the reference map to be prepared under
subregulation (1) shall be divided into blocks by the—
(a)

meridian of Greenwich and by meridians that are at a distance from that
meridian of two minutes of longitude; and

(b)

equator and by parallels of latitude that are at a distance from the equator of
two minutes of latitude,

each of which is bounded by—
(c)

portions of two of those meridians that are at a distance from each other of
two minutes of longitude; and

(d)

portions of two of those parallels of latitude that are at a distance from each
other of two minutes of latitude.

(3) Where any block as so constituted would be partly outside the geographical area
of Grenada, only that part of the block that is within the geographical area as described
herein shall be constituted as part of the geographical area of Grenada.
(4) The blocks referred to in subregulation (1) shall be constituted by reference to the
latitudes and longitudes based on the World Geodetic System, 1984, or such other
geodetic system as may be specified by the Minister by notice published in the Gazette.
(5) Each block on the reference map prepared pursuant to subregulation (1) shall be
given either a number or a letter, or both, for the purpose of identification.

### 5.

Reference map to be deposited
(1) The reference map prepared pursuant to regulation 4 shall be available for
inspection at a place to be specified by the Minister by notice published in the Gazette.
(2) A reference map certified by the Minister is admissible in any proceedings and in
the absence of evidence to the contrary, is proof of the truth and accuracy of its contents.

### 6.

Reservation of blocks
(1) The Minister may, by notice published in the Gazette, declare that a block
identified in the notice (not being a block or blocks in respect of which a licence is in
force) shall not be subject of a licence.
(2) While a declaration under subregulation (1) remains in force in respect of a block
or blocks, a licence shall not be granted in respect of that block or those blocks.

(3) The Minister may, by notice published in the Gazette, vary or revoke any notice
published under this Regulation.

## PART III

Exploration Licence

### 7.

Application for exploration licence
(1) A person may make an application to the Minister for the grant of an exploration
licence in respect of a block.
(2) An application for the grant of an exploration licence shall be—
(a)

made in the form approved for in the First Schedule;

(b)

accompanied by the fee provided for in the Second Schedule.

(3) An application for the grant of an exploration licence—
(a)

shall provide in respect of the person or, if there are more than one person in
respect, of each person making the application, the name and address of the
person and where that person is a company, the names and nationality of the
directors of the company and, if the company has a share capital, the name
of any person who is the beneficial owner of more than five per cent of the
issued share capital;

(b)

shall, be in respect of the number of blocks to be approved by the Minister;

(c)

shall be accompanied by—
(i) the proposals of the applicant for work and minimum expenditure in
respect of each block specified in the application, and in particular,
details of the work to be carried out at different stages should the
licence be granted,
(ii) particulars of the technical and industrial qualifications of the
applicant and his or her employees,
(iii) particulars of the technical and industrial resources available to the
applicant,
(iv) particulars of the kinds of financial resources available to the
applicant, including capital, credit facilities and guarantees so
available,
(v) proposals with respect to the training and employment of citizens of
Grenada; and

(d)

may set out any other matters that the applicant wishes the Minister to
consider.

(4) Where an application relates to more than one block, the block identified in the
application for the grant of an exploration licence shall be—
(a)

so situated as to form a single area; and

(b)

such that each block in the area has a single side in common with at least one
other block in the area.

(5) On receipt of the application the Minister shall review the application in order to
determine whether or not a licence should be granted.
(6) In reviewing an application the Minister may—

(a)

cause such investigations, negotiations or consultations to be carried out as
he or she considers necessary;

(b)

by notice in writing require the applicant to furnish to the Minister, within
the time stipulated in the notice, any further information relevant to the
application which the Minister may require.

(7) In addition to any further information which the Minister may request in any
effort to assist him or her in granting a licence, the Minister may request that the applicant
commissions at his or her own expense an independent study by a consultant which the
Minister shall approve for the purpose of—
(a)

assessing the feasibility of the programme of development operations
proposed in his or her application; or

(b)

assessing or making recommendations about such other matters arising out
of or connected with, the application.

### 8.

Grant of application for exploration licence
(1) Subject to these Regulations, the Minister may by notice in writing—
(a)

grant an exploration licence on such terms and conditions as he or she may
determine; or

(b)

refuse to grant an exploration licence.

(2) Where the Minister refuses to grant a licence, the Minister shall within fourteen
days of his or her decision, issue a notice in writing to that effect.
(3) Where the Minister grants an exploration licence subject to such terms and
conditions pursuant to subregulation (1)(a), the Minister must notify the applicant in
writing within fourteen days of his or her decision.
(4) A notice under subregulation (3) must inform the applicant of the terms and
conditions under which the licence will be granted and must give the applicant a period of
sixty days in which the applicant must respond stating whether or not he or she accepts
the licence subject to the terms and conditions stated therein.

### 9.

Restriction on grant of exploration licence
An exploration licence shall not be granted in respect of a block that is, at the time the
application for the licence is made—
(a)

comprised in a licence; or

(b)

reserved by declaration under regulation 6.

### 10.

Contents of exploration licence
(1) An exploration licence shall—
(a)

state the date of the grant of the licence;

(b)

identify the exploration area; and

(c)

state the terms and conditions on which the licence is granted.

(2) The Minister shall include as a term or condition in an exploration licence a
provision whereby the Minister receives the right to acquire an interest in any venture to
explore for or recover petroleum which may be carried on in any block in the exploration
area.

### 11.

Rights conferred by exploration licence

Subject to these Regulations and to the conditions specified in the licence, an
exploration licence confers on the holder of that licence the exclusive right while it
remains in force to explore in the exploration area for petroleum, and to carry on such
operations and execute such works as are necessary for that purpose.

### 12.

Term of exploration licence
Subject to this Part and any conditions specified in the licence or to which the licence
is otherwise subject, an exploration licence remains in force—
(a)

for an initial period not exceeding four years commencing on and including
the date on which the licence is granted, as stated in the licence; and

(b)

where the licence is renewed under regulation 15 for a further period not
exceeding two years in respect of a first renewal, and a further period not
exceeding two years in respect of a second renewal.

### 13.

Renewal of exploration licence
(1) Subject to this Regulation and regulation 14, the holder of an exploration licence
may apply to the Minister in the form provided in the First Schedule for a renewal of the
licence in respect of any discovery block in the exploration area for which the exploration
licence was granted in the first instance.
(2) An application to the Minister for the renewal of an exploration licence—
(a)

shall, subject to subregulation (2), not be made later than ninety days before
the day on which the licence intended is due to expire;

(b)

shall be accompanied by—
(i) particulars of the work carried out in, and the amount expended in
respect of, the exploration area during the term of the exploration
licence up to and including the date of the application or where the
application is for a second renewal of the licence, during the period of
the first renewal of the licence up to and including the date of the
application,
(ii) adequate proposals of the applicant for work and minimum
expenditure in respect of the block identified in the application during
the renewal period being applied for, and
(iii) the fees provided for in the Second Schedule;

(c)

may only be made twice after the initial licence was granted; and

(d)

may set out any other matters that the applicant wishes the Minister to
consider.

### 14.

Application for renewal to be in respect of reduced area
(1) The number of blocks in respect of which an application for the renewal of an
exploration licence may be made shall not exceed the number which is one half in total of
the number of blocks in respect of which the exploration licence was first issued or last
renewed as the case may be.
(2) In determining, for the purpose of this Regulation, the number of blocks in respect
of which an exploration licence was first issued or last renewed, any discovery block
subsisting in the exploration area at the date on which the application for the renewal is
made shall not be taken into account.
(3) The block specified in an application for the renewal of an exploration licence
shall be the block that relates to particular sections that—

(a)

constitute a single area or not more than three discrete areas;

(b)

are such that each particular section in each area has a side in common with
at least another particular section in that area.

### 15.

Grant or refusal of renewal of exploration licence
(1) Subject to subregulation (2), on application duly under regulation 13 for the
renewal of an exploration licence, the Minister may grant a renewal of the licence.
(2) The Minister may refuse to grant the renewal of an exploration licence if the
holder of the licence is in default, or if the proposals with respect to work and expenditure
during the renewal period accompanying the application pursuant to regulation 13 are not
adequate.
(3) The Minister shall not refuse to grant the renewal of an exploration licence—
(a)

unless he or she has given to the applicant written notice of his or her
intention to do so—
(i) giving in the notice particulars of the intended refusal, and
(ii) subject to subregulation (4) where the applicant is in default, by
stating a date before which the applicant may take remedial action or
make representation to remedy the default.

(4) The Minister shall not refuse to grant the renewal of an exploration licence where
the applicant has not before the date of the application for renewal been given notice of
the default and reasonable time to remedy the default.
(5) The Minister shall give notice of his or her decision for the renewal of an
exploration licence in writing to an applicant and if the Minister is prepared to grant the
renewal, he or she shall give particulars of any variation of the conditions of the
exploration licence which are required to give effect for the renewal of such licence.
(6) An applicant shall, within sixty days after he or she is given notice pursuant to
subregulation (5), give written notice to the Minister of his or her willingness to accept
the renewal of the exploration licence subject to the variation of the conditions as
proposed by the Minister.
(7) Where the applicant fails to give written notice within the time stated under
subregulation (6) the application shall lapse.
(8) Where—
(a)

an application for the renewal of an exploration licence has been duly made;
and

(b)

the exploration licence granted to the applicant expires—
(i) before the Minister grants, or refuses to grant, the renewal of the
exploration licence, or
(ii) before the application lapses under subregulation (6),

the first exploration licence granted to the applicant shall continue in force until the
Minister grants or refuses to grant the renewal of the exploration licence save and except
where the licence is cancelled or the application for renewal is withdrawn.
(9) Where an exploration licence would otherwise cease to be in force in respect of
any block, in an exploration area wherein petroleum has been discovered, then, by virtue
of this subregulation, the licence shall, unless sooner determined by surrender or
cancellation under the Act, continue in force in respect of that discovery block until a
notice is served pursuant to regulation 19(1), or until the expiry of the period of thirty
days referred to in that regulation, whichever first occurs.

(10) Where a notice given under regulation 20(1) states that a discovery of petroleum
in any block or blocks in an exploration area is, in the opinion of the licensee, of potential
commercial interest and the exploration licence relating to the discovery block or blocks
would otherwise cease to be in force then, by virtue of this subsection, the licence shall,
unless sooner determined by surrender or cancellation under the Act continue in force in
respect of the discovery block or blocks, until the expiry of—
(a)

the period of one year specified in regulation 20(2); and

(b)

any further period allowed by the Minister under the proviso to
regulation 20(2).

### 16.

Duties of holder of exploration licence
(1) Subject to subregulations (3) and (4), the holder of an exploration licence shall in,
or in relation to the exploration area for which the licence is granted, meet the
requirements, with respect to work and expenditure—
(a)

stipulated in the licence; and

(b)

of each programme submitted pursuant to subregulation (2).

(2) The holder of an exploration licence shall, not later than one month before the
anniversary in each year of the date of the licence was granted—
(a)

submit to the Minister in detail a report of work and expenditure carried out
or made in the year of the term of the licence;

(b)

submit to the Minister in detail an adequate programme with respect to work
and expenditure to be carried out or made for the ensuing year.

(3) The Minister may on application made in writing to him or her by the holder of an
exploration licence, amend or suspend any obligation arising under subregulation (1),
either conditionally or unconditionally.
(4) Subject to paragraph (b), the holder of an exploration licence may amend the
details of any programme of work and expenditure which he or she is required under
subregulation (1)(b) to carry out or make:
Provided that—
(a)

the holder of the licence shall forthwith give notice in writing to the Minister
of any such amendment, and shall provide in the notice, details of and the
reason for the amendment; and

(b)

no such amendment shall have effect so as to reduce the minimum
requirements of the overall programme of work and expenditure to be
carried out or made during the term of the exploration licence.

(5) Where the holder of an exploration licence fails to carry out any part of the
requirement with respect to work and expenditure stipulated in the exploration licence, or
each programme submitted by him or her pursuant to subregulation (1) then, without
prejudice to any other right which may be invoked by the Minister in respect of that
failure, the relevant provision of the licence, if any, shall apply for the purpose of
determining the liquidated damages payable to the State by the holder of the exploration
licence on account of such failure.

### 17.

Relinquishment of exploration area
(1) Any block relinquished under regulation 34 shall, unless the Minister otherwise
directs, be such as to ensure that the remaining exploration area constitutes a continuous
area.

(2) Where under regulation 34 any block is relinquished, the exploration licence
granted in respect of the holder ceases to have effect from the date of the relinquishment
of the block.

### 18.

Exclusion of block from exploration area
Where a development licence is granted in respect of any block in an exploration area
to the holder of an exploration licence the block ceases to be part of the exploration area.

### 19.

Discovery of petroleum to be notified to Minister
(1) Where a discovery of petroleum is made in an exploration area, the holder of the
exploration licence in relation to that area—
(a)

shall—
(i) forthwith inform the Minister in writing of the discovery,
(ii) within a period of thirty days from the date of the discovery, furnish to
the Minister particulars in writing of the discovery, and
(iii) promptly run tests in respect of the discovery and thereafter forthwith
submit to the Minister evaluated test results in respect of the
discovery; and

(b)

shall promptly take all steps that are reasonable, in the circumstances
relating to the discovery, to ascertain the quantity of the petroleum reservoir
to which the discovery relates.

(2) Where petroleum has been discovered in an exploration area, the Minister may,
from time to time, by notice in writing served on the holder of the exploration licence
direct him or her to furnish the Minister, within the period specified in the notice,
particulars in writing of—
(a)

the chemical composition and physical properties of the petroleum;

(b)

the stratigraphical position and depth of the discovery;

(c)

any other matters relating to the discovery that are specified by the Minister
in the notice.

(3) The Minister may, by instrument in writing, exempt the holder of an exploration
licence wholly or partly from the requirements of subregulation (1)(b), subject to such
conditions as the Minister may specify in the instrument of exemption.

### 20.

Discovery of petroleum of potential commercial interest
(1) Where petroleum has been discovered in an exploration area and the holder of the
exploration licence has, pursuant to regulation 19(1)(a)(iii), submitted to the Minister
evaluated test results in respect of the discovery, he or she shall, within thirty days of the
date of submission of the evaluated results, serve on the Minister a notice stating in his or
her opinion whether or not the discovery of petroleum is of potential commercial interest.
(2) Where pursuant to subregulation (1), a notice served on the Minister states that
there is a discovery of petroleum in an exploration area and, in the opinion of the holder
of the exploration licence, the discovery may be of potential commercial interest, the
holder of the exploration licence may, unless such licence is sooner determined by
surrender or cancellation under the Act, within a period of one year after the date on
which such notice is so served, apply under regulation 23 for the grant of a development
licence in respect of the discovery block in the exploration area:
Provided that the Minister may, on application by the holder of the exploration
license in writing, allow the holder of the exploration licence such further time, which in

his or her opinion is reasonable in the circumstances of the case, to apply for the grant of
a development licence.
(3) If the holder of an exploration license fails within the period specified in
subregulation (2), or within such further period as the Minister may allow under the
proviso thereto, to apply for a development licence in respect of any discovery block in
the exploration area, the Minister may, if the exploration licence is then in force in respect
of the discovery block, by notice served on the holder of the exploration licence, direct
that the licence shall cease to have effect in respect of the discovery block with effect
from the date specified in the notice.

### 21.

Discovery of petroleum not of potential commercial interest
(1) Where a notice served under regulation 20 states that the discovery of petroleum
in any discovery block in an exploration area is not, in the opinion of the holder of the
exploration licence of potential commercial interest—
(a)

subject to subregulation (2) the Minister may, if the exploration licence is
then in force for the discovery block within a period of twelve months from
the date on which the notice under subregulation (1) is served on him or her,
direct by notice in writing served on the holder of the exploration licence
that the licence shall cease to have effect with respect to such discovery
block, and thereupon the licence shall cease to have effect in respect of the
discovery block; and

(b)

during the aforesaid period of twelve months, notwithstanding that during
the period the Minister has not pursuant to paragraph (a) directed that the
licence shall cease to have effect in respect of the aforesaid discovery block,
and during any period thereafter, regulation 19(1)(b) and (2) shall not apply
in respect of the discovery, and any direction given under subregulation (2)
before the commencement of the aforesaid period of twelve months in
respect of the discovery and not carried out before such commencement shall
cease to have effect.

(2) The Minister shall not give a direction under subregulation (1)(a) unless—
(a)

he or she has given to the holder of the exploration licence a notice of his or
her intention to do so;

(b)

he or she has specified in that notice a reasonable time within which the
holder of the exploration licence may make representations with respect to
that notice; and

(c)

on evidence available to him or her and after taking into account any
representation made by the licensee, he or she is of the opinion that the
discovery is of potential commercial interest.

### 22.

Investigations
(1) Where the holder of an exploration licence has served a notice under regulation 20
on the Minister stating that a discovery of petroleum is, in his or her opinion of potential
commercial interest, the Minister may, by written notice served on the holder of the
exploration licence direct him or her to carry out, within a period specified in the notice
of not less than one year, such investigations and studies for the purpose of assessing the
feasibility of the construction, establishment and operation of an industry for the
production of petroleum in the discovery block concerned.
(2) The investigations and duties referred to in subregulation (1) may include, where
appropriate—
(a)

technical and economic feasibility studies relating to the recovery,
processing and transport of petroleum from the discovery block;

(b)

studies of proposed sites for facilities that would be required by the industry
referred to in subregulation (1);

(c)

studies of port or berthing facilities, and roads, pipelines or other
transportation facilities;

(d)

investigations into—
(i) suitable water facilities and reticulation systems for industrial and
town purposes,
(ii) the location and design of a suitable airstrip and associated landing
and terminal facilities, if so required for that industry, and
(iii) the generation and transmission of electricity if so required for that
industry;

(e)

investigations into the development, if so required, of a suitable town for the
industry referred to in subregulation (1), including the design of housing
facilities and associated civic, cultural and social facilities;

(f)

investigation of any other works, services or facilities that may be required
for that industry in relation to the discovery block or blocks;

(g)

studies of future labour requirements for that industry; and

(h)

physical impact studies into the possible effects of that industry on the
environment.

(3) The holder of the exploration licence shall furnish to the Minister within the
period specified in the notice under subregulation (1), such reports, analysis and data
resulting from the investigations and studies carried out under the regulation as the
Minister, by notice served on the licensee holder of the licence, may require.

## PART IV

Development Licence

### 23.

Application for development licence
(1) The holder of a valid exploration licence may, within the period of one year
specified in regulation 20(2) or within such further period as may be allowed by the
Minister under the proviso thereto, make an application to the Minister in the form
provided for in the First Schedule for the grant of a development licence in respect of any
discovery block in the exploration area, which following appraisal, can be shown to
contain a petroleum reservoir or part of a petroleum reservoir.
(2) Subject to subregulation (3) and regulation 14(3), a person who is not the holder
of an exploration licence may make an application to the Minister in the form provided
for in the First Schedule for the grant of a development licence.
(3) An application under subregulation (2) must be accompanied by data supplied by
the applicant which shows that there is a petroleum reservoir located in a block of
potential commercial interest.

### 24.

Content of application for development licence
(1) An application for the grant of a development licence—
(a)

shall be accompanied by the fee provided for in the Second Schedule, by the
Minister;

(b)

shall identify the block or blocks in respect of which the application is made;

(c)

shall be accompanied by detailed proposals by the applicant—
(i) for the construction, establishment and operation of all facilities and
services for and incidental to the recovery, processing, storage and
transportation of petroleum from the proposed development area, and
(ii) with respect to the training and employment of citizens of Grenada;

(d)

may set out our other matter that the applicant wishes the Minister to
consider; and

(e)

may be withdrawn by the applicant by a written notice given to the Minister;

(f)

shall be accompanied by evidence of the financial capability of the applicant.

(2) In addition to any further information which the Minister may request in any
effort to assist him or her in granting a licence, the Minister may request that the applicant
commissions at his or her own expense an independent study by a consultant which the
Minister shall approve.

### 25.

Grant of development licence
(1) Subject to these Regulations, the Minister may by notice in writing to the
applicant—
(a)

grant a development licence on such terms and conditions as he or she may
determine;

(b)

subject to regulation 26, refuse to grant a development licence.

(2) An applicant shall within sixty days after he or she is given notice pursuant to
subregulation (1) that the Minister is prepared to grant a development licence subject to
such terms and conditions stipulated in the licence issue a notice in writing to the Minister
of his or her willingness to accept the proposed licence.
(3) Where the applicant fails to give notice within the time stipulated under
subregulation (2), the application shall lapse.

### 26.

Restrictions on grant of development licence
(1) The Minister shall not grant a development licence to an applicant unless the—
(i) proposals of the application would ensure the most efficient, beneficial and
timely use of the petroleum resources concerned,
(ii) applicant has adequate financial resources and technical and industrial
competence and experience to carry on effective development operations,
(iii) applicant is able and willing to comply with the conditions on which the
licence is proposed to be granted,
(iv) applicant’s proposals for the employment and training of citizens of Grenada
are satisfactory,
(v) applicant proposals with respect to the procurement of goods and services
obtained within Grenada are satisfactory,
(vi) exercise of any option given to the State under regulation 10(2) has been
completed or arrangements satisfactory to the Minister have been made for
that purpose.
(2) Where the Minister refuses to grant a development licence, the Minister shall
specify in the notice the particulars of the ground of the intended refusal, and provide the
applicant with a reasonable date by which the applicant may make representations in
respect of the particulars so stated.

(3) Where a notice under subregulation (2) states that the applicant is in default or the
proposals made by the applicant require amendment, the applicant may within the time
specified in the notice remedy the default or amend the proposals.
(4) Where the applicant has not, within the date so specified in the notice—
(i) made any representations or otherwise dealt satisfactorily with the matters
referred to in the notice, or
(ii) in the case referred to in subregulation (3) remedied the default or suitably
amended the proposals,
the Minister shall refuse to grant the development licence.
(5) A Development licence shall not be granted to an applicant under regulation 23(2)
in respect of a block which is, at the time of the making of an application already subject
to a licence granted to a person other than the applicant.

### 27.

Contents of development licence
(1) A development licence—
(a)

shall—
(i) state the date on which the licence is granted,
(ii) identify the block to which the licence relates, and
(iii) state the conditions subject to which the licence is granted; and

(b)

may contain such other matters as the Minister may determine for the
purposes of subregulation (2) or (3) or for any other purpose of the Act.

(2) There may be included in a development licence a provision with respect to the
duty of the holder of the development licence to supply petroleum or petroleum products,
to the extent specified in the licence, to meet the requirements of Grenada.
(3) There may be included in a development licence, conditions with respect to the
refining, disposal or sale of petroleum which may be recovered in the development area.

### 28.

Rights conferred by development licence
(1) Subject to the Act and to conditions specified in a development licence, the holder
of a development licence, while it remains in force, has exclusive rights to—
(a)

carry on exploration and development operations in the development area;

(b)

sell with the approval of the Minister or otherwise dispose of petroleum
recovered; and

(c)

carry on such operations and execute such works in the development area as
are necessary for or in connection with, any matter referred to in paragraph
(a) or (b).

(2) The Minister shall refuse to grant a renewal of a development licence if the holder
of the development licence is in default unless in the opinion of the Minister special
circumstances exist which justify the granting of the renewal notwithstanding the default.
(3) A development licence may only be renewed once and the period for which a
development licence may be renewed shall not exceed ten years.
(4) The provisions of regulations 13 and 15 apply, mutatis mutandis, in relation to the
procedure for renewal of a development licence.

### 29.

Terms of development licence

(1) A development licence, unless sooner determined by surrender or cancellation
under the Act, shall by virtue of this Regulation, continue in force for—
(a)

a period of twenty years next after the date of the grant of the licence; and

(b)

any period for which the licence is renewed under regulation 28.

(2) Notwithstanding the expiry of the term of a development licence, the licence shall,
unless sooner determined by surrender or cancellation under the Act, continue in force by
virtue of this subregulation, in respect of any block to which the licence relates if an
application has been duly made, for the grant of a renewal of the licence in respect of that
block until the—
(a)

application is finally dealt with by the grant of, or refusal to grant, the
renewal; or

(b)

application lapses.

### 30.

Unit development
(1) In this Regulation, “unit development”, in relation to the petroleum reservoir,
means the co-ordination of operations for the recovery of petroleum being carried on or to
be carried on in a development area in which there is part of the reservoir, with other
operations for the recovery of petroleum being carried on or to be carried on in any other
area in which there is part of the same reservoir.
(2) The holder of a development licence may, from time to time, enter into an
agreement in writing for or in relation to the unit development of a petroleum reservoir.
(3) The Minister may, on his or her own motion on or application made to him or her
in writing by a holder of a development licence in whose development area there is a part
of a particular petroleum reservoir, for the purpose of securing the more effective
recovery of petroleum from that petroleum reservoir, direct any such holder whose
development area includes part of that petroleum reservoir, to enter into an agreement in
writing with the person or persons within the period specified by the Minister for or in
relation to the unit development of the petroleum reservoir, and to submit the agreement
with the Minister forthwith for approval.
(4) Where the holder of a development licence—
(a)

is directed under subregulation (3) to enter into an agreement for or in
relation to the unit development or a petroleum reservoir and fails to do so
within the specified period; or

(b)

enters into such an agreement and the agreement is not approved with the
Minister in accordance with subregulation (3), the Minister may, by notice
served on the holder, direct him or her to submit to the Minister, within the
period specified in the notice, a scheme for or in relation to the unit
development of the petroleum reservoir, and the holder shall comply
forthwith.

### 31.

Directions as to recovery of petroleum
(1) Where petroleum is not being recovered in a development area and the Minister is
satisfied that there is recoverable petroleum in that area, he or she may by instrument in
writing served on the holder of the development licence, direct the holder to take all
necessary and practicable steps to recover that petroleum.
(2) Where the Minister is not satisfied with the steps taken or being taken by the
holder of the development licence to whom a direction has been given under
subregulation (1), the Minister may, by instrument in writing served on the holder, issue
such directions as specified in the instrument, as the Minister thinks necessary for or in
relation to the recovery of petroleum in the development area.

(3) Where petroleum is being recovered in a development area, the Minister may by
instructions in writing served on the holder of the development licence, direct the holder
to take all necessary and practicable steps to increase the rate at which the petroleum is
being recovered to such rate, not exceeding good oil field practices, as the Minister
specifies in the instrument.
(4) Where the Minister is not satisfied with the steps taken or being taken by the
holder of a development licence to whom a direction has been given under
subregulation (3), the Minister may, by instrument in writing serve on the holder, issue
such directions, specified in the instrument, as the Minister thinks necessary for or in
relation to the increase of the rate at which petroleum is being recovered in the
development area.
(5) Nothing in this Regulation or in any direction given under this Regulation shall be
construed as requiring the holder of a development licence to do anything which is not in
accordance with good oilfield practices or to refrain from doing anything which is in
accordance with good oilfield practices.

## PART V

General

### 32.

Prohibition against disclosure of information
(1) For the purpose of this part, “licence” means an exploration licence and a
development licence.
(2) Subject to subregulation (1), no information furnished, or information in a report
submitted pursuant to the Act by the holder of a licence shall be disclosed to any person
who is not a Minister or a public officer who requires the information to carry out his or
her functions, except with the consent of the holder of the licence.
(3) Nothing in subregulation (1) shall be deemed to prevent the disclosure of a
licence, where the disclosure is made—
(a)

after the licence has ceased to have effect over the land to which the licence
relates;

(b)

for, or in connection with, the administration of the Act;

(c)

for the purpose of, or in connection with, any legal proceedings;

(d)

to any consultant to the Government for the purpose of facilitating the
performance by the consultant or any functions under the consultancy
arrangement;

(e)

for, or in connection with, the preparation by or on behalf of the State of
statistics in respect of exploration or development operations relating to
petroleum;

(f)

for, or in connection with, the determination of any liability of the licensee to
make any payments to the State or the Government; or

(g)

for, or in connection with, any matter or purpose specified in a petroleum
agreement.

(4) Where the holder of a licence is a party to a petroleum agreement the right of the
holder of the licence, his or her servants or agents to disclose information about
exploration or development operations under the licence shall be subject to any
restrictions or limitations in that respect specified in the agreement.

(5) Any person who discloses information in contravention of this Regulation
commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to the penalty provided for in
section 29(3) of the Act.

### 33.

Information, reports and accounts, etc.
(1) The holder of a licence shall during the term of the licence and on expiry or
termination of the licence furnish such information, submit such reports and keep, such
records and accounts as may from time to time be required by the Minister or as may be
specified in the licence.
(2) The holder of a licence shall—
(a)

establish immediately after the grant of the licence, and keep established
while the licence subsists, an address in Grenada;

(b)

give to the Minister notice of that address and of any variation of the address
which may be made from time to time; and

(c)

during the term of licence have a manager who is resident in Grenada.

### 34.

Surrender
(1) The holder of a licence may at any time when the licence is in force, by giving to
the Minister not less than three months previous written notice in the case of an
exploration licence and, not less than twelve months previous written notice in the case of
a development licence, relinquish any block in the area covered by the licence identified
in the notice.
(2) A notice under subregulation (1) shall—
(a)

state the date on which the licensee wishes the relinquishment to have effect;

(b)

where only some of the blocks subject to the licence are to be relinquished,
identify blocks to be relinquished;

(c)

give particulars of exploration or development operations carried on since
the licence was granted or last renewed, whichever is the latter, in respect of
the blocks to be relinquished; and

(d)

be supported by such records and reports in relation to those operations as
the Minister may reasonably require.

(3) Where, pursuant to subregulation (1), a notice of relinquishment is submitted by
the licensee, the Minister—
(a)

shall, if not all of the blocks subject to an exploration licence or a
development licence are relinquished, amend the licence accordingly; or

(b)

shall, in any other case, cancel the licence, and in either case the Minister
shall give notice to the licensee of the amendment or, as the case may be, the
cancellation.

(4) The relinquishment of any block does not affect any liability incurred by the
holder of the licence before the date on which the relinquishment takes effect.

### 35.

Work practices for holder of licence
(1) The holder of a licence shall—
(a)

carry out all exploration and development operations in the exploration or
development area in a proper, safe and workmanlike manner and in
accordance with good international accepted oilfield practices;

(b)

take all reasonable steps necessary to secure the safety, health and welfare of
persons engaged in those operations; and

(c)

act in accordance with these Regulations, and with any directions given
under the Act and with any direction given, restriction imposed or
requirement made by the Director under these Regulations.

(2) In particular, and without limiting the generality of subregulation (1), the holder of
a licence shall—
(a)

control the flow and prevent the waste or escape in the exploration or
development area of petroleum, gas (not being petroleum), or water;

(b)

prevent the escape in the exploration or development area of any mixture or
water or drilling fluid and petroleum or any other matter;

(c)

prevent damage to petroleum bearing strata in any area within or outside the
area subject to the licence;

(d)

keep separate in the manner as the Minister by notice in writing served on
the licence directs—
(i) each petroleum reservoir discovered in the exploration or development
area, and
(ii) such of the sources of water, if any, discovered in the exploration of
development area;

(e)

prevent water or any other matter entering any petroleum reservoir through
the wells in the exploration or development area except when required by,
and in accordance with, good oilfield practices;

(f)

prevent the pollution of any aquifer water-well, spring, stream, river, lake,
reservoir, estuary, beach, bay, harbour or area or sea by the escape of
petroleum, salt water, drilling fluid, chemical additive, gas (not being
petroleum), or any other waste product or effluent; and

(g)

furnish to the Minister, prior to the drilling of any well, a detailed report on
the technique to be employed, an estimate of the time to be taken, the
material to be used and the safety measures to be employed, in the drilling of
the well.

(3) Where the consent in writing of the Minister has been obtained, nothing in this
Regulation shall prevent a licensee from flaring natural gas in accordance with the term
and instrument of consent.
(4) Nothing in this Regulation shall prevent the holder of a licence from flaring
natural gas where, in an emergency, flaring is required to safeguard the health and safety
of persons in the exploration or development area or to prevent damage to the property of
any person in the exploration or development area.
(5) The holder of a licence shall furnish to the Minister reasonable notice of his or her
intention to abandon any well, and the closure or plugging of any well shall be carried out
only with the prior consent in writing of the Minister and in the manner approved by the
Minister.
(6) The holder of a licence who contravenes or fails or neglects to comply with a
requirement of this Regulation commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction
to the penalty provided for in section 29(3) of the Act.
(7) In proceedings on a prosecution of an offence under this Regulation, it shall be a
sufficient defence if the accused person proves that he or she promptly took all reasonable
steps to comply with the requirements of this Regulation.

### 36.

Maintenance, etc., of property

(1) The holder of a licence shall—
(a)

maintain in good condition and repair all structures, equipment and other
property in the area subject to the licence and used in connection with the
operations in which he or she is engaged;

(b)

remove from that area all structures, equipment and other property that are
not either used or to be used in connection with the operation in which he or
she is engaged; and

(c)

take reasonable steps to warn persons who may, from time to time, be in the
vicinity of any such structure, equipment or other property of the presence of
the structure, equipment or other property and the possible hazards resulting
therefrom.

(2) Subject to the Act, subregulation (1) shall not apply in relation to any structure,
equipment or other property that was not brought into the area subject to a licence by or
with the authority of the licensee.
(3) A licensee who contravenes subregulation (1)(a), (b) or (c) commits an offence
and is liable on conviction to the penalty stipulated under section 29(3) of the Act.

### 37.

Drilling near boundaries
(1) The holder of a licence shall not drill a well or any part thereof which is less than
one thousand meters from a boundary of the area subject to the licence except with the
consent in writing of the Minister and in accordance with such conditions, if any, as are
specified in the instrument of consent.
(2) Where a licensee does not comply with subregulation (1), the Minister may, by
notice in writing served on the holder, directing him or her to do one or more of the
following within the period specified in the notice, namely to—
(a)

plug the well;

(b)

close off the well;

(c)

comply with such directions relating to the drilling or maintenance of the
well as are specified in the instrument.

(3) A person who fails or neglects to comply with a direction given under
subregulation (2) commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction is liable to the
penalty stipulated in section 29(3) of the Act.

### 38.

Removal of property by licensee, etc.
(1) Where a licence has been cancelled or has expired, or has ceased to comprise any
relinquishment area the Minister may, by notice in writing served on the person who is or
was the holder of the licence direct that person, within the period specified in the
instrument to—
(a)

remove or cause to be removed from the relinquished area, concerned, all
property brought into that area by any person engaged or concerned in the
operations authorised by the holder of the licence, or to make arrangements
that are satisfactory to the Minister with respect to that property;

(b)

plug or close off, to the satisfaction of the Minister, all wells made in that
area by any person engaged or concerned in those operations; and

(c)

make provision, to the satisfaction of the Minister, for the conservation and
protection of the natural resources in that area.

(2) Nothing in this Regulation or in any direction given under this Regulation by the
Minister, shall be construed as requiring any person who is or who was the holder of a

licence to do anything which is not in accordance with good oilfield practices or to refrain
from doing anything which is in accordance with good oilfield practices.
(3) A person to whom a direction under subregulation (1) is given who refuses or fails
to comply with the direction within the period specified in the instrument by which the
direction was given shall be guilty of an offence and is liable on summary conviction to
the penalty stipulated in section 29(3) of the Act.

### 39.

Removal and sale of property by Minister, etc.
(1) Subject to regulation 37(2), where a direction under regulation 41 has not been
complied with, the Minister may—
(a)

do or cause to be done all or any of the things required by the direction to be
done;

(b)

remove or cause to be removed in such manner as he or she thinks fit, all or
any of the property from the relinquishment area concerned;

(c)

dispose of, in such manner as he or she thinks fit, all or any of the property
referred to in paragraph (b); and

(d)

if he or she has served a copy of the instrument by which the direction was
given on a person whom he or she believe to be an owner of the property or
part of the property, sell or cause to be sold by public auction or otherwise as
he or she thinks fit, all or any of the property referred to in paragraph (b) that
belongs, or that he or she believes to belong, to a particular person.

(2) The Minister may deduct from the proceeds of a sale of property under
subregulation (1) that belongs, or that he or she believes to belong, to a particular
person—
(a)

all or any part of any costs and expenses incurred by him or her under that
subregulation in relation to that property;

(b)

all or any part of any costs and expenses incurred by him or her in relation to
the doing of anything required by a direction under regulation 41 to be done
by the person; and

(c)

all or any part of any fees or amounts due and payable under the Act, these
Regulations or a licence by the person.

(3) The costs and expenses incurred by the Minister under subregulation (1)—
(a)

if incurred in relation to the removal, disposal or sale of property, shall be a
debt due by the owner of the property to Grenada; or

(b)

if incurred in relation to the doing of anything required by a direction under
regulation 41 to be done by a person who is or was a licensee, shall be a debt
due by that person to Grenada, and to the extent to which they are not
recovered under subregulation (2) may be recovered in a court of competent
jurisdiction.

(4) Subject to subregulation (3), no action lies in respect of the removal, disposal or
sale of property under this Regulation.

### 40.

Penalty for late payments
(1) Where the liability of a person under the Act, the subregulations or a licence to
pay an amount is not discharged on or before the time when the amount is payable, there
is payable by that person an additional amount calculated at the rate of one-third of per
cent per day, upon so much of that amount as from time to time remains unpaid, to be
computed from the time that the amount becomes payable until it is paid.

(2) The Minister may, in a particular case, for reasons that in his or her opinion are
sufficient, remit the whole or part of an amount payable under this Regulation.

### 41.

Information to be furnished, etc.
(1) Where the Minister has reason to believe that a person is capable of giving
information or producing or making available books or documents relating to exploration
or development operations, or to petroleum obtained from an exploration or development
area or the value of petroleum so obtained, he or she may by notice in writing served on
that person, require that person to—
(a)

furnish that information to him or her in writing within the period and in the
manner specified in the notice; or

(b)

attend before him or her, or a person specified in the notice at such time and
place as is so specified and there to answer questions relating to those
operations or relating to petroleum obtained and to produce such books and
documents in his or her custody or power relating to those operations, the
petroleum obtained or the value of petroleum obtained as are so specified.

(2) A person is not excused from furnishing information, answering a question or
producing or making available books or documents when required to do so under this
Regulation on the grounds that the information so furnished, the answer to the question or
the production of or making available the books or documents might tend to incriminate
him or her or make him or her liable to penalty, but the information so furnished or his or
her answer to the question shall not be admissible in evidence against him or her in any
proceedings other than proceedings for an offence against this Regulation.
(3) Where books or documents are made available pursuant to a requirement under
subregulation (1)(b), the person to whom the books or documents are made available may
make copies of or take extracts from the books or documents.
(4) Any person who—
(a)

refuses or fails to comply with a requirement in a notice under
subregulation (1) to the extent to which he or she is capable of complying
with it;

(b)

in purported compliance with a requirement referred to in
subregulation (1)(a), knowingly or recklessly furnishes information that is
false or misleading in a material particular; or

(c)

when attending before the Minister or any other person under a requirement
referred to in subregulation (1)(b), knowingly or recklessly makes a
statement or produce or makes available a document that is or books that are
false or misleading in a material particular,

commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to the penalty stipulated in
section 29(3) of the Act.

### 42.

Survey of wells, etc.
(1) The Minister may, at any time, by notice in writing served on a licensee, direct
him or her to—
(a)

carry out a survey of the position of the well, structure or equipment
specified in the notice; and

(b)

furnish promptly to the Minister a report in writing of the survey.

(2) Where the Minister is not satisfied with a report of a survey furnished to him or
her under subregulation (1) by the licensee, the Minister may, by notice in writing served
on the licensee, direct him or her to furnish promptly such information in writing as he or
she may specify in connection with the survey.

(3) If a person to whom a direction is given under subregulation (1)(a) fails or
neglects to comply with the direction, the Minister may cause to be carried out any survey
specified in the notice containing the direction.
(4) The costs and expenses incurred pursuant to subregulation (3) in carrying out a
survey are a debt due to Grenada and are recoverable in a court of competent jurisdiction
notwithstanding that the licensee is convicted of an offence under subregulation (5).
(5) A person to whom a direction is given under subregulation (1) or (2) who fails or
neglects to comply with the direction commits an offence and liable to the penalty
stipulated in section 29(3) of the Act.

### 43.

Measurement of petroleum
(1) A licensee shall measure or weigh by a method or methods customarily used in
good oilfield practices and from time to time approved by the Minister all petroleum
obtained from an area subject to a licence.
(2) The holder of a licence shall not make any alteration in the method or methods of
measurement or weighing used by him or her or any appliances used for that purpose
without the consent in writing of the Minister; and the Minister may in any case require
that no alteration shall be made save in the presence of a person authorised by the
Minister.
(3) The Minister may, from time to time, direct that any weighing or measuring
appliance shall be tested or examined in such manner, upon such occasion or at such
intervals, and by such means as may be specified in the direction.
(4) If any measuring or weighing appliance shall upon any such test or examination as
is mentioned in subregulation (3) be found to be false or unjust the appliance shall, if the
Minister so determines after considering any representations in writing made by the
licensee concerned, be deemed to have existed in that condition commencing from the
date immediately following the date on which the appliance was last tested or examined
pursuant to subregulation (3), and any royalty and other amount affected thereby payable
under the Act, these Regulations, the licence or the petroleum agreement for the relevant
period shall be adjusted accordingly.
(5) For the purpose of measuring natural gas won and saved from an area subject to a
licence the volume of the natural gas shall be calculated at an absolute pressure of one
atmosphere and at a temperature of 60 degrees Fahrenheit.

### 44.

Control over companies
(1) Where a development licence is granted to a company, the company shall not,
after the date of the grant of the licence without the written consent of the Minister—
(a)

register the transfer of any share or shares in the company to any particular
person, or his or her nominee; or

(b)

enter into agreement with any particular person, if the effect of doing so
would be to give to the particular person or his or her nominee control of the
company.

(2) On application made to him or her for his or her consent under this Regulation, the
Minister shall give his or her consent if he or she considers that the public interest would
not be prejudiced, but otherwise shall refuse to give his or her consent; and for the
purpose of considering any such application the Minister may call for and obtain such
information as he or she considers necessary.
(3) For the purpose of this Regulation—
(a)

a person shall be deemed to have control of a company if—

(i) the person or his or her nominee holds or the person and his or her
nominee hold, a total of, twenty per cent or more of the equity shares
in the company, or
(ii) the person or his or her nominee is, or the person and his or her
nominee are, entitled to appoint, or prevent the appointment of, a
sufficient number of directors to form a quorum at meetings of
directors;
(b)

“equity shares”, in relation to a company, means shares in the company
having voting rights, and includes preference shares other than preference
shares not having voting rights; and

(c)

“preference shares” means shares which carry the right to payment of a
dividend of a fixed amount, in priority to payment of a dividend on another
classes of shares, whether with or without other rights.

## PART VI

Financial

### 45.

Prohibition on disposal of petroleum
(1) If the holder of a development licence fails to pay any royalty payable by him or
her on or before the due date, or any extension thereof allowed by the Minister, the
Minister may, by order served on the holder of licence, prohibit the removal of, or any
dealings in or with, any petroleum from the development area held by that holder, until all
outstanding royalty has been paid or until an agreement has been made and accepted by
the Minister, for the payment of a royalty and the holder shall comply with the order.
(2) If after a period of six months the holder of a licence still has outstanding royalty
or no agreement has been made, the Minister may revoke the licence.

### 46.

Recovery of royalty, etc.
(1) Royalty, rent or other amounts payable pursuant to the Act, these Regulations, or a
licence shall be a debt due to Grenada and may be recovered in a court of competent
jurisdiction.
(2) A certificate of the Minister certifying that a specific amount of royalty, rent or
other amount is payable by a specified person shall, in any proceedings instituted against
that person for the recovery of any such royalty, rent of amount, be received as prima
facie proof of that fact, but without prejudice to the right to adduce evidence in rebuttal.

## PART VII

Power of Entry and Offences

### 47.

Power of entry, etc.
(1) For the purpose of these Regulations, the Director, may at all reasonable times—
(a)

enter any area, structure, vehicle, vessel, aircraft or building that, in his or
her opinion, has been, is being or is to be used in connection with—
(i) exploration operations, or
(ii) development operations;

(b)

inspect and test, or have tested by a qualified person, any machinery or
equipment that, in his or her opinion, has been, is being or is to be used in
connection with any of the operations referred to in paragraph (a);

(c)

take or remove for the purpose of analysis or testing in connection with an
offence against the Act or these Regulations, samples of petroleum, water or
other substances from a well;

(d)

inspect, make extracts from, and make copies of, any document relating to
any of the operations referred to in paragraph (a);

(e)

with respect to the health and safety of persons employed by a holder of a
licence in or in connection with any of the operations referred to in
paragraph (a) issue directions to and impose restrictions on the holder, or
any persons so employed, by instrument in writing;

(f)

order, by instrument in writing—
(i) the cessation of operations on or in, and the withdrawal of all persons
from, any area, structure or building that is being used in connection
with any of the operation referred to in paragraph (a), or
(ii) the discontinuance of the use of any machinery or equipment, which
he or she considers unsafe, unless and until such action as is necessary
for safety and specified in the instrument is taken and completed;

(g)

make such examinations and inquiries as are necessary to ensure that the
provisions of the Act, these Regulations, and any directions issued,
restrictions imposed or orders made under the Act and these Regulations,
and any directions issued, restrictions imposed or orders made under the Act
and these Regulations are being complied with; and

(h)

obtain and record statements from witnesses, and appear at or conduct
inquiries held regarding accidents occurring in the course of any of the
operations referred to in paragraph (a), and appear at inquests, and call and
examine witnesses and cross-examine witnesses.

(2) Before exercising any of his or her powers under subregulation (1), if there is any
person present who is or appears to be in charge of the area, structure, vehicle, vessel,
aircraft, building, machinery, equipment or matter or thing in respect of which the power
is about to be exercised, the Director shall identify himself or herself to that person and to
any person to whom he or she is about to give an order or a direction.
(3) Any person who is aggrieved by a decision, direction or order of the Director
made under this Regulation may appeal in writing to the Minister who shall, as soon as
practicable, hear and dispose of the appeal, but the bringing of the appeal does not affect
the execution and operation of the decision, direction or order appealed from pending
disposition of the appeal.
(4) On appeal under subregulation (3), the Minister may rescind or affirm the
decision, direction or order appealed from or may make a new decision, direction or order
in substitution therefore.
(5) In exercising his or her powers under subregulation (1), the Director may be
accompanied by any person who the Director believes has special or expert knowledge of
any matter being inspected, tested or examined.
(6) A person who is an occupier or person in charge of any building, structure or
place, or the person in charge of any vehicle, vessel, aircraft, machinery or equipment
referred to in subregulation (1), shall provide the Director with all reasonable facilities
and assistance (including the provision of necessary means of transport) for the effective
exercise of the powers under this Regulation.
(7) Any person who—

(a)

without reasonable excuse, obstructs, or hinders the Director in the exercise
of his or her powers under this Regulation;

(b)

knowingly or recklessly makes a statement or produce a document that is
false or misleading in a material particular to the Director while engaged in
carrying out his or her duties and functions under this Regulation; or

(c)

with intent to mislead or deceive the Director when so engaged, does any act
or withholds any information,

commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to the penalty stipulated in
section 29(3) of the Act.

### 48.

Obstruction of holder of licence
Any person who, without reasonable excuse, obstructs, hinders or prevents the holder
of a development or the holder of an exploration licence in or from the doing of any act
which that holder is authorised to do by the Act, these Regulations of his or her licence,
commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to the penalty stipulated in
section 29(3) of the Act.

### 49.

Offence committed by body corporate
When an offence which has been committed by a body corporate is proved to have
been committed with the consent or convenience of, or to be attributable to any neglect
on the part of, a director, manager, secretary or other similar officer of the body
corporate, or any person who was purporting to act in any such capacity, as well as the
body corporate, shall be guilty of that offence and liable to be proceeded against and
punished accordingly.

### 50.

Miscellaneous offences
(1) Any person who—
(a)

in, or in connection with any application under these Regulations or his or
her licence knowingly or recklessly gives information which is false or
misleading in a material particular;

(b)

in any report, return or affidavit submitted in pursuance of any provisions of
these Regulations or his or her licence, knowingly or recklessly includes or
