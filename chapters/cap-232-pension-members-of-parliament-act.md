## CHAPTER 232

PENSION (MEMBERS OF PARLIAMENT) ACT
An Act to provide for the payment of pension and gratuity to former members of
Parliament.
[Act No. 18 of 1989 amended by Act No. 16 of 1990.]

[14th July, 1989.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Pension (Members of Parliament) Act.
2\. Interpretation
In this Act—
“member of Parliament” means a member of the Parliament of Grenada established
by section 23 of the Constitution, or of the Legislature established by the Grenada
Constitution Order, 1967, and includes an office holder, but does not include Her
Majesty;
“office holder” means a former President of the Senate or Speaker of the House of
Representatives;
“previous Legislature” means a Legislative Council of Grenada preceding the
Legislature established by the Grenada Constitution Order, 1967, at any time not
earlier than 7th November, 1951;

“salary” includes basic salary, personal allowance, and duty allowance but does not
include transportation allowance, entertainment allowance, housing allowance, or any
other allowance whatsoever.
3\. Pension to former members of Parliament
(1) Every former member of Parliament shall, from the commencement of this Act, be
eligible for a pension as specified in section 4, if he or she—
(a)

has served as a member for a period of eight years in the aggregate, whether
such service was continuous or intermittent;

(b)

has ceased to be a member; and

(c)

has either—
(i) attained the age of fifty-five, or
(ii) is incapable of discharging his or her duties as a member by reason of
infirmity of mind or body of such a nature that is likely to be
permanent:
Provided that the service of an elected member of a previous
Legislature who was serving as a member of the House of
Representatives on the 3rd March, 1967 (being the date of the coming
into operation of the Grenada Constitution Order, S.I. 227 of 1967)
shall be counted as pensionable service for the purpose of this Act.

(2) A person shall not be deemed to have ceased to be a member of Parliament by
reason only of the dissolution of Parliament if he or she is elected to the House of
Representatives or appointed to the Senate at the next Parliament, but where he or she is
not so re-elected or re-appointed he or she shall be deemed to have ceased to be a member
from the date of the dissolution.
(3) A person who has ceased to be a member of Parliament after having served as
such for a period of not less than eight years before attaining the age of fifty-five shall, if
he or she is not eligible for pension under subsection (1)(c)(ii), become eligible for
pension with effect from the date of his or her attaining the age of fifty-five or, in the case
of any such person who attained the age of fifty-five before the commencement of this
Act, with immediate effect.
4\. Duration and computation of pension
(1) The annual amount of pension to a person under section 3 shall continue to be
paid during his or her lifetime and shall be computed—
(a)

in the case of a person who has served eight years but less than fifteen years
in the aggregate, at one-third of the highest annual salary drawn by that
person;

(b)

in the case of a person who has served for fifteen years or more in the
aggregate, at one-half of the highest annual salary drawn by that person:

Provided that in the case of a person who is returned to the House of
Representatives or is re-appointed to the Senate, eligibility for pension shall be subject to
the provisions of section 5.
(2) The pension payable to a person shall be apportioned and paid on a monthly basis.
5\. Pension of returning members
(1) If a person who is eligible for pension is returned to the House of Representatives
or re-appointed to the Senate, no pension shall be payable to him or her during the period
for which he or she again draws salary and allowances as a member of Parliament.

(2) From the date that a person referred to in subsection (1) again ceases to be a
member of Parliament and discontinues drawing salary and allowances as such a member,
he or she shall thereafter be eligible for a pension at a rate to be fixed having regard also
to the period, if any, for which he or she was a member subsequent to the fixation of
pension on the earlier occasion.
(3) Where the amount of the salary payable to a member on his or her being returned
or re-appointed to Parliament is less than the amount of the pension last drawn by him or
her, he or she may, at his or her option, be paid an amount equivalent to the amount of
pension drawn by him or her as his or her salary.
6\. Gratuity to former members of Parliament
(1) Every person who has served as a member of Parliament or in a previous
Legislature for not less than four years shall, if he or she ceased to be a member after the
3rd March, 1967, be entitled to receive a gratuity of an amount equal to one-fifth of the
aggregate salary drawn by him or her during the period for which he or she was eligible
to draw salary as such member:
Provided that, in the case of a person who is re-elected or re-appointed as a member
after receiving a gratuity, he or she shall be entitled to receive only the difference, if any,
in the amount of gratuity arrived at on a recalculation after taking into account the period
for which he or she was a member on such re-election or re-appointment.
(2) Any period during which a member of Parliament was an office holder shall be
included for the purpose of subsection (1) as if he or she were a member of Parliament.
7\. Personal representatives to be paid pension or gratuity in case of death
In the case of a person who dies while serving or after having served as a member of
Parliament the pension or gratuity for which he or she would have been otherwise eligible
shall be paid to his or her legal personal representative.
8\. Pension and gratuity charged on Consolidated Fund
All amounts payable by way of pension or gratuity under this Act shall be a charge on
the Consolidated Fund.
9\. Removal of difficulties, etc.
If any difficulty arises in giving effect to the provisions of this Act or any doubt arises
in relation to the interpretation of this Act, the Governor-General may, by Order, remove
the difficulty or resolve the doubt.

### 1.

Citation
This Order may be cited as the Gratuity Payments to Former Parliamentarians
(Interpretation) Order.

### 2.

Definition
In this Order, “Salary” includes basic salary, personal allowance, and duty allowance,
but does not include transportation allowance, entertainment allowance, housing
allowance, or any allowance whatsoever.

### 3.

Interpretation
For the avoidance of doubt, section 6(1) of the Pension (Members of Parliament) Act,

## Chapter 232 of the Revised Laws of Grenada, 1990, shall be interpreted to mean that on

the dissolution of every Parliament every person who has served as a member of
Parliament for not less than four years shall be entitled to receive a gratuity of an amount
equal to one-fifth of the aggregate salary drawn during the period for which that person
was eligible to draw salary as such member.
Her Majesty’s officers and loving subjects in Grenada, and all others whom it may
concern, are hereby required to take due notice hereof and give their ready compliance
accordingly.
