## CHAPTER 136

HOTEL PROPRIETORS LIABILITY ACT
An Act to provide for certain rights and liabilities of hotel proprietors.
[Act No. 51 of 1979.]

[27th June, 1979.]
1\. Short title
This Act may be cited as the Hotel Proprietors Liability Act.
2\. Interpretation
In this Act—
“guest” means a person received into a hotel to partake of food or drink or to use
sleeping accommodation provided therein;
“hotel” means any place or establishment held out by a proprietor as offering food
and drink and, if so required, sleeping accommodation to any person presenting
himself or herself who is willing and able to pay the contracted price for the services
and facilities provided and who is in a fit and proper state to be received therein;
“proprietor” means a person having the management or control of a hotel and the
conduct of business therein, and includes a body of persons (whether corporate or not)
owning a hotel.
3\. Liability of a proprietor for damage to or loss of the property of a guest
(1) Subject to subsection (2), a proprietor is liable to make good to a guest any
damage to or loss of the guest’s property brought to the hotel where—

(a)

at the time the damage or loss occurred, sleeping accommodation was
engaged by the guest;

(b)

the damage or loss occurred during the period commencing with the
acceptance of the guest into the hotel and ending with his or her departure
therefrom at the end of his or her stay;

(c)

at the time the damage or loss occurred, the property was on the premises of
the hotel; and

(d)

the guest has, without undue delay, informed the proprietor or his or her
agent or employee of the loss or damage and is able to prove the value of the
loss.

(2) A proprietor is not liable under subsection (1) to make good to a guest any
damage or loss in respect of—
(a)

a domestic animal;

(b)

a vehicle or any property left in a vehicle;

(c)

property tendered for safe keeping but refused in accordance with section
5(2);

(d)

property lost or damaged due to—
(i) an act of God,
(ii) civil commotion,
(iii) the default, neglect or wilful act of the guest or of a person
accompanying or visiting him or her or in his or her employment.

(3) A proprietor shall not have any lien on property left in a vehicle, or on a vehicle or
any domestic animal.
4\. Limits of liability
(1) Subject to subsection (2), the liability of a proprietor under section 3 shall not
exceed one hundred dollars in respect of any one article or two hundred and fifty dollars
in respect of the property of any one guest.
(2) Subsection (1) does not apply—
(a)

where the article or property has been stolen, lost or damaged by reason of
the wilful act or the default or neglect of the proprietor or his or her agent or
employee;

(b)

where the article or property has been accepted for safe keeping in
accordance with section 5;

(c)

unless, at the time when the property was brought to the hotel, a notice
printed in plain type in the form of the Schedule was conspicuously
displayed in a place—
(i) at or near the reception desk, or
(ii) at or near the entrance to the hotel, or
(iii) in the bedroom allocated to the guest,

so that it may easily be read by the guest.
(3) Where an article or property has been accepted for safe keeping in accordance
with section 5 the liability of a proprietor to a guest in respect of that article or property
shall not exceed the value thereof as declared by the depositor at the time it was accepted.
(4) A proprietor may, by written notice or special agreement, limit his or her liability
for loss or damage to the property of a guest where he or she undertakes or renders a

service in relation to that property but a proprietor may not otherwise contract out of
liability imposed upon him or her by this Act.
5\. Property in safe keeping
(1) A proprietor or his or her employee shall accept and deposit in safe keeping all
property tendered for that purpose by a guest.
(2) Notwithstanding subsection (1), a proprietor or his or her employee may refuse to
accept any property so tendered which appears to be noxious, unwholesome or dangerous
or which, having regard to the size or standing of the hotel, appears to be cumbersome or
of excessive value.
(3) Where property tendered for safe keeping consists of or includes money,
securities, jewellery or other valuables, a proprietor or his or her employee may as a
condition precedent to acceptance require—
(a)

the guest to permit him or her to inspect the property;

(b)

that the value of the property be declared to him or her; and

(c)

that the property be fastened and sealed in a container in his or her presence
by the depositor.

(4) For the purposes of this section, “employee” means an employee authorised, or
appearing to be authorised, to accept property for safe keeping.
6\. Proprietor’s lien on the belongings of a guest
Except as provided by section 3(3) a proprietor shall have a lien on the belongings of a
guest for the payment of the price owing for food, drink, accommodation, or other
services or facilities provided for him or her by the proprietor; and the proprietor shall be
entitled to retain those belongings and, at any time after the expiration of thirty days, to
cause the same to be sold by public auction and to recover from the proceeds thereof all
or part of the price together with the costs and expenses of the sale.
7\. Offence to refuse guest on ground of race, etc.
A proprietor who refuses to receive as a guest any person by reason of that person’s
race, religion or colour, is guilty of an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a
fine of one thousand dollars and to imprisonment for six months.
8\. Proprietor’s right to demand advance payment
A proprietor may lawfully demand payment of any reasonable sum in advance from a
guest or prospective guest who, if he or she desires to remain as a guest, shall tender the
sum demanded.

Notice to Guests
[Section 4(2)(c).]

Under the provisions of the Hotel Proprietors Liability Act, a hotel proprietor may in
certain circumstances be liable to make good the loss of or damage to the property of a
guest even though it was not due to any default of the proprietor or the staff of the hotel.

This liability, however—
(a)

extends only to the property of guests who have engaged sleeping
accommodation;

(b)

is limited to one hundred dollars in respect of any one article or two hundred
and fifty dollars in respect of the property of any one guest except where the
article or property has been accepted for safe keeping;

(c)

does not extend to any kind of vehicle nor any property left in a vehicle, nor
to a domestic animal;

(d)

where property has been accepted for safe keeping, may be limited by
written notice or special agreement.
