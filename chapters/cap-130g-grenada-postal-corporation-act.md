## CHAPTER 130G

GRENADA POSTAL CORPORATION ACT

An Act to provide a legislative basis for the establishment, powers and duties of the
Grenada Postal Corporation, and for matters connected therewith and incidental
thereto.
[Act No. 21 of 1996 amended by Act No. 4 of 1997, Act No. 4 of 2002, Act No. 21 of 2008.]

[24th January, 1997.]

## PART I

Preliminary

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Grenada Postal Corporation Act.

### 2.

Interpretation
In this Act—
“Board” means the Board of the Grenada Postal Corporation established under
section 8(1);
“business plan”, for a financial year, means—
(a)

the business plan approved under section 36A; and

(b)

all amendments to the business plan approved under that section,

for the financial year;
“carrier” includes aircraft and boats of all types;
“Chairperson” means the Chairperson of the Board of the Corporation;
“Corporation” means the Grenada Postal Corporation established by section 3;
“Director” means the Director of Post appointed in accordance with section 12(1);
“financial year” means the financial year referred to in section 36;
“inland post” means an establishment for the receipt, carriage and delivery by post,
between places within Grenada, of postal packets including postal packets received
from places beyond Grenada and postal packets posted in Grenada for transmission to
places beyond it;
“letters” means letters, papers, newspapers, books, parcels and also all other
packages authorised to be conveyed by post either by this Act or by the regulations;
“mail bag” means a bag, box or parcel or any other envelope in which postal
packets are conveyed, whether or not it contains postal packets;
“master” means the captain or other person in charge of any carrier which brings
postal packets to Grenada;
“Minister” means the Minister responsible for postal services;
“post” includes all postal communications by air, land or by water (except by
outward bound carrier not being employed by, under or on behalf of the Corporation
to carry postal packets);
“postal packet” includes letters, papers, newspapers books, parcels and anything
authorised to be conveyed by post and in the course of such conveyance by post from
the time of delivery to a post office to the time of its delivery to the person to whom it
is addressed;

“postal service” means the conveyance of postal packets, the sale of stamps, postal
orders, money orders, the rental of post boxes the transmission and receipt of
messages and the provision of related services;
“Universal Postal Union” means the Universal Postal Union established by the
Berne Convention.

## PART II

Establishment, Constitution and Management of the Corporation

### 3.

Establishment of Grenada Postal Corporation
(1) There is hereby established the Grenada Postal Corporation which shall be a body
corporate to which sections 47 and 49 of the Interpretation and General Corporation
Provisions Act, Chapter 153, apply.
(2) If any document needs to be sealed by the seal of the Corporation, the affixing of
the seal must be—
(a)

approved by resolution of the Board;

(b)

authenticated by the signature of the Chairperson or Deputy Chairperson and
one other member of the Board.

(3) A document which does not need to be sealed may be signed by the Chairperson,
the Deputy Chairperson or the Director, and a decision of the Board may be signified in
like manner.

### 4.

Functions of the Corporation
The Corporation shall, in accordance with the policy determinations of the Board,
provide postal services throughout Grenada so as to satisfy all reasonable demands or
anticipated demands for such services on a strictly commercial basis.

### 5.

Powers of Corporation
The Corporation—
(a)

shall have the exclusive right to provide postal services throughout Grenada;

(b)

notwithstanding anything contained in any other enactment, may provide
limited banking services by means of which money is remitted, whether by
means of money orders or otherwise;

(c)

notwithstanding anything contained in any other enactment, may provide
limited telecommunication services involving the transmission and receipt of
facsimile messages;

(d)

shall have the exclusive right to permit and sell stamps in Grenada;

(e)

subject to this Act, shall have the exclusive right to rent or lease post boxes.

### 6.

Power to licence
(1) The Corporation may licence any person to provide any postal service in Grenada
for such period as the Board prescribes.
(2) A licence under subsection (1) shall be subject to the payment of the prescribed
fees and subject to such conditions as the Board, with the approval of the Minister,
determines.

### 7.

Membership of the Universal Postal Union
The membership of the Universal Postal Union shall be held by the Government but
the Minister may with the approval of the Cabinet make arrangements with the Union for
the Corporation to be represented therein if such representation is warranted.

### 8.

Board of the Corporation
(1) The executive powers of the Corporation shall be exercised by a Board which
shall be constituted in the manner specified in subsection (2).
(2) The Board shall consist of seven members who shall be appointed by the Minister
to represent such interests, including that of Government, as the Minister determines.
(3) The members appointed by the Minister shall hold office for a period not
exceeding three years and shall be eligible for re-appointment.
(4) A member of the Board who cannot attend a meeting of the Board on account of
illness or other temporary cause or who will be temporarily absent from the State shall
inform the Chairperson accordingly.
(5) A member of the Board appointed by the Minister who absents himself or herself
without leave of the Chairperson from three consecutive meetings of the Board shall
cease to be a member.
(6) The Minister shall appoint a Chairperson and a Deputy Chairperson of the Board
from amongst the members appointed pursuant to subsection (2).
(7) The Schedule shall have effect in relation to the procedure of the meetings of the
Board.

### 9.

Secretary to the Board
(1) The Chairperson shall designate an employee of the Corporation to be Secretary to
the Board.
(2) The Secretary shall perform such duties in relation to the meetings of the Board as
the Chairperson determines.

### 10.

Duties of the Board
(1) The Board shall be responsible for the formulation of general policy regarding the
functions of the Corporation as prescribed by this Act.
(2) The Board shall in the formulation of policy have regard to—
(a)

efficiency and economy;

(b)

the social, industrial, commercial and general developmental needs of
Grenada;

(c)

the desirability of improving and developing postal services and attendant
operating systems; and

(d)

developments in the field of communications, banking and related fields.

### 11.

General directions by Minister
Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act, the Minister may give written
directions to the Board on matters of general policy and the Board shall give effect to
such directions.

## PART III

Officers and Staff of the Corporation

### 12.

Director of Post
(1) There shall be a Director of Post hereinafter referred to as “the Director” who
shall be the Chief Executive Officer of the Corporation and who shall perform such duties
and exercise such powers as specified in this Act.
(2) Subject to subsection (3), the Director shall be appointed by the Board on such
terms and conditions as the Board determines.
(3) No person may be appointed to the office of Director without the written approval
of the Minister.

### 13.

Other staff
(1) The Board may, from time to time, appoint such other staff as may be required as
the circumstances of the Corporation demands.
(2) The Board may in writing delegate its powers under subsection (1) to the Director
or another senior officer of the Corporation.

### 14.

Employment and secondment of public officers
(1) Notwithstanding anything contained in any other enactment and subject to
subsection (2), where a person who immediately prior to the commencement of this Act
was employed in the public service and attached to the Post Office Department, but has
subsequently resigned, the Corporation shall employ such person on terms and conditions
that are no less favourable to those enjoyed by that person in the public service prior to
his or her resignation.
(2) The Corporation shall not employ any person under subsection (1) if that person
had indicated expressly his or her unwillingness to be so employed.
(3) The Public Service Commission may, subject to such conditions as it may impose,
approve appointment or secondment of an officer in the public service to any office
within the Corporation and an officer so appointed or seconded shall, in relation to
pension, gratuity or other allowances, and in relation to other rights as a public officer, be
treated as continued service in the public service in Grenada.
(4) Nothing contained in subsection (1) shall have any bearing or relevance to an
agreement between the Corporation and an individual concerning employment with the
Corporation.

### 15.

Preservation of posts
Notwithstanding anything contained in any other enactment, where an officer is
seconded to the Corporation under section 14 his or her post or an equivalent post shall be
deemed to be subsisting for the duration of such secondment.

### 16.

Accrued pensions
A secondment to the Corporation shall not affect any accrued pension rights of an
employee which derived from service in the public service of Grenada.

### 17.

Status of seconded officer
(1) For the avoidance of doubt it is hereby declared that an officer seconded to the
Corporation shall continue to be in the public service of Grenada until such time when he
or she resigns, retires or otherwise leaves the public service.

(2) The Corporation may enter any arrangement with the Government with respect to
an employee who is seconded pursuant to section 13.

### 18.

Pension scheme and medical insurance
(1) Subject to the National Insurance Act, Chapter 205, the Corporation may, with the
approval of, and subject to the general policy directions by the Minister, provide for the
establishment and maintenance of a pension plan and medical insurance for the benefit of
its officers and staff.
(2) Without prejudice to the generality of subsection (1), the pension plan may
empower the Corporation to—
(a)

pay gratuities, pensions or superannuation allowances to or the widows,
families or dependents of its officers and staff;

(b)

establish contributory and superannuation funds for the benefit of its officers
and staff; and

(c)

enter into and carry into effect agreements with any general insurance
company with respect to a pension scheme.

## PART IV

Postal Matters Generally

### 19.

Duties of Director
The Director shall have the following duties—
(a)

attend at the General Post Office daily and otherwise as the regulations may
prescribe, and also at such times as may be necessary for the receipt and
delivery of postal packets from and to carriers and of all other postal packets;

(b)

receive, arrange and deliver all postal packets posted at or coming through
the General Post Office;

(c)

sell postage stamps and receive all monies payable for stamps or for the
postage of letters in lieu or in the absence of stamps, or otherwise payable to
him or her;

(d)

superintend the working and management of the inland post;

(e)

make up and deliver to and also receive from the mail agents of any foreign
government mail bags containing postal packets in such manner as the
regulations may prescribe;

(f)

implementation of the policies of the Board as communicated to him or her;
and

(g)

perform such other duties as the Board requires.

### 20.

Exclusive privilege of Director re postal packets
(1) The Director shall, within Grenada, have the exclusive privilege of conveying
postal packets from one place to another, and shall also have the exclusive privilege of
performing all the incidental services of receiving, collecting, sending, dispatching and
delivering all postal packet, except in the following cases, that is to say—
(a)

letters sent by a friend on his or her way, journey, or travel, so that those
letters be delivered by that friend to the person to whom they are directed;

(b)

letters sent by a messenger, concerning the private affairs of the sender or
receiver thereof;

(c)

commissions or returns thereof, and affidavits and writs, process and
proceedings, or returns thereof, issuing out of any Court;

(d)

letters sent out of Grenada by a private carrier (not being a carrier carrying
postal packets under contract);

(e)

letters of merchants, owners of carriers of merchandise, or the cargo or
loading therein, sent by those carriers of merchandise or by any person
employed by those owners for the carriage of those letters according to their
respective directions, and delivered to the respective persons to whom they
are directed without paying or receiving hire or reward, advantage, or profit
for the same in any form;

(f)

letters concerning goods or merchandise sent by common known carriers, to
be delivered with the goods which those letters concern, without hire or
reward or other profit or advantage for receiving or delivering those letters,

but nothing herein contained shall authorise any person to make a collection of those
excepted letters for the purpose of sending them in the manner hereby authorised.
(2) Subject as aforesaid, the following persons are expressly forbidden to carry a
letter, or to receive or collect or deliver a letter, although they do not receive hire or
reward for it, that is to say—
(a)

common known carrier, their employees or agents, except a letter concerning
goods in any conveyance of their own;

(b)

owners, masters or commanders of carriers sailing or travelling between
ports or places within Grenada, or their employees or agents, except in
respect of letters of merchants, owners of goods on board;

(c)

passengers or other persons on any carrier.

### 21.

Contracts for the conveyance of postal packets
(1) The Director is hereby authorised to enter into, on behalf of the Corporation,
contracts with the agents of any carrier for the conveyance of postal packets on such
terms and subject to such conditions as may be agreed in this regard.
(2) A contract under subsection (1) shall be in accordance with any rules, regulations
or like instruments of the Universal Postal Union.

### 22.

Duties of Grenada Port Authority regarding postal packets
(1) The Grenada Ports Authority shall receive postal packets conveyed to Grenada by
ship and deliver the same to the Corporation in accordance with any written directions
given by the Comptroller of Customs.
(2) In this section, “Grenada Ports Authority” means the Grenada Ports Authority
established by the Ports Authority Act, Chapter 247.

### 23.

Directions by the Comptroller of Customs
(1) The Comptroller of Customs may for the purposes of the Customs (Control and
Management) Act, Chapter 75B, give written directions to the agents or employees of the
Corporations and of the Grenada Port Authority with respect to postal packets arriving at
or leaving any port in Grenada.
(2) The agents or employees of the Corporation and of the Grenada Port Authority
shall give effect to such directions by the Comptroller.

### 24.

Access to post offices
The Corporation shall ensure that the Comptroller of Customs is provided with space
at any post office and access thereto for the purpose of the examination of postal packets
and the collection of duties thereon as required by law.

### 25.

Payment of postage and other charges
(1) There shall be charged in respect of all postal packets such rates of postage and
other charges as may be prescribed by the Board under this Act with the approval of the
Minister.
(2) There shall be charged in respect of other postal services such charges as may be
prescribed by the Board under this Act with the approval of the Minister.

### 26.

Exemption
The Minister may by order make such exemption from the payment of postage or
other charges that are payable by virtue of section 25.

### 27.

Unpaid postage and recovery thereof
(1) The charges for unpaid or underpaid postal packets brought into Grenada for
delivery within Grenada shall be in accordance with the charges laid down by the
Universal Postal Union Convention for the time being in force.
(2) The postage or other charges imposed by or under this Act by the Corporation
may be sued for and recovered in civil proceedings before a magistrate at the instance of
the Corporation notwithstanding that the amount to be recovered exceeds the normal
monetary limit on the jurisdiction of the Magistrate’s Court.

### 28.

Detention of letters
The Director may detain any postal packets posted or sent by post contrary to this Act
or the regulations and he or she may open such postal packets and either return them to
the senders thereof or forward to their destination charged in either case with such rates of
postage as the regulations may, from time to time, prescribe.

### 29.

Appeal from decision of Director
A person aggrieved by a decision of the Director with respect to any question whether
a letter posted or sent by the inland post is a letter within the meaning of this Act or the
regulations may appeal from that decision to the Board and the Board may reverse,
confirm or modify the Director’s decision.

## PART V

Funds and Property of the Corporation and Accounting Matters Generally

### 30.

Funds of the Corporation and use thereof
(1) The funds of the Corporation shall consist of—
(a)

such money as may be voted by Parliament;

(b)

any loan made to the Corporation by the Government or any other person or
organisation;

(c)

money derived from the sale of postal services; and

(d)

money received by the Corporation by way of terminal fees; and

(e)

money derived from clearance fees, storage fees, commissions and profits on
remittances;

(f)

any other money lawfully received by the Corporation by way of grant or
otherwise for the purposes of the Corporation.

(2) The money referred to in subsection (1) shall on receipt thereof be placed to the
credit of the Corporation at a bank determined by the Board.

### 31.

Investment of money
The Board may invest the funds of the Corporatior in a prudent manner so as to
enhance the financial position of the Corporation.

### 32.

Power to borrow and guarantee of borrowing
(1) Subject to this Act, the Corporation may with the prior consent of the Minister,
after he or she has consulted the Minister of finance, borrow money that is required to
meet its obligations or to discharge any of its functions, under this Act.
(2) The Minister may, with the approval of Parliament, guarantee in such manner and
or such conditions as he or she considers appropriate, the repayment of the principal,
interest and other charges with respect to any authorised borrowing by the Corporation.
(3) Where the Minister is satisfied that there has been default in the payment of any
principal monies or interest guaranteed under this section he or she shall direct payment
out of the Consolidated Fund of the amount in respect of which there has been default.
(4) The Corporation shall make payment to the Accountant-General, at such time and
in such manner as the Minister may direct, of the amount so directed in or towards the
repayment of a sum debited to the Consolidated Fund in fulfilment of a guarantee given
under this section, and in or towards the payment of interest on what is outstanding for
the time being in respect of the sum so debited at such rate as the Minister may direct, and
different rates of interest may be directed as regards different sums and interest for
differing periods.
(5) For the purposes of subsections (2), (3) and (4), “Minister” means the Minister of
finance.
(6) The Corporation may, with the prior written consent of the Minister of finance,
pledge or mortgage any of its assets as security for a loan, whether or not the loan is
guaranteed under this section.

### 33.

Vesting of property
All existing post offices in Grenada shall by virtue of this section be deemed to be
vested in the Corporation free from encumbrances.

### 34.

Reserve Fund
(1) The Corporation shall establish and maintain a Reserve Fund which shall be dealt
with and applied as the Board determines.
(2) The Reserve Fund shall be derived from the surplus of the Corporation and shall
be in such amount as the Board, after consultation with the Minister, determines.

### 35.

Payment into Consolidated Fund
The Corporation shall in each quarter pay into the Consolidated Fund such amount as
the Minister directs in writing.

### 36.

Financial year
The financial year of the Postal Corporation is the twelve months period beginning on
the 1st day of January and ending on the 31st day of December.

### 36A.

Annual business plan
(1) The Board shall, not later than four months before the commencement of each
financial year and in such form as the Minister requires, prepare in respect of the financial
year, and submit to the Minister, a proposal for a business plan for the financial year that
must contain—
(a)

a statement of the Corporation’s objectives and priorities in carrying out its
responsibilities for the financial year and the following two financial years;

(b)

a comprehensive business plan that—
(i) shows how resources, including but not limited to financial resources,
will be allocated to meeting the objectives and priorities of the
Corporation for the financial year, and
(ii) includes pro forma financial statements as required by the Minister;

(c)

a comparison of the pro forma financial statements with the actual financial
statements for the previous financial year;

(d)

a statement as to how the Board proposes to measure its performance in
carrying out its responsibilities in the financial year; and

(e)

any other information required by the Minister by written notice to the
Board.

(2) The Minister may, on the request of the Board, extend the time for submitting a
proposal for a business plan.
(3) the Minister shall, as soon as practicable, consider the proposal for a business plan
and may—
(a)

approve the proposal as submitted;

(b)

with the approval of the Board, amend the proposal and approve it as
amended; or

(c)

refer the proposal back to the Board with directions that the Board take any
further action with respect to it that the Minister considers appropriate.

(4) Where the Minister refers the proposal for a business plan back to the Board under
subsection (3)(c), he or she shall provide the Board with his or her reasons for not
approving it.
(5) A proposal for a business plan that is referred back to the Board under
subsection (3)(c) must be resubmitted to the Minister as directed by the Minister and,
when it is resubmitted, subsections (3) and (4) apply.
(6) When a proposal in relation to a financial year is approved by the Minister, it
becomes the business plan for that financial year.
(7) The Board—
(a)

may, of its own motion, submit to the Minister a proposal to amend an
approved business plan; and

(b)

shall, on request of the Minister and within the time required by the Minister,
submit to the Minister a proposal to amend an approved business plan.

(8) Subsections (3), (4), (5) and (6) apply to a proposal submitted to the Minister
under subsection (7).

### 36B.

Board obligated to implement business plan
The Board—
(a)

shall, in each financial year, implement the business plan for the financial
year; and

(b)

establish a mechanism for monitoring the implementation of the business
plan.

### 37.

Accounts
(1) The Board shall—
(a)

keep proper books of account of its income and other receipts and
expenditures; and

(b)

ensure that—
(i) all money received are promptly brought to account,
(ii) all payments out of its money are correctly made and properly
authorised, and
(iii) adequate control is maintained over its property and over the incurring
of liabilities by the Authority.

(2) The books of account kept under subsection (1) shall—
(a)

be sufficient to record and explain the Corporation’s transactions;

(b)

enable the Corporation’s financial position to be determined with reasonable
accuracy at any time; and

(c)

be sufficient to enable financial statements to be prepared and audited in
accordance with this section.

(3) Within three months after the end of each financial year, the Board shall cause to
be prepared—
(a)

the following financial statements together with proper and adequate
explanatory notes—
(i) a statement of the assets and liabilities of the Corporation at the end of
the financial year,
(ii) a statement of the revenue and expenditure of the Corporation during
the financial year,
(iii) such other financial statements for the financial year as may be
specified in writing by the Minister; and

(b)

an annual report of the Corporation on the implementation of the business
plan and such other matters as the Board considers advisable or the Minister
directs.

(4) Without delay after the completion of the financial statements and the annual
report, the Board shall furnish a copy of each to the Director of Audit.

### 38.

Audit by Director of Audit and report to the House of Representatives
(1) Not later than three months after receipt of the financial statements and annual
report from the Board, the Director of Audit shall audit the financial statements in
accordance with the Audit Act, Chapter 22A.
(2) Without delay, after the completion of his or her audit of the Corporation, the
Director of Audit shall submit a copy of his or her report together with the financial
statements and annual report to the Minister and the Board.

(3) The Minister shall, not later than seven days after the House of Representatives
first meets after he or she has received the report together with the financial statements
and the annual report of the Corporation, lay it before the House of Representatives.
(4) If the Minister fails to lay the report together with the financial statements and the
annual report of the Corporation before the House of Representatives in accordance with
subsection (3), the Director of Audit shall transmit the report, the financial statements and
the annual report to the Speaker who shall, as soon as practicable, present them to the
House of Representatives.
(5) As soon as reasonably practicable after the report together with the financial
statements and the annual report of the Corporation have been laid before the House of
Representatives, the Board shall cause the report, the financial statements and the annual
report of the Corporation to be published in the Gazette.

### 39.

Accountant’s report
The accountant shall, as soon as practicable, but not later than three months after the
end of each financial year, send copies of the audited financial statement and report
thereon to the Corporation.

### 40.

Report to Minister
(1) The Corporation shall, as soon as practicable but not later than one month after the
receipt of the audited financial statement and report under section 39, submit a separate
report to the Minister containing—
(a)

the audited financial statement and the report thereon received from the
accountant;

(b)

a review of the activities of the Corporation during the preceding year in
such form as the Minister directs or approves.

(2) The Minister shall cause a copy of the audited financial statement and report
thereon together with the review to be laid in both Houses of Parliament within one
month of the receipt thereof.

## PART VI

Offences and Procedure for Offences

### 41.

Misconduct of the Corporation’s employees
An employee of the Corporation who—
(a)

whilst so employed or whilst any post bag or letter is in his or her custody,
care or possession lose such mail bag or postal packet;

(b)

conduct himself or herself in such a manner, whether by way of drunkness,
carelessness or otherwise whereby the safety of a mail bag or postal packet is
endangered;

(c)

collects, receives, conveys or delivers a postal packet otherwise than in the
ordinary course of the post;

(d)

gives any false information of any assault or attempt at robbery upon him or
her; or

(e)

Repealed

(f)

fails or refuses to use due and proper care and diligence to convey safety all
mail bags and postal packets committed to his or her keeping in accordance
with this Act or the regulations made thereunder,

commits an offence and shall be liable, on summary conviction, thereof to a fine of five
thousand dollars or imprisonment for a term of one year.

### 42.

Detaining or opening postal packets
(1) Subject to subsection (2), an employee of the Corporation who for reward
receives, conveys or delivers any letter otherwise than in the ordinary course of the post
and every such employee who, contrary to his or her duty, opens or procures to be opened
or suffers to be detained or delayed any mail bag or postal packet commits an offence and
shall be liable, on summary conviction, thereof to a fine of five thousand dollars.
(2) Nothing contained in subsection (1) shall extend to the opening or detaining or
delaying of any postal packet returned for want by reason that the person to whom it is
directed is dead or cannot be found or has refused to receive it, nor to the opening or
detaining or delaying of any postal packet in accordance with an express warrant in
writing under the hand of the Minister.

### 43.

Master tampering with mail
The master of any ship or captain of any aircraft who—
(a)

opens a sealed mail bag with which he or she is entrusted for conveyance; or

(b)

takes out of a mail bag with which he or she is entrusted for conveyance any
postal packet or other thing,

commits an offence and shall be liable, on summary conviction, thereof to a fine of ten
thousand dollars.

### 44.

Using cancelled stamps
Any person who uses or causes or allows to be used in prepayment of postage, any
postage stamp, postal card or stamped envelop or any stamp out or taken from any such
envelope which has already been used for a like purpose commits an offence and shall be
liable, on summary conviction, thereof to a fine of one thousand dollars or imprisonment
for three months.

### 45.

Using unlicensed postal franking machine
A person who uses a postal franking machine without there being in force a licence to
use the same commits an offence and shall be liable, on summary conviction, thereof to a
fine of ten thousand dollars and, in the case of a continuing offence, to a further fine of
one thousand dollars for each day or part thereof during which the offence continues after
a first or subsequent conviction for the offence.

### 46.

Prohibition on use of post boxes
(1) No person shall engage in the sale, rental, lease or any other commercial
transaction with respect to post boxes unless such person is licensed to do so by the
Corporation.
(2) A person who contravenes this section commits an offence and shall be liable, on
summary conviction, thereof to a fine of ten thousand dollars.

### 47.

Prohibition on postage
(1) A person who posts or attempts to post a postal packet which—

(a)

encloses any explosive substance, dangerous substance filth, noxious or
deleterious substance, any sharp instrument not properly protected, any
weapon, any living creature which is either noxious or likely to injure other
postal packets in the course of conveyance or any employee of the
Corporation;

(b)

encloses any indecent or obscene print, painting photograph, lithograph
engraving, book, card or any indecent or obscene article, whether similar to
the foregoing or not;

(c)

has on the packet or on the cover thereof any words, marks or design of an
indecent, obscene or grossly offensive character,

commit an offence and shall be liable, on summary conviction, thereof to a fine of ten
thousand dollars or to imprisonment for twelve months, or both such fine and
imprisonment.
(2) The detention in a post office of any postal packet on the ground of its being in
contravention of this section shall not exempt the sender thereof from any proceedings
which might have been taken if the packet had been delivered in due course of post.

### 48.

Disclosing messages
An employee of the Corporation who—
(a)

discloses or aids or abets in the disclosure of the contents of any facsimile
message to a person, other than the sender or the person to who it is
addressed; or

(b)

detains or delays or procures or suffers to be detained or delayed without
reasonable excuse the forwarding or delivery of any facsimile message,

commits an offence and shall be liable, on summary conviction, thereof to a fine of one
thousand dollars.

### 49.

Procedure regarding recovery of fines
Fines under this Act shall be incurred whether the letter is sent singly or with anything
else, or the incidental service is performed in respect of a letter either sent or to be sent
singly or together with some other letter or thing; and in any proceedings for the recovery
of any such fine the burden shall rest upon the person that the act in respect of which the
fine is alleged to have been incurred was done in conformity with this Act.

### 50.

Reward to informer
The Board may, with the approval of the Minister, award to an informer any part of a
penalty recovered under the Act or regulations, not exceeding one half thereof, if the
information given was material to the conviction.

Procedure of the Board of the Corporation
[Section 8(7).]

1\. Meetings generally
(1) The Chairperson shall preside over all meetings of the Board and on the absence
of the Chairperson the Deputy Chairperson shall preside.
(2) The Board shall meet at such times as may be necessary or expedient for the
transaction of business, and such meetings shall be held at such place and time and on
such days as the Chairperson determines.
(3) The Chairperson shall not permit more than two months to elapse between
meetings.
(4) Where three or more members of the Board by written notice signed by them and
addressed to the Chairperson request a meeting of the Board be held for the purpose
specified in such notice, the Chairperson shall, within seven days of the receipt of such
notice, convene such a meeting.
(5) Without prejudice to anything contained in subparagraph (4), the Chairperson
shall convene a meeting of the Board if requested to do so by the Minister.
2\. Quorum
(1) The quorum necessary for the transaction of the business of the Board shall be
five.
(2) For the purpose of determine whether there is a quorum a person who attends a
meeting shall be treated as being present notwithstanding that, under paragraph 3 he or
she is disqualified from voting at that meeting.
3\. Withdrawal from meeting
Where the Chairperson or another member has, directly or indirectly, any pecuniary or
personal interest in a contract or proposed contract or another matter and is present at a
meeting of the Board at which such a contract or matter is the subject of consideration, he
or she shall at the meeting and as soon as practicable after the commencement thereof
disclose the fact, and shall withdraw from the meeting until such time as the contract or
matter has been discussed and a vote taken thereon.
4\. Decisions of the Board
A decision of the Board with regard to any question shall be determined by a majority
of all the members present at that meeting; but in any case where the votes of the
members are equally divided, the Chairperson presiding at the meeting shall have a
casting vote in addition to his or her own vote.
5\. Minutes
The minutes of the proceedings of each meeting of the Board shall be kept in such
manner as the Board determines and the minutes of any meeting must be confirmed at the
next meeting of the Board.
6\. Remuneration of members of the Board
The Chairperson and members of the Board shall be paid such fees and allowances out
of the funds of the Corporation as the Minister from time to determines.
7\. Power to regulate
Subject to this Schedule, the Board may regulate its own proceedings.

GRENADA POSTAL SERVICE RULES
[Section 17.]

[1st December, 1936.]
1\. Citation
These Rules may be cited as the Grenada Postal Service Rules.
2\. Hours of business

(1) Subject to any special arrangements which may be made by the PostmasterGeneral, post offices throughout the State shall be open for business on ordinary week
days from 8.45 a.m. to 3.30 p.m. post offices shall not be open for business on Sundays
and public holidays.
(2) The General Post Office, St. George’s and all District Post Offices shall be closed
at noon every Saturday. The District Offices shall open for the receipt of letters by mail
bus up to 5 p.m.
(3) If mails from the United Kingdom, Canada or the United States of America are
received during the afternoon on any working day, the General Post Office, St. George’s,
shall remain open for the sorting of letters into private letter boxes, but not later than
6 p.m.
(4) If mails as specified above are received not later than 9 p.m. on the day preceding
a bank or public holiday, or on a Saturday when the following Monday is a holiday, the
mails shall be opened the same evening and sorted into private letter boxes:
Provided that in no case shall the post office remain open later than 11 p.m.
(5) During such exceptional opening no postal business shall be transacted, other than
the delivery of letters.
(6) Money order business shall be transacted only between the hours of 8.45 a.m. and
3.30 p.m. Where mails are closed before 3.30 p.m. on any day, the issue of money orders
on particular countries shall cease half an hour before the relevant mails are closed. This
restriction shall not apply to postal orders.
3\. City deliveries: St. George’s
There shall be a house to house delivery of letters three times daily on ordinary
working days, namely, at 8.30 a.m., 10.30 a.m. and 2.30 p.m., within the limits of the city
of St. George’s, the postal boundaries of which shall be as follows—
(a)

the northern boundary shall commence at the Tempe Mt. Gay bridge and
follow the left bank of the Tempe River to its junction with the St. John’s
River, and thence follow the left bank of the St. John’s River to its junction
with the sea;

(b)

the eastern boundary shall commence at the Tempe Mt. Gay bridge and
continue in an easterly direction along the Tempe stream to the Tempe
bridge on the Tempe Second Class Road, thence along the said road crossing
the St. David’s Main Road and connecting up with the Lowther’s Second
Class Road to the St. George’s Lower Main Road on the southern entrance
to the Botanical Gardens, thence to the sea at its nearest point;

(c)

the southern boundary shall be the sea;

(d)

the western boundary shall be the sea in a northerly direction along the
seashore to its junction with the Northern boundary at the mouth of the St.
John’s River.

4\. District deliveries
(1) District Postmasters will take all steps in their power to ensure the speedy delivery
of letters to the persons entitled to the same.
(2) Letters which remain unclaimed for one month shall be returned to the General
Post Office, St. George’s for disposal.
5\. Inland mails

(1) Every inland mail shall be accompanied by a letter bill which shall bear a serial
number commencing with the number 1 on the first of January in each year. There shall
be a separate series in each dispatching office for each office of destination.
(2) Letter bills shall be stamped with the post marks of the post office of origin and of
the office of destination, and shall contain the following information, namely, the date
and hour of dispatch, the mode of conveyance, a list of registered letters in the mail, total
due for taxed letters in the mail and such additional information as may be prescribed
from time to time. The bills shall be signed by the dispatching officer and the receiving
officer.
(3) A way-bill shall be delivered with the mails to the person conveying the mails,
and on the conclusion of the journey the way bill shall be returned to the office of origin.
(4) All persons receiving bags of mails shall give a receipt for the same on the
appropriate way-bill.
6\. External mails
The hour for closing mails shall be fixed by the Postmaster-General.
7\. Late-fee letters
(1) When circumstances permit, the Postmaster-General may extend the hours for
closing the mails exclusively for letters posted in the special late-fee box at the General
Post Office, St. George’s. Such letters must be fully prepaid, and in addition, must bear
an extra five cents stamp, and letters not so stamped will not be forwarded.
(2) In these cases, but at no other times, the late-fee letter box will be open.
(3) Registered letters should not be posted in the late-fee box and any letter marked
“registered” thus improperly posted will not be forwarded in the late mails.
8\. Waybills
The Postmaster-General shall furnish the master, or his or her agent, of every ship and
the driver of every bus by which mails are conveyed with a way-bill specifying the
numbers of bags of mails and their destinations and shall obtain a receipt from such
master or agent.
9\. Registration
(1) If it is proved to his or her satisfaction that a letter or packet duly admitted to
registration has been entirely lost whilst in his or her custody, the Postmaster-General
may, subject to the conditions set out below, pay to the sender compensation not
exceeding the sum of ten dollars.
(2) No compensation will be paid in respect of—
(a)

any packet which has been lost through a cause beyond control (e.g.,
tempest, shipwreck, earthquake or war);

(b)

any packet which the post cannot account for in consequence of the
destruction of official documents through a cause beyond control;

(c)

any packet containing a prohibited article;

(d)

any packet in respect of which a claim is not made within a year of the date
of posting.

10\. Chargeable packets

Any postal packet which in the opinion of the Postmaster-General contains any
dutiable articles, or on which the postage is insufficiently prepaid, and all small packets
and external parcels shall be withheld from delivery until the necessary charges have been
paid.
11\. Notices
A notice shall be addressed as soon as possible after receipt of the mails to the
addressee of any postal packet withheld from delivery, and of any registered letter or
external parcel.
12\. Air mails
(1) Letters received by air mail are to be treated with the utmost dispatch. Where
immediate delivery is not practicable the addressee should be notified by telephone if
possible.
(2) Letters for dispatch by air mail may be posted in an ordinary letter box, but
senders are recommended to hand such letters to a counter officer, especially if they are
posted near the time of dispatch.
13\. Money order correspondence
Money orders issued at any Money Order Office other than St. George’s shall be
advised to the General Post Office, St. George’s, and no correspondence or
communications of any description regarding the money order service shall be conducted
by District Postmasters save with the Postmaster-General at the General Post Office.
14\. Accounts
The Postmaster-General shall render promptly to the Postal Administrations or
departments concerned all accounts, returns and statements due to be rendered, and the
several District Postmasters shall keep such accounts and shall render such returns as the
Postmaster-General may require.
15\. Statistics
(1) Once in each year from the first to the twenty-eighth day of July inclusive or at
such other periods as may be prescribed a strict account shall be kept for statistical
purposes of the number dispatched in the inland mails of each group of postal packets, as
follows—
(a)

letters;

(b)

postcards;

(c)

printed papers, commercial papers and samples;

(d)

small packets;

(e)

blind literature;

(f)

external parcels;

(g)

inland parcels.

Registered articles are to be included in the group to which they belong.
(2) During these statistical periods the necessary particulars shall be entered on the
letter bills, the number of postal packets (other than external parcels) will be multiplied
by 13, and the resulting figures will be regarded as the approximate number of postal
packets dispatched during the year for the purpose of the Blue Book and other returns.

(3) Similar records will be kept in the General Post Office St. George’s, in respect of
external mails:
Provided that in the case of external parcels the precise numbers are to be recorded
and entered in the returns.
(4) Where statistics are recorded in any year in accordance with the International
Postal Convention, it will not be necessary to observe a special statistical period for local
purposes in respect of external mails, and the statistics recorded for International purposes
will be used for the purposes of the Blue Book and other official returns.
16\. Definition
In these Rules, unless the context requires otherwise the term “letters” includes every
kind of postal packet except parcels.

POST OFFICE (CASH ON DELIVERY SERVICE) REGULATIONS
[Section 18.]

[1st December, 1936.]
1\. Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Post Office (Cash on Delivery Service)
Regulations.
ARTICLE 1
Parcels subject to the collection of trade charges can be exchanged between Grenada
and the United Kingdom.
ARTICLE 2
(1) The maximum amount of trade charge in the case of parcels for delivery in
Grenada is fixed at forty pounds per parcel, and in the case of parcels for delivery in the
United Kingdom, at forty pounds per parcel.
(2) In calculating the amount of trade charge, fractions of a penny are ignored.
ARTICLE 3
(1) Each Administration fixes the special fee to be levied from the sender of a parcel
subject to a trade charge. This fee must not exceed—
(a)

five cents for each five dollars or fraction of five dollars to be collected, in
the case of parcels posted in Grenada;

(b)

five pence for each one sterling pound or fraction of one sterling pound to be
collected, in the case of parcels posted in the United Kingdom.

(2) Each Administration shall communicate to the other the special fee fixed in its
service by virtue of the preceding paragraph, as well as any alteration subsequently made
in it.
(3) This fee is shared between the Administration of the country of origin and that of
the country of destination in the manner prescribed in the Detailed Regulations.
ARTICLE 4
(1) In addition to the special fee fixed by the preceding Article, the post office of the
country in which a parcel subject to a trade charge is posted may collect a posting fee
from the sender, and the post office of the country of destination of a parcel subject to a
trade charge may collect a delivery fee from the addressee.
(2) The posting fee must not exceed—
(a)

in the case of a parcel posted in Grenada, five cents per parcel;

(b)

in the case of a parcel posted in the United Kingdom, five pence per parcel.

(3) The delivery fee must not exceed—
(a)

in the case of a parcel delivered in Grenada, ten cents per parcel;

(b)

in the case of a parcel delivered in the United Kingdom, five pence per
parcel.

(4) Each Administration shall communicate to the other the special fees fixed in its
service by virtue of this Article, as well as any alterations subsequently made in them.
(5) These fees shall be retained by the office which collects them.
ARTICLE 5
The amounts of trade charges collected are liquidated by means of trade charge money
orders which are issued free of all charges. The amount of a trade charge money order
which cannot be delivered remains at the disposal of the Administration of the country of
origin of the parcel on which the trade charge is payable.
ARTICLE 6
In principle the amounts of trade charges should be paid in by the addressee of the
parcel and paid out to the sender in gold; but each Administration shall have power to
receive and employ for this purpose any other money legally current in its country,
provided that due allowance is made for any difference in the exchange value.
ARTICLE 7
(1) The loss of a parcel subject to a trade charge entails responsibility upon the Postal
Service in the conditions fixed by the regulations for the exchange of parcels by parcel
post between Grenada and the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland.
(2) After the delivery of the parcel the post office of the country of destination is
responsible for the amount of the trade charge, unless it can be proved that the parcel and
the relative dispatch note (if one is used) did not, when transmitted to its service, bear the
indications prescribed in the Detailed Regulations for parcels subject to trade charges.
Nevertheless, an application as to the disposal of the amount of a trade charge is only
entertained if made within a year of the posting of the parcel.
ARTICLE 8
The sender of a parcel posted under this arrangement may not have the amount of the
trade charge cancelled or reduced after posting.
ARTICLE 9

The two Administrations undertake to admit parcels subject to trade charges in transit
through their services. Nevertheless, the accounts relative to the trade charges collected
will be drawn up and settled directly between the post offices of origin and destination of
the parcels subject to the trade charges.
ARTICLE 10
The two Administrations are authorised to fix by common consent the measures of
detail necessary for ensuring the performance of the present rules and to modify them
from time to time in accordance with the needs of the service.
ARTICLE 11
These Regulations shall come into force on the date to be agreed upon between the
two Administrations. They will remain in force until the expiry of one year from the date
on which either Administration shall give notice to the other of its intention to terminate
them.

ARTICLE I
1\. On parcels subject to trade charges, and on the dispatch notes, if any, relating to
them, the word “Remboursement” must be written or printed boldly besides the address,
and after it, the amount of the trade charge must be shown, in Latin characters, in the
money of the country of origin, without erasure or correction, even if certified.
2\. Each parcel subject to a trade charge must be accompanied by a separate dispatch
note, in services in which dispatch notes are used.
3\. Each parcel subject to a trade charge and the relative dispatch note, if one is used,
must bear a red label with the word “Remboursement” in Latin characters.
4\. Each parcel subject to a trade charge posted under this arrangement must bear on
the cover and on the back of the relative dispatch note if one is used, a request signed by
the sender that, if the parcel is undeliverable at the expiration of fifteen days from its
arrival at the place of destination, it is to be—
(a)

abandoned; or

(b)

returned to him or her at his or her expense.
ARTICLE II

Each parcel subject to a trade charge is entered on the parcel bill with a note of the
amount of the trade charge.
ARTICLE III

---

### 1.

Every parcel subject to a trade charge is accompanied by a trade charge money
order in conformity with or analogous to the Specimen B annexed to the present rules.
This money order, which is attached to the dispatch note, or to the parcel bill when no
dispatch note is used, should bear a statement of amount of the trade charge in the
currency of the country of origin, and should show, as a general rule, the sender of the
parcel as payee of the order. It is free to each Administration however, to have the orders
relating to the parcels originating in its service addressed to the offices of origin of the
parcels or to other offices.

### 2.

Entries in pencil will not be allowed on trade charge money orders.
ARTICLE IV

Except by contrary agreement between the Administrations of origin and of
destination, the amounts of the trade charge Orders are converted into the currency of the
country of destination by the Post Office of that country, which uses for this purpose the
same rate of conversion as it uses for the coversion of ordinary Money Orders drawn on
the country of origin of the parcels.
ARTICLE V
1\. Immediately after collecting the trade charge, the office of destination, or any
other office designated by the Administration of the country of destination fills in the part
“Indications de Service” of the trade charge order, and, after impressing it with its date
stamp, returns it free of postage to the country of origin in the manner prescribed by
Article XXIV, section 1 of the Detailed Regulations for the execution of the Principal
Convention of the Postal Union, or any Article which may be substituted therefor.
2\. Trade charge orders are paid under the conditions determined by each
Administration with a view to ensure the payment of the amounts of the trade charges to
the senders of the parcels.
ARTICLE VI
1\. Parcels subject to the collection of trade charges can be redirected if the new
country of destination maintains with that of origin an exchange of trade charge parcels.
The parcels are, in case of redirection, accompanied by the trade charge orders made out
by the service of origin. The new office of destination acts in the settlement of the trade
charge as if the parcel had been directly consigned to it.
2\. In the case of an application for redirection to a country which does not maintain
with that of origin an exchange of trade charge parcels, the parcel is treated as one which
cannot be delivered.
ARTICLE VII
1\. Trade charge orders relating to parcels which, for any reason whatever, are
returned to origin should be cancelled by the office which returns the parcels and should
be annexed to the dispatch notes, or, if no dispatch notes are used, to the bill on which the
parcels are advised.
2\. Trade charge orders mislaid, lost, or destroyed before the collection of the trade
charge, are replaced, without formalities, by new orders bearing the heading “Duplicate”.
The request for a duplicate is addressed directly to the office of origin of the parcel.

### 3.

Trade charge orders mislaid, lost or destroyed after the collection of the trade
charge are likewise replaced by duplicates, or by authorities to pay, after proof by the two
post offices that the orders have not been paid or refunded.
ARTICLE VIII
1\. Trade charge orders which it has not been possible to deliver to the payees within
the period of validity fixed by the Agreement concluded between the two Administrations
for the exchange of money orders are, at the expiration of the period of validity, receipted
by the office of the country of payment, and are claimed from the office which issued
them.
2\. Trade charge orders which have been delivered to the payees and of which the
payees have not claimed payment within the period of validity fixed by the rules for the
transaction of money order business between the two Administrations are replaced by
authorities to pay. These authorities to pay are drawn up by the office which issued the
orders, as soon as it has been able to ascertain that the originals have not been paid within
the period of validity. They are receipted by the post office of destination and claimed by
it in the first account rendered after their receipt.

ARTICLE IX
1\. Trade charge money orders of which payment cannot be effected for one of the
following reasons—
(i) incorrect, insufficient or doubtful description of the name or residence of the
payees,
(ii) differences or omissions of names or amounts,
(iii) erasures of, or additions to, the entries,
(iv) omission of stamps, signatures or other service indications,
(v) use of irregular forms,
shall be corrected by the post office which has issued them.
2\. For this purpose, the orders shall be returned as soon as possible, officially
registered, to the office of origin by the office of destination.
ARTICLE X
1\. The settlement relative to trade charges paid by each post office on behalf of the
other post office is effected by means of trade charge accounts (Specimen A annexed) the
final totals of which are carried to the General Account between Grenada and the United
Kingdom, under the headings “Trade Charge Money Orders”.
2\. In the trade charge accounts, which are accompanied by the paid and receipted
trade charge orders, the orders are entered in alphabetical sequence of the offices of issue
and in numerical sequence of their issue at that office. At the end of the account the office
which has drawn it up deducts from the total sum of its credit a half per cent.,
representing the share of the other office in the trade charge fee.
3\. The verification of the trade charge accounts is effected according to the rules for
the transaction of money order business between the two post offices.

POST OFFICE (INSURED BOXES TO THE UNITED KINGDOM)
REGULATIONS
[Section 17.]

[1st December, 1936.]
1\. Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Post Office (Insured Boxes to the United
Kingdom) Regulations.
2\. Insured boxes
Articles of gold and silver, precious stones, jewellery and other articles of a like
nature, which are prohibited from transmission in insured letters, may be sent from
Grenada as “insured boxes” to the United Kingdom and to any other place with which the
United Kingdom has a similar service.
3\. Prohibited articles

The under-mentioned classes of articles are prohibited from transmission in insured
boxes for any destination—
Articles the admission or circulation of which in the country of destination is
prohibited, letters or notes which may serve as a substitute for
correspondence, current coin, bank notes or securities payable to bearer,
bonds and articles included in the category of commercial papers. It shall,
however, be permissible to insert in an insured box an open invoice confined
to the particulars which constitute an invoice, and also a simple copy of the
addressee’s name and address, and the name and address of the sender.
4\. Return of prohibited article
A box containing any prohibited article is stopped and returned to the sender.
5\. Weight and size of boxes
An insured box must not exceed 2 lbs. in weight and must not measure more than 12
inches in length, 4 inches in breadth, and 4 inches in height. The box employed for
packing must be strongly made of metal or wood, and when a wooden box is used the
wood must be at least one-third of an inch thick.
6\. Sealing of insured box
An insured box must be tied crosswise with a single strong string (not two or more
pieces joined), the two ends of the string being held together under a seal of fine wax
bearing a private mark. The box must also be sealed on the four sides with seals identical
with that used for the ends of the string. All the seals must be of the same kind of wax,
and must bear distinct impressions of the same private device. A coin may not be used for
sealing; and the device may not consist merely of straight, crossed, or curved lines which
could readily be imitated. The box must not be wrapped in paper, but the surfaces of the
top and bottom must be covered with white paper for the inscription of the addressee’s
name and address and the insured value of the contents and for the impression of the
official stamps.
7\. Customs declaration
(1) An insured box is subject to the Customs Regulations and laws in force in the
country of destination, and must be accompanied by a customs declaration prepared by
the sender. Customs declarations of the non-adhesive form used for parcels should be
used for boxes posted in Grenada; but the heading of the first column of the form should
be altered to “Insured Boxes-boites avec valeur déclarée” when the form is used for an
insured box.
(2) The number of customs declaration forms which must accompany an insured box
varies according to the requirements of the country of destination and is indicated in the
relative table in the British Postal Guide. The customs declaration must be prepared with
great care, and must specify precisely the style of packing and include a description of the
box, its gross weight and the total value of the contents. The new weight and value of
each of the different kinds of article contained in the box must also be shown separately.
8\. Erasures or corrections
An insured box must not bear any erasure or correction in the address at the time of
posting.
9\. Refusal to insure

If a box tendered for insurance does not, in the opinion of the officer of the post office
to whom it is tendered, fulfil the foregoing conditions as to packing and sealing, it is his
or her duty to refuse to insure it. Nevertheless, the onus of properly enclosing, packing
and sealing the box lies upon the sender; and the post office assumes no liability for loss
arising from defects which may not be observed at the time of posting.
10\. Amount of insurance
The amount for which a box is insured must be written by the sender both in words
and in figures, at the top of the address side of the cover, thus—
“Insured for seventy-five dollars ($75.00)”. No alteration or erasure of the inscription
is allowed.
11\. Insurance fee
The insurance fee must be prepaid in addition to the full postage by means of postage
stamps which the sender must affix to the cover. As few stamps as possible must be used
to prepay the postage and insurance fees. They must not be folded over the edge of the
cover, and when more stamps than one are used, they must be affixed with spaces
between them. No box will be accepted for transmission unless both postage and
insurance fees are fully prepaid.
12\. Regulations
Such regulations relating to insured boxes as are now or hereafter shall be in force in
the United Kingdom, except where inapplicable or at variance with the foregoing or any
other local regulations hereafter in force, shall also be taken as governing the
transmission of insured boxes to and from this State.

POST OFFICE (PARCELS INSURANCE WITH THE UNITED STATES OF
AMERICA) REGULATIONS
[Section 17.]

[15th April, 1936.]
1\. Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Post Office (Parcels Insurance with the United
States of America) Regulations.
2\. Insurance fees
Parcels intended for transmission to the United States of America may be insured
against loss, rifling, or damage on payment of an insurance fee in addition to the postage,
and other prepaid charges at the following rates—
To secure compensation not exceeding—
$100.00 ............................. 20 cents
$200.00 ............................. 40 cents
$250.00 ............................. 50 cents
3\. Limits on insurance
No parcel will be insured for a sum exceeding two hundred and fifty dollars, nor will
an insured person be indemnified for an amount above the real value of the contents of
the insured parcel.
4\. Coin, bullion, etc.
Every parcel containing coin, bullion, jewellery or other precious article must be
insured. Every parcel containing jewellery or any other precious article exceeding five

hundred dollars in value must be packed in a box measuring not less than 2 feet 6 inches
in length and girth combined.
5\. Sealing of insured parcels
Insured parcels must be closed and securely sealed with wax, lead, or otherwise, but
the country of destination shall have the right to open them (including the right to break
the seals) in order to inspect the contents. All the seals must be of the same kind of wax
or lead and must bear distinct impressions of the same private device. An insured parcel
must not be addressed to initials or in pencil.
6\. Amount of insurance
Each insured parcel must bear on the outside a statement of the amount of the insured
value expressed in United States currency.
7\. Stamping of insured parcels
Each insured parcel must be marked or labelled or stamped “Insured” in a conspicuous
manner on the address side, and in close proximity to such endorsement there must
appear the insurance number given the parcel. The customs declaration, if not gummed to
the parcel, must also be marked or labelled or stamped “Insured”.
8\. Labels, etc.
The labels or stamps on insured parcels must be so placed that they cannot serve to
conceal injuries to the covers. They must not be folded over two sides of the cover so as
to hide the edges.
9\. Delivery advice
The sender of an insured parcel may obtain an advice of delivery upon payment of an
additional fee of ten cents. When an advice of delivery is desired, the sender or the office
of posting shall write or stamp on the parcel in a conspicuous manner the words “Return
receipt requested”, “Advice of delivery requested”, or boldly, the letters “A.R.” A fee of
ten cents will also be charged on a request for information as to the disposal of an insured
parcel if the sender has not already paid the special fee to obtain an advice of delivery.
10\. Application for indemnity
No application for indemnity will be entertained unless a claim or an initial inquiry,
oral or written, shall be filed by the claimant or his or her representative within a year
commencing with the day following the posting of the insured parcel.
11\. Compensation
No compensation will be paid in respect of—
(a)

any parcel containing matter prohibited transmission in the parcel post mails;

(b)

any damage, rifling, or loss caused by the fault or negligence of the sender,
or the addressee or the representative of either;

(c)

any parcel which contains matter of no intrinsic value or exceptionally
fragile or perishable matter;

(d)

any parcel seized by the customs for false declaration of contents;

(e)

any parcel not made up in the prescribed manner.

Nor will compensation be paid for indirect loss, injury or damage or for loss or
damage arising from causes beyond control. The insurance fees are not in any case
returned.

POST OFFICE (PRIVATE LETTER BOXES) REGULATIONS
[Section 17. Amended by SRO 17 of 1990.]

[9th December, 1950.]

---

### 1.

Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Post Office (Private Letter Boxes) Regulations.

### 2.

Service
Post office lock boxes may be rented at annual rates payable in advance for the
convenience of the public in the delivery of mail.

### 3.

Charges
The charges for private letter boxes shall be at the rates hereinafter set out, payable in
advance—
(a)

Keys.—A box-holder who loses his or her key shall pay to the cashier at the
General Post Office where that box is located the sum of three dollars before
a new key will be assigned to him or her.

(b)

Rental.—The annual charges are as follows—
(i) for a large box ...................... $150.00
(ii) for a small box ..................... $100.00

(c)

Period.—The rental of a private letter box may commence at any period of
the year, but shall be paid in advance in all cases to thirty-first December,
next ensuing.

### 4.

Conditions for use
The use of a private letter box is restricted to one individual, family, firm or
corporation—
(i) Individuals: An individual renting a box may have placed therein—
(a)

mail addressed to himself or herself;

(b)

mail addressed to members of his or her family;

(c)

mail addressed to his or her servants or other employees who reside in
his or her house;

(d)

mail addressed to a relative or other persons who permanently reside
in his or her house as do the other members of his or her family;

(e)

mail directed to a visitor temporarily residing with him or her;

(f)

mail addressed to his or her care or the number of his or her box by
persons who wish him or her to take care of such mail for them for not
more than thirty days.

(ii) Firms: A firm renting a private letter box may have placed therein—
(a)

mail addressed to the name of the firm;

(b)

mail addressed to any of its officials and office employees;

(c)

by the written consent of the firm; any member thereof may have mail
addressed to himself or herself or members of his or her family
deposited in the box assigned to the firm.

(iii) Corporation: Government Department: A corporation or Government
Department renting a box may have placed therein—
(a)

mail addressed to its name;

(b)

mail addressed to any of its officials and office employees;

(c)

mail addressed to students at an educational institution;

(d)

mail matter addressed to the inmates of any public institution, when
the rules of such institution permit of such a course.

(iv) Society or Association: A society or association may rent a box, but a box so
rented shall not be used by individual members of such society or
association other than the officers thereof when addressed by their official
title.

### 5.

Applications
(1) Persons desiring private letter boxes shall apply in writing to the PostmasterGeneral and shall furnish particulars of requirements together with information relative to
the mail to be delivered through the box under regulation 4 of these Regulations.
(2) Upon receipt of such application, the Postmaster-General shall allot the available
boxes according to priority of application.
(3) Boxes shall not be assigned to the use of any person until the application has been
approved and the appropriate rent paid in connection therewith.

### 6.

Misdelivery
Correspondence erroneously delivered or, through faulty address, accidentally stored
into the wrong private letter box, shall immediately be returned to the post office by the
person receiving same so delivered as aforesaid. Should this not be done, or where there
is proof of serious negligence on the part of the box holder, or the possessor of the key

whereby the correspondence is kept back or delayed, the Postmaster-General may give
notice in writing to the box holder that the use of the box shall forthwith be discontinued.

### 7.

Injury or damage to private letter box
Any injury or damage to a private letter box arising out of the wilful act of the renter
or his or her agent shall be made good by the Postmaster-General at the expense of the
renter who shall on demand repay to the Government the cost thereof.

### 8.

Access to boxes
Box holders shall have access to their private letter boxes at the various post offices
daily, including Sundays and public holidays, from 7 a.m. to 8 p.m.

### 9.

General rules
(1) No liability will be accepted by the post office in respect of any postal article
which may be delivered under the arrangements for private letter boxes, to a person not
entitled to receive the same. Correspondence will, however, only be handed over at a post
office counter on production of a check slip issued by the box holder in respect of each
application for counter delivery.
(2) The post office reserves the right to suspend the private letter box service when
postal deliveries are suspended generally.
(3) Managers of firms, corporations, hotels, schools, institutions, departments and
clubs, shall not hold unclaimed mail addressed to their care for a longer period than ten
days, except at the request of the person addressed, and shall redirect the same for
forwarding if the then address is known; otherwise such mail should be returned to the
post office for proper disposal.

### 6.

Rates of postage and supplemental services
(1) The prepaid rates of postage on postal packets originating in the State of Grenada
and the charges on insured letters, boxes and parcels shall be as set forth in the First
Schedule to these Regulations.
(2) The rates of miscellaneous postal services shall be as set forth in the Second
Schedule to these Regulations.

### 7.

Limits of weights and dimensions
The maximum weights and dimensions of letters and other articles admitted to the
Post shall be as set forth in the Third Schedule to these Regulations.

### 8.

Late letter fee
Postal packets for destinations outside the State of Grenada may be posted at the
General Post Office, St. George’s, after the advertised time of closing the mail, up till
such hour as the Postmaster-General may by Notice appoint, on payment of a special fee
of ten cents for each packet payable by means of postage stamps affixed to the postal
packet. Such packets must be handed in to an authorised post officer at the mails counter
for acceptance.

### 9.

Reply-paid post cards
(1) Reply-paid post cards shall not be issued in Grenada.
(2) The reply halves of reply-paid post cards issued by other Postal Administrations
shall be admitted to the post subject to the regulations of the Universal Postal Union.

### 10.

Reply coupons
(1) Reply coupons shall not be issued in Grenada.
(2) Reply coupons issued in other countries of the Universal Postal Union shall be
exchanged at any Money Order Office in Grenada for postage stamps valued as follows—
International Coupons .............................. 10 cents
British Commonwealth Coupons ............... 5 cents

### 11.

Insured postal articles
(1) Insured letters, boxes and parcels shall be exchanged only with Postal
Administrations participating in the service.
(2) The maximum insured value of insured postal articles in Grenada shall be two
hundred and fifty dollars.
(3) The charges upon insured postal articles shall be fully prepaid and shall consist of
the insurance fee and other charges specified in the First and Second Schedules to these
Regulations.
(4) Insured postal articles shall not be accepted for transmission through the post by
air mail services.
(5) The preparation of postal articles for insurance shall be in accordance with the
requirements of the post office as laid down in the Post Office Guide.
(6) If an article tendered for insurance does not, in the opinion of the post officer to
whom it is tendered, fulfil all insurance conditions as to packing and sealing, or customs
declaration, it shall be the duty of the post officer to refuse to insure it. The onus of
properly enclosing, packing and sealing the packet lies upon the sender; and the post
office assumes no liability for loss arising from defects which may not have been
observed at the time of posting.

### 12.

Parcel post
(1) The rates of postage for prepayment on parcels tendered and accepted into the
post at Grenada are as set out in the Fourth Schedule to these Regulations.

(2) The prepaid rates of postage on parcels shall not exceed one dollar for each pound
weight whether expressed by the pound or by scales not exceeding three, seven, eleven,
and twenty-two pounds, or otherwise (for example, in the case of Canada 20 lbs.).
(3) There shall be charged on each parcel received from abroad listed as parcel post,
and on each postal article the contents of which are liable to import duty a parcel
clearance fee of fifteen cents.
(4) There shall be charged on each parcel received from abroad listed as parcel post,
and on each postal article the contents of which are liable to import duty, a storage fee as
set out in the Second Schedule.

### 13.

C.O.D. parcels
(1) Parcels subject to the collection of trade charges may be exchanged with other
countries subject to such arrangements or agreements as may be made with the Postal
Administration concerned.
(2) The maximum amount of the trade charge in respect of a parcel shall not exceed
two hundred dollars.
(3) The fees to be charged in respect of parcels subject to trade charges shall be as set
out in the Fifth Schedule.

### 14.

Parcels for free delivery
(1) Parcels to be delivered free of customs and other charges may be exchanged with
other countries subject to such agreements or arrangements as may be in force with the
countries concerned.
(2) The fee to be paid by the sender in respect of each such parcel shall be the amount
set out in the Second Schedule to these Regulations.
(3) The minimum rate of deposit to be paid by the sender at the time of posting shall
be twenty-five cents for every one dollar or fraction thereof of the declared value of the
parcel subject to a minimum of fifty cents:
Provided that the Postmaster-General at his or her discretion may demand the
payment of a greater amount having regard to the approximate customs charges payable
in the country of destination (if known):
And provided further that the Postmaster-General shall be under no liability in
respect of the parcel in the event of the deposit being inadequate for the purpose.

### 15.

Registration
(1) Any postal packet may be registered except a magazine post packet for Canada
(including Newfoundland) and a parcel.
(2) The registration fee must be prepaid in addition to the postage.
(3) Articles for registration must comply with the detailed regulations as laid down in
the Post Office Guide.
(4) The sender of a registered article addressed abroad may obtain an advice of its
delivery on application at the office of posting on payment of the relative fee set out in
the Second Schedule.
(5) The fee must be paid in stamps affixed to the article by the sender and cancelled
with the Post Office date stamp.
(6) The article for which the sender applies for an Advice of Delivery must be marked
with the inscription “Avis de Reception” or the letters “A.R.”.

### 16.

Compensation
(1) If any uninsured parcel while under the control of the Grenada Post Office be lost
or damaged, the maximum compensation payable in respect thereof shall be the
prevailing equivalent in local currency of the Gold Franc as follows—
Parcel not exceeding 3 lbs in weight ........................................................................... 10 gold francs
Parcel not exceeding 7 lbs in weight ........................................................................... 15 gold francs
Parcel not exceeding 11 lbs in weight ......................................................................... 25 gold francs
Parcel exceeding 11 lbs and not exceeding 22 lbs (20 lbs in case of Canada) ............ 40 gold francs

(2) In no case shall more than the actual declared value of the parcel lost or of the
damage sustained be payable.
(3) In no case will compensation be given when such loss or damage arises from any
fault or neglect of the sender or from the nature of the contents.
(4) If any registered postal article while under the control of the Grenada Post Office
be lost or damaged the maximum compensation payable in respect thereof shall be
fourteen dollars in local currency.
(5) Compensation shall not be payable in the case of—
(a)

any packet which has been lost through a cause beyond control, generally
speaking where no question of negligence arises;

(b)

loss due to the act of the Queen’s enemies;

(c)

any postal article which the post office cannot account for in consequence of
the loss of official documents through a cause beyond control;

(d)

any packet containing a prohibited article;

(e)

any packet in respect of which an application is not made within a year of
the date of posting;

(f)

loss or damage of the contents of a registered packet as distinct from the loss
of the entire packet;

(g)

transfer from a British Post Office to another Postal Administration, unless
that Administration decides that compensation shall be paid;

(h)

force majeure, that is to say, tempest, shipwreck, earth-quake, war, or other
unavoidable occurrence.

### 17.

Air mail
(1) The postal zones, for the purposes of postage by air under these Regulations, are
contained in Part I of the Ninth Schedule and the rates for such postage are contained in

## Part II of the said Schedule.

(2) (a) The air mail fees on post cards shall apply only to the single post cards sold
by the post office and to private single post cards not exceeding in weight one-fourth of
an ounce each.
(b) Nothing may be affixed to post cards intended for transmission by air mail
except the necessary stamps and air mail labels. Any post card not complying with these
conditions shall be regarded as a letter and shall not be forwarded by air mail unless the
air mail fee applicable to a letter is fully prepaid.
(c) Post cards shall not be accepted for transmission a decouvert through an
intermediary administration which does not prescribe a special rate for post cards unless
the same are prepaid at the letter rate.
(3) (a) The air mail fee, that is to say the amount over the ordinary postal charges in
the rates of postage prescribed for air mail correspondence must be fully prepaid.

(b) If the packet is insufficiently prepaid and the amount prepaid represents at least
the air mail fee, the packet shall be surcharged in the usual manner double the deficiency
in ordinary postage and forwarded by air mail.
(c) If the packet is partly prepaid with an amount less than the air mail fee, the
packet shall not be transmitted by air mail and will be forwarded in the ordinary mails
marked, if necessary, for surcharge in the usual manner.

### 19.

a.m., and 3.30 p.m., (Saturdays 12 noon). Where mails are closed before the closing hour

the relevant mails are closed. This restriction shall not apply to postal orders.
(2) Money orders shall be exchanged between Inland Post Offices and with other
countries according to such agreements or arrangements as may be made with the

### 20.

Postal orders
(1) British Postal Orders shall be issued and paid at money order offices or at such
post offices as the Cabinet may direct in accordance with such arrangements as may be
made with Her Majesty’s Postmaster-General.
(2) The denominations of postal orders to be issued in the State of Grenada shall be
prescribed by the Cabinet and notified in the Gazette. Pending the issue of any such
notification the issue shall be restricted to the following denominations, namely—
1/-; 1/6d; 2/-; 2/6d; 3/-; 4/-; 5/-; 7/6; 10/-; 15/-; 20/-; 21/-; 40/-.
(3) Postage stamps of the State not exceeding two in number, or 10 cents on orders up
to $1.00 and 25 cents on orders above $1.00 may be affixed to postal orders issued in the
State of Grenada in order to increase the paying value thereof.
(4) The Permanent Secretary (Finance) shall receive and keep all blank postal orders
supplied to the State of Grenada by the Postmaster-General of the United Kingdom and
shall from time to time issue to the Postmaster-General such supplies of postal orders as
he or she may require for sale to the public.

(5) The rates set forth in the Seventh Schedule to these Regulations shall be the rates
of poundage on British Postal Orders issued in the State of Grenada (including therein the
poundage payable by the Grenada Post Office to Her Majesty’s Postmaster-General).

### 21.

Power to suspend issues
In the event of sudden fluctuations in the banking rates of exchange which might
involve loss to the Postal Administration of Grenada in the settlement of postal orders or
money orders transactions with other countries the Permanent Secretary (Finance) shall
have power to instruct the Postmaster-General to suspend temporarily the issue of postal
orders or money orders on any country.

### 22.

Gratuities for sea carriage
(1) The gratuities payable to owners, masters or agents of ships conveying mails
(other than ships under contract or in respect of which a subsidy is paid or in respect of
which other arrangements exist for the conveyance of mails) shall be at the rates specified
in the Eighth Schedule to these Regulations.
(2) No gratuities shall be payable in respect of empty bags.
(3) All external mails will be delivered to or be received from owners, masters of
carriers or agents of carriers at the General Post Office, St. George’s, the Grenville Post
Office, or the Post Office at Hillsborough (Carriacou), as the case may be, and at no other
place in the State.

### 23.

Obsolete stamps
Prohibition of the postal use of obsolete stamps shall be prescribed by the Cabinet
from time to time notified in the Gazette.

### 24.

Definitions
In these Regulations, unless the context otherwise requires—
“letters” includes postcards and other articles when dispatched by letter post;
“other articles” means commercial papers, printed papers of every kind including
articles for the blind, samples of merchandise and small packets;
“parcels” means postal packets sent by parcel post;
“postal packet” includes letters and other articles as defined above.

POST OFFICE (TELEGRAPH MONEY ORDER SERVICE WITH THE UNITED
KINGDOM) REGULATIONS
[Section 17.]

[1st December, 1936.]

---

### 1.

Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Post Office (Telegraph Money Order Service
with the United Kingdom) Regulations.

### 2.

Maximum amount
Telegraph money orders for sums not exceeding the maximum amount allowed in the
case of ordinary money orders shall be exchanged between the United Kingdom and the
State of Grenada.

### 3.

Charges
The remitter of a telegraph money order shall be required to pay, in addition to the
ordinary money order commission the cost of the telegram of advice at the ordinary (or
deferred) rate per word from one country to the other, and also a supplementary fee of
twelve cents. The telegrams of advice shall be charged for at the deferred rate and shall be
subject to all the conditions governing the transmission of messages accepted at that rate,
unless the remitter prefers to telegraph at the full rate. A form of receipt (or certificate of
issue) showing the amount of the remittance and the charges, shall be given to the
remitter.

### 4.

Private messages
The remitter of a telegraph money order shall be allowed, on paying for the additional
words required, to add to the official telegram of advice any short communication in
English which he or she may wish to send to the payee, and may also, if he or she so
desires, prepay the cost of a telegraphic reply to such communication. The full rate per
word should be charged for the prepaid reply.

### 5.

Transmission of telegram of advice
(a)

All telegrams of advice for orders payable in the United Kingdom shall be
transmitted to the office of destination through the Central Telegraph Office
in London, and all telegrams of advice of orders payable in Grenada shall be
transmitted through the Exchange Office at St. George’s.
Form of Telegram of Advice

(b)

The telegrams of advice shall be prepared in accordance with the following
specimen—
(1)
L.C.O.
(4)
Joseph Allen

(6)
John Fuller Grand Hotel
(8)

(2)
Mandat 123

(3)
Birmingham
(5)
Seventeen pounds fifteen
shillings
(7)
Seventeen pounds (break
signal)

to pay your passage.
Note.—If the payee is a woman, the prefix Mrs. or Miss should appear before her name unless
the Christian name is given; but in all cases the remitter bears the consequences if the address of
the telegram of advice or that of the payee is incorrect or insufficient to insure payment to the
proper person.
(1) Supplementary instruction required for deferred telegrams only. This indication “L.C.O.”
should always begin the telegram. When a reply is prepaid, the indication “RP” should
appear between the letters “L.C.O.” and the word “Mandat”.
(2) Serial number of order inserted at Exchange Office in substitution for the Issuing Officer
Serial Number signalled from the office of issue. The exchange office series of numbers
should begin at 1 on the 1st January in each year.
(3) Office of payment.
(4) Name of remitter.
(5) Amount in sterling in words.
(6) Name and address of payee.
(7) Repetition of the number of pounds.
(8) Private message, if any, for the payee.
A registered telegraphic address may be used to indicate the payee’s address provided that his or
her name is also given. In such cases the symbol “c/o” should be inserted between the name of the
payee and the registered address, thus—”John Fuller c/o Ajax”.

(c)

If the office of payment is not a telegraph office, the name of the nearest
telegraph office should be written after the name of the office of payment. If
the sender is unable to indicate the nearest telegraph office, the telegram of
advice may be accepted at his or her risk provided that the name of the
country of destination is added after the name of the office of payment.

(d)

If a telegraph money order is intended to be called for at a post office, the
words “Poste Restante” must be written instead of an address after the
payee’s name. In the absence of these words it must be assumed that the
order is to be sent out for delivery.

(e)

If the remitter desires to receive an advice of payment the words “Advise
payment” should appear as the first words of the text of the telegram of
advice. In such cases the Exchange Office of the country of payment must
arrange for an advice of payment to be sent by post to the Exchange Office
of the country of issue for transmission to the remitter.

### 6.

Procedure on receipt of telegraph
On receipt of the inward telegram of advice in the Central Telegraph Office, London,
or at the Exchange Office at St. George’s, as the case may be, it must first be seen that the
serial number of the order advised is the next number in the sequence of the series proper
to the country of origin and the particulars must then be signalled under the same number
to the proper office of payment.

### 7.

Method of payment
On receipt of the telegram of advice at the office of payment a money order (or a
notice of the arrival of the order) must be prepared and delivered to the payee. Any
private message for the payee must be communicated to him or her at the same time
either on an ordinary telegraph form or on a detachable slip affixed to the telegraph
money order.

### 8.

International conditions to apply
The general international conditions for deferred telegrams, together with the
regulations of the International Telegraph Convention (Lisbon Revision) or any

regulations which may in future be substituted therefor, shall apply to the telegrams of
advice and other telegrams sent in connection with money orders, to the apportionment of
the telegraph charges and to the re-imbursement of such charges.

### 9.

Allowance to country of payment
The post office of the country of issue shall account to the post office of the country of
payment for the same percentage on the amount of telegraph money orders advised as in
the case of ordinary orders.

### 10.

Entry of particulars in Advice lists
The particulars of telegraph money orders, including the full addresses of the payees,
shall be entered separately at the end of the ordinary Advice list or on separate sheets
headed “Advised by telegraph”, and the amounts of such orders shall be included in the
total amounts of the lists. The exchange office serial number of each order as well as the
serial number of the order of the office of issue must be shown in the list.

### 11.

Telegrams to be compared with entries in lists
When the advice lists reach the Office of Exchange of the country of destination, the
telegrams of advice which have been received shall be carefully checked against the
relative entries in the lists. Any difference between the amounts stated in the lists and the
amounts in the telegrams of advice, or any other irregularities shall be reported to the
country of issue, by paid service telegram if necessary.

### 12.

Responsibility for errors, etc.
(1) In the case of errors or fictitious telegrams the responsibility for any losses
involved, other than the loss of telegraph charges, shall be borne by the Administration in
whose service the error or fraud was committed. In case it may be impossible to
determine in which service the error or fraud was committed, or in cases of fraud or error
in connection with the transmission of telegrams over the wires of intermediate countries
or cable companies, the responsibility for any losses involved, other than the loss of
telegram charges, shall be shared equally by the British and the Grenada Post Offices.
(2) In other respects telegraph money orders shall be subject to the same regulations
as ordinary orders.

POST OFFICE (UNDELIVERED POSTAL PACKETS) REGULATIONS
[Section 17.]

[1st December, 1936.]
1\. Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Post Office (Undelivered Postal Packets)
Regulations.
2\. Undelivered packets
All undelivered postal packets (letters and parcels), addressed or unaddressed, and all
articles found loose in any District Post Office, should be sent to the General Post Office,
St. George’s in order that they may be put in course for return to the owners, or retained
to meet inquiries respecting them, or disposed of as directed in these Regulations.
3\. Detention of undelivered packets
All letters, postcards, packets, newspapers and parcels originating in the State and
directed to any address in the State, if undelivered, shall be detained for a period of one
month and then sent to Head Office to be opened and returned to the writer if the sender’s
name and address cannot otherwise be obtained.
If from abroad, and not called for, at the end of the two months from the date of
receipt, all postal matter except parcels per parcel post should be returned to the Head
Office for return to the country of origin.
4\. Refused or abandoned packets
Parcels per parcel post that have been refused by the addressees, or that from any other
cause cannot be delivered, shall be dealt with in accordance with the provisions of the
Parcel Post Convention at the time existing between this State and the Country of origin.
Any such parcels as may be abandoned by the sender shall at the expiration of three
months from the date of its receipt at the post office, and after one month’s advertisement
in the Gazette, be sold, and the proceeds thereof shall be applied to the payment of the
duty payable thereon and all other charges, if any, and any surplus shall be paid to the
owner, if known, but if such owner cannot be found, the surplus shall be carried to the
Consolidated Fund, to abide the claim of the owner, if such claim is made good within a
year from the time of sale; and if such parcel does not contain goods of sufficient value to
pay the duty, then, after the month’s notice as aforesaid, the same shall be forfeited.
5\. Returned packets
Whenever any parcel originating in Grenada shall be returned to the State as
undeliverable, notice of such return shall be published in the Gazette, and if after one
month from the date of the first publication of such notice, no claim is made thereto and
the return postage and other charges thereon paid, then the same may be sold and the
proceeds applied in or towards the payment of the unpaid postage and the other charges
thereon.
6\. Unaddressed packets
Any letters of packets found in the post without any address shall, if the sender’s name
and address are unknown, be opened to enable delivery.
