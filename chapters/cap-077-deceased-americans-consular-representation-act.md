## CHAPTER 77

DECEASED AMERICANS CONSULAR REPRESENTATION ACT
An Act to give effect to Article III of a Convention between the United Kingdom and
the United States of America relative to the disposal of real and personal
property of United States citizens who die in Grenada.
[31st December, 1901.]

---

### 1.

Short title
This Act may be cited as the Deceased Americans Consular Representation Act.

### 2.

Notice of death to be given
Whenever a citizen of the United States of America dies in Grenada without having
therein any known heirs or testamentary executors, the Minister responsible for foreign
affairs shall forthwith cause a notice to that effect to be given to the nearest consular
officer of the United States of America.

### 3.

Powers and duties of the Consul or his delegate
(1) It shall be lawful for the said consular officer or a delegate appointed by him
under his hand and the seal of his office to take possession and have the custody of the
estate of the deceased person and to administer the same, but if any portion of the estate
of the deceased person consists of personal estate the consular officer shall immediately
apply for, and shall be entitled to obtain from a court, letters of administration of the
personal estate of the deceased, limited in such manner and until such time as the court
thinks fit.
(2) A consular officer shall not be required to give any security in relation to such
administration.

### 4.

Act extends to Puerto Rico
This Act extends to the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico as if it were part of the United
States of America.
