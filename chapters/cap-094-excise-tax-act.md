## CHAPTER 94

EXCISE TAX ACT
An Act to provide for the imposition and collection of excise tax on imported and
locally manufactured goods, as part of the programme of tax reform pursuant to
the introduction of Value Added Tax, and for related matters.
[Act No. 4 of 2010.]

[1st February, 2010.]

## PART I

Preliminary

---

### 1.

Short title and commencement
(1) This Act may be cited as the Excise Tax Act.
(2) Subject to subsection (3), this Act shall come into operation on the day to be
appointed by the Minister by Order published in the Gazette.
(3) To the extent necessary to give effect to the provisions of this Act relating to
Registration and Administration in order to prepare for the implementation of excise tax,

## Part V of this Act shall come into effect on the day on which the Act is assented to by the

Governor-General.

### 2.

Interpretation
(1) In this Act—

“applicant” means a person who makes an application under section 10;
“approved form” means a form approved, in writing, by the Comptroller or the
Comptroller of Customs;
“approved warehouse” means a place approved as a warehouse by the Comptroller
under section 11(1) for the purpose of manufacturing excisable goods;
“commencement date” means the date referred to in section 1(2);
“Common External Tariff” means the CARICOM Common External Tariff (SRO
37 of 1999), as set out in the Fifth Schedule of the Customs Act;
“Comptroller” has the meaning assigned to it under the Income Tax Act, No. 30 of
1994;
“Comptroller of Customs” has the meaning assigned to it under the Customs Act,
1960;
“consideration” has the meaning assigned to it under the VAT Act;
“entered”—
(a)

in relation to an import or export of goods, has the meaning assigned to it
under the Customs Act, Chapter ; and

(b)

in relation to a removal of goods manufactured in the State, means the
removal of the goods for home use from a warehouse;

“entered for export” means entered for export in accordance with the requirements
of the Customs Act;
“entered for home use” means—
(a)

in relation to excisable goods imported into the State—
(i) an entry of the goods for home use under the customs laws, or
(ii) if no such entry is made, an import of goods that are deemed, under
this Act or the Customs laws, to have been entered for home use; and

(b)

in relation to excisable goods manufactured in the State, the first removal,
other than a removal referred to in section 11(1)(b), of those goods from a
warehouse;

“excisable goods” means goods imported into, or manufactured in Grenada, which
are liable to excise tax as specified in the First Schedule;
“excise tax” means—
(a)

excise tax imposed under this Act; and

(b)

any amount required to be brought to account under this Act as excise tax, or
otherwise deemed under this Act to be excise tax;

“exempt goods” means goods that are not subject to excise tax as are specified in
the Second Schedule or otherwise under this Act;
“export” has the meaning given in the Customs Act, Chapter;
“fair market value” has the meaning assigned to it under the VAT Act, Chapter;
“home use” means consumption, use, or application in Grenada;
“import” has the meaning assigned to it under the Customs Act, Chapter;
“import duty” means a duty of customs charged on the import of goods under the
Customs Act, Chapter;
“manufacture” means make or produce excisable goods, and includes—

all processes, including intermediate or incomplete processes, undertaken in
making or producing the goods;

filtering, diluting, or blending excisable goods with other goods, (including
other excisable goods);

(c)

putting excisable goods, for the first time, into a container in which they may
be presented for sale, or from which they may be dispensed; and

(d)

labelling or marking, for the first time, containers filled with excisable
goods;

“manufacturer” includes a person who manufactures excisable goods;
“Minister” means the Minster responsible for finance;
“officer” means—
(a)

a person who is an officer for the purposes of the Customs laws, or a person
who is authorised by the Comptroller of Customs to carry out the functions
or duties of an officer under this Act; or

(b)

a person who is authorised by the Comptroller to carry out the functions or
duties of an officer under this Act;

“person” has the meaning assigned to it under the VAT Act, Chapter;
“premises” includes—
(a)

a place, whether or not that place is enclosed or built on; and

(b)

a structure, vehicle, vessel, or an aircraft;

“registered manufacturer” means a person who is declared under section 11 to be a
registered manufacturer;
“Regulations” means Regulations made under this Act;
“related persons” has the meaning assigned to it under the VAT Act, Chapter;
“removal” means in relation to excisable goods manufactured in the State, the
removal of those goods from a warehouse to another place in the State, including
another warehouse; and the terms “remove” and “removed” shall be construed
accordingly;
“repealed taxes” has the meaning assigned to it under the Value Added Tax
(Transitional Provisions) Act, 2007;
“sale” means an agreement or any other transaction, (including a barter
transaction), under which ownership of goods, or the right to dispose of goods as
owner, passes, or is intended to pass from one person to another for consideration;
“State” means the State of Grenada;
“tax fraction” has the meaning assigned to it under the VAT Act, Chapter;
“tax period” has the meaning assigned to it under the VAT Act, Chapter;
“VAT Act” means the Value Added Tax Act, 2009;
“warehouse” means a place for the depositing, keeping, manufacturing, or securing
of excisable goods.
(2) The classification and description of goods which bear heading numbers
designated in the Common External Tariff, as set out in the Fifth Schedule to the Customs
Act, 1960, are to be interpreted in accordance with the General Rules for Interpretation of
the Harmonised System set out in that Schedule.

## PART II

Imposition of Excise Tax

### 3.

Imposition of excise tax
(1) Subject to the provisions of this Act, excise tax shall be imposed at the rates
specified in the First Schedule on—

excisable goods manufactured in the State; and

(b)

excisable goods imported into the State.

(2) The excise tax imposed under subsection (1)(a) becomes payable at the time when
the goods are entered for home use, and shall be paid to the Comptroller, at the time and
in the manner specified in section 17, by the manufacturer from whose warehouse the
goods are removed.
(3) The excise tax imposed under subsection (1)(b) shall be paid to the Comptroller of
Customs, at the time and in the manner specified in section 17, by the person who enters,
or is required to enter the goods for home use.
(4) All excisable goods manufactured in the State shall be deemed to be goods
manufactured for sale, unless and until the contrary is proved.
(5) All excisable goods found in a warehouse, shall be deemed to have been
manufactured in such warehouse.

### 4.

Deemed entry of excisable goods
(1) If a registered manufacturer cannot, to the satisfaction of the Comptroller, account
for a quantity of excisable goods manufactured by him during a tax period, the registered
manufacturer shall be liable to pay excise tax under section 3, on the excisable goods, as
if such goods had been entered for home use from the warehouse of the registered
manufacturer, during the tax period in which the deficiency arose.
(2) A registered manufacturer shall notify the Comptroller of any discrepancy
between his or her actual and recorded inventory within seven calendar days of becoming
aware of the discrepancy.

### 5.

Assessment of excise tax
(1) Where—
(a)

The First Schedule specifies a rate of excise tax payable by reference to a
quantity measured by volume or weight;

(b)

goods are imported or removed from a warehouse in a container intended for
sale with, or of a kind usually sold with, the goods in a retail sale; and

(c)

the container is marked, labelled, or commonly sold as containing, or
commonly reputed to contain, a specific quantity of such goods,

the container is deemed to contain not less than that specific quantity, for the purpose of
determining the excise tax payable in respect of the goods.
(2) Where the First Schedule specifies a rate of excise tax payable by reference to the
value of excisable goods, the value of the goods shall be—
(a)

where the goods are imported, the sum of—
(i) the value of the goods determined under the Customs Act for the
purpose of assessing import duty on the goods at ad valorem rates,
whether or not such import duty is payable on the goods, and

(ii) to the extent that they are not included in subparagraph (i), the amount
of any taxes, duties, fees, or other charges payable on the entry of the
goods into the State, (other than excise tax or VAT); or

where the goods are manufactured in the State and are sold at the time they
are entered for home use, the consideration paid or payable in relation to the
sale, minus, (if the value so calculated includes VAT), the tax fraction of that
value; or

(c)

where the goods are manufactured in the State and are not sold at the time
they are entered for home use, the fair market value of the goods at the time
they are entered for home use, minus, (if the value so calculated includes
VAT), the tax fraction of that value.

### 6.

Relief for goods damaged or destroyed
Excise tax shall not be imposed on excisable goods that have not been entered for
home use from the manufacturer’s warehouse, if the goods—
(a)

are destroyed by fire or other natural causes; or

(b)

have deteriorated or been damaged while stored in the registered
manufacturer’s approved warehouse and have been securely disposed of in a
manner satisfactory to the Comptroller.

### 7.

Exemption for goods exported before entry for home use
Excise tax shall not be imposed in respect of excisable goods that are exported if—
(a)

in the case of imported goods, they are entered for export without first being
entered for home use; or

(b)

in the case of goods manufactured in the State, they are removed from a
warehouse and immediately placed under the control of the Comptroller of
Customs and entered for export.

### 8.

Temporary importation
(1) The Comptroller of Customs may grant permission for the importation of
excisable goods without payment of excise tax if—
(a)

the Comptroller of Customs is satisfied that the goods—
(i) are imported only for temporary use or a temporary purpose,
(ii) are the bona fide property, and are for the exclusive use, of a person
temporarily in the State, and
(iii) will be exported within three months from the grant of the permission;
and

(b)

the person to whom the permission is granted deposits with the Comptroller
of Customs an amount equal to the excise tax payable on the goods.

(2) Where goods imported under subsection (1) are not exported within the period
specified in subsection (1)(a)(iii), the Comptroller of Customs shall bring to account as
excise tax, the deposit made under subsection (1)(b).
(3) Notwithstanding subsections (1) and (2), the Comptroller of Customs may, if he or
she considers it appropriate, allow a further period for the export of the goods.
(4) Where goods referred to in subsection (1) are exported within the time specified in
subsection (1), or within a further period allowed under subsection (3), the Comptroller of
Customs shall refund the deposit made under subsection (1)(b), in accordance with the
established customs procedure, with respect to the payment of refunds.

## PART III

Registration and Administration

### 9.

Prohibition
(1) A person shall not manufacture excisable goods in any warehouse unless—

the person is the holder of a registration certificate issued by the
Comptroller, and has entered into a security arrangement with the
Comptroller, pursuant to the provisions of this Act;

(b)

the warehouse in which the person manufactures such excisable goods, has
been declared by the Comptroller to be an approved warehouse, pursuant to
the provisions of this Act.

(2) A person who contravenes the provisions of subsection (1) commits an offence
and is liable on summary conviction—
(a)

to a fine not exceeding ten thousand dollars, or a term of imprisonment not
exceeding two years; and

(b)

to pay the excise tax on all such excisable goods manufactured by him from
the date on which registration under section 10 was due.

### 10.

Application for registration
(1) A person who intends to manufacture excisable goods in Grenada, shall make an
application to the Comptroller, in the prescribed manner, to be a registered manufacturer
and to have the warehouse in which he intends to manufacture and store excisable goods be
declared an approved warehouse, pursuant to the provisions of the Act.
(2) An application under subsection (1), shall be made no later than thirty days prior
to the date on which the applicant intends to commence manufacturing excisable goods,
and shall contain such particulars as may be prescribed.

### 11.

Grant or refusal of application
(1) Where the Comptroller is satisfied that an applicant will be carrying on the
business of manufacturing excisable goods in the State, and have met the prescribed
requirements with respect to the establishment of a warehouse, the Comptroller shall,
within twenty-one days of receipt of the application, approve the application, and subject
to section 12, issue to the applicant, a registration certificate declaring—
(a)

the applicant to be a registered manufacturer; and

(b)

the warehouse from which the applicant intends to manufacture and store
excisable goods, to be an approved warehouse.

(2) Where the Comptroller is not satisfied that the applicant has met the requirements
under the Act, he shall, within twenty-one days of receipt of the application, notify the
applicant, in writing, of the decision, the reasons for arriving at the decision, and the right
of the applicant to have the decision reviewed, pursuant to the provisions of section 29.
(3) A registration certificate may contain such terms, conditions or restrictions as the
Comptroller deems necessary, and shall be evidence that—
(a)

the person named in it is a duly registered manufacturer for the purposes of
this Act;

the warehouse named therein, is an approved warehouse for the depositing,
keeping, manufacturing, or securing of excisable goods for the purposes of
this Act.

### 12.

Lodging of security arrangement
(1) The Comptroller shall not issue a registration certificate under section 11, unless
the person to whom the registration certificate is to be issued, enters into such security
arrangement with the Comptroller.
(2) The security arrangement referred to under subsection (1) shall remain in effect so
long as the person remains a registered manufacturer, and shall be such amount, and in
form, as may be determined by the Comptroller.

### 13.

Cancellation of registration
(1) The Comptroller may, by issuing notice in writing to a registered manufacturer,
cancel the registration certificate of the registered manufacturer, if he or she—
(a)

fails to comply with the terms and conditions of his or her registration
certificate; or

(b)

fails to comply with the provisions of this Act.

(2) The Comptroller shall not cancel a registration certificate under subsection (1),
unless he has given the registered manufacturer an opportunity to be heard.
(3) Where a registered manufacturer ceases to manufacture and store excisable goods,
he shall, within fourteen days of such cessation, notify the Comptroller, in writing, of that
fact, stating—
(a)

the date on which he has ceased to manufacture and store excisable goods;
and

(b)

the date on which he anticipates that all excisable goods will be entered for
home use, or exported from his or her approved warehouse.

(4) Where the Comptroller receives a notification under subsection (3), he or she
shall, by notice in writing, cancel the registration certificate of the registered
manufacturer, and such cancellation shall take effect from the first day on which there are
no remaining excisable goods in the approved warehouse of the registered manufacturer.
(5) Where the registration certificate of a registered manufacturer is cancelled, the
registered manufacturer shall immediately return the registration certificate and any
certified copies thereof to the Comptroller.
(6) If, immediately after the cancellation of a registered manufacturer’s registration
certificate under subsection (4), there are excisable goods remaining in the approved
warehouse of the registered manufacturer, these goods are deemed to have been entered
for home use on the preceding day.
(7) The cancellation of a manufacturer’s registration, does not affect any obligation or
liability of the manufacturer under this Act, in respect of anything done or omitted to be
done by the manufacturer, while the manufacturer was registered, including the
obligations to pay excise tax and file returns.

### 14.

Duties of registered manufacturer
(1) Subject to the provision of this Act, a registered manufacturer shall—
(a)

store all excisable goods on which excise tax has not been paid in his or her
approved warehouse;

(b)

enter excisable goods for home use only from his or her approved
warehouse; and

(c)

shall complete at the time of entry of excisable goods for home use, such
records, forms or documents, in such manner and form, as the Comptroller
shall require in relation to the entry.

(2) A registered manufacturer shall inform the Comptroller, in writing, when—

a person, other than the registered manufacturer, has succeeded him in the
manufacturing of excisable goods;

the name, or description of the business carried on by the registered
manufacturer in relation to excisable goods has changed,

there is an enlargement, or substantial variation in the nature or quantity of excisable
goods manufactured by him.
(3) A notice under subsection (2) shall be in writing in the form approved by the
Comptroller, and shall be served on the Comptroller, no later than fourteen days prior to
the occurrence of the events stipulated under the said subsection.

### 15.

Persons manufacturing excisable goods prior to commencement of Act
(1) A person who manufactures excisable goods prior to the commencement of this
Act, and who continues to do so after the commencement of this Act, shall within one
month after the commencement of the Act make an application under section 10 to be a
registered manufacturer and to have any warehouse at which the person manufactures
excisable goods declared to be an approved warehouse.
(2) A manufacturer to whom subsection (1) applies shall not commit an offence under
section 9(2) or Part V if the action, events or activity to which the offence relates occurs
before the person is required by subsection (1) to apply for registration and approvals, or
during the period between the date on which the application is made and the date on
which the Comptroller—
(a)

issues the person with a registration certificate and approves the person’s
warehouse; or

(b)

notifies the person that the application has been refused.

(3) Subsection (2) shall not relieve the manufacturer of the obligation to pay excise
tax under this Act.
(4) A manufacturer to whom subsection (1) applies is liable to pay excise tax on
excisable goods manufactured in the State if they are entered for home use on or after the
date of commencement of the Act irrespective of whether the goods were manufactured
before the date of commencement of the Act.

## PART IV

Collection and Recovery

### 16.

Excise tax returns
(1) A registered manufacturer shall file an excise tax return for each tax period, no
later than twenty-one days after the end of the tax period, whether or not any excise tax is
payable for the tax period to which the return relates.
(2) An excise tax return required under subsection (1) shall—
(a)

be filed with the Comptroller;

(b)

be in the prescribed form;

(c)

provide such information as the form requires in relation to excisable goods
entered for home use by the manufacturer, during the tax period to which the
return relates; and

(d)

contain such other information as may be specified in that form.

### 17.

Payment of excise tax by registered manufacturers
(1) Where excise tax is payable by a registered manufacturer under section 3, in relation
to excisable goods entered for home use from his or her approved warehouse during a tax
period, the excise tax shall be paid to the Comptroller no later than twenty-one days after
the end of the tax period.
(2) The liability to pay excise tax arises by operation of this section, and shall not
depend on the Comptroller making an assessment of the excise tax due, nor on the
registered manufacturer making a return in accordance with section 16.
(3) A registered manufacturer shall not enter excisable goods for home use from his
or her approved warehouse, if the entry would result in the amount of excise tax payable
by the manufacturer, but not yet paid, exceeds the amount of the security arrangement
under section 12 upon registration, unless—
(a)

the Comptroller, on application in writing by the manufacturer, gives
permission for the entry;

(b)

the registered manufacturer, subject to the approved agreement of the
Comptroller, increases the amount of the security arrangement given under
section 12; or

(c)

in any other case, the registered manufacturer pays the excise tax payable on
the goods, before the excisable goods are entered for home use.

(4) Permission granted under subsection (3)(a) may be continuous or one-off.
(5) For the purposes of subsection (3), excise tax payable includes, excise tax that has
not been paid due to the fact that the time allowed for payment under subsection (1) has
not elapsed.
(6) For the avoidance of doubt, where a manufacturer who is required to pay excise
tax in accordance with this section, makes one or more payments of excise tax payable
for a tax period, before the twentieth day of the following tax period, including where an
amount is paid under subsection (3)(c), each such payment reduces the amount payable
on the twentieth day of the following tax period.

### 18.

Payment of excise tax by importers
(1) The excise tax payable by a person under section 3 in relation to excisable goods
imported into the State, shall be paid to the Comptroller of Customs before the goods are
entered for home use.
(2) For the purposes of subsection (1)—
(a)

a passenger who imports any baggage for which no entry is required under
the Customs Act, is deemed to have entered the baggage for home use at the
time the baggage is delivered to the passenger in the State; and

(b)

the addressee of goods imported by post for which no entry is required under
the Customs Act, is deemed to have entered the goods for home use at the
time the goods are delivered to the addressee.

(3) Where a person who imports excisable goods is required to enter the goods for
home use under the Customs Act, but does not do so, excise tax is payable at the time
when the goods are imported.
(4) For the purposes of collecting and enforcing the payment of excise tax imposed
under this Act on excisable goods imported into the State, the Customs Act and any other
enactment relating to the import of goods shall apply, as if excise tax were an import
duty.

### 19.

Interest on late payments

(1) A registered manufacturer who fails to pay part or all of the excise tax payable by
the due date for payment under this Act, shall be liable to pay interest on the amount
unpaid, at the rate prescribed under section 68 of the VAT Act, for each tax period or part
of a tax period during which it remains unpaid.
(2) Interest payable under subsection (1) shall be recoverable under this Act, as if it
were excise tax payable under this Act.

### 20.

Refunds for overpayments
(1) If a person has paid excise tax, and the amount paid exceeds the amount that this
person is required to pay under this Act, the person shall be entitled to a refund equal to
the amount of the excess paid.
(2) An application for a refund under this section shall be made to the Comptroller,
within three years from the end of the month in which the goods are entered for home use.

### 21.

Refunds of excise tax paid on raw materials
(1) A registered manufacturer who uses excisable goods as raw materials in the
manufacture of other excisable goods in the State, shall be entitled to a refund of—
(a)

the excise tax, if any, paid by the manufacturer under section 3(1)(a) or (b),
in respect of an entry of the raw materials for home use; or

(b)

any excise tax that the Comptroller is satisfied was paid, by a person from
whom the registered manufacturer acquired the raw materials, in respect of
an entry for home use of those raw materials.

(2) An application for a refund under this section shall be made to the Comptroller,
within twelve months from the date on which the excisable goods were used as raw
materials in the manufacture of other excisable goods.

### 22.

Refunds of excise tax paid on exports
(1) A person who imported excisable goods into the State, shall be entitled to a refund
of the excise tax paid by the person when the goods were entered for home use, if the
goods are in compliance with the conditions for payment of a drawback of duties under
the Customs Act.
(2) A registered manufacturer shall be entitled to a refund of the excise tax paid by
the person when the goods were entered for home use, if the Comptroller is satisfied that
the goods were exported from the State without first being used in the State.
(3) An application for a refund under this section shall be made within twelve months
from the date on which the goods are exported, or put on board the ship or aircraft in
which they are exported.

### 23.

Refund procedures
(1) An application for a refund of excise tax under this Part shall be filed with the
Comptroller, whether or not the excise tax to be refunded was paid to the Comptroller or
Comptroller of Customs, and shall—
(a)

be filed in the approved form and manner; and

(b)

contain the information specified in the form.

(2) If a person who is entitled to a refund under subsection (1) makes an application for
the refund within the time stipulated under this Act, the Comptroller shall pay the refund, or
the amount, if any, remaining after the refund has been applied in accordance with
subsection (3), within two tax periods from the date on which the application is filed.

(3) If a refund is payable to a person under this Part, the Comptroller may apply part
or all of the refund firstly in reduction of any excise tax or interest due and payable by the
person under this Act, then against any other taxes or duties collected by the Comptroller
or the Comptroller of Customs, including any repealed taxes payable by the person.
(4) For the purpose of subsection (3), the Comptroller shall not pay a refund without
first consulting the Comptroller of Customs to determine whether the person applying for
the refund has any outstanding liabilities for amounts referred to in that subsection.
(5) Where the Comptroller does not pay a refund within the time required under this
section, interest is payable by that Comptroller at the rate prescribed for the purposes of
section 52(4) of the VAT Act.
(6) If an amount is refunded to a person in error, the person is required to repay the
amount, together with interest at the rate prescribed under section 68 of the VAT Act,
starting from 14 calendar days after the person is notified by the Comptroller, and both the
amount and the interest are recoverable from the person as excise tax payable under this Act.

## PART V

Offences and Penalties

### 24.

Application of fixed penalty
The provisions of Part XVI of the VAT in relation to fixed penalties, shall apply mutatis
mutandis to fixed penalties imposed under this Part.

### 25.

Manufacturing excisable goods outside approved warehouse
(1) A person shall not manufacture excisable goods at any other place other than at an
approved warehouse.
(2) A person shall not store excisable goods on which excise tax has not been paid, at
any place other than in an approved warehouse.
(3) A person shall not remove at any time, a quantity of excisable goods from his or
her warehouse which exceeds the amount of security he was required to lodge under
section 12.
(4) A person who contravenes the provision of subsection (1), (2) or (3), commits an
offence, and is liable, on summary conviction, to a fine not exceeding one hundred thousand
dollars, or to a term of imprisonment not exceeding two years.
(5) A fixed penalty of sixty thousand dollars may be charged for the offences referred
to in subsections (2), (3) and (4).

### 26.

Failure to complete documents
(1) A person shall not enter excisable goods for home use without completing such
records, forms or documents as required by the Comptroller.
(2) A person who contravenes the provisions of subsection (1) commits an offence,
and is liable, on summary conviction, to a fine not exceeding ten thousand dollars, or to a
term of imprisonment not exceeding six months.
(3) A fixed penalty of five thousand dollars may be charged for the offence referred to
in subsection (2).

### 27.

Failure to enter into security arrangement

(1) A person who manufactures excisable goods in the State, or enters such goods for
home use, without having entered into a security arrangement in accordance with the
provisions of this Act, commits an offence, and is liable, on summary conviction to a fine
not exceeding one hundred thousand dollars, or a term of imprisonment not exceeding
two years.
(2) A fixed penalty of ten thousand dollars may be charged for the offence referred to
in subsection (1).

### 28.

Failure to file return
A manufacturer who fails to file an excise tax return on the day prescribed under
section 16, is liable to be charged a late fee of—
(a)

$1,000 for the first tax period or part of a tax period during which the return
is unfiled; and

(b)

$2,000 per tax period for each subsequent tax period or part of thereof
during which the return remains unfiled.

## PART VI

Appeals and Reviews

### 29.

Appeals and reviews
(1) The following are reviewable decisions for the purposes of Part XVIII of the VAT
Act—
(a)

a decision under section 4(1) to impose excise tax on goods for which a
deficiency has arisen;

(b)

a decision under section 11 not to grant an application for registration, or to
impose terms, conditions, or restrictions on a registered manufacturer;

(c)

a decision under section 13 to cancel, or cancel, a registration, including a
decision as to the date of effect of a cancellation;

(d)

a decision under Part IV not to pay a refund, or a decision as to the amount
of a refund payable;

(e)

a decision under section 12 with respect to the entering of a security
arrangement.

## PART VII

Administration

### 30.

Administration
(1) The Comptroller has the responsibility for the general administration and
enforcement of this Act and for carrying out the provisions of this Act in relation to
excisable goods manufactured in the State.
(2) The Comptroller of Customs has the responsibility for the general administration
and enforcement of this Act, and for carrying out the provisions of this Act in respect of
excisable goods imported into the State.
(3) Unless a contrary intention appears, in relation to excisable goods imported into
the State, the provisions of the customs laws, including but not limited to the Customs
Act and any enactment amending or replacing that Act, apply, so far as they are relevant,

and with such exceptions, modifications, and adaptations as are necessary, as if excise tax
were an import duty.
(4) The Comptroller of Customs may exercise any power conferred on him by the
customs laws, as if a reference to duty in that legislation included a reference to excise tax
charged under this Act on an import of goods.
(5) The Comptroller may exercise any power conferred by the VAT Act, as if a
reference to VAT in that Act included a reference to excise tax charged under this Act.
(6) For the avoidance of doubt, section 57 of the VAT applies in respect of both the
Comptroller and the Comptroller of Customs, as if it were part of this Act, and as if
references to the taxes or tax laws included a reference to excise tax and to this Act.

### 31.

Delegation
(1) The Comptroller may delegate in writing, a duty, power, or function conferred on
him under this Act, other than the power of delegation conferred by this subsection.
(2) A delegation under this section, shall not prevent the exercise of such power, duty,
or function by the Comptroller, and does not relieve him from responsibility for the
actions of the person to whom the delegation is made.
(3) The Comptroller may, at any time, revoke in writing, a delegation under this section.

### 32.

Debt due to the Crown
(1) Any excise tax or interest imposed on any person under this Act—
(a)

is, when it becomes payable, recoverable as a debt owing to the Crown; and

(b)

may be sued for and recovered in a court of competent jurisdiction, by the
Crown.

(2) In any suit under subsection (1), the production of a certificate signed by the
Comptroller or the Comptroller of Customs stating—
(a)

the name and address of the person who is the defendant in the suit; and

(b)

the amount of excise tax, interest, or penalty, as the case may be, due and
payable by the person,

shall be conclusive evidence that that amount is payable by, and due from, the person.

### 33.

Regulations
(1) The Minister may make Regulations—
(a)

for any matter that this Act requires or allows to be prescribed by
Regulations; and

(b)

for any matter that is necessary or convenient to be prescribed, in order to
better carry out or give effect to the provisions of this Act.

(2) Without limiting the generality of subsection (1), the Regulations may provide
for—
(a)

transitional or saving provisions consequent on the coming into operation of
this Act;

(b)

the duties and functions of officers and other persons appointed or employed
under this Act;

(c)

the form of returns to be made, the particulars to be included in the returns,
and the persons by whom, and the time when or within which, such returns
are to be made.

(3) The Minister may, by Order published in the Gazette and approved by affirmative
resolution of the Parliament, amend the First Schedule and the Second Schedule.

### 34.

Repeal
(1) The Excise Act, Chapter 94, and the Excise (General Commodities) Act, Chapter
95, are repealed.
(2) All provisions of the Acts repealed by subsection (1), including but not limited to
the rules governing the levy, payment, assessment, reporting, and recovery of the taxes
imposed under those Acts, continue to apply to goods taxed under those Act prior to their
repeal.

## PART I

Preliminary

---

### 1.

Citation
These Regulations may be cited as the Excise Tax Regulations.

### 2.

Definitions
In these Regulations, unless the context otherwise requires—
“Act” means the Excise Tax Act, Chapter 94;
“tax-paid excisable goods” means—
(a)

excisable goods in respect of which excise tax has been paid; and

(b)

excisable goods manufactured in the State that have been entered for home
use but for which excise tax has not yet been paid because the due date for
payment under section 17 of the Act has not arrived; and

“tax-unpaid excisable goods” means excisable goods that are not tax-paid excisable
goods.

## PART II

Registration of Manufacturers and Approval of Warehouses

### 3.

Applications for registration under the Act
For the purposes of section 10, an application for registration shall be accompanied by
the following—

documents evidencing the applicant’s intention to manufacture excisable
goods including—
(i) copies of the articles of association or similar founding documents,
(ii) the names, addresses, qualifications, and other identifying information
in relation to the directors, if any and senior employees of the
applicant,
(iii) the names and identifying details of the accountants, lawyers, and
agents, if any, of the applicant;

(b)

evidence of the applicant’s ability to maintain proper accounts and records;

(c)

evidence of the applicant’s compliance with his or her obligations under
other taxation laws;

(d)

a procedure statement or manual, acceptable to the Comptroller, setting out
how tax-unpaid excisable goods will be handled, secured, tracked, and
monitored while in the warehouse, and on entry into and removal from the
warehouse and specifying the persons, or classes of persons, who will have
access to the goods, and under what conditions;

(e)

in relation to the particulars of the application for approval of a warehouse—
(i) the configuration and secure perimeters of the warehouse,
(ii) the location and physical details (size, materials of construction, etc)
of the area to be designated as the warehouse, and
(iii) the warehouse’s external security (fences, etc.) and internal security
(entries, exits, alarms, lights, etc.).

### 4.

Requirements for approval of warehouses
(1) Pursuant to section 11 of the Act, in order for a warehouse to be declared an
approved warehouse, the warehouse must be controlled or operated by the applicant making
the application to be a registered manufacturer, and the applicant who shall satisfy the
Comptroller that there are adequate checks and balances in place so that the applicant may
determine—
(a)

the nature of the goods manufactured or stored in the warehouse;

(b)

the estimated value of those goods, and the manufacturer’s estimated excise
tax liability in respect of a subsequent removal of those goods from the
warehouse; and

(c)

the expected method of disposal of the goods by way of local sales, export
sales, use in the manufacture of other excisable goods, or other means of
disposal, including an estimate of the anticipated percentages of each type of
disposal.

(2) The registered manufacturer shall provide proof that tax-paid excisable goods will
not be stored in the warehouse except as allowed under regulation 5(2).
(3) This Regulation shall not prevent the Comptroller from approving only part of
particular premises as an approved warehouse if that part of the premises is clearly
delineated and secured to the satisfaction of the Comptroller.

### 5.

Operating an approved warehouse
(1) Only the following persons may enter a warehouse at which tax-unpaid excisable
goods are stored—

an officer or employee of the registered manufacturer, or of a carrier
delivering or removing goods from the warehouse;

(b)

any other person authorised by the manufacturer;

(c)

the Comptroller, or an officer to whom the Comptroller has delegated such
powers under the Act; or

(d)

any other person to whom the Comptroller has given written approval to
enter such premises.

(2) Tax-paid excisable goods may not be stored in an approved warehouse unless—
(a)

the goods are to be used as raw materials in manufacturing other excisable
goods; and

(b)

the tax-paid excisable goods to be used as raw materials are separately stored
and secured away from the place where tax-unpaid excisable goods are
stored.

(3) A registered manufacturer shall provide and maintain at his or her approved
warehouse—
(a)

adequate lighting;

(b)

correct weights and scales; and

(c)

all personnel necessary for—
(i) weighing excisable goods received into, excisable goods
manufactured in, and excisable goods removed from the approved
warehouse, and
(ii) taking stock of all excisable goods in the warehouse.

(4) A registered manufacturer shall prepare and retain any records and documentation
required by the Comptroller in relation to the deposit, keeping manufacture or removal of
excisable goods from an approved warehouse of the manufacturer.
(5) An approved warehouse, and any records the registered manufacturer is required
to keep, shall be subject to inspection by the Comptroller at any time and without prior
notice.

## PART III

Removal of Excisable Goods fromApproved Warehouses

### 6.

Records of excisable goods removed from a warehouse
(1) Tax-unpaid excisable goods may only be removed from an approved warehouse
with authorisation from the Comptroller and in accordance with the terms and conditions
imposed by the Comptroller on the registered manufacturer from whose approved
warehouse the goods are to be removed.
(2) The Comptroller may specify that a manufacturer may remove tax-unpaid
excisable goods from a warehouse only in the physical presence of, and under the
supervision of, an officer of the Comptroller.
(3) Requirements relating to the removal of tax-unpaid excisable goods under
subregulation (2) may include a “two-key” system under which the officer and the
registered manufacturer each hold keys to the area of the approved warehouse in which

the tax-unpaid excisable goods to be removed are held, and both keys are required to
open any lock that secures entry into that area.

### 7.

Permission to remove goods to another approved warehouse
(1) A registered manufacturer may apply to the Comptroller for permission to remove
tax-unpaid excisable goods from an approved warehouse to another approved warehouse.
(2) The Comptroller may approve an application under subregulation (1), subject to
such terms and conditions as the Comptroller may specify, if—
(a)

both approved warehouses are operated and controlled by the registered
manufacturer; and

(b)

the registered manufacturer enters into an agreement with the Comptroller
regarding the means by which the goods will be transported between the
approved warehouses, and specifying the security arrangements for ensuring
that the goods reach their destination.

(3) An approval to remove tax-unpaid excisable goods from one approved warehouse
to another may be a continuing approval or a one-off approval.
(4) Except in situations covered by regulation 5(2), permission may not be given
under this Regulation to remove tax-unpaid excisable goods to a warehouse at which taxpaid excisable goods are stored.
(5) Where a registered manufacturer removes tax-unpaid excisable goods from an
approved warehouse (“the first warehouse”) to another approved warehouse (“the second
warehouse”) in accordance with an approval given under this Regulation, and in
compliance with the conditions set out in the agreement referred to in subregulation 2(b)—
(a)

for the purpose of determining if and when the goods are entered for home
use, the removal from the first warehouse is treated as if it did not occur and
the removal, if any, from the second warehouse is treated as if it were the
first removal of those goods from a warehouse; and

(b)

if the registered manufacturer is unable to prove to the satisfaction of the
Comptroller that the goods which left the first warehouse were the same
goods, if any, that were delivered to the second warehouse, subregulation (a)
does not apply.

## PART IV

Offences and Penalties

### 8.

Prohibition on dilution of alcoholic beverages
(1) A manufacturer who, otherwise than in the process of manufacturing other
excisable goods in an approved warehouse, dilutes alcoholic beverages falling under
Customs Tariff Heading 22 as provided for First Schedule of the Act, after recording or
declaring the quantity manufactured for the purposes of determining the manufacturer’s
excise tax liability, commits an offence under these Regulations and is liable, on summary
conviction, to a fine not exceeding twenty-five thousand dollars, or to a term of
imprisonment not exceeding three years.
(2) For the avoidance of doubt—

diluting alcoholic beverages falling under Customs Tariff Heading 22 as
provided and then repackaging, or re-sealing, the beverages constitutes the
manufacture of other excisable goods; and

diluting alcoholic beverages when they are served in glasses or other
containers in which they are sold for consumption at the time and place
where they are served, for example, by mixing spirits with carbonated drinks
or
fruit juices, or mixing wine with fruit juices, shall not constitute
manufacture and is not a contravention of this Regulation.

### 9.

Offences in relation to warehouses
A manufacturer who stores tax-paid excisable goods in an approved warehouse in
contravention of regulation 5(2), or who fails to keep tax-paid excisable goods that are
raw materials separated from tax-unpaid excisable goods as required by
regulation 5(2)(b), commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction, to a fine not
exceeding ten thousand dollars, or to a term of imprisonment not exceeding two years.

## PART V

Objections and Appeals

### 10.

Reviewable decisions
The following decisions made under these Regulations are reviewable decisions for
the purposes of Part XVIII of the VAT Act—
(a)

a decision under regulation 6 not to authorise the removal of tax-unpaid
excisable goods from an approved warehouse, or to impose terms and
conditions on the removal; and

(b)

a decision under regulation 7 not to authorise the removal of tax-unpaid
excisable goods from one approved warehouse to another, or to impose
terms and conditions in relation to the removal.

## PART VI

Records and Returns

### 11.

Books and records to be kept
(1) For the purposes of the Act and in accordance with Part XIX of the VAT Act, a
registered manufacturer is required to keep such accurate books and records as are
necessary and relevant to explaining the manufacturer’s excise tax returns and supporting
the manufacturer’s determination of its excise tax liabilities under the Act.
(2) Without limiting subregulation (1), a registered manufacturer shall maintain, and
be able to produce on demand, such records as are required to evidence at any time—
(a)

all excisable goods that have entered each approved warehouse of the
manufacturer;

(b)

all excisable goods that have been removed from the warehouse;

(c)

all other goods that have entered and been removed from the warehouse, if
those goods are raw materials used in the manufacture of excisable goods at
the warehouse, or are used to contain, store, label, or measure excisable
goods held or manufactured at the warehouse; and

(d)

all excisable goods stored in the warehouse (including the physical location
of the goods within the warehouse).

(3) The records referred to in subregulation (2) shall include appropriate, relevant,
and accurate links to production and sales records, and to excise tax returns.
