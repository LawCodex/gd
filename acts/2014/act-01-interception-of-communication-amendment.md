AN ACT to amend the Interception of Communications Act
No. 22 of 2013.

BE IT ENACTED by the Queen’s Most Excellent

and the House of Representatives of Grenada, and by the
Authority of the same as follows–

---

### 1.

This Act may be cited as the
INTERCEPTION OF COMMUNICATIONS
(AMENDMENT) ACT, 2014.

### 2.

In this Act–
“principal Act” means the Interception of Communications Act No. 22 of 2013.

Short title.

Interpretation.

Amendment
of section 5 of
principal Act.

2014

### 3.

Sub-section (2) of section 5 of the principal Act is
hereby repealed and substituted with the following new subsection–
“(a) the communication is intercepted in accordance
with an interception warrant issued pursuant to
section 8 by a judge;
(b) subject to sub-section (3), that person has reasonable
ground for believing that the person to whom or by
whom the communication is transmitted consents
to the interception;
(c) the communication is intercepted as an ordinary
incident to the provision of communications
services or to the enforcement of any law in force
in relation to the use of those services;
(d) the communication is intercepted as an ordinary
incident to the provision of communications
services or to the enforcement of any law in force
in relation to those services;
(e) the interception is of a communication made
through a communications network that is
configured as to render the communication readily
accessible to the general public;
(f) the interception is of a communication transmitted
and received within an internal network that is used
to serve the needs of the company or household
and is done by a person who has–
(i)

a right to control the operation of use of the
network; or

(ii) expressed or implied consent of a person
referred to in subparagraph (i).
(g) the communication is one sent by or intended for a
person who has consented to the interception; and
(h) the communication intercepted by an authorised
officer–
(i)

for the purpose of an emergency;

(ii) for the purpose of preventing death or injury
or any damage, to a person’s physical or
mental health;
(iii) for the purpose of mitigating any injury or
damage to a person’s physical or mental
health;
(iv) in the interest of national Security;
(v) for the prevention or detection of an
offence for which the penalty on conviction
is imprisonment for ten years or more,
and includes an offence where death,
imprisonment for the remainder of a person’s
natural life or life imprisonment is a penalty
fixed by law;
(vi) for the purpose of giving effect to the
provisions of any international mutual
assistance agreement,
and any communication so intercepted may be used for the
purpose of an application under section 6 or 13, but shall not
be admissible as evidence in any criminal proceedings.”

Amendment of
section 21 of
principal Act.

2014

### 4.

Section 21 of the principal Act is amended by–
(a) inserting after sub-section (1) the following new
sub-section–
“(1a) An authorised officer who intercepts
a communication in pursuance of section
5(2) (h), shall ensure that any record of
information obtained from the interception
that is not related to the objective of the
interception is destroyed immediately.”
(b) inserting after sub-section (4) the following new
sub-section–
“(4a) A person required to destroy any
record of information in accordance with
this section who fails to do so commits an
offence and is liable on summary conviction
to a fine not exceeding twenty thousand
dollars or to a term of imprisonment not
exceeding two years.”
