AN ACT to amend the Constitution of Grenada so that after
other amendments are carried out Chapters and Sections
would be repositioned and renumbered and references to
existing sections would be altered correspondingly if
necessary in both the body of the Constitution and in the
Schedules.
[ By Proclamation ].
BE IT ENACTED by the Queen’s Most Excellent Majesty,
by and with the advice and consent of the Senate and the
House of Representatives of Grenada, and by the authority of
the same as follows–

---

### 1.

—(1)This Act may be cited as the
CONSTITUTION OF GRENADA (RESTRUCTURING)
(AMENDMENT) ACT, 2016.

Short title,
commencement and
alteration of
the Constitution.

(2) This Act shall come into effect on such date as may
be appointed by Proclamation issued by the Governor-General
and published in the Gazette; and different dates may be
appointed for the various provisions of this Act.
(3) This Act shall be read and construed as altering the
Constitution.
Interpretation.

### 2.

In this Act–
“Constitution” means the Constitution of Grenada
which is set out in Schedule 1 to the Grenada
Constitution Order, 1973.

Insertion of
section in the
Constitution.

### 3.

The Constitution is altered by inserting after section 39
the following–
“Formal alterations to this Constitution
39A. —(1) Upon enactment and commencement
of a Bill to alter this Constitution in accordance
with section 39, and subject to the provisions of
this section, the Attorney-General may make the
following formal alterations to the provisions of
this Constitution–
(a)

repositioning, rearranging or renumbering the provisions of this Constitution in
any group or sequence as may be more
convenient;

(b)

altering the references to provisions of
this Constitution that have been renumbered under paragraph (a), in other
provisions of this Constitution and in
the Schedules to this Constitution;

(c)

amending grammatical and typographical errors; and

(d)

amending terms and expressions to
achieve the objects of the Bill to alter
this Constitution.

(2) Nothing in subsection (1) shall be taken to confer on
the Attorney-General the power to make any alteration that
affects the substance of this Constitution or any part of it.
(3) An alteration to this Constitution under this section
shall not come into effect unless and until–
(a)

the alteration has been approved by a
resolution of both Houses of Parliament
supported by the votes of a majority of all
the members of each House; and

(b)

the resolution approving the alteration has
been published in the Gazette.”.

Passed by the House of Representatives this 21st day of June, 2016.
WILLAN A. THOMPSON
Clerk to the House of Representatives.
Passed by the Senate this 13th day of July, 2016.
WILLAN A. THOMPSON
